## Core engine
PARALLEL LINES THAT FEEL THEIR WAY AROUND THEIR SUBJECT: goodbad.png's recurring system sends a family of parallel hairlines across a field and lets an invisible mask (a void, an obstacle, a terrain ridge, a map's geography) bend, bunch, and gap them. The drawing is the tension between the regular grid and the thing it wraps. The watchable payoff is reading an invisible shape purely from how the lines behave around it: voids (f00, f06, f10) make streamlines clump and part like water around a stone; chevron folds (f11) stack lines into dense ridges; real geography (f05) replaces the mask with OpenStreetMap data. Tone comes from stipple dots and line density (f04), color arrives as bold two-tone layers or as saturated gradient grounds (f01, f08, f13), and the finished works are physically drawn by the HP 7550A on paper with a pen change per color pass.

## Evolution
- 2020-01 to 02, dense fields and first plots: white-on-sienna line fields wrapping a central void (f00), spiky coral overlays on contour terrain (f01), black-paper gold-ink parametric fans (f03 "Exhibit C"), two-color stipple rosettes (f04). Everything is system-driven and physical.
- 2020-02 to 03, data era: Somerville MA plotted from OSM with anvaka's tool (f05), city-street series of Boston and Somerville, "First plots being sent out!" Print-like map works sold and shipped.
- 2020-03 to 05, line-field heyday: Sailor Blue (f06), Roots (f07), Brite lites (f08), two-tone chevron terrain (f11), the Tame Impala typography poster (f10), twisted ribbon bands on gradients (f13), dune dash-fields (f17). Minimal grounds, one or two ink colors, maximum air.
- 2020-04 to 09, application era: Lost Cities card-deck remix with plotted motifs on physical cards (f12), framed map prints "in the wild" with gold ink on black paper (f15), "Made some prints today! These are going to four different states".
- 2020-10, hardware reveal and quiet: the HP 7550A goes up for sale (f18); posting stops in the accessible set from 2020-10-29.
- 2021 to 2022, coda: one 2021-06-03 "In the wild" carousel, then the final accessible post, 2022-03-06: eight layers of cut paper stacked into a Zion canyon relief (f19), the iso-contour system rebuilt as a physical papercut.
