# Remedios Varo: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #317.

**Site:** Remedios Varo Uranga (Angeles, Spain, December 16, 1908 -- Mexico City, October 8, 1963; she claimed 1913 from the late 1930s onward, a femme-enfant fiction rejected by both wikipedias and the catalogs). No Instagram: verified absence, not a gap. Spanish surrealist painter, Mexico from late 1941 to her death; she never took Mexican citizenship (unlike her friend Leonora Carrington). Advertising illustrator for Bayer in Mexico and mosquito/malaria poster work in Venezuela 1947-49 as the day job; the mature painted oeuvre is concentrated in the last decade, 1952-1963, after her marriage to Walter Gruen ended commercial work. Identity anchors: the tower-workroom; vehicle-structures; music as physics; the double; threads as medium; the oneiric domestic laboratory; birds as transit devices; the architecture of parting; the witness; chance against precision.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Agustin Lazo study: the portrait. In 1945 Varo painted "A mi amigo Agustin Lazo" -- a gouache dedication, inscribed "A Agustin Lazo afectuosamente" (Sotheby's), showing him entering a glowing tower interior with a checkered floor and a sail-bicycle at his side. Lazo (#316) ran surrealism at chamber scale, one intrusion per sheet, never a scaffold; Varo takes his grammar and turns the chamber itself into a machine. Where Lazo's horse is the one thing allowed velocity, Varo's birds are the one thing allowed to exit -- everything else is held inside the instrument, worked by a figure who is tending an apparatus. The triangle (#311-313) painted people being witnessed; Villaurrutia (#314) wrote doubles; Varo builds the room the double is manufactured in. She is the only member of this chain who ever treated the fantastic as an engineering problem.

## M1. The tower-workroom: architecture as instrument

The cutaway chamber is Varo's signature container: the building organizes an operation, and the cut wall is the viewing aperture. In *Bordando el manto terrestre* (1961) a hexagonal tower holds four hooded embroiderers under a veiled overseer; the walls double as a dovecote, and the cloth of the earth -- cities, fields, roads -- pours out of the windows as the product. In *Hacia la torre* (1960) the beehive tower is the destination: the whole procession, on its one-wheeled machines, is engineered toward it. In *Papilla estelar* (1958) the lantern-room is an observatory-kitchen where a funnel catches moonlight and tubes feed star-porridge to a caged celestial body. In *Tailleur pour dames* (1957) the fitting room stages layered temporal states -- each customer carries a translucent double exposure of a future or past self, and the architecture holds them all at once. The mechanism: the room is never scenery. It is a device with inputs (moonlight, threads, people), a process (embroidery, funneling, fitting), and an output (the earth's mantle, porridge, a altered body). The figure inside is an operator, not a protagonist.

Against the chain: Castellanos (#313 M4) made architecture a second sitter -- geometry that weighed on the figures. Varo makes architecture the first machine -- the figures work inside it. Lazo (#316 M1) changed only the backdrop; Varo rebuilds the room as a circuit. Villaurrutia's tower was a symbol (the statue's night); Varo's tower is a plant with shifts.

**Takeaway:** Cut one wall off the room and run it like a workshop -- threads, desks, overseers, each element placed by function. Watch the interior: when the building processes an input and extrudes a world, the mechanism is on.

## M2. Vehicle-structures: the domestic object as vessel

Varo never paints a normal vehicle. She wears one, inverts one, or shrinks one. In *Exploracion de las fuentes del rio Orinoco* (1959) the red coat IS the boat -- the explorer paddles inside her own garment, and the coat's sleeves read as hull ribs. In *La huida* (1961) an umbrella inverted into a boat carries the fleeing couple, the handle as tiller, the canopy as hull. In *Nave astral* (1960, title uncertain per gaps) a seed-pod ship flies on wire rigging. In *Catedral vegetal* (1957) a wheeled kiosk-cabin is drawn by a winged beast. In *Hacia la torre* (1960) the procession rides one-wheeled bicycle-machines with sails and gears exposed. In the 1945 Lazo portrait, a sail-bicycle waits at the tower door -- the device grammar already fully formed eight years before the mature work. The mechanism: the vessel keeps the object's proportions and materials -- it reads as an umbrella, a coat, a cabin first -- and performs a vessel's job second. The viewer must notice the function transfer. Nothing about the object changes; only its employment does.

Against the chain: Lazo (#316 M8) gave one motif a monopoly on motion (the horse); Varo gives motion to objects that have never moved, and the vehicle is always improvised from what was at hand. Nobody else in the chain built transit. The triangle's figures sat; Varo's furniture travels.

**Takeaway:** Take any household object -- umbrella, coat, cabin -- and give it a journey; it must keep its object's proportions while doing a vessel's job. Watch the transfer: when the umbrella is doing the work of a boat, the mechanism is on.

## M3. Music as physics: sound that moves matter

Sound is never accompaniment in Varo; it is a substance that lifts, siphons, and extrudes. In *El flautista* (1955) the player stands before a half-built transparent tower and the flute lifts carved spiral stones off the ground -- the notes visibly hoist them. In *Creacion de las aves* (1957) a tiny violin hangs at the artist-alchemist's chest and a string/pen transmits music to the page; the Hyperallergic reading (AIC exhibition) is explicit: the violin siphons music onto the paper, a magnifying glass filters starlight, a lab device vacuums colors from the window. In *Armonia* (1956) found objects -- a leaf, a crystal, a bird, a pearl -- are placed on staff lines that run through the room as a three-dimensional page; the architecture is scored. The mechanism: music is rendered as a transfer medium with visible plumbing -- lifted stones, vacuumed color, a string that is also a pen. The impossible is made procedural by giving it a conduit.

Against the chain: Villaurrutia (#314) called Lazo's painting "como la musica de Erik Satie" -- music as simile. Varo makes music a machine: Satie's quietude is the surface, but underneath there are gears. Lazo (#316 M10) ran quietude as system; Varo's quietude is the sound a working machine makes.

**Takeaway:** Sound is a substance with plumbing -- lift, siphon, extrude, never decorate. Watch the conduit: when the music has a visible pipe to the page, the mechanism is on.

## M4. The double: identity as a countable thing

Varo's selves are plural and physical: a face can be worn, thrown away, met in a box, or doubled in time. In *El encuentro* (1959) a veiled woman whose body is flowing water opens a chest and finds her own face inside; the water from the chest merges with her body -- the self is a liquid that recognizes itself in a container. In *Mujer saliendo del psicoanalista* (1960) a second face peers from the cloak at neck level -- MAM reads it as "her other self" -- while she dangles a discarded masked head, and per Varo's own letter to her brother she is dropping her father's head into the well ("arrojando a un pozo la cabeza de su padre"); the basket holds psychological waste, including a clock for the fear of arriving late; the doctor is inscribed as "Dr. FJA (Freud, Jung, Adler)". In *Tailleur pour dames* (1957) every customer carries a translucent double exposure -- the fitting room fits you across your own times. The mechanism: the double is always an object -- a face in a chest, a head on a string, a ghost at the shoulder. It can be counted, carried, discarded. Varo's own letters treat the symbols as inventories, not mysteries.

Against the chain: Villaurrutia (#314) ran the double as the core engine of his poems (self/double, night/setting); Varo runs it as prop inventory -- the double you can put in a basket. Castellanos (#313 M8) painted two independent paintings on one canvas; Varo paints two people in one body and lets you count them.

**Takeaway:** Identity is fluid and countable -- a face can be worn, thrown away, met in a box, doubled in time. Watch the inventory: when the self shows up as an object with a count, the mechanism is on.

## M5. Threads as medium: life as fiber

Thread is Varo's signal cable between the figure and the machine or the world. In *Premonicion* (1953) three veiled Fates trail glass threads from their feet to a reel-and-spool machine under a starry sky; the threads weave a veil-pond on the floor around them. In *Bordando el manto terrestre* (1961) the embroidery threads ARE the earth's mantle -- cities and fields pour from the tower windows as stitching. The mechanism: spooling is fate (the reel-and-spool machine is a fate engine), and embroidery is creation at landscape scale (the stitch is a road, the thread a river). The thread is always taut, always connected, always doing two jobs -- holding the figure to the machine, and carrying the product out to the world.

Against the chain: nobody else in the chain gave thread a load-bearing role. Angel's adjacencies (#312 M6) are spatial collisions; Varo's thread is a physical connection, the wire the synoptic image never had. The thread makes her simultaneity mechanical rather than collage.

**Takeaway:** Threads connect the figure to the machine or the world; spooling is fate, embroidery is creation at landscape scale. Watch the line: when the thread is taut and carrying something, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The oneiric domestic laboratory: kitchen and atelier as observatory

Domestic furniture is re-plumbed into apparatus; the room's fixtures form a circuit the figure tends. In *Papilla estelar* (1958) a funnel catches moonlight from a circular ceiling aperture and tubes feed star-porridge down to a caged celestial body -- the kitchen serves the sky. In *Planta insumisa* (1961) a botanist's bench -- flasks, funnels, test tubes -- stands behind crystalline formula-plants that grow as geometry. In *Creacion de las aves* (1957) the desk is an alembic catching moonlight through a circular window while a palette of vacuumed colors feeds the pen. In the 1945 Lazo portrait the tower interior glows behind a checkered floor and is entered like a house -- the laboratory was already domestic before it was cosmic. The mechanism: no fixture is decorative. The funnel funnels, the desk distills, the window filters. The dream is not the content of the room; it is the wiring diagram of the room.

Against the chain: Lazo (#316 M1) left the room intact and changed the backdrop; Varo keeps the room recognizable -- funnel, desk, window -- and changes what flows through the pipes. The triangle painted the figure; Varo paints the fixture the figure is tending. Nobody else in the chain made the furniture do work.

**Takeaway:** Domestic furniture is re-plumbed into apparatus -- the funnel funnels, the desk distills, the window filters. Watch the circuit: when the room's fixtures form a path the figure is tending, the mechanism is on.

## M7. Birds as transit devices: the captured sky

Birds are the one thing in Varo's system allowed to exit. In *Creacion de las aves* (1957) the drawn bird lifts off the page into real flight -- the drawing completes itself by leaving. In *Hacia la torre* (1960) the birds fly while the procession grinds toward the tower on its machines. In *El minotauro* (1959) the freed soul is a small bird resting in the monster's hand -- transcendence is a released animal. In *Cazadora de astros* (1956) the huntress nets the sky itself and cages a crescent moon; the Hyperallergic account confirms the technique behind the robe -- wet paint folded for a symmetrical Rorschach blot -- and the moon in the cage is the sky rendered as catchable substance. The mechanism: the sky is matter to be hunted, netted, caged, and drawn into flight. Everything the tower-workroom manufactures -- threads, mantles, porridge -- is terrestrial; the bird is the one product that refuses the room and leaves through the top of the frame.

Against the chain: Lazo's horse (#316 M8) is the free variable of his system, the one thing allowed velocity. Varo's bird is Lazo's horse taken to its conclusion -- not just moving but departing. Angel's birds (#312 M9) are signatures; Varo's are escapes that the composition cannot hold.

**Takeaway:** Birds always exit the system; the sky is a substance to be hunted, netted, caged, and drawn into flight. Watch the top of the frame: when the one flying thing is leaving and everything else is working, the mechanism is on.

## M8. Architecture of parting: separation as building

Emotion is rendered as spatial structure rather than faces or gestures. In *La despedida* (1958) two figures occupy separate archways, moving apart; the connection between them is not a gaze but a wet dark path on the floor -- the floor remembers what the bodies are ending. In *La huida* (1961) the couple shares one umbrella-boat, but the torrent they ride is doing the separating -- togetherness and parting happen in the same vessel, and the water decides. The mechanism: the architecture carries the affect. Archways, paths, torrents -- these are not settings for the parting; they ARE the parting. Faces are downturned, blank, or turned away; the feeling lives in the floor plan.

Against the chain: the triangle (#311-313) put the feeling in the face -- Lozano's closed eyes, Castellanos's withheld gaze. Villaurrutia (#314) put the feeling in the image's second meaning. Varo puts it in the architecture: nobody needs to look sad if the building is doing the leaving. Lazo erased the face (#316 M3); Varo keeps the face and moves the feeling into the masonry.

**Takeaway:** Render separation as spatial structure -- archways, paths, torrents -- rather than faces or gestures. Watch the floor plan: when the building is doing the leaving, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The witness: the small creature as observer

A small animal sits at the composition's center and does nothing but watch. In *La despedida* (1958) a cat peers from a dark doorway at the exact center of the frame while the humans part -- the parting is staged for an audience of one animal. Christie's (end to end) identifies the cat as Varo's alter ego; *Simpatia* (1955, unviewed) reportedly shows her with cat-adjusted features and three cattails; EN Wikipedia notes cats as observers, conveyors of symbolism, "familiars" across the oeuvre. The mechanism: the witness is always small, always central, always inactive. It gives the viewer a proxy -- the only figure in the painting who sees the whole operation the way the viewer does. In a system where every figure is an operator tending a circuit, the witness is the one who refuses a job.

Against the chain: nobody else in the chain placed an observer inside the frame. Lazo's shadow (#316 M5) is evidence of an absent operator; Varo's cat is evidence of a present non-operator. The witness makes the machinery self-conscious: the painting knows it is being watched, by something inside itself.

**Takeaway:** The small animal never acts -- it occupies the center of the composition and watches. Watch the proxy: when the one unoccupied figure sees the whole operation the way you do, the mechanism is on.

## M10. Chance against precision: the two-pass build

Process first, then undermined. The Hyperallergic account of the AIC "Science Fictions" exhibition (end to end) describes Varo's method: meticulous and pre-planned -- large drawings on translucent paper, transferred to the surface -- then deliberately undermined with chance techniques: graftage/grattage, blotting, decalcomania, sgraffito, spattering, sponging. The robe of the *Cazadora de astros* (1956) is wet paint folded into a symmetrical Rorschach blot. EN Wikipedia (end to end): from the mid-1950s she worked on gessoed hardboard (smooth side, sanded), scratched fine lines irregularly (possibly with the quartz crystals on her easel), painted the background environment BEFORE the figures, glazed in layers, finished details with fine brushes and sgraffito, signed in sgraffito, inlaid mother-of-pearl so faces catch light "like flecks of the moon" (Hyperallergic), and varnished with self-made damar resin. Christie's (end to end): underdrawings transferred to gessoed fibreboard, luminous colors in layers of oil and varnish, details with a single-hair brush, decalcomania as designs pressed from paper or aluminium foil. The mechanism: the underdrawing is engineering; the surface is weather. The precision is what makes the chance legible -- the blot reads as sky because the tower behind it was drawn straight.

Against the chain: Lazo stippled his nights (#316 M9) as facture; Varo builds chance into the structure -- background-first painting, then figures, then sgraffito cuts. Nobody else in the chain worked in two deliberate passes. The triangle's matte and flat systems are one-pass; Varo's impossible machines need the precision pass first, because the impossible has to be engineered before it can be weathered.

**Takeaway:** Build in two passes -- exact underdrawing, then chance texture over it. Watch the layers: when the straight-drawn machine wears a weathered, blotted skin, the mechanism is on.

## Evolution

1932-36 Barcelona: the Logicofobista show, advertising work with Gerardo Lizarraga, cadavre exquis games -- *L'Agent Double* "would anticipate her personal style" (ES wiki). 1937-40 Paris: Benjamin Peret's circle, Oscar Dominguez, Esteban Frances -- "the timid and humble one of a listener" (ES wiki), little output, one documented low point (forging de Chiricos). 1941-49 Mexico and Venezuela: the Bayer illustrations as steadiest income, the 1945 Lazo dedication portrait (the tower-workroom and sail-bicycle already present), the 1947-49 Venezuelan expedition (mosquito studies, malaria posters). 1952-63 mature Mexico: the Gruen marriage ends commercial work, the technique crystallizes -- gessoed hardboard, background-first painting, sgraffito finish, single-hair detail, mother-of-pearl inlay -- and the masterworks arrive in a run: *El flautista* 1955, *Cazadora de astros* 1956, *Creacion de las aves* 1957, *Papilla estelar* 1958, *Hacia la torre* 1960, *Bordando el manto terrestre* 1961. October 8, 1963: heart attack, at work. The arc is the opposite of Lazo's: where Lazo withdrew in 1950 and the oeuvre closed like a door, Varo's last decade is the engine running at full power -- the impossible made procedural, then made routine, then made perfect.

## Aesthetic logic: the impossible made procedural

Every painting asks what apparatus would let this impossible thing happen, then builds the apparatus on the page. The tower is a plant. The umbrella is a boat. The violin is a siphon. The thread is a cable. The figures are not dreamers; they are technicians -- hooded, veiled, aproned -- tending circuits that the painter has already wired. That is why the precision pass (M10) comes first: the fantastic has to be engineered before it can be weathered, or it collapses into decoration. Palette logic: ochre and gold luminous grounds, figures lit as if by moonlight or furnace-light; darkness is a texture -- scratched, folded, sponged -- never a void. The viewer is never lost because the machine is always legible: input, process, output. Varo's surrealism is the only one in this chain that a viewer could operate. Given the funnel, the thread, and the moon, you could run the shift yourself.
