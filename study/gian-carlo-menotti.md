## Aesthetic logic

The logic of Gian Carlo Menotti's work is that a man who wrote his own words refused to let opera be a thing that happens to other people, and the opera written for a medium is the refusal made practical, because a radio listener and a television viewer are an audience too, and the work must fit the box it is made for, and the child as moral witness is the refusal made innocent, because the smallest figure in the room is the only one who has not learned to lie, and the libretto as the composer's own text is the refusal made complete, because the man who writes the words owns the story, and the supernatural intruder is the refusal made domestic, because a ghost in a parlor is more frightening than a ghost in a castle, and the one-room opera is the refusal made cheap, because one room and one door are enough for a tragedy, and the consul's window is the refusal made political, because a system that never shows its face is the truest villain, and the telephone as plot engine is the refusal made modern, because a small machine can carry a whole fate, and the melodrama of the ordinary is the refusal made democratic, because a vase and a ball are enough, and the Italian-American double identity is the refusal made personal, because the composer is both countries at once and will not choose, and the festival as a work is the refusal made institutional, because a stage is not enough, a town is needed, and the work revised across decades is the refusal made stubborn, because a failure is a draft, and the puppet and clown figure is the refusal made small, because the truth fits in a puppet theatre before it fits in a house, and the deathbed confession is the refusal made final, because the last sentence is the true one, and the spoken-word opera is the refusal made plain, because sometimes the music must step aside and let the words walk, and the Broadway run is the refusal made public, because an opera must survive nightly audiences, and the anti-avant-garde device is the refusal made audible, because the strange sound belongs to the machine and the lovers sing plainly, and the proof is that Amahl is still sung every Christmas by children who have never heard of the avant-garde, and the festival at Spoleto is still running fifty years after its founder died, and the man who wrote the first opera for television is remembered by the living room, not by the academy.

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. RELATIONSHIP LENGTH WITH BARBER: "more than 40 years" vs "30 years". The Barber main article and the Menotti infobox, 1928 to 1970, give more than forty years, and Simple Wikipedia says Barber "lived with Gian Carlo Menotti for 30 years", and EBSCO says "For thirty years Menotti lived in a house, called Capricorn... that he bought with Barber". The thirty-year figure counts the house, 1943 to 1972 is twenty-nine years, not the relationship. Use "more than 40 years" for the relationship and 1943 to 1972 for Capricorn. Also #424 C15.

C2. CAPRICORN OCCUPANCY: "shared for over forty years" vs 1943 to 1972. Classic Cat and en-academic say the two men "bought a house together in Mount Kisco... which they named 'Capricorn' and shared for over forty years", and the main article says purchased 1943, retreat "up until 1972", put up for sale 1970, sold 1972. The "over forty years" reading conflates the relationship with the house, and the documented house span is twenty-nine years.

C3. SIBLING COUNT: sixth of ten vs sixth of eight. The main article says Menotti "was the sixth of ten children of Alfonso and Ines Menotti", and Classic Cat, EBSCO, and musicianguide all say "the sixth of eight children". C4. MILAN CONSERVATORY START: 1924 at age 13 vs 1923.
