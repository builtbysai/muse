## The life: born into rubble, the picture built like a ruin

Anselm Kiefer was born on 8 March 1945 in Donaueschingen, Germany, the son of a German art teacher, named after the 19th-century painter Anselm Feuerbach; he is a German painter and sculptor, one of the most prominent figures in the Neo-Expressionist movement of the late 20th century. He was born a few months before the end of World War II and grew up surrounded by the devastation of the war: EBSCO records that on the day of his birth the house next door to his family's home was bombed, and that he later said he grew up playing in rubble and credited the bricks and concrete of his childhood for his fascination with ruins; Tate likewise records him building small houses out of rubble as a child. The air-raid-shelter birth sometimes repeated about him is NOT verified: no source read end to end states it, and the hospital-cellar variant rests on search snippets only and conflicts with the EBSCO version, so neither is asserted. He was raised in a Catholic home in the Black Forest near the eastern bank of the Rhine; the family moved to Ottersdorf in 1951, he attended public school in Rastatt, and he graduated from high school in 1965. (C25698, leg 1 C25575-C25579; synthesis C25665, C25666)

At 18 he set out on a year-long tour of Van Gogh sites in the Netherlands, Belgium and France. He studied pre-law and Romance languages at the University of Freiburg in 1965-66, then switched to art after three semesters, studying at the academies in Freiburg and Karlsruhe under Peter Dreher and Horst Antes, and received an art degree in 1969; Britannica dates the abandonment of law to 1966. TheArtStory's spelling "Freiberg" for the university town is treated as a typo for Freiburg. (C25699, leg 1 C25580-C25582; synthesis C25667)

In the early 1970s he studied informally with Joseph Beuys on occasional visits to Dusseldorf -- the Met's wording, and the form the #709 top-pick line uses. The sources do not agree, and the disagreement is carried, not resolved: Stadel says flatly that from 1970 to 1972 he studied under Beuys at the Dusseldorf academy, TheArtStory and EBSCO imply a 1970 move or transfer to Dusseldorf, and Ropac says he visited Beuys and took part in some of his actions; Anthony Bond's lecture is explicit that he went to Dusseldorf "not as a student but as part of the coterie" around Beuys, and Beuys's 1972 expulsion from the academy bounds the contact. Beuys was the first to say clearly that the Occupations photographs were art, and Kiefer joined Beuys's ecological action "Save the Woods" in the Grafenberger Wald in December 1971; he was also skeptical of Beuys's utopian direct-democracy politics. (C25700, leg 1 C25583; leg 3 C25641-C25644; synthesis C25668)

In the summer and autumn of 1969, aged 24 and a student at Karlsruhe, Kiefer undertook Occupations: photographing himself performing the forbidden Sieg Heil salute, in outfits from hippie gowns to paramilitary uniforms (including his father's Wehrmacht uniform), at sites carrying traces of an imperial past -- in France at Montpellier, Sete and Arles, in Switzerland at Bellinzona, in Italy at Rome, Paestum, Caprarola, Pompeii and by the crater of Vesuvius -- after rehearsing in his Karlsruhe home-studio and the nearby countryside. The photographs took the collective title Heroic Symbols, from a 1943 article in the Nazi arts magazine Die Kunst im Deutschen Reich; he showed them in his first solo exhibition at Galerie am Kaiserplatz, Karlsruhe, in 1969, and 18 were published in Interfunktionen in 1975, causing scandal and frequent misreading as endorsement rather than interrogation. Tate records that authorship of the location photographs is not conclusively settled and that 200-300 Heroic Symbols photographs exist, of which he could afford to enlarge only a fraction. (C25701, leg 1 C25584-C25586; leg 2 C25605; synthesis C25669)

In 1971 he moved to Hornbach and established a studio in the attic of a former schoolhouse; his output in this first period is known as The German Years, and he remained in the Neckar-Odenwald-Kreis until 1992, working later in a converted brick factory in Buchen and, from 1988, a former brick factory in Hopfingen. In 1991, after twenty years in the Odenwald, he left Germany to travel (India, Mexico, Japan, Thailand, Indonesia, Australia, the United States), and in 1992 he established himself in Barjac, France, transforming the derelict silk factory La Ribaute into a Gesamtkunstwerk of glass buildings, archives, storerooms and subterranean chambers. The size of La Ribaute is a variant: 35 hectares per Wikipedia against 200 acres per TheArtStory and EBSCO. The France move date is a variant: 1992 per Wikipedia, the Met and EBSCO against 1993 per Ropac's 2026 biography and a 2025 Amsterdam listing; 1992 is used as the museum-majority date. (C25702, leg 1 C25587-C25589; synthesis C25670, C25671)

During 2008 he left Barjac for Paris, moving his work to a former La Samaritaine depository warehouse in Croissy-Beaubourg; the move itself is a variant (110 lorries to a 35,000 sq ft warehouse per Wikipedia, 70 trucks per EBSCO for the German-studio move, 36,000 square meters per TheArtStory). He spent the summer of 2019 living and working at Barjac, and Ropac (2026) says he lives and works between Paris and Barjac. He has lived and worked in France since 1992 and primarily in Paris since 2008, and was awarded Austrian citizenship in 2018. His first wife's name is unresolved: Wikipedia's infobox says Monika Kiefer, Tate says he met Julia in 1969 and she later became his first wife; his second wife, Austrian photographer Renate Graf, divorced him in 2014. (C25703, leg 1 C25589-C25591, C25604; synthesis C25671)

His materials are the spine of the work: straw, ash, clay, lead and shellac, with lead, broken glass and dried flowers or plants added as the paintings grew larger and more encrusted. His attraction to lead began when he repaired aging pipes in his first house; straw represents energy, gold in colour, releasing heat when burned, and the resulting ash makes way for new creation. Straw entered his work for the first time in the 1981 Margarete/Shulamite series, and lead grew dominant after he bought the lead roof of a Cologne cathedral being redone in the 1980s. (C25704, leg 1 C25592; synthesis C25672)

The breakthrough came with Georg Baselitz: together they represented Germany at the Venice Biennale in 1980, a presentation chosen by Klaus Gallwitz that, per TheArtStory, validated Neo-Expressionism and catapulted Kiefer's career. Comprehensive solo exhibitions followed at Kunsthalle Dusseldorf (1984), Art Institute of Chicago (1987), Neue Nationalgalerie Berlin (1991), the Met (1998), Fondation Beyeler (2001), Fort Worth (2005), the Hirshhorn (2006), and SFMOMA and Guggenheim Bilbao (2007), with later solos including the Sezon Museum Tokyo (1993), Bibliotheque nationale de France (2015), Albertina Vienna (2016), the Hermitage (2017), the Rodin Museum (2017), Met Breuer (2018) and Astrup Fearnley (2019); he was also featured at the 1997 Venice Biennale with a solo show at the Museo Correr. In 2007 he became the first artist commissioned to install a permanent work at the Louvre since Georges Braque in 1953, and inaugurated the Monumenta series at the Grand Palais with works honouring Paul Celan and Ingeborg Bachmann. The Royal Academy retrospective was NOT in 2007 -- the first major UK retrospective ran 27 September to 14 December 2014, arranged chronologically with works conceived for the Main Galleries. (C25705, leg 1 C25593-C25596; synthesis C25673, C25674)

In 2015 the Centre Pompidou, the Bibliotheque Nationale and the Museum der bildenden Kunste Leipzig hosted a retrospective for his 70th birthday; the Pompidou show was his first retrospective in France in over 30 years, nearly 150 works, closing 18 April 2016, but its exact start date was not verified (the Pompidou official page returned nothing; a commonly cited 16 December 2015 start is not asserted). Later shows include Met Breuer's Provocations (2017), Angel's Fall at Palazzo Strozzi, Florence (2024), Anselm Kiefer: Becoming the Sea at the Saint Louis Art Museum (18 October 2025 to 25 January 2026), the Milan site-specific installation The Women Alchemists / Le Alchimiste at Palazzo Reale (7 February to 27 September 2026, part of the Winter Olympics showcase), and Nymphaeum at Thaddaeus Ropac Paris Pantin (5 September to 25 October 2026, more than 20 new paintings, mostly dated 2025). The Milan canvas count is a variant: 42 per Wikipedia and Gagosian against 38 per the Brooklyn Rail and ArtDaily. (C25706, leg 1 C25597-C25600; synthesis C25675)

Honours include the Hans-Thoma-Preis (1983), Carnegie Prize (1985), Wolf Prize in Arts and Goslarer Kaiserring (1990), Praemium Imperiale (1999), Officer of the Ordre des Arts et des Lettres (2002), Foreign Honorary Member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2004), Austrian Decoration for Science and Art and the German Order of Merit (2005), the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade (2008, the first time given to a visual artist), the Adenauer-de Gaulle Prize (2009), the College de France Chair of Artistic Creation (2010 in the list, 2011 in the text -- a Wikipedia-internal variant), the Leo Baeck Medal (2011), the J. Paul Getty Medal (2017), the Prize for Understanding and Tolerance from the Jewish Museum Berlin (2019), and the Knight Commander's Cross of the Order of Merit and the German National Prize (2023), plus honorary doctorates from Turin (2014), St Andrews (2015), Antwerp (2015), Freiburg (2017) and Brera (2020), and honorary membership of the Royal Academy (1996). Kiefer is alive as of 2026: CBS on 11 January 2026 called him one of the world's most prolific living artists, the Saint Louis retrospective ran into January 2026, the Milan and Ropac shows ran through 2026, and EBSCO records his election in 2026 as a domestic member of the Order Pour le merite. Wim Wenders's 3D documentary Anselm (2023) is about him. No obituary was found or relied on. (C25707, leg 1 C25601-C25603; synthesis C25676, C25677)


## M1 -- Lead as skin and as weight

Lead is the material Kiefer trusts with history: it is laid on canvas, cast as books, wedged between concrete tower levels, and formed into a ship, a film reel and a camera. It is the base material of alchemy, and it is described as the only material heavy enough to bear the burden of history. The lead is not a colour choice -- a grey paint could do that -- it is a weight choice: the picture carries literal mass, the books need two people to lift, the towers compress lead wedges under ninety tons of concrete. Evidence: W08 Order of the Angels (lead on canvas), W09 Red Sea (lead on canvas), W12 Zweistromland (about 200 lead books), W13 Breaking of the Vessels (41 lead books), W14 Melancholia (lead, glass, steel, ash), W16 Seven Heavenly Palaces (lead books and wedges compressed under concrete). (C25708, leg 2 C25625; synthesis C25687)

The watchable behaviour is weight made visible: a surface that sags, a book that cannot be opened, a tower that presses down on the books inside it. The viewer reads the heaviness before the subject -- the lead tells the eye that what is shown is not light, not recent, not easily carried away.

**Takeaway:** use a material whose weight is the meaning, not a colour that stands for it; the picture must feel heavy before it is read. (C25709)

## M2 -- Straw, ash, earth and clay as material that is also the image

Straw is never decoration in Kiefer: it stands for golden hair, for a wheat field, for energy and for something that can burn, and the ash it leaves behind makes way for new creation. The straw physically emerges from the surface -- tendrils curling upward like smoke from chimneys, ending in candle-like flames -- so the image and the material are the same thing seen twice. Ash and clay make the ground itself; the field in the picture is made of the stuff a field is made of. Evidence: W05 Margarethe and W06 Dein goldenes Haar, Margarethe (straw as hair and field), W08 Order of the Angels (straw and lead on canvas), W09 Red Sea series and W19 Exodus (straw affixed to canvas in the lower landscape register), W17 Palmsonntag (clay, earth and palm fronds on fibreboard). (C25710, leg 2 C25625; synthesis C25687)

The watchable behaviour is a material that changes state in front of the viewer: straw that could burn, ash that already has, clay that is still wet. The picture is a field that remembers being a field.

**Takeaway:** let the material do the depicting -- straw for hair, ash for ground -- so the picture cannot be separated from what it is made of. (C25711)

## M3 -- Monumental scale that exceeds the body

Kiefer's paintings run to several metres wide, his towers to 14-18 metres high, his bookshelves to 17 feet tall, and his installations fill a hall; the viewer must walk around or under the work rather than take it in at a glance. The scale is not spectacle for its own sake -- it is the only size at which a ruined landscape, a drowned sea or a library of lead can be shown without miniaturising the subject into an illustration. Evidence: W08 Order of the Angels 330 x 555 cm, W09 Red Sea 278.8 x 425.2 cm, W11 Osiris and Isis 379.73 x 561.34 cm, W13 Breaking of the Vessels 378.5 x 836.9 x 518.2 cm, W16 Seven Heavenly Palaces towers 14-18 m at 90 tons each, W20 Actaea 560 x 760 cm. (C25712, leg 2 C25628; synthesis C25688)

The watchable behaviour is a body dwarfed by what it looks at: the viewer walks, cranes, steps back, and still cannot hold the whole thing. Scale turns looking into a physical act.

**Takeaway:** make the picture bigger than the viewer's field of view, so looking becomes walking and the subject keeps its size. (C25713)

## M4 -- Text, name and inscription written on the surface

Titles, names of historical figures, Hebrew inscriptions and poem lines are written, scrawled or incised directly on the work, so reading is part of looking. The name is not a label added afterwards -- Margarethe's name is scrawled in black on the canvas, the names of Arminius, Thusnelda, Holderlin, Klopstock, Fichte and Schleiermacher are written in the branches of Varus, the sefirot labels are connected by copper wire in Breaking of the Vessels, and the NASA codes of Sternenfall are transcribed on glass plaques lying on the floor. Evidence: W05 Margarethe (name scrawled in black), W03 Varus (names written in the branches), W08 Order of the Angels (title and Aeropagite Dionysius written on the canvas), W13 Breaking of the Vessels (Hebrew sefirot labels connected by copper wire, Ain-Sof on glass), W19 Exodus (Hebrew inscriptions from the book of Exodus), W15 Sternenfall (NASA alphanumerical codes on canvas and on floor plaques). (C25714, leg 2 C25626; synthesis C25688)

The watchable behaviour is a picture that has to be read as well as seen: the eye moves from image to word and back, and the word changes what the image is -- a field becomes a poem, a sky becomes a catalogue.

**Takeaway:** write the name on the picture itself; the inscription is the mechanism that turns a landscape into a history. (C25715)

## M5 -- Charred, burnt, scorched and broken surfaces

Surfaces are cracked, blackened, torn, bent, burnt, dipped in acid or shattered, and broken glass is left on the floor as part of the work. The damage is not applied on top of a finished picture -- the picture is made by damaging it: books dipped in acid, torn, bent and burnt; straw tips curled and blackened like singed locks; glass panes broken at irregular intervals; a scorched surface under soiled dresses. Evidence: W05 Margarethe (straw tips curling and blackened, like singed locks), W12 Zweistromland (books dipped in acid, torn, bent, burnt), W13 Breaking of the Vessels (broken glass among the books and on the floor), W08 2007 version (scorched surface, soiled dresses), W16 Seven Heavenly Palaces (glass panes broken at irregular intervals). (C25716, leg 2 C25629; synthesis C25689)

The watchable behaviour is a surface that has already survived something: the viewer sees the burn before the image, and the image reads as what is left after the fire.

**Takeaway:** damage the surface as the making, not as the finish -- the burn is the picture's history, not its patina. (C25717)

## M6 -- Layering, overpainting, collage and reworking

Photographs, woodcuts, fabric, found objects, circuit boards and earlier paintings are cut, pasted, collaged and painted over, so the surface is a sediment of earlier states. The Red Sea is oil, lead, woodcut, photograph and shellac on one canvas; Auszug aus Aegypten is paint, charcoal and string on cut-and-pasted gelatin silver on board; Osiris and Isis has a real circuit board, copper wires and pieces of a porcelain sink attached; the Nymphaeum paintings layer emulsion, oil, acrylic, shellac, gold leaf, sediment of electrolysis and collage of canvas on canvas. Evidence: W09 Red Sea, W10 Auszug aus Aegypten, W11 Osiris and Isis, W20 Nymphaeum, W04 Wege der Weltweisheit (woodcut sheets grafted together). (C25718, leg 2 C25626; synthesis C25689)

The watchable behaviour is a surface with a past: edges of older images showing through, a photograph half-buried in paint, a circuit board that belongs to another century than the myth it illustrates. The picture is an excavation, not a surface.

**Takeaway:** build the picture in layers that stay visible -- the earlier state showing through is the depth. (C25719)

## M7 -- Mythic, historical and scriptural citation as structure

A named myth, battle, poem or scripture supplies the plan of the work, not just its title. Varus and Wege der Weltweisheit are built on the Teutoburg Forest of 9 AD; Margarethe and Sulamith on Celan's Death Fugue; Order of the Angels on Pseudo-Dionysius and his nine choirs; Red Sea and Exodus on the parting of the sea; Breaking of the Vessels on Luria's Kabbalah and the ten vessels; Seven Heavenly Palaces on the Sefer Hechalot; Nymphaeum on Ovid, Daphne and Carya. The citation is structural: the nine choirs give the picture nine rocks and nine lines; the ten vessels give the bookcase ten markers connected by wire; the seven palaces give the installation seven towers. Evidence: W03, W04, W05, W07, W08, W09, W13, W16, W19, W20. (C25720, leg 2 C25631; synthesis C25690)

The watchable behaviour is a picture whose plan can be counted: nine of this, ten of that, seven towers, forty-one books -- the myth arrives as a number before it arrives as a story.

**Takeaway:** let the source text dictate the count and the layout, not just the title; the myth is the blueprint. (C25721)

## M8 -- The book as object, library and unreadable archive

Books are made of lead, too heavy to lift alone, fused shut, burnt, or set in shelves like a building; knowledge is shown as weight, not as text to read. Zweistromland holds about 200 lead books in two bookcases named after the Tigris and Euphrates and positioned to resemble an open book; some books weigh about 600 pounds and could be browsed only by more than one person, while others are fused together so their knowledge is sealed off. Breaking of the Vessels sets 41 lead books in a steel bookcase under a glass half-circle inscribed Ain-Sof, the infinite. Evidence: W12 Zweistromland, W13 Breaking of the Vessels, W16 Seven Heavenly Palaces (lead books and wedges between concrete levels), W18 Secret Life of Plants (monumental leaden books, leaden piles of books with bronze sunflowers). (C25722, leg 2 C25627; synthesis C25690)

The watchable behaviour is a library that refuses to be read: the books are there, heavy and present, and their pages will not open. Knowledge becomes an object you can walk around but not use.

**Takeaway:** make the book too heavy to open -- the archive's weight is the point, and the unreadability is the content. (C25723)

## M9 -- Scorched-field landscape with a low, unresolved horizon

A wide, empty, furrowed or burnt field fills the lower part of the picture under a heavy sky, with a path or a horizon that leads nowhere or ends in a dead end. The Varus forest path leads to a dead end; the Margarethe wheat fields recede into the distance; the Order of the Angels earth is wounded and fractured below a low horizon line; the Red Sea is a vast emptied panorama; Exodus is a lower register of paint and straw landscape under an upper register whirling in concentric circles; Palmsonntag lays a fallen palm tree on the gallery floor. Evidence: W05, W03, W08, W09, W19, W17. (C25724, leg 2 C25630; synthesis C25691)

The watchable behaviour is a horizon that will not resolve: the eye follows the path to where it should arrive and finds nothing there -- the field is the destination, and the destination is empty.

**Takeaway:** keep the horizon low and the path unresolved; the empty field is the subject, not the background. (C25725)

## M10 -- Sky as archive, and the heaven-earth axis

The sky is coded, numbered and mapped, and objects, dresses, ladders, lines and falling stars travel between sky and earth without arriving. In Sternenfall every star is identified by the alphanumerical code NASA uses to classify celestial bodies, lines link points into imaginary constellations, and codes transcribed on glass plaques lie on the floor under the canvas as the fallen stars; the numbers also recall those tattooed on the arms of Jews imprisoned in the Nazi camps. The Melancholia tower is crowned by a glass polyhedron taken from Durer's 1514 engraving Melancholia, with the falling stars at its base. In Order of the Angels nine lines stretch from the sky to nine rocks, and nine dresses numbered 1 to 9 rise; in Exodus a ladder links the terrestrial and the celestial, and a bicycle whirls in concentric circles above a straw landscape; in Secret Life of Plants every plant has its equivalent star in heaven. Evidence: W15, W16, W08, W19, W18. (C25726, leg 2 C25632; synthesis C25691)

The watchable behaviour is traffic between two realms that never completes: stars fall and lie on the floor, dresses rise and never arrive, a ladder stands between earth and heaven and nobody climbs it. The axis is the picture.

**Takeaway:** code the sky and leave the passage open -- the journey between heaven and earth is the work, and arrival would end it. (C25727)

## The aesthetic logic: the ruin is the method, the myth is the measure

Kiefer's whole career is one procedure performed fifty ways: take a myth, a battle, a poem or a scripture, and build it out of materials that have already been through something -- lead, straw, ash, clay, broken glass -- at a scale that exceeds the body, with the name written on the surface and the sky coded above it. The lead carries the weight of history. The straw burns and leaves ash that makes way for new creation. The book is too heavy to read. The field is scorched and the horizon does not resolve. In each case the artist sets up a system -- a material, a count, a citation, a scale -- and then lets the system carry what his hand alone could not. That is why his work is the opposite pole of Baselitz's even though the two men are now tied by patronage and a shared pavilion: Baselitz sets up a procedure that drains content from a motif, Kiefer sets up a procedure that loads content into a material, until the lead itself is the history. (C25728, leg 2 aesthetic logic; synthesis C25665-C25694)

Three habits hold the whole career together. First, the material is the argument: straw for hair and for energy, ash for ground, lead for history, clay for earth -- the picture cannot be separated from what it is made of, and a description of the materials is already a description of the meaning. Second, the citation is the structure: nine choirs, ten vessels, seven palaces, forty-one books, two rivers -- the myth arrives as a count before it arrives as a story, and the count dictates the layout. Third, the scale is the experience: the viewer walks around a bookcase, under a tower, along a palm tree laid on the floor, and the body is the measure of the picture, not the eye alone. (C25729, leg 2 aesthetic logic; synthesis C25665-C25694)

The evolution is the proof. 1969: the Occupations photographs (W01) -- a young painter performing the forbidden salute at the sites of empire, misread as endorsement, defended by Beuys as art. 1973-77: the empty German rooms and forests (W02, W03, W04) -- Deutschlands Geisteshelden with no heroes present, Varus with a path to a dead end, Wege der Weltweisheit with the portraits of German history intertwined by a log fire. 1981-85: the Celan paintings and the monumental interiors (W05, W06, W07, W08, W09, W10) -- straw as Margarethe's golden hair, Sulamith's tomb-like hall, nine rocks under nine lines from the sky, the parted sea as a photograph of corrugated metal on the studio floor. 1985-91: the library and the vessel (W11, W12, W13, W14) -- a circuit board above a mastaba, 200 lead books in two bookcases like an open book, 41 lead books under Ain-Sof, Melancholia in lead, glass, steel and ash. 1998-2015: the sky and the tower (W15, W16) -- stars coded with NASA numbers and fallen onto the floor, seven concrete towers of ninety tons each crowned by Durer's polyhedron. 2005-2026: the palm, the plants, the exodus, the nymphs (W17, W18, W19, W20) -- a fallen tree on the gallery floor, leaden books with bronze sunflowers, a bicycle whirling above a straw landscape, mountain nymphs eight metres wide under a burnished sky in sediment of electrolysis and gold leaf. The late work does not soften the procedure; it widens it, from German history to Ovid, and the answer in every case is the same -- build the myth out of the heaviest materials, write its name on it, and leave the horizon unresolved. (C25730, leg 1 C25575-C25604; leg 2 C25605-C25634; synthesis C25665-C25694)
