## M1. The expulsion and the self-teaching

Twice expelled, then self-made. Treccani: expelled from the Istituto di arti decorative, then from the Accademia in 1912 after a serious dispute with Calosci, and "malgrado questo", in spite of this, he showed etchings at Pistoia in 1913. it.wikipedia: "Prosegue pertanto come autodidatta", he therefore continued as a self-taught man. Cambi: he continued to educate himself as a self-taught artist, refining his skills through Celestino Celestini's free school of engraving. Estorick: expelled following a disagreement with a teacher. What survives as mechanism is that he learns by walking: as a boy he skipped school for solitary walks, taverns, card and billiard rooms, workshops, "per disegnare e prendere appunti dal vero", to draw and take notes from life, per Treccani. The street, not the classroom, is his academy, and the man who was thrown out of art school twice then taught the school itself, taking its painting chair in 1942, see M16. The mechanism is the outsider's eye, the same mechanism as Farani's M1 in #432, and the two studies rhyme: neither man learned his trade in a classroom, and both ran their houses by judgement.

**Takeaway:** let the street teach what the school expelled, and the piece should be drawn from walking, not from a lesson.

## M2. The Futurist year and what survived of it

1913 to 1914, then a deliberate abandonment, but the decomposition stays as structure. Treccani: "Con il 1914 si avvio l'anno futurista di Rosai", with the Futurist year of Rosai there began, opened by the oil on cardboard Dinamismo Bar Sanmarco and then the oil on canvas with collage Scomposizione di una strada, and in April and May 1914 he was invited to the Esposizione libera futurista in Rome at the Galleria Sprovieri. Rosai's own line, from his Nota autobiografica in Il Frontespizio, April 1937: "Mi vollero tutti conoscere, mi fecero elogi, che ricevetti come enormi ricompense e mi invitarono a unirmi a loro", they all wanted to know me, they praised me, and they invited me to join them. Estorick's version: "They invited me to join them and from that very day I became a militant Futurist." What survived is not speed or noise but scomposizione, breaking a street, a bar, a still life into planes, which returns as the geometric block-building of the mature work, and the Uffizi on Piazza del Carmine in 1922 reads it directly: strictly geometric shapes, a severe palette, a Cubist influence. The collage habit survives too: Natura morta, il banco del falegname, 1914, built with fragments and scraps of printed paper, per Palazzo Citterio, and the war paintings, which "pur attingendo alla sintesi futurista, accolsero nuovi spunti dal linguaggio del Doganiere", though drawing on the Futurist synthesis, took new cues from the language of the Douanier Rousseau, per Treccani. He kept the break and threw away the noise.

**Takeaway:** keep the break and throw away the noise, and the piece should be built from planes, not from speed.

## M3. The return to order and the Cezanne volumes

After the war, volumes built through colour, sharp contours, rich colour. it.wikipedia: the post-Futurist phase is characterised by the return to order, where what emerges are volumes, sharp contours and rich colour, and "in particolare, l'uso dei volumi e dei colori di Rosai si ispira fortemente a Cezanne", in particular Rosai's use of volumes and colours is strongly inspired by Cezanne. Treccani: Soffici moved toward Valori plastici, the review of Mario Broglio, and proclaimed the return to classicism and to the study of the true, "in una sorta di realismo purificato che traeva le sue basi dall'asciutta semplicita giottesca", in a sort of purified realism that drew its basis from the dry simplicity of Giotto. The Artgate summary of the Paesaggio of about 1922: he had begun to organise his compositions by means of volumes developed through the use of colour. MASI Lugano on the return-to-order painters: the recovery of classical forms and of the compositional and formal simplification proper to the primitive and Renaissance Italian tradition. The watchable behaviour is a landscape or a street reduced to a few interlocking coloured blocks that still read as one specific place, and the place is Florence, and the blocks are the walls.

**Takeaway:** build the place from coloured blocks and let the blocks be the drawing, and the piece should read as one street, not as a style.

## M4. The small format and the essentiality

The mature picture is small, and smallness is the point. Treccani: "Nacquero quadri di piccolo formato che avevano elaborato l'essenzialita geometrica del cubismo, ora declinata partendo dallo studio del vero, con originali assonanze metafisiche, per la silente astratta concentrazione, malinconica a tratti, che avvolgeva figure e cose", there were born small-format pictures that had worked out the geometric essentiality of Cubism, now declined starting from the study of the true, with original metaphysical assonances, for the silent abstract concentration, melancholic at times, that wrapped figures and things. The Uffizi on Piazza del Carmine: he would paint the squares and the narrow streets in small works. Sizes seen this leg: Belvedere 1923, 40 by 30 cm; Piazza del Carmine 1922, 50 by 72 cm; Il muro del Carmine 1924, 38 by 54 cm; I fidanzati 1934, 70 by 49.7 cm; the Cambi lots mostly 30 to 70 cm. The late La Firenze di Rosai canvases, ten large canvases of 1951 to 1954, are the exception that proves the rule, and they are described as prevalence of light and bereft of human presence, per exibart, that is essentiality scaled up. The watchable behaviour is a whole street, with its wall, its roofs, its silence, held in a panel the size of a tray, and the smallness is what makes the silence monumental, because nothing in the picture is allowed to be merely big.

**Takeaway:** make the panel small and the silence large, and the piece should hold a whole street in the size of a tray.

## M5. The metallic firm light

Light that does not flicker; it stands. Treccani, the key phrase: "la qualita della luce, metallica e ferma", the quality of the light, metallic and firm, in the same sentence as M4. The light is what makes the small format monumental: figures and things wrapped in silent abstract concentration. Museo Novecento 2026: tensions between light and shadow, stability and precariousness, belonging and solitude. The watchable behaviour is a wall or a face lit as if by a single cold source, no modelling softness, edges that stay hard even in shadow, and the contrast is with the late white period, M20, where the same firm light turns pale and the colour range brightens, and the light stays firm while everything else lets go.

**Takeaway:** light the wall with one cold source and let the edges stay hard, and the piece should be lit like metal, not like air.

## M6. The street game as subject

Toppa, briscola, cards: the game is the picture's engine, not its anecdote. Treccani: at the 1920 solo, Partita a briscola and Giocatori di toppa, "primo di una lunga e fortunata serie di opere con lo stesso tema", first of a long and fortunate series of works with the same theme, and in the late 1920s, "la grande versione dei Giocatori di toppa del 1929", the great version of the Giocatori di toppa of 1929. MASI Lugano on the 1920 version: "il gruppo di personaggi di strada e restituito per sottrazione. Attraverso pochi attributi e senza indulgenze per la critica sociale verso un'umanita ai margini, Rosai sa raggiungere una grande intensita pittorica", the group of street characters is returned by subtraction, through few attributes and without indulgences for social criticism toward a humanity at the margins, Rosai knows how to reach a great pictorial intensity. it.wikipedia's works list adds a Giocatori di toppa of 1928 and Giocatori di carte of 1943, and the three dates are three real versions, not one disputed date, see C1. Toppa is a Tuscan street game, a form of bowls played against a wall; briscola is a card game. The game gives Rosai what he needs: men bent over one shared focus, in the street, for no money that matters, with their backs to the viewer half the time. The players do not perform; they attend. The watchable behaviour is a circle of men whose attention converges on a point on the ground, and the picture's centre is an act of looking, not a face, and the game is the only plot the picture needs.

**Takeaway:** put the game in the middle and let the looking be the plot, and the piece should converge on one point on the ground.

## M7. The wall as a character

The wall is not background; it is the other actor. Il muro del Carmine, 1924, oil on canvas, 38 by 54 cm, in the Mart, Collezione Giovanardi, Rovereto; Muro rosso, 1945; Treccani: "omini seduti all'osteria, in piedi o appoggiati a un muro", little men seated at the tavern, standing or leaning on a wall; the Uffizi on Piazza del Carmine: past the boundary wall of Palazzo Rospigliosi Pallavicini, we glimpse a hedge, a tree and a bunch of sloping roofs. The wall does three jobs: it closes the composition, so no horizon is needed; it gives the plebeian figure something to lean on, see M8; and it is itself a Florentine surface, plaster, stain, height. A toppa game needs a wall to play against, and a street needs a wall to be a street, and Rosai's walls are the reason his streets have no distance in them, because the wall is the distance. The watchable behaviour is a tall flat plane that takes half the picture and refuses to be scenery, and the men are small against it, and the smallness is the point.

**Takeaway:** give the wall half the picture and let the men lean on it, and the piece should close on a flat plane, not on a horizon.

## M8. The plebeian figure as wood, as geometry

Men built like carpentry. Treccani: "I personaggi rosaiani, in particolare, palesavano una radice plebea dichiaratamente toscana e insieme risentimenti anarchici, umori patetici e rivoltosi, oltre la cronaca: omini seduti all'osteria, in piedi o appoggiati a un muro, erano per l'artista modelli di una umanita barbarica, fuori dal tempo sebbene ambientata nei vicoli piu umili di Firenze fra il Carmine, Santo Spirito, San Frediano o nelle prode di Villamagna, condotti con sconcertante essenzialita, aspro realismo, tavolozza terrosa e asciutta", Rosai's characters, in particular, showed a plebeian root declaredly Tuscan and together anarchic resentments, pathetic and rebellious humours, beyond the news: little men seated at the tavern, standing or leaning on a wall, were for the artist models of a barbaric humanity, outside time though set in the humblest alleys of Florence between the Carmine, Santo Spirito, San Frediano or the banks of Villamagna, conducted with disconcerting essentiality, harsh realism, earthy and dry palette. The Portuguese Wikipedia, from the Garzanti encyclopaedia: figures of geometric type and wooden aspect, with a certain caricatural air, in a somewhat abstract setting. The carpenter's son paints men the way his father built furniture: block, joint, plane. The grotesque Soffici praised in 1920, "la sottolineatura di certo grottesco, non disgiunto da una poeticita e bellezza recondite e sui generis", the underlining of a certain grotesque, not disjoined from a recondite poetry and beauty of its own kind, is the same mechanism: the figure is simplified until it is almost a mask, and the mask is where the poetry sits. The watchable behaviour is a seated man reduced to three blocks, head, torso, legs, who still reads as one particular man, and the particularity survives the reduction, because the reduction is the portrait.

**Takeaway:** build the man from three blocks and let the mask be the portrait, and the piece should read as one particular man, not as a type.

## M9. The earthy dry palette

Colour as dust, plaster and dry earth. Treccani: "tavolozza terrosa e asciutta", earthy and dry palette. The Uffizi: his use of an earthy palette, a severe palette. it.wikipedia on the late work: "una cromia dai toni sordi e cupi", a chromatism of dull and dark tones. Difesa, quoted in Treccani: "il contrario matematico dei toscani, dei nostri grandi maestri toscani, tutta secchezza, sobrieta ed essenzialita", the mathematical opposite of the Tuscans, of our great Tuscan masters, all dryness, sobriety and essentiality. Rosai's own confession: "punto o poco m'attrae il colorismo, ovvero il cromatismo alla spagnuola", little or nothing attracts me in colourism, that is, in Spanish-style chromatism. The watchable behaviour is ochres, umbers, plaster whites, a single cold blue or dull red held back until it matters, and the palette is a moral choice before it is a taste, because the dryness is the refusal of decoration, and the refusal is the style.

**Takeaway:** paint with dust and plaster and hold the red back until it matters, and the piece should be dry before it is coloured.

## M10. Living two lives, the guilt after his father's suicide

it.wikipedia: "Nei suoi scritti giovanili rivela di sentirsi colpevole di quella morte, e di dover vivere due vite, la sua e quella del padre", in his youthful writings he reveals he feels guilty for that death, and that he must live two lives, his own and his father's. Treccani: the father Giuseppe, oppressed by debts, killed himself, drowning in the Arno in 1922, and the debts fell on Ottone, who took over the carpentry workshop from 1923 to 1926 and painted less. Estorick: his output diminished in the 1920s partly owing to his need to support his family following the suicide of his father in 1922. The mechanism is that the double life is literal, painter by vocation and carpenter by debt, and then psychological, the survivor living for the dead, and it explains the 1923 to 1926 gap, the small formats, painting stolen from the workshop day, and the gravity of the mature work, because the men in the street are also living on borrowed time. The study uses the phrase "vivere due vite" as Rosai's own, not as a critic's gloss, and the two lives are the reason the work is never light, because one of the lives in it is already dead.

**Takeaway:** let one life in the picture already be dead, and the piece should carry its debt in its gravity, not in its story.

## M11. The writer-painter double life

Books as a second medium, not a sideline. Treccani's bibliography lists the autobiographical volumes: Il libro di un teppista, Florence 1919; Via Toscanella, preface by Soffici, Florence 1930; Dentro la guerra, Rome 1934; Difesa, in Il Frontespizio, VIII (1936), 3 (March), pages 6 to 10; Vecchio autoritratto, preface by Carlo Bo, Florence 1951; plus Lettere 1914-1957, edited by Vittoria Corti, Prato 1974. The Museo Novecento writings volume runs from his beginnings with Lacerba in 1914 until the last days of life: costume articles, short essays of artistic and literary criticism, autobiographical passages, narrative sketches also in the Florentine vernacular. The Gabinetto Vieusseux holds the Fondo Ottone Rosai: manuscripts 1913 to 1957 in four boxes and seventy-eight files, periodicals with articles by and about Rosai, and two block-notes of Difesa drafts datable to the Difesa composition period. The writing is where Rosai says what the painting does: the Difesa is the theory, the books are the life, and the 2026 show's title says it, "Poeta innanzitutto", poet above all else. The watchable behaviour for a prompt is text entering the picture as a street sign, a shouted title, a read-aloud voice, Rosai reading his own street, and the words are not captions, they are part of the street.

**Takeaway:** let the painter also write, and the piece should carry its own words in it, not under it.

## M12. The Difesa self-defence, 1936

The artist answers his critics in public, and the answer is the poetics. Treccani, quoting Difesa, read aloud at the Lyceum in the spring of 1936 and published in parallel in Il Frontespizio, March 1936, page 6: the charges, "Si e detto che non so disegnare, che manco di colore, che non ho tecnica, che la mia arte e popolaresca, che i miei quadri sono una stornellata", it has been said that I do not know how to draw, that I lack colour, that I have no technique, that my art is popular, that my pictures are a stornellata, a street song. The answers, in Rosai's words: "Ch'io non sappia disegnare mi sembra un'affermazione troppo grossa, dal momento che in me la passione d'esprimere e cosi forte, che un dato oggetto, prima ancora di averlo disegnato, lo avverto gia vivere nelle sue forme, realizzate nella mia mente e nei nervi, da sentirmelo quasi uscire dalla punta delle dita", that I do not know how to draw seems to me too large an affirmation, since in me the passion to express is so strong that a given object, even before I have drawn it, I already feel living in its forms, realised in my mind and in my nerves, as if I felt it almost come out of the tips of my fingers. On colour: "Relativamente al colore, confessero che punto o poco m'attrae il colorismo, ovvero il cromatismo alla spagnuola", as for colour, I will confess that little or nothing attracts me in colourism, that is, in Spanish-style chromatism. On the figure: "Che importa a me che un malleolo sia ben lisciato, ben fatto? Io voglio scoprire l'anima della mia creatura, il suo viso interno: voglio trovare il suo dramma: essere quella santita di luce e di spazio dipinti in cui si esala il suo grido", what does it matter to me that an ankle be well smoothed, well made? I want to discover the soul of my creature, its inner face: I want to find its drama: to be that holiness of light and painted space in which its cry is exhaled. The full Difesa was NOT read end to end this leg, only Treccani's excerpts, and the study says so. The mechanism is drawing as a bodily event, the object living in the nerves before the hand moves, colour refused as decoration, the portrait as the discovery of an inner face and a drama, the painting as a holiness of light and space in which a cry is exhaled.

**Takeaway:** draw from the nerves and find the inner face, and the piece should exhale one cry, not show one ankle.

## M13. The black-contour nudes

The male nude closed by a heavy black line, isolated on a neutral ground. Treccani on the postwar work: "i nudi virili chiusi da un pesante contorno nero che li isolava da un fondo neutro, intriso e livido", the virile nudes closed by a heavy black contour that isolated them from a neutral ground, soaked and livid. it.wikipedia and queerplaces: a crucial part of his oeuvre is composed of portraits of male nudes, proletarian youths whom he met in the streets of Florence and who generally became his lovers as well as his models, as revealed in the biography written by his close friend Piero Santi. Giovanni Testori, quoted in queerplaces: an exhibition of Rosai's nudes would mean linking them to his homosexuality, but those who could organise such an exhibition are afraid of showing this connection. Several nudes were shown at the 1995 centenary. ## M15. The tabernacle Crucifixion as public devotion

A sacred picture given away, on a street corner, for the city. it.wikipedia: in 1954 in Florence he painted and donated free, following the initiative of the Comitato per l'estetica cittadina to renew the ancient ruined tabernacles with works by contemporary artists, a Crocifissione, which testifies to the persistence of Rosai's interest in the Tuscan fifteenth and sixteenth century tradition: Giotto and Masaccio are still the points of reference of a language that has become, with the years, ever more harsh and surly, exasperating its own expressionist root, reducing painting to brutal signs and adopting a chromatism of dull and dark tones, distorting physiognomies into masks of a crude primitivism. ## M20. The white period

The last light: pale, bright, empty of people. exibart: "The pieces from the so-called 'white period,' characterised by the prevalence of light and by sophisticated chromatic variations, concludes the work and the life of the artist. ## Evolution

Six phases. Phase 1, apprenticeship and expulsion, 1906 to 1913: first drawings 1906; the Istituto di arti decorative; expelled; the Accademia under Calosci; the free school of the nude; Celestini's engraving school; expelled 1912; etchings after Craig; Siena 1913, Duccio and the Lorenzetti brothers; reading Baudelaire, Mallarme, Kipling, Dostoevskij, Wilde; first shows 1913. Phase 2, the Futurist year and the war, 1913 to 1919: the Lacerba circle; Dinamismo Bar Sanmarco 1913; Scomposizione di una strada 1914; the Sprovieri Esposizione libera futurista, April to May 1914; volunteer Wednesday 9 December 1914; the Granatieri; the Arditi; Monte Grappa; Aiutante di Battaglia Wednesday 10 October 1917; Il libro di un teppista 1919; the Futurist idiom already loosening at the front, taking in Rousseau. Phase 3, the small-format decade, 1919 to 1929: still lifes 1919; first solo, Palazzo Capponi, November to December 1920, twenty-nine paintings; marriage and his father's suicide 1922; the Gonnelli benefit show; the Rome Bragaglia solo, end of 1922, over fifty paintings; the carpentry workshop 1923 to 1926; return 1927, the Selvaggio group, Bilenchi, Pratolini; Suonatori 1928; the great Giocatori di toppa 1929; the I Mostra regionale toscana and the XVI Biennale 1928; the break with Il Selvaggio 1928; the language is fixed, small format, geometric essentiality, metallic firm light, earthy dry palette, plebeian figures, street games, walls. Phase 4, crisis, break and consecration, 1929 to 1936: Il Bargello and Il Frontespizio 1929; the Lateran Pacts 1929 and Per lo svaticanamento dell'Italia; the break with Soffici, the pamphlet of 1931; the book Via Toscanella 1930; the Milione failure 1930, a year without painting; Dentro la guerra serialised by Ungaretti, published 1934; L'Universale from 1931; leaves the carpentry at the end of 1931; the pupils Caponi and Donnini; Palazzo Ferroni 1932, the first success; Milan, Galleria delle Tre Arti, 1933, curated by Persico; seven paintings at the Venice Biennale 1934; the studio in Via San Leonardo 1934; the Tinti illustrations 1935; the station panels 1935; the II Quadriennale; Warsaw; Difesa, the Lyceum, spring 1936; the painting grows clearer, lighter, more vibrant in renewed contact with life, per Treccani. Phase 5, teacher and war, 1939 to 1945: the Liceo Artistico 1939; the Academy chair 1942; religious subjects; the house in Via de' Benci a GAP refuge; the anti-fascist attack after 8 September 1943; Fanciullacci and Gentile 1944; self-portraits and crucifixions of touching expressive power, per Cambi; isolation and anger harden into the black-contour nudes. Phase 6, postwar rarefaction and the white period, 1945 to 1957: ostracism, then slow recovery; the hermetic poets gather round; the Galleria Il Fiore; Verzocchi 1949 to 1950; Vecchio autoritratto 1951; the Premio del Fiorino 1951; the XXVI Biennale retrospective 1952, curated by Parronchi; the La Strozzina solo 1953, by Ragghianti; the tabernacle Crocifissione 1954; the Amici portraits 1949 to 1955; La Firenze di Rosai 1951 to 1954; the XXVIII Biennale retrospective 1956; the Ivrea show and death Monday 13 May 1957; international shows in New York, Cairo, Paris, Zurich, London, Madrid and the German cities.

## Aesthetic logic

The Rosai logic is that the street is the academy and the small panel is the monument. He was expelled from the school and taught himself by walking, and the walking never stopped: the taverns, the card rooms, the workshops, the alleys between the Carmine and San Frediano, and the picture is what the walk leaves behind, a small panel the size of a tray, holding a whole street. The second logic is that the picture is built by subtraction: the Futurist decomposition survives as block-building, the Cezanne volumes survive as coloured blocks, the figure survives as three wooden blocks, the palette survives as dust and plaster, and what is taken away is the anecdote, the decoration, the horizon, the crowd. The wall takes the horizon's place, the game takes the plot's place, the looking takes the face's place. The third logic is that the man lives two lives and the picture carries both: the carpenter's son who paints men like furniture, the painter who writes books, the fascist squadrist who shelters partisans, the expelled student who takes the chair, the teacher whose school becomes the charge against him. The picture is never light because one of the lives in it is already dead, and the light in it is metallic and firm because the light is the only thing that does not die. The last logic is the white period: the players leave, the people are removed, the wall and the cupola stay, painted almost white, and the city is alone, and the aloneness is the calm the whole life was working toward.

## Load-bearing corrections

C0. "GRADUATED 1912" AGAINST EXPELLED 1912: en.wikipedia says "Rosai graduated from the Florence Academy of Fine Arts in 1912". Treccani says he was expelled from the Accademia in 1912 after a serious dispute with Calosci. it.wikipedia says he was expelled after a few years for bad conduct. Estorick and Cambi agree with expulsion. This file uses EXPELLED, never graduated. The error propagates to arteforlife.com, which copies en.wikipedia verbatim; a downstream copy is not a second source, see C29.

C1. GIOCATORI DI TOPPA DATES: 1920 first version, Treccani and MASI; 1928 version, it.wikipedia works list; 1929 great version, Treccani. All three are real; the series ran for decades. Do not collapse to one date.

C2. SERENATA AND CONCERTINO: Treccani, on the 1920 solo, gives "Serenata o Concertino", one work, two titles, 1920. it.wikipedia's Masaccio sentence cites "Il concertino, 1927". Either the same picture re-dated, or a second version of 1927. UNRESOLVED. Present both datings with the "o" in the title.

C3. MARRIAGE YEAR AND THE FORCED-MARRIAGE STORY: Treccani says Rosai met Francesca Fei in 1921 and she became his wife the following year, 1922. it.wikipedia and Estorick tell a forced-marriage story tied to the post-Lateran-Pacts outing in 1929 and the 1938 fascist-police warning, with no date and the bride named only as a childhood friend. If the bride is Fei, the forcing cannot be dated 1929, he was already married seven years. If the forcing story is true, the bride may be someone else, but every source names Fei as the wife, donation "Francesca Fei ved. Rosai". The likeliest reading is that the marriage was 1922 and the 1929 and 1938 material, the outing and the police warning, is a separate, later pressure, wrongly attached to the wedding in the it.wikipedia narrative. This file dates the marriage 1922, per Treccani, and reports the forcing story as an undated, single-tradition account.

C4. THE 1938 FASCIST-POLICE WARNING: queerplaces, copying it.wikipedia's older text, says fascist police in 1938 gave him a strong-armed warning about his frequenting of male prostitutes, and he avoided deportation only because of his Fascist membership card. Treccani says only that he suffered discrimination for personal reasons, specifically his homosexuality, never openly declared. No date, no police, no card in Treccani. The 1938 detail is single-tradition and is flagged as such, not stated as Treccani fact.

C5. AMICI 27 AGAINST 28: RESOLVED, with a qualifier. The Comune di Firenze owns 27, SIGEC catalogue, quantity 27, donation 1963, deliberation 9 April 1963, folios 3 to 4, numbers 1 to 27. The Comune deliberation lists 28, but number 28 is the Autoritratto con Palazzo Vecchio, which by format and support belongs to the La Firenze di Rosai series, not the Amici, per the SIGEC records.

C6. AMICI 27 AGAINST EXIBART'S 30: exibart counts the 30 friend portraits found in the studio in 1957, 1949 to 1955; SIGEC counts the 27 that reached the Comune; Santini, 1960, page 221, via SIGEC, notes many further portraits beyond the Comune's. Use "27 in the Comune bequest; about 30 in the studio group; more in private hands".

C7. TONDINI COUNT: exibart gives 18 portraits, 1939 to 1943. it.wikipedia gives no count. No conflict, but the study cites 18 to exibart and notes some are now in private collections.

C8. CAMBI BIRTH YEAR 1885: the Cambi page header and every lot header read "(Firenze, 1885 - Ivrea, 1957)". Rosai was born 1895, Treccani, both Wikipedias, Estorick, and Cambi's own biography text. A template error; do not copy.

C9. WAR MEDALS: it.wikipedia says two silver medals and the rank Aiutante di Battaglia on 10 October 1917. en.wikipedia says a Bronze and a Silver Medal for Military Valor. Treccani says only that he was decorated, without specifying. UNRESOLVED. Do not state a medal count without the dissent.

C10. ENLISTMENT PHASES: it.wikipedia says volunteer 9 December 1914, incorporated 22 January 1915, 1st Granatieri di Sardegna. en.wikipedia says in late 1917 he enlisted in the XIX Shock Battalion, later renamed the XXIII. These are two different enlistments, 1914 volunteer and 1917 Arditi, and en.wikipedia's phrasing collapses them. Treccani: he left as a soldier at the outbreak of war, then was taken into the Arditi assault troops on Monte Grappa. Keep the two phases distinct.

C11. FIRST SOLO AGAINST FIRST SHOW: Treccani calls the November to December 1920 Palazzo Capponi show the first solo, 29 paintings. But Treccani also records a November to December 1913 Via Cavour show of 15 paintings and 2 sculptures, and Palazzo Citterio calls 1913 his first solo show. The 1920 show is the first solo in the sense the artist and Soffici counted it; 1913 was a small-room exhibition. This file says "first solo show, Palazzo Capponi, November to December 1920" and notes the 1913 Via Cavour show separately.

C12. SPROVIERI 1914, JOINT OR NOT: en.wikipedia says Rosai held a joint exhibition with Soffici at the Galleria Sprovieri, Rome, in 1914. Treccani says in April and May 1914 he was invited to take part in the Esposizione libera futurista in Rome at the Galleria Sprovieri, a group Futurist show, with no joint with Soffici stated. The joint claim is en.wikipedia-only; do not use it without the flag.

C13. VIA TOSCANELLA, PAINTING 1922 AGAINST BOOK 1930, NOT A CONFLICT, BUT A TRAP: same title, two media, eight years apart. Sources cite one when they mean the other. The brief's vinyl of Rosai reading Via Toscanella was NOT FOUND in any search: three searches, no record, no discography entry, no archive holding. The 2026 Museo Novecento show plays recordings of Bigongiari, Luzi, Montale and Ungaretti reading their own poems, not Rosai. Do not use the vinyl claim.

C14. "BARONCELLI" 1932: attested only by the Montevarchi exhibition text, eventiintoscana.it, and MET: the rediscovery and correct identification of a 1932 painting "Baroncelli", plus a preparatory drawing disproving the plein-air thesis. No image seen, no catalogue entry found, unattested in Treccani and it.wikipedia. Single-source; flagged.

C15. DEATH PLACE: Treccani says he died 13 May at the Albergo Dora, Ivrea. it.wikipedia says in a room at the Albergo Dora. They agree. en.wikipedia says died in Ivrea, Turin, in 1957, where Turin is the province, not the city. No conflict; note the province reading.

C16. MILIONE 1930: Treccani says the Milan Il Milione solo resolved itself in a failure and produced a year without painting. en.wikipedia says Persico organised a solo at the Galleria del Milione in 1930, with no outcome stated. Consistent; the failure detail is Treccani-only.

C17. TEACHING POSTS: 1939 Liceo Artistico, professor of drawn figure, per it.wikipedia, teacher of the Reale Liceo Artistico, per Treccani, and 1942 Academy chair of painting. All sources agree. en.wikipedia's "obtained a teaching post at the Academy in 1942" omits the 1939 post; not a conflict, an omission.

C18. DONATI PUPILLAGE SOURCE ASYMMETRY: the teacher link is attested on the DONATI side, it.wikipedia's Danilo Donati page plus its Ukrainian, Russian and French versions, and NOT in Treccani's Rosai entry, which never names Donati. This file cites the link as Donati became Rosai's pupil in the immediate postwar years, and notes the Accademia setting comes from the translated Donati pages, not from Rosai scholarship. See D1.

C19. BACON ADMIRATION: single source, the Montevarchi exhibition text, eventiintoscana.it: "di cui Francis Bacon apprezzava in particolare gli autoritratti e i nudi degli anni Quaranta", of which Francis Bacon appreciated in particular the self-portraits and nudes of the 1940s. No Bacon quotation, no citation, no catalogue reference found in two searches. Treat as an exhibition text claim, not a verified Bacon statement.

C20. BASELITZ RESTAURANT ANECDOTE: arte.it, via director Sergio Risaliti quoting Baselitz: "Se in quel locale avevo in mano una foto o un catalogo di Rosai, il cameriere veniva e diceva: 'Oh, Ottone Rosai'", if in that place I had in hand a photo or a catalogue of Rosai, the waiter came and said: "Oh, Ottone Rosai". The restaurant is unnamed; the quote reaches us second-hand, Baselitz to Risaliti to the press. Usable with the attribution chain stated. The 1965 Florence stay itself is solid, Villa Romana scholarship, Christie's, Morgan Library biography, six-month fellowship.

C21. IL SELVAGGIO DATES: en.wikipedia says he began contributing to the magazine Il Selvaggio in 1926. Treccani says he took part in the first Selvaggio group show in 1927 and broke with Il Selvaggio in 1928. Contributing in 1926 against exhibiting in 1927 are different acts; no conflict if kept distinct.

C22. DE CHIRICO PRAISE, PALAZZO FERRONI 1932: Cambi only, "publicly praised by Soffici and De Chirico". Treccani records the show as the first success and names no praiser. Single source; flagged.

C23. LA SPEZIA CIGAR INCIDENT: it.wikipedia only, hedged in the source itself, "Sembra che tanta fosse la sua rabbia", it seems that so great was his rage. Undated, "in quel periodo", in that period, the 1950s. Passani, 1926 to 2019, is corroborated as a real painter who met Rosai, Massa Carrara Netweek, 2026 centenary. The incident is anecdote, not documented fact; report it as told.

C24. FANCIULLACCI AND GENTILE 1944: it.wikipedia only, citing Montanelli, Battaglia and Cervi, Storia d'Italia volume 15: Fanciullacci brought wounded to Rosai's house; Rosai defended him from fascists; the reproach "Bella impresa uccidere un povero vecchio". Treccani says only that the Via de' Benci house became a refuge for GAP partisans. The Gentile reproach is it.wikipedia-only; flagged.

C25. BIRTHPLACE STREET: Cambi only, "Born on Via Cimabue in the heart of an ancient working-class neighborhood". Treccani says "in un quartiere popolare", in a working-class quarter, no street. Single source; usable with "Cambi gives the street as Via Cimabue".

C26. DIPLOMA AGAINST EXPULSION: it.wikipedia says "Conseguito il diploma all'Istituto Statale d'Arte frequenta l'Accademia di Belle Arti, da cui viene espulso dopo pochi anni per cattiva condotta", having obtained the diploma at the State Art Institute he attends the Academy of Fine Arts, from which he is expelled after a few years for bad conduct. Treccani says he was expelled from the Istituto, then expelled from the Accademia in 1912. A diploma from the Istituto is incompatible with expulsion from it unless the diploma predates the expulsion; the it.wikipedia sentence is loose. de.wikipedia says a three-year training at the Istituto delle Arti Decorative plus Academy courses from 1909. This file follows Treccani: expelled twice, no diploma claimed.

C27. TWO BIENNALE RETROSPECTIVES, NOT ONE: Treccani says 1952, XXVI Biennale, retrospective curated by Parronchi. en.wikipedia and it.wikipedia say 1956, XXVIII Biennale, large retrospective. Both are real; do not present 1956 as the only retrospective.

C28. SECONDA MOSTRA DEL NOVECENTO 1929: en.wikipedia says he took part in the Seconda Mostra del Novecento Italiano in Milan in 1929. Treccani does not name it in the narrative, it names the II Quadriennale, Warsaw, and the Biennale. No contradiction; the participation is en.wikipedia-only.

C29. "GRADUATED" PROPAGATES: arteforlife.com copies en.wikipedia verbatim, "Rosai graduated from the Florence Academy of Fine Arts in 1912". A downstream copy is not a second source. Same for the queerplaces page, which copies it.wikipedia. When counting corroboration, copies do not count.

C30. DINAMISMO BAR SANMARCO DATE STRADDLE: Treccani places it at the opening of the Futurist year 1914, "Con il 1914 si avvio l'anno futurista di Rosai, inaugurato dall'olio su cartone Dinamismo Bar S. Marco", with 1914 there began the Futurist year of Rosai, inaugurated by the oil on cardboard Dinamismo Bar S. Marco, while en.wikipedia dates the painting 1913. The work may be 1913 and the year 1914, or the dating differs. Present as "1913, shown as the opening work of the Futurist year, 1914".

C31. "NO CAFE" 1946: pt.wikipedia says the air of abstraction intensified after the Second World War, "No cafe, 1946". No Italian source in this leg confirms a 1946 cafe painting under that title; it.wikipedia lists Figure al caffe 1941 and Giocatori di carte 1943. Do not cite "No cafe 1946" without further verification.

C32. MONTEVARCHI DATES: MET headline says the show at Montevarchi runs 9 April to 12 July, inauguration 5 April at 18.30. eventiintoscana lists the same show 21 January to 6 June 2021, and its start date is 19 April 2021. The year is 2021, the centenary of the 1920 first solo. The 9 April to 12 July span against the 21 January to 6 June listing disagree; the show was likely extended or re-dated. Use "Montevarchi, Palazzo del Podesta, 2021, 9 April to 12 July per MET" and note the listing discrepancy. Inauguration Monday 5 April 2021, date-checked, see Study method note.
