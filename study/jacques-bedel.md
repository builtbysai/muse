## The life: the architect who hid cities in books

Jacques Bedel was born in Buenos Aires on 7 August 1947, the son of the poet Rene Bedel, and grew up in a family circle frequented by artists and writers. He entered the UBA Faculty of Architecture in 1965 and graduated in 1972, developing his art entirely self-taught in parallel -- no academy, no workshop. His first exhibition was Cromosombras in 1967, kinetic illuminated mobiles whose shadows projected colours; his first solo show, Opformas, followed at Galeria Galatea in 1968, with curved mirrors multiplying the image to infinity. Reflection and infinity were named as lifelong themes from that year.

In 1970 he began working on desmaterializacion -- micro-perforated steel, then mirror-polished metal that erases volume by reflection -- and showed Antimateria in Escultura, follaje y ruidos, the first occupation of a public square by the newly created CAYC, beginning a decades-long collaboration with its director Jorge Glusberg. In November 1971, after an encounter with the Polish theatre director Jerzy Grotowski, the Grupo de los Trece was founded at CAYC: thirteen artists including Bedel and Benedit, named for Grotowski's Theatre of 13 Rows (C9920). The joining-date tension is flagged, not smoothed over: ICAA puts Bedel in the November 1971 founding meeting; CVAA and the official site narrate his joining under December 1972 (C9921).

The career then runs in four phases, and the hinge is the book. (1) Light, mirror, disappearance, 1967-1971: kinetic shadows, mirrors, a modular metal city with music (Ciudad para armar, Paris Biennial 1969), magnetic fields moving small bodies (Energeia, 1971, silver medal -- refused to withdraw in protest against censorship). (2) Hypothesis, catastrophe, the book, 1972-1979: the Hipotesis heliographs -- deadpan technical reports on disasters that have not happened -- and the first book-sculpture in 1973, La vuelta al hogar. Hipotesis para el incendio de una iglesia. The book-cities, Las ciudades de plata, were begun 1975-77 and shown at the XIV Sao Paulo Biennial in 1977, where the group won the Grande Premio Itamaraty, the first time Brazil's Foreign Ministry prize went to a Latin American representative (C9924). The same years: Graficos argentinos '74 in Chicago with Benedit (C9923); the mid-1970s nine, renamed Grupo CAYC from 1975 (C9925); the Recoleta commission with Testa and Benedit (C9926). (3) Roll, sphere, totem, 1980-1999: the lead roll Memoria de America (1980-81), the counterweight spheres Labyrinthos (1985) and Res Vitae (1995), the totem El puno de plata on the ruins of Manoa (1990), the scripture rolls Verbum (1992) and the Skylab data rolls Ignis (1996), Venice again in 1999 with the Apocalipsis rolls. The Fulbright year is unresolved -- 1980, 1981 or 1982, with 1982 best supported (C9927). (4) Transparency, combination, photography, 2000-2023: the transparent-leaf books Radiografia de la Pampa (2004) and Ad infinitum (2013/2017), the shadow-projecting photographs Elogio de la sombra and Rapsodias (MNBA Chile, 2023). Alongside all of it ran an architecture practice -- estancia houses, Malabrigo winning the architecture Gold Medal in 1987 -- and more than 45 awards, 14 international biennials, 450+ exhibitions (the FNA's 500+ is the higher figure, C9938). The Ficciones retrospective at Recoleta in 2005 covered forty years; the FNA Premio Trayectoria came in 2024 (C9937). No teaching post, directorship or academy membership is recorded in any source read; do not invent one (C9940).

## M1 -- The hidden-city book: a whole territory inside a closed volume

A book of standard bibliographic size, bound in burlap, sits closed on a shelf looking ordinary. Open it and a miniature ruined city is inside: remains of pyramids, traces of pre-Columbian settlements, ruins of colonial churches with their cloisters, coated in mineral patinas -- reddish, silvery, bluish -- made by mineralizacion and electrolytic iron and aluminium plating. The objects are, in CVAA's phrase, "verdaderos simulacros a escala", true scale simulacra. The first book is W07 La vuelta al hogar. Hipotesis para el incendio de una iglesia (1973); the series is W08 Las ciudades de plata (books begun 1976, shown Sao Paulo 1977; new version of the reliefs 1994). Nothing in the sources describes hinges, clasps or page counts; the opening is the reveal mechanism itself, and the city is hidden while the book is closed. The watchable behaviour is a two-state object: closed, it is inert and ordinary; open, a whole territory appears at small scale. The city is a secret the container keeps, and the opening is the event.

**Takeaway:** the book is not a picture of a city. It is a container that keeps a city, and the work happens in the moment the container opens. A piece on M1 needs a closed state that looks unremarkable and an opening that is the whole drama.

## M2 -- The angle-mirage relief: silver that only appears when you move

The companion cuadro-relieves of 1977 are aerial views of the same vestiges, almost hidden in the topography, whose metallic reflections appear or disappear with the viewer's position. The Herlitzka+Faria text says the works "demand the spectator move to discover the silver gleams, repeating, with their movements, the search that guided the conquistadores". The mechanism descends from W04 Antimateria (1970), where micro-perforated steel and mirror polish make a solid seem transparent according to viewing angle, and it returns in the two-viewer paintings of 1998-2000 (La ciudad invisible and kin, unnumbered), where two people standing in different places see different images at the same time. The watchable behaviour is a surface that flashes silver, then goes dull, as the viewer walks past; the mirage is a function of viewpoint, not of the object. The conquistador parallel is the point: the silver was never in the ground, it was in the looking.

**Takeaway:** the mechanism to steal is viewpoint as the trigger. Do not animate the silver; animate the viewer's position relative to it, and let the flash be the reward for moving.

## M3 -- The counterweight sphere at the edge: balance that looks impossible

A hollow sphere with a mobile internal counterweight can be placed in limit positions that defy gravity: at the centre of its base it is harmony, at the edge it is alarming tension. W13 Labyrinthos (1985, First Prize of the Argentine Association of Art Critics) and El Dios reencontrado (1986, a large rotating sphere on bearings); W16 Res Vitae (1995, Kwangju Biennale) adds labyrinths carved on the sphere's skin -- a 96 cm sphere on a 110 cm base, electrolytic aluminium on a hollow sphere with counterweight (the 1997 catalogued example date is unstable against the 1995 series date, C9942). The sphere is the only Bedel object meant to be touched and moved into an unstable position. The watchable behaviour is a sphere holding a position that looks impossible, trembling at the rim of its plinth; pushed, it turns and settles, the maze on its skin rotating with it.

**Takeaway:** M3 is the strongest "balance at the edge" mechanism in the oeuvre, and the only one where touch is earned: in the source work the sphere is placed by hand. A piece on M3 should let the viewer drag the sphere to the rim and leave it there, trembling.

## M4 -- Transparent-leaf combinatorics: twelve leaves, inexhaustible images

Books with transparent leaves that allow rotation, inversion and translation of the sheets, producing an extraordinary number of image combinations. W18 Radiografia de la Pampa (made 2004, shown 2007) is the first statement; W19 Ad infinitum (developed 2013; shown Maman Fine Art, Buenos Aires, 2017, curated by Rodrigo Alonso) fixes the number at twelve interchangeable leaves, yielding "an incommensurable number of combinations". Individual leaves are digital prints on polycarbonate. The watchable behaviour is shuffling a small fixed set of transparent layers and getting a new image every time; no leaf is ever "the" image, and the image is the sum of the stack. This is the most directly generative book in the oeuvre: a finite set plus three operations (rotate, flip, shift) is a combinatorial image machine.

**Takeaway:** the mechanism is counting, not drawing. Twelve leaves is small enough to hold and large enough that the combinations are, for practical purposes, inexhaustible. A piece on M4 should show the count -- the leaves, the operations, the new image -- and never pretend one arrangement is the final one.

## M5 -- The unrolling data roll: reading as unrolling

A lead roll reveals its content only as it is unrolled: territory in W12 Memoria de America (first lead roll, 1980-81, Gran Premio de Honor at Montevideo), scripture in W15 Verbum (1992, twelve rolls, sacred texts from the Egyptian Book of the Dead to Genesis to the Koran), scientific data in W17 Ignis (1996, twelve rolls reproducing the measurement band of the solar flare of 15 June 1973 recorded by Skylab, electrolytic iron on cloth). Earlier, 1983: lead rolls on messages sent to outer space, El mensaje de Arecibo. The roll is a variant of the book -- a writing support in patinated metal, unrolled rather than opened -- and the title Memoria de America was reused forty years later for a 200 x 200 cm engraved-polyethylene panel (2021), a different object that must not be merged with the roll (C9943). The watchable behaviour is an image that exists in time: you cannot see it all at once, and unrolling is reading. The content changes -- territory, scripture, flare data -- but the rule does not.

**Takeaway:** the roll makes time the viewing condition. A piece on M5 should be a long horizontal band that scrolls or unrolls under the viewer's drag, revealing a city, then a text, then a flare curve, never the whole at once.

## M6 -- Magnetic displacement: an invisible field moves the object

Polished geometric elements and magnets that allow a small body -- spheres, a mirror -- to be displaced. W05 Energeia (1971, Silver Medal, II Certamen Nacional de Investigaciones Visuales; also shown as Fuerza magnetica referida a energia) is the first invisible-force work: the "action" is a field, not a depicted object. Related 1971 works in the same passage (Hipotesis para una prision, La gran muralla) use acrylic with wood and rough granite blocks. The watchable behaviour is smooth, indirect motion: a small sphere or mirror drifts across a polished plane following a field the viewer cannot see, and the motion is the work. The political register is attached to the same year -- Bedel refused to withdraw the prize as repudiation of censorship under the de facto government -- but the mechanism itself is pure physics.

**Takeaway:** M6 is the quietest mechanism in the study: no container, no opening, no text, only a field and a small body obeying it. A piece on M6 should make the field visible only through what it moves.

## M7 -- The hypothesis report: bureaucratic calm describing ruin

A deadpan technical document -- a heliograph with legend, plan and text -- describing the destruction of a real place that has not happened. W06 Hipotesis para la verificacion de los danos producidos por un terremoto en el centro de la ciudad de Buenos Aires (May 1972, signed "jacques bedel / cayc 5-1972", SURDOC record 2-4355, inventory GE-452, MNBA Chile, the only Bedel work in this study with a museum inventory number, C9941) is the anchor; the same family runs through Hipotesis para la destruccion de una casa, Hipotesis para la desaparicion del Pico Ojos del Salado, and the bridge-destruction hypotheses of 1972-73, the last two as maquettes, not only on paper. The maquette form -- a small model of a real bridge, slated for destruction -- is the bridge between the heliograph and the book-city: a model inside a fiction. The watchable behaviour is bureaucratic calm describing ruin: a city plan with an official-looking caption that annotates its own destruction, block by block, and the fiction is delivered as a report.

**Takeaway:** M7 is the only mechanism in this study where the text does the work. A piece on M7 should build the document first (plan, legend, stamp) and let the destruction arrive as annotation, not explosion.

## M8 -- The shadow-projection photograph: the picture is the shadow

A digital photograph printed on polycarbonate or PVC; lit, it projects its own shadow, and the image becomes ghostly and imprecise. Elogio de la sombra (2011-13, title citing Borges) states it; W20 Rapsodias (MNBA Chile, 2 February - 23 April 2023, curators Gabriel Palumbo and Florence Baranger) is the full statement: digitally printed photographs on polycarbonate or PVC, images superimposed, shadows played with -- giant clouds, stains evoking the mountain range seen from the air, inert plastics making their way like water between emerging cement blocks, urban environments captured against the light. The late work returns to the two oldest obsessions -- cloud and shadow (W01, 1967) and the aerial view of a territory (W08, 1977) -- but the book has dissolved into the photograph and the reveal now happens in light, not by opening. The watchable behaviour is a landscape on a translucent panel whose shadow -- larger, softer, slightly wrong -- is the real picture, and moving the light moves the image.

**Takeaway:** M8 inverts M1: the book hid the city inside itself; the photograph throws the city outside itself, onto the wall. A piece on M8 should let the viewer drag the light, and let the shadow be the image.

## The aesthetic logic: the object with two states

Bedel's aesthetic is the logic of the two-state object. Everything he makes has a closed state and an open state, and the viewer switches between them by moving, opening, unrolling, reordering, or shifting the light. The book is closed, then open; the relief is dull, then flashing; the sphere is centred, then at the edge; the roll is wound, then unrolled; the leaves are stacked, then shuffled; the photograph is flat, then casting a shadow. The beauty is never in one state alone -- it is in the switching, and in the fact that the switching is done by a body in the room, not by the artist.

Against Benedit, his predecessor in this chain, the contrast is exact and it is the study's hinge. Benedit builds a container and lets a living agent complete it: the bees choose, the mice solve, the plant dies from kindness. Bedel builds a container and lets the viewer complete it: the hand opens the book, the feet find the silver, the drag unrolls the data. Benedit's system answers back with behaviour; Bedel's system answers back with a second state. Both are architects of systems, but Benedit's agent is alive and Bedel's agent is the viewer. The through line of Bedel's own career is the container getting lighter: the burlap book (1976) becomes the lead roll (1980), the roll becomes the transparent leaves (2004), the leaves become the photograph's shadow (2023) -- the city stays hidden, but the hiding place dissolves until only light is left. A study that misses the two states misses Bedel; a piece prompt that shows only one state -- the open book, the flashing relief, the centred sphere -- is not a Bedel prompt. The prompt must contain the switch.
