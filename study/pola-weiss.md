# Pola Weiss: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-05. ig-artist-study #348.

**Site:** Pola Maria Weiss Alvarez (Mexico City, Saturday 3 May 1947 - Mexico City, Sunday 6 May 1990, aged 43), Mexican video artist, television producer and teacher, widely credited as the pioneer of video art in Mexico and of videodanza, the video dance. Daughter of Leopoldo Weiss, of Alsatian origin, and Emma Alvarez, Mexican; younger sister Kitzia, who composed music for some of her videos. Raised in Colonia Roma Sur; her grandmother Maria took her to the cinema three or four times a week, which is where her love of the moving image began. Trained in classical ballet as a child and in modern and contemporary dance later; studied English literature, played the piano, and loved being in front of a camera. At the Universidad Nacional Autonoma de Mexico she first aimed at political science, then took cinematography courses at the Centro Universitario de Estudios Cinematograficos and graduated in communication and journalism in 1975 with the first thesis in video in the university's history, against institutional policy. As a student she worked in broadcast television at Televisa and the state-owned Canal 13. In 1975 she traveled in Europe studying artistic, experimental and educational television at the BBC in London, the RAI in Rome, the VPRO in Holland and the ORTF in Paris; in 1976, in New York, she met Nam June Paik and Shigeko Kubota, who changed her life. She founded her production company arTV in 1977, made her first video, Flor Cosmica, the same year, and founded the Taller Experimental de Video at the Facultad de Ciencias Politicas y Sociales. In 1978 she showed Ciudad-Mujer-Ciudad and Somos mujeres at Salon 77-78: Nuevas tendencias at the Museo de Arte Moderno. Her work was shown at the Centre Georges Pompidou in 1979, at the Venice performance meeting the same year and at the Venice Biennial in 1984; she taught at the academies of Sint Joost in Breda and AKI in Enschede in the early 1980s, worked for the VPRO, and founded the television project Videocosmos on Canal 9 in 1983. In 1987 a retrospective of her work was shown at the Festival de Montbeliard in France, where she was named the most important video artist in Latin America, and she sat on the jury of the IV Festival Internacional de Cine, TV y Video in Rio de Janeiro. She made more than thirty-eight video works and many more unfinished ones. Her father Leopoldo, whom she called Polito, fell ill with cancer in 1988 and died in the spring of 1989; her last video, Inertia, is dated 1989; she took her own life on 6 May 1990, three days after her forty-third birthday. Her will left her work to TV UNAM, whose videoteca preserves it; her personal archive was rescued by her former student Edna Torres Ramos and is now the Fondo Pola Weiss at the Centro de Documentacion Arkheia of the Museo Universitario Arte Contemporaneo, one of its most visited holdings. She was almost forgotten in Mexico for twenty-five years. The return came late: La TV te ve at MUAC in 2014; five works in Radical Women at the Hammer Museum in 2017; Arteonica at the Museum of Latin American Art in 2024; the TV UNAM series La creacion de Pola in 2022; and Alejandra Arrieta's feature documentary Pola Weiss in 2023.

**Doorway:** From Grobet, whose book partner was Lara, back to the room where Lara and Mayer answered the 1978 invitation, to Weiss, who answered the same invitation with video. The bridge needs a correction first, because the brief that sent this study said Grobet answered the invitation too, and she did not. Aceves Sepulveda's history of the show names the five invited women as Lara, Mayer, Weiss, Silvia Naranjo and Jesusa Rodriguez, of fifty-nine artists in all, and Grobet's own study never places her there. So the doorway is not three women in one room. It is two women in the room and a third who arrives through the book: Grobet made Se escoge el tiempo with Lara in 1983, five years after Lara hung her Ventanas beside Mayer's Tendedero and Weiss's video in the Museo de Arte Moderno. Mayer hung about eight hundred pink cards on a clothesline frame and asked what women most hated about the city; Lara hung ten small dark-framed collages that looked inward at the room instead of out at the street; Weiss showed two video-performances, Ciudad Mujer Ciudad and Somos mujeres, in which a naked woman danced over the city and indigenous women walked its streets. Cards, collages, video: three answers to the same invitation, and the video is the one that moves. Where Mayer's line grows sideways because strangers keep pinning cards to it, and Lara's line grows in a spiral because she keeps returning to the same house and finding a different sentence there, Weiss's line grows because the camera keeps moving, strapped to her body, and the image keeps feeding back onto the screen in front of the audience. Mayer handed the sentence to strangers; Lara kept it for herself; Weiss handed the camera to her own body and let the body do the talking.

## M1. The camera as a second body

The camera is not held; it is worn. Weiss called her portable video camera her "escuincla", her kid, her little girl, a daughter with her own identity and point of view, and she danced with it strapped to her shoulder, on her hip, in her hands, while she moved. Her own account, quoted by Garbayo-Maeztu, is the mechanism stated as a sentence: "Descubri que el tripie no me dejaba moverme, descubri entonces que tenia un magnifico tripie, digo, bipie, que son mis piernas" (I discovered the tripod would not let me move, so I discovered I had a magnificent tripod, I mean, a bipod, which are my legs). The tripod is rejected because it fixes the camera to one point and makes the body serve the machine; the legs replace it, so the camera moves when the body moves and the image shakes, sways, climbs and falls with the dance. The photograph viewed first-hand of Weiss in profile with the big white-and-black portable camera on a tripod shows the machine she refused: a bulky box with a viewfinder hood, a thing that wants a stand. The photographs of La Venusina viewed first-hand show the refusal in practice: Weiss in a white tunic and white boots, the camera at her waist and then on her shoulder, walking toward the lens while a crowd watches, the camera cable trailing behind her like an umbilical cord. Aceves Sepulveda's reading, quoted by Garbayo, carries the mechanism one step further: Weiss adopted the camera as a prosthesis to explore and widen the limits of bodily experience, and each video was a birth, an alumbramiento, a copulation with the escuincla that produced the work the way a body produces a child. The mechanism is the camera that stops being a tool and becomes a limb and then a child. A camera on a tripod records a dance; a camera on a shoulder dances, and the footage is the dance, not a record of it. **Takeaway:** the legs are the tripod, and the camera is the kid. Watch the shake: when the image moves because the body that carries it is dancing, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Grobet's camera (#347 M5) also made her body the medium, but her body tore out of a foil box and peeled photographs off itself while the camera stood still and watched; Weiss's camera moves with the body and is the body, which is why her version is a dance and Grobet's is a strip-tease.

## M2. The feedback loop: the TV that sees you

The monitor shows the performance while the performance is happening, so the audience watches itself being watched. The title of the MUAC retrospective says it: La TV te ve, the TV sees you. In La Venusina renace y reforma (1980), on the esplanade of the Auditorio Nacional, Weiss danced in front of mirrors with her camera on her shoulder while monitors projected the live feed, so the crowd saw her, saw what she saw, and saw themselves seeing her, all at once. Garbayo's description is exact: "Si Pola los mira, los rapta con la camara. La pantalla devuelve las imagenes de sus cuerpos. A Pola la ven, ven lo que ella ve y se ven viendola." The flyer for the performance, viewed first-hand, says the same thing in Tununa Mercado's words: Weiss is "simultaneamente creadora y creatura", creator and creature at once, and she captures the images and sounds around her "en su absoluta momentaneidad mientras danza", in their absolute immediacy while she dances. The mechanism is the loop closed in public. A normal television broadcast goes one way, from the studio to the living room; Weiss's performance goes both ways at once, from the camera to the monitor to the crowd and back into the camera, because the crowd is in the shot and the shot is on the screen in front of them. The 18-year-old spectator quoted by Garbayo felt it in her body before she could name it: "se me enchino el cuerpo... Me senti extensa" (my skin went goosebumpy... I felt extensive). The loop is also the early work: Flor Cosmica (1977) is built on video feedback, the camera pointed at its own monitor until the image blooms into a flower, which is the same mechanism in miniature, the signal eating its own tail until it makes a shape. **Takeaway:** the screen shows the room, and the room is in the screen. Watch the monitor: when the audience appears on the screen it is watching, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's Tendedero (#344 M1) also made the audience part of the work, but her audience wrote on cards and pinned them, so the participation was slow, written and kept; Weiss's audience is captured live and returned instantly, which is why her version is a loop and Mayer's is a census.

## M3. Chroma key and psychedelic color

The video signal is bent until it makes colors television was never meant to show. Weiss worked the formal qualities of the medium the way a painter works pigment: chroma key, color saturation, solarization, feedback, loops and visual repetition. The frames viewed first-hand are the proof. The Ciudad Mujer Ciudad frame of the crucified woman (idis-pola-01) is a solarized field of orange, green and blue blobs with heavy scan lines, the woman's body outlined in white against colors that do not exist in the street she was filmed on. The second Ciudad frame (gch2) is green at the top, blue at the bottom, the city grey in the middle, the body flesh-toned between them. The Toti Amiga thumbnail is washed entirely green, a face barely legible in the tint. The Inertia thumbnail is a cityscape drowned in magenta and purple light flares. The arTVing thumbnail is a face in pink and magenta behind a purple cloud-shaped title card. The mechanism is color as argument, not decoration. Commercial television in Mexico in the 1970s wanted clean, naturalistic color that disappeared into the program; Weiss pushed the saturation until the color became the content, because the color is what the machine does when you stop telling it to behave like a window. Flor Cosmica opens in black and white and then blooms into yellow, violet and pink lights that form a flower, the tonalities turning to yellow, red and blue, with what the English Wikipedia calls "feminine, organic and notoriously sexual connotations". The kaleidoscope is the flower: feedback makes the shape, color makes it mean something. **Takeaway:** bend the signal until the color is the content. Watch the saturation: when a street, a face or a body is drowned in one impossible color that the machine made, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Grobet's painted cactus (#347 M6) also put one impossible flat color into a real landscape, but her color was house paint on a plant, applied by hand and then photographed; Weiss's color is the video signal itself misbehaving, which is why her version is electronic and Grobet's is a prank.

## M4. The body that speaks

The female body talks without words, by overflowing. Garbayo's whole article is built on this mechanism: Weiss puts a body on screen that says more than it means to say, transcribing what is inside directly onto its outside surface, skipping the checkpoint of the logos, the Name of the Father. The voice-over in Ciudad Mujer Ciudad, quoted by Garbayo, is the mechanism spoken aloud: "No hay agua, tengo seca la boca... pero contengo agua, porque lloro, cuando rio, cuando adolezco, cuando sudo. Y me sale agua, de por dentro, desde mi cuerpo. Han bloqueado todos los rios, teniamos tanta agua que sobraba. No! No hay agua!!" The body holds water and the city does not; the body leaks, cries, sweats, bleeds, and the leaking is the speech. The frame viewed first-hand (gch2) shows the mechanism as an image: the female sex in the foreground, huge, and the city framed between the thighs, small, so that the body is the frame and the city is what is seen through it. Garbayo notes the sex is set against the Torre Latinoamericana as the phallic emblem of modernity. Mi Co-Ra-Zon (1986) returns to the fluid six years later: the blood of an abortion, her own, broths from the vagina while the earthquake-ruined city plays behind it, a purple drop falling on a white flower, the virginity wrapped in a bodily flow. The mechanism is the excess that cannot be contained. A body that fits the city would be a body that behaves; Weiss's body is excessive, it does not fit, it spills, and the spilling is the argument that the city is the thing in lack, not the body. Mayer saw it at once, in the quote both ROZENSTRAAT and Garbayo carry: the protagonist was "una mujer real, con cicatrices y celulitis", a real woman with scars and cellulite, "Era el cuerpo femenino visto por una mujer", the female body seen by a woman, the first time Mayer had seen an unidealized female body in Mexican art. **Takeaway:** the body talks by leaking, and the city is the thing that is dry. Watch the fluid: when water, tears, sweat or blood leaves a body on screen and the city behind it stays grey and thirsty, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Lara's drawings (#345) also let the body speak without permission, but her body speaks in handwriting inside the drawing, a sentence the image cannot get rid of; Weiss's body speaks in fluids the camera cannot edit out, which is why her version is a spill and Lara's is a sentence.

## M5. City-woman-city

The city and the woman are the same body, twice. Ciudad-Mujer-Ciudad (1978) is the mechanism's home and its title is its formula: city, woman, city, the woman sandwiched between two cities, or the city sandwiched between two women. The video is eighteen minutes of chroma key, color saturation and psychedelic effects in which the naked body of the actress Vivian Blackmore dances over panoramic views of mountains, skies, rivers and the urban areas of Mexico City, dissolving as shots of the city, its people and its pollution appear, to a voice-over by Weiss herself, with birdsong and laughter behind. The woman drives an imaginary car, dances into the "suffocating" space with her pubis, and at the end presents herself crucified as a martyr, her tears the healing fluid the city lacks. The mechanism is the metonymy made literal by the keying: the woman's body is superimposed on the city until the two cannot be separated, the exploited, polluted, dry, restless city and the fertile, naked, female body sharing one frame. Somos mujeres (1978) is the same mechanism from the street: indigenous women in the busy streets of Mexico City, saturated colors, the camera zooming at full speed without a tripod, a woman throwing things at the camera, a text in dialect left untranslated. The mechanism is the city as a body that has been cut. Weiss's reading, carried by the sources, is that the rivers were tubed, the water was cut off, and the only way the city can make water now is by crying, which is why the woman cries, laughs, sweats and bleeds on screen: she is the city's missing water, walking around in a body. **Takeaway:** the woman is the city's water, and the city is the woman's cage. Watch the superimposition: when a naked body and a city share one frame and neither can be peeled off the other, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Jimenez's march photographs (#346) also put women's bodies in the city, but her camera stands still and the march passes it, so the city is a background the women walk through; Weiss's city is keyed into the body itself, which is why her version is a superimposition and Jimenez's is a vigil.

## M6. The eye is the heart

Seeing is feeling, and the eye is the organ that beats. Mi Co-Ra-Zon (1986), her masterwork by consensus, opens on Weiss's mouth spelling out the title syllable by syllable, "mi co-ra-zon", and then she says "mi ojo es mi corazon", my eye is my heart. The STUNAM shot-by-shot analysis, read end to end, follows the exchange: a blue point moves across a black field and grows, recalling the Flor Cosmica of 1977, until it becomes a sequined heart vibrating to the music; other hearts appear, pierced by red shafts of light; a cut to Weiss's eye leads to a female pubis, a thread of blood running down the crotch, a purple drop falling on a white flower, purity against impurity; the eye watches itself, the body watches itself, and the watching is the feeling. The mechanism is the eye traded for the heart. A camera is an eye; a heart is a feeling; Weiss's claim is that they are the same organ doing the same work, which is why the video is built out of eyes and hearts and nothing else for long stretches: the electrocardiogram flashing, the crystalline tears, the red beating heart behind her body as she collapses in a V, face down, suggesting the death of her child. The YouTube thumbnail viewed first-hand shows the gold heart shape in a dark purple field, and the unidentified compilation's thumbnail shows the other half of the exchange, an extreme close-up of an eye filling the frame with small red hearts floating at its edge. The mechanism is also the title spelled out on the mouth: the word is broken into syllables and spoken by the lips, so the title is not read but mouthed, and the mouth is where the eye and the heart meet. **Takeaway:** the eye beats, and the heart sees. Watch the exchange: when an eye appears where a heart should be, or a heart where an eye should be, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Grobet's Hora y media (#347 M4) also made light do the work of an organ, but her light destroyed the photograph it made; Weiss's eye-heart makes the image and feels it at the same time, which is why her version is a pulse and Grobet's is a fade.

## M7. The video self-portrait

The autobiography is the material, and the letter to the father is the script. Autovideato (1979), the title means video self-portrait, is a short visual essay in which Weiss writes to her father, confides that she has found her calling in video art, acknowledges his hesitation and disapproval, and tells him she has found a new way of perceiving the world through video. The Pijamasurf essay takes its title from the letter: "Video-tro modo la vida", video, another way of life, her apology for not being, as he wanted, "an intellectual who deceives by speaking". The mechanism is the self-portrait that argues. A self-portrait usually shows a face; Weiss's shows a life being justified to the one person whose approval she wants and cannot have, and the justification is the work itself, which is why the video mixes a nude dancing body (mocking the censors, who held that a nude female body was pornography if it moved and eroticism if it was still) with chroma-keyed images of herself layered over herself. The orange self-portrait viewed first-hand, Weiss holding a Canon camera above her head with both hands and staring straight out, is the mechanism in a still: the camera replaces the face, or rather the camera is held up like a crown over the face, and the face looks at the viewer through the machine it holds. Almost every video after 1979 has her in it, because from Papalotl and Videodanza onward she discovered herself as a fundamental part of every production, and few of her works do not carry her physical presence or narrate a moment of her life. The mechanism is the life used as footage. A diary is written in private; Weiss's diary is recorded on tape, edited, and broadcast, so the private becomes public without ceasing to be private, which is why the work feels confessional and exhibitionist at once. **Takeaway:** the life is the footage, and the father is the first viewer. Watch the address: when a video talks to one named person about the artist's own life, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Lara's notebooks (#345 M3) were also a diary, but her diary is drawn in private and the sentence is written inside the image; Weiss's diary is performed for a camera and a monitor, which is why her version is a broadcast and Lara's is a book.

## M8. The Venusina

The artist is an alien, from Venus, and the outsider is the role. La venusina renace y reforma (1980), the Venusian woman reborn and reformed, was the first video performance in Mexico, and the mechanism is the alien as a way of being seen. Weiss wore white tunic and white boots, danced in front of mirrors at the Auditorio Nacional, then moved the performance outside to the esplanade because the indoor presentation was cancelled, so the alien landed on the pavement in the open air. The name was not a costume but a job description: she was a Venusian, a woman from Venus, who was going to live among men, and she had discovered in dance the essence of the female gender. The photographs viewed first-hand show the alien in the world: white clothes against the concrete of the Auditorio Nacional, a building under construction behind her, a crowd of spectators watching her walk toward them, the camera at her waist and then on her shoulder, the cable trailing behind her. The mechanism is the outsider made visible by a different uniform. An artist who calls herself a Venusian is not asking to be accepted; she is announcing that she comes from somewhere else and will not pretend otherwise, which is why the performance works in public, in the street, where the audience cannot mistake her for one of them. The flyer viewed first-hand, red and orange duotone with Tununa Mercado's text, carries the mechanism in its own wording: Weiss is "objeto y sujeto al mismo tiempo de un performance", object and subject at the same time of a performance, which is the Venusian's position exactly, both the thing looked at and the one doing the looking. **Takeaway:** call yourself an alien, and dress like one, and the street has to look. Watch the white: when a woman in white walks through a grey public space carrying a camera, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Grobet's cactus painted green (#347 M6) was also an outsider dropped into a landscape, but her outsider was a plant, silent and stuck in place; Weiss's outsider dances, which is why her version is a landing and Grobet's is a planting.

## M9. The wordplay name

The name is the work, and the pun is the signature. Weiss renamed everything she touched, starting with herself and her medium: she was a "teleasta", from "cineasta" (filmmaker), because a cineasta makes cinema and a teleasta makes television, and she made television art, arTV, which is also the word "art" with TV in it, arTV, a pun that turns the medium into the signature and the signature into the medium. The wordplay went on through her whole career, the nicknames she gave herself and her work: POLArizado, POLAca, POLAina, POLAr, POLAnco, POLAque, each one a word with her name POLA inside it, polarized, Polish, legging, polar, Polanco (the Mexico City neighborhood), a POLAca, a Polish woman, which is also the name of her Polish heritage through her father. The mechanism is renaming as self-invention. A name that is given to you is a label; a name you build out of puns is a work, which is why Weiss's wordplay is not a joke on the side of the videos but the same move as the videos: take the given thing, television, a name, a city, and bend it until it says your name. The production company was arTV, the television project was Videocosmos, the self-portrait was Autovideato, the eclipse video was Extrapolacion, the performance was La Venusina, and every title is a pun or a compound that does the same thing, squeezing two words into one until the new word means the work. **Takeaway:** put your name inside the word, and the word becomes yours. Watch the pun: when a title is a compound that could only have been made by its maker, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's Tendedero (#344) also renamed a thing, a clothesline into an exhibition, but her renaming was one word moved into a new place; Weiss's renaming is a word rebuilt from the inside, which is why her version is a pun and Mayer's is a relocation.

## M10. The loop and repetition

The same thing happens again, and the second time it means something else. Weiss built her videos out of loops and visual repetition, the same image returning until it stops being an image and starts being a structure. Flor Cosmica (1977) is the mechanism at its purest: the camera pointed at its own monitor, the signal feeding back into itself, the image blooming into a flower that opens and closes and opens again, black and white turning into yellow, violet and pink lights, the tonalities shifting to yellow, red and blue. The loop is the flower: feedback makes the shape, repetition makes it last, and the video is thirteen or fifteen minutes of the same flower coming back, which is why the length is disputed in the sources and why it does not matter, because a loop has no proper length. Videodanza viva videodanza (1979) is the loop as dance: the body repeats a gesture, the camera repeats the shot, the video repeats the sequence, and the repetition is the dance, not the decoration of it. The mechanism is the return that changes the thing returned. A loop in a video is not a failure to move on; it is a decision to stay with one image until it shows everything it has, which is why Weiss's loops feel meditative rather than mechanical, the way a dancer repeats a step to find what is inside it. **Takeaway:** repeat the image until it stops being an image and starts being a structure. Watch the return: when the same flower, the same gesture or the same face comes back and the second time it means something the first time did not say, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Grobet's Hora y media (#347 M4) also repeated a shape, a light triangle, but her repetition was a slow destruction, the image fading as it was made; Weiss's repetition is a return, the image coming back stronger, which is why her version is a loop and Grobet's is a fade.

## M11. The image that looks back

The image is not a copy; it is a presence that returns the gaze. The sources carry this mechanism under the Nahuatl word ixiptlah, the image as the presence of a god, a vessel in which the divine is made present, which is how the pre-Hispanic image was understood, and how Weiss understood her own: the video is not a record of a body but a body, and it looks back at the viewer the way a god looks back from an image. Garbayo's reading of the Ciudad Mujer Ciudad frame viewed first-hand is the mechanism as an image: the woman's sex in the foreground, huge, and the city framed between her thighs, small, so that the body is not looked at but looks, and the viewer is the one being seen through the frame. The mechanism is the gaze reversed. A photograph shows a thing; a Weiss video shows a thing that sees you, which is why the work feels confrontational rather than illustrative, and why the censors were right to be afraid of it: a nude body that looks back cannot be consumed the way a nude body that is looked at can be. The La Venusina performance is the mechanism in public: Weiss dances in front of mirrors, the mirrors show her, the monitors show her, the crowd sees itself being seen, and the loop closes, because the image has looked back and the audience has felt it. **Takeaway:** make the image look back, and the viewer becomes the one being watched. Watch the gaze: when a body on screen seems to see you through the screen, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Lara's drawings (#345) also made the image look back, but her image looks back in silence, a face drawn and left on the wall; Weiss's image looks back in motion, with a camera on its shoulder, which is why her version is a return of the gaze and Lara's is a stare.

## M12. The idiot box into the magic box

Television is not a box for idiots; it is a box for magic, if you know how to use it. Weiss's motto, carried by every source, was to transform the "idiot box" into a "magic box", and her whole practice was the proof: she took the television set, the thing that sat in the living room and talked down to the family, and used it to make art, to teach, to broadcast dance, to show a naked woman dancing over a city, to tell a father that video was another way of life. The mechanism is the democratization of the medium. A television set is cheap, it is everywhere, it is in every house in Mexico, and it does not need a museum to work; Weiss's claim was that the video artist does not need the museum either, because the television is the museum, the gallery, the cinema and the classroom, all at once, and the work can be broadcast to anyone with a set. She put the theory into practice: her videos were shown on Canal 9 in the Videocosmos project in 1983, a space of her own on television, where she showed her own work and the work of others, and she taught video at UNAM, at Sint Joost in Breda, at AKI in Enschede, and on the VPRO in Holland, spreading the practice the way a broadcaster spreads a signal. The mechanism is the exhibition space moved into the home. A painting needs a wall; a video needs a set, and the set is already there, which is why Weiss's work could reach people who never went to a museum, and why her oblivion was a political fact rather than an aesthetic one: the museum system could forget her because she did not need it, and the television system could forget her because she used it against itself. **Takeaway:** the set is the gallery, and the broadcast is the opening. Watch the channel: when a work is made to be broadcast rather than hung, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Jimenez's archive (#346 M4) was also a public act, but her public was the march, the street, the crowd that showed up; Weiss's public was anyone with a television set, which is why her version is a broadcast and Jimenez's is a gathering.

## M13. Teaching as mobilization

The class is a movement, and the lesson is to make the work yourself. Weiss taught at UNAM for her whole professional life, and the mechanism is the teaching that makes more artists. In her first year at the Taller Experimental de Video, in 1977, she gave her students the task of collecting the garbage in the studio and using it as the material for a video, because the garbage was the world and the world was the material, and the lesson was that you do not need money or permission to make a video, you need a camera and a thing to point it at. The mechanism is the pedagogy that removes the excuse. A teacher who tells you to use the garbage is not teaching you a technique; she is teaching you that the technique is not the point, the point is the making, which is why her students, Edna Torres Ramos among them, carried her archive and her practice forward after her death. She taught in Mexico, in the Netherlands, on the VPRO, and in the summer courses in Morelia and Cuernavaca, and she founded the Taller Experimental de Video at UNAM and the Videocosmos project on television, both of which were classrooms as much as they were exhibitions, places where people learned to make video by making it. The mechanism is the movement made by teaching. A single artist can be forgotten; a teacher makes a school, and a school makes a movement, which is why Weiss's influence is larger than her filmography, and why the archive she left behind is not a pile of tapes but a room full of students who know how to use a camera. **Takeaway:** teach the making, and the movement makes itself. Watch the classroom: when a student picks up the garbage and makes a video out of it, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Grobet's teaching (#347) was a workshop too, but her workshop was a darkroom, a place where the image was made slowly and carefully; Weiss's workshop was a street, a studio, a television set, which is why her version is a mobilization and Grobet's is a craft.

## M14. The archive that outlives the artist

The work survives because someone saved it, and the saving is the work's second life. Weiss's will left her videos to TV UNAM, whose videoteca preserves them, and her personal archive, the papers, the tapes, the photographs, the flyers, was rescued by her former student Edna Torres Ramos, who cataloged it and made it the Fondo Pola Weiss at the Centro de Documentacion Arkheia of the Museo Universitario Arte Contemporaneo, one of its most visited holdings. The mechanism is the archive as a second act. A video artist who dies at forty-three, whose work was shown on television and in festivals but rarely in museums, whose name was dropped from a Jumex exhibition on the women of Mexico because she was not in the collection, is easy to forget, and Weiss was forgotten, for twenty-five years, until the archive made her unforgetable. The MUAC retrospective La TV te ve in 2014, the five works in Radical Women at the Hammer in 2017, the Arteonica show at MOLAA in 2024, the TV UNAM series La creacion de Pola in 2022, and the Arrieta documentary in 2023, are all second acts made possible by the archive, because the archive is the thing that survives when the artist does not. The mechanism is the rescue that makes the return possible. A work that is not saved is a work that never happened; Weiss's work happened because Torres Ramos saved it, and the saving is the reason this study exists. **Takeaway:** the archive is the second life, and the student is the archivist. Watch the shelf: when a tape survives because someone kept it, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Jimenez's archive (#346) was also rescued, by her daughter and by the Centro de la Imagen, but her rescue was a family act, a daughter saving a mother's photographs; Weiss's rescue was a student act, a pupil saving a teacher's tapes, which is why her version is a school and Jimenez's is a family.

## Evolution

1947-1975, the child who went to the cinema. Born in Mexico City on Saturday 3 May 1947, raised in Colonia Roma Sur, taken to the cinema three or four times a week by her grandmother Maria. Classical ballet as a child, modern and contemporary dance later, English literature, piano. At UNAM she first aimed at political science, then took cinematography at the Centro Universitario de Estudios Cinematograficos, and graduated in communication and journalism in 1975 with the first video thesis in the university's history. As a student she worked at Televisa and Canal 13. No work from before 1977 survives in the catalog, and the gap matters: the first thing we know about her work is the first video, not a student exercise.

1975-1977, the trip and the first tape. In 1975 she traveled in Europe studying artistic, experimental and educational television at the BBC, the RAI, the VPRO and the ORTF. In 1976, in New York, she met Nam June Paik and Shigeko Kubota, and Paik recognized her as the pioneer of video art in Mexico at the IX Encuentro Internacional del Videoarte in 1977. She founded arTV in 1977 (the year is disputed, 1977, 1978 and 1972 are all given; 1977 is used here) and the Taller Experimental de Video at UNAM the same year, and made Flor Cosmica, a video feedback flower, her first tape. The mechanism that appears first is the loop, M10, and the body-camera, M1, which is already there in the first work, because the flower is made by the camera looking at its own monitor.

1978-1980, the salon and the world. Salon 77-78 at the Museo de Arte Moderno, spring 1978, fifty-nine artists, five women, Weiss showing Ciudad-Mujer-Ciudad and Somos mujeres, two video-performances that put a naked woman over the city and indigenous women in its streets. The work was censored for commercial television for its frontal female nudity, which is the first proof that the body that speaks, M4, was heard. In 1979 the Centre Georges Pompidou in Paris showed eight or nine of her works, and she performed Videodanza viva videodanza at the Palazzo Grassi in Venice. In 1980 she performed La venusina renace y reforma at the Auditorio Nacional, the first video performance in Mexico, moving it outside to the esplanade when the indoor presentation was cancelled, and the Cahiers du Cinema reviewed her work. The mechanism that appears now is the city-woman-city, M5, and the Venusina, M8, the alien who dances in public.

1981-1983, the Netherlands and the television. She taught at Sint Joost in Breda and AKI in Enschede, worked for the VPRO, and in 1983 founded Videocosmos on Canal 9, a space of her own on television, and made Toti amiga, arTVing, La Carrera, David I and the rest of the 1983 list, a burst of short works that show the loop, M10, and the wordplay, M9, at their most playful. The mechanism that appears now is the idiot box into the magic box, M12, the broadcast as exhibition space.

1984-1987, the masterwork and the retrospective. Videorigen de Weiss in 1984, twenty-one minutes, a self-portrait in video; Videodanza at the Venice Biennial the same year; Mi Co-Ra-Zon in 1986, ten minutes, the eye is the heart, M6, the masterwork, made from the 1985 earthquake and a miscarriage; Merlin in 1987, eight minutes; a retrospective at the Festival de Montbeliard in 1987, where she was named the most important video artist in Latin America, and a jury seat at the IV Festival Internacional de Cine, TV y Video in Rio de Janeiro. The mechanism that appears now is the eye-heart, M6, and the video self-portrait, M7, at their most intense, because the life has become the footage and the footage has become the life.

1988-1990, the last tape and the death. Her father Leopoldo fell ill with cancer in 1988 and died in the spring of 1989; her partner since 1982, Fernando Mangino, was with her to the end. Inertia, eight minutes, is dated 1989, her last video. She took her own life on Sunday 6 May 1990, three days after her forty-third birthday. The circumstances are contested and the on-camera version is a rumor, not a fact; see the study-method note. The mechanism that appears now is the archive, M14, because the work has to survive her, and it does, because she left it to TV UNAM and her student saved the rest.

1990-2026, the oblivion and the return. Almost forgotten in Mexico for twenty-five years, dropped from the Jumex exhibition on the women of Mexico in 2019 because she was not in the collection, and then the return: the TV UNAM retrospective in 1998, the MUAC exhibition La TV te ve in 2014, five works in Radical Women in 2017, Arteonica at MOLAA in 2024, the TV UNAM series La creacion de Pola in 2022, the Arrieta documentary in 2023, and this study in 2026. The mechanism that appears now is the archive, M14, again, because the return was made by the people who kept the tapes.

## Aesthetic logic

Weiss's aesthetic is the body that dances with a machine until the machine dances too. The logic is not decoration but necessity: the camera is heavy, the tripod is fixed, the television is a box, the city is dry, the body is wet, and the work is what happens when the body refuses to behave the way the machine wants it to. The color is the signal misbehaving, the loop is the signal eating itself, the feedback is the room seeing itself, the fluid is the body leaking, the wordplay is the name bending, the alien is the artist refusing to belong. Every mechanism in this study is the same move in a different medium: take the given thing, the camera, the television, the city, the name, the body, and bend it until it says your name. The work is not beautiful in the way a painting is beautiful; it is beautiful in the way a dance is beautiful, because it moves, because it repeats, because it looks back. The pieces that last are the ones where the mechanism is on: the flower that blooms from feedback, the woman who dances over the city, the eye that is a heart, the alien who walks through a crowd with a camera on her shoulder. The pieces that do not last are the ones where the mechanism is off, where the video is a record of a dance rather than a dance, where the color is decoration rather than argument, where the body is shown rather than heard.

## Network

The network that is evidenced, not assumed. Monica Mayer: the strongest link, same 1978 salon, the two of them the only two women in the show whose work is still talked about together; Mayer met Weiss at the salon, saw Ciudad Mujer Ciudad there, and quoted it in her own work and in her history of the period; Mayer's Tendedero and Weiss's video are the two answers to the same invitation that keep being paired, and Mayer testifies in La creacion de Pola, chapters two and three. Magali Lara: same 1978 salon, both in Radical Women, both in the Grobet orbit, but no direct collaboration found; the link is the room, not a work. Shigeko Kubota: met in New York in 1976, close friends, the model for Weiss's feminizing of video, the shared approach to the medium as a body; the link is personal and artistic, and it is evidenced in the sources that name Kubota as the friend who changed her life. Nam June Paik: met in New York in 1976, recognized her as the pioneer of video art in Mexico at the 1977 IX Encuentro; the link is the recognition, and it is evidenced. Mathias Goeritz and Sebastian: the organizers of Salon 77-78, the men who invited the five women; the link is the invitation. Silvia Naranjo and Jesusa Rodriguez: the other two invited women, the rest of the five; the link is the room. Miguel Ehrenberg: the Mexican co-representative with Weiss at the 1977 IX Encuentro, his piece has no surviving record; the link is thin but evidenced. Edna Torres Ramos: her former student, the rescuer of the archive, the author of the 2010 STUNAM essay, the cataloger of the Fondo Pola Weiss; the link is the rescue. Fernando Mangino: her partner from the end of 1982 to her death, met around 1972 on the university television program "El debate ante la imagen"; the link is personal. Kitzia Weiss: her younger sister, who composed music for some of her videos; the link is family. Tununa Mercado: wrote the flyer text for La Venusina; the link is the text. Jorge Glusberg: organized the 1977 IX Encuentro; the link is the show. Dante Hernandez Miranda: wrote the 2000 monograph; the link is the book. Aline Hernandez and Benjamin Murphy: curated La TV te ve at MUAC in 2014; the link is the retrospective. Gabriela Aceves Sepulveda: wrote the 2017 chapter and the 2019 book that place Weiss in the history of Mexican feminist art; the link is the history. Alejandra Arrieta: made the 2022 series and the 2023 documentary; the link is the return. Gustavo Sainz: an early supporter; the link is the encouragement. Froylan Lopez Narvaez: reviewed her work in 1977; the link is the review. Federico Luna: composed music for her videos; the link is the sound.

The links that were suggested and could not be evidenced, and are recorded here so they are not repeated: Kati Horna, no evidence, Weiss studied at the Facultad de Ciencias Politicas y Sociales, not at the Escuela Nacional de Artes Plasticas where Horna taught, and no source places them together; Felipe Ehrenberg, no direct evidence, only his brother Miguel is evidenced as a co-exhibitor in 1977, and Felipe was in London exile for much of the period; Marcos Kurtycz, no evidence at all in any source read. These three are dropped as evidenced links. The Grobet link runs through Lara, not through the salon: Grobet made Se escoge el tiempo with Lara in 1983, five years after Lara and Weiss were in the same room.

## Study-method note

This study was built from 16 sources read end to end and 6 partial reads, 45 works cataloged from 1977 to 1989, and 15 distinct images viewed first-hand, listed in the Depth line. No full video was watched; the Vimeo copy of Ciudad Mujer Ciudad cited by Garbayo is dead, and the YouTube thumbnails viewed are thumbnails, not footage, so every video description in this study rests on the sources' frame descriptions and on stills, not on viewed footage. The weekdays were verified with date -d in the America/New_York timezone: born Saturday 3 May 1947, died Sunday 6 May 1990, aged 43 years and 3 days; the 19 September 1985 earthquake was a Thursday. Load-bearing corrections, 24 in all: (1) the doorway correction, Grobet was not in Salon 77-78, the five invited women were Lara, Mayer, Weiss, Naranjo and Rodriguez; (2) arTV founded 1977, 1978 and 1972 all given, 1977 used; (3) Flor Cosmica length, about 5 minutes against 15 minutes, unresolved; (4) Ciudad-Mujer-Ciudad dated 1978 against 1979 in Garbayo captions and TV UNAM chapter three, 1978 used; (5) Mi Corazon dated 1986 against 1985 in a blog and an art listing, 1986 used; (6) Mi Corazon title in three forms, Mi Corazon, Mi Co-Ra-Zon, Mi ojo es mi corazon, all carried; (7) Videodanza title in three forms, Videodanza viva videodanza, Videodanza vida videodanza, Videodanza viva video danza, variants carried; (8) Pompidou 1979, eight or nine works, against 1982 in one source, 1979 used; (9) Venice 1979 and 1984 against "1980 Venice Biennale" in one listing, the 1979 event was the Palazzo Grassi performance meeting, not the Biennale proper; (13) death on camera, unverified and contested, "believed in front of her camera" against "never been proven" against "her camera recorded each death rattle" against an explicit denial, suicide on 6 May 1990 carried as fact, the on-camera version carried as rumor, and one source says firearm;
