## M1. The milkman from Ohio

Webb Miller of the United Press taught him, in New York in 1934-1935, to "always write as if writing to a milkman from Ohio", and Montanelli carried the lesson for life: every article must be readable and understandable by anyone, even a milkman from Ohio. The milkman is the mechanism, because a history written for the milkman cannot hide behind academic apparatus, and the plain sentence is the piece, and the piece must show a dense scholarly paragraph thinning into one clear sentence that a milkman could follow, and the sentence staying clear.

**Takeaway:** write for the milkman, and the piece should show the thinning, not the thickening.

## M2. History of men, not of ideologies

The Storia d'Italia is, in the Italian article's words, "Storia di uomini piu che di ideologie o fatti economico-sociali": history of men rather than of ideologies or economic and social facts, with the portraits of the characters standing out and frequent recourse to anecdote. The man is the mechanism, because a century told through its faces is watchable where a century told through its structures is not, and the faces are the piece, and the piece must show a crowd of centuries resolving into single faces, each face carrying its century.

**Takeaway:** give the century a face, and the piece should show the resolving, not the crowd.

## M3. The actualising parallel

Montanelli updated Roman history by comparing Saint Ambrose to Henry Ford or to John Davison Rockefeller, and by calling the Great Wall one of the Maginot lines of all time; the Italian article calls this the "interpretazione attualizzante", the actualising interpretation of the facts of the past. The parallel is the mechanism, because a past event laid flat against a present one makes both visible at once, and the laying-flat is the piece, and the piece must show an ancient scene sliding over a modern one until the two line up, and the line-up doing the explaining.

**Takeaway:** lay the past flat on the present, and the piece should show the line-up.

## M4. Chiaroscuro portraiture

In L'Italia dei secoli d'oro the great figures are presented "utilizzando la magistrale tecnica del chiaroscuro", the masterly chiaroscuro technique, stressing human faults often neglected in school manuals: Dante reproached for the anachronism of his imperial faith, Petrarca admired for a worldly disenchantment that shelters him from politics, Boccaccio unable to monetise his diplomatic posts for a comfortable old age. The chiaroscuro is the mechanism, because a hero shown half in light and half in shadow is a person, and a hero shown all in light is a statue, and the half-light is the piece, and the piece must show the light moving across the face until the fault appears, and the fault staying visible.

**Takeaway:** light only half the face, and the piece should show the fault appearing.

## M5. The one-sentence verdict

After Santander he wrote that it had been "una lunga passeggiata militare con un solo nemico: il caldo", a long military walk with only one enemy, the heat; at his trial for the article he replied, "Show me a single casualty of that battle, because a battle without casualties is not a real battle!" The verdict is the mechanism, because one sentence that names the whole battle replaces the communique, and the replacement is the piece, and the piece must show a long official dispatch collapsing into one sentence, and the sentence standing alone.

**Takeaway:** collapse the dispatch into one sentence, and the piece should show the collapse.

## M6. The eyewitness anchor

"I was there" is the spine of the reportage: the letters from Abyssinia, the Winter War from Helsinki and the front, the only Western journalist with Martha Gellhorn in Helsinki on 30 November 1939, the Greek campaign, the Hungarian rising of 1956. The anchor is the mechanism, because a claim tied to a place and a date the writer stood in cannot be dismissed as opinion, and the standing-there is the piece, and the piece must show a dateline stamping itself onto the scene, and the stamp holding.

**Takeaway:** stamp the dateline on the scene, and the piece should show the stamp holding.

## M7. The letter to the father

He wrote about the Abyssinian war in letters to his father Sestilio, who, without Montanelli's knowledge, sent them to Ugo Ojetti, who published them regularly in the Corriere della Sera. The forwarded letter is the mechanism, because a private letter that becomes public reportage without the writer knowing it turns intimacy into journalism, and the forwarding is the piece, and the piece must show a sealed envelope opening itself into a newspaper column, and the handwriting becoming print.

**Takeaway:** let the private letter become the column, and the piece should show the opening.

## M8. The serialised history

Storia di Roma was published first in instalments in La Domenica del Corriere from 1954, on Dino Buzzati's suggestion, and only then collected in a volume in 1957; the Storia dei Greci and then the whole Storia d'Italia followed the same road from the weekly page to the book. The instalment is the mechanism, because a history delivered one week at a time makes the reader wait for the next century, and the waiting is the piece, and the piece must show a book unbinding itself into weekly pages, and the pages arriving one by one.

**Takeaway:** unbind the book into weeks, and the piece should show the arriving, not the binding.

## M9. The readers' room

La Stanza di Montanelli began as "Montanelli pensa cosi" in La Domenica del Corriere, a full page Buzzati gave him, and became the place where the journalist answered readers on the hottest topics of the day; from 1995 to 2001 he answered a letter a day on the page in the Corriere della Sera, and the best letters and answers were collected in Le Stanze and Le nuove Stanze. The room is the mechanism, because a newspaper that answers one reader a day is a conversation, not a lecture, and the conversation is the piece, and the piece must show letters arriving at a door and answers coming back through it, and the door never closing.

**Takeaway:** open the room to one letter a day, and the piece should show the door staying open.

## M10. The false note in the chorus

La stecca nel coro, the false note in the chorus, is his own name for the stance: going against the current, and the daily column in Il Giornale was called Controcorrente, against the current, sarcastic comment on the facts and people of the day. The false note is the mechanism, because one voice singing off-key in a chorus makes the whole chorus audible, and the off-key note is the piece, and the piece must show a chorus singing in unison until one voice breaks away, and the break doing the work.

**Takeaway:** sing the false note, and the piece should show the break.

## M11. The demythologising reversal

The Santander dispatch, the Greek campaign he refused to glorify, the "mild colonialism" he defended for thirty years and then retracted: the pattern is to take a received heroic account and replace it with the unheroic fact, the single mule-kick casualty, the pummelling, the poison gas. The reversal is the mechanism, because a myth turned inside out shows its lining, and the turning is the piece, and the piece must show a heroic poster flipping over to reveal the casualty list on the back, and the flip happening in plain sight.

**Takeaway:** flip the poster to its back, and the piece should show the casualty list.

## M12. The hold-your-nose vote

On the eve of the 1976 elections he told his readers to vote for the Christian Democrats, "turarsi il naso", hold your nose, to keep the Communists from power; the American Mag memoir records the slogan and notes that the conservative New York Times columnist William Safire borrowed the line in 1992 for the American presidential race. The held nose is the mechanism, because a vote cast while holding your nose is a public act of distaste, not of love, and the distaste is the piece, and the piece must show a hand marking a ballot while the other hand holds the nose, and both hands visible at once.

**Takeaway:** show both hands, and the piece should show the distaste, not the love.

## M13. The newspaper poured into the history book

The last eleven volumes with Mario Cervi were built, in Cervi's own account, by pouring the two journalists' newspaper production into the historical essay: "L'attualita raccontata dai due giornalisti di lunga esperienza in presa diretta sul giornale fu cosi travasata nel saggio storico." The pouring is the mechanism, because today's front page, decanted into a history volume, becomes tomorrow's past while it is still wet, and the decanting is the piece, and the piece must show newsprint dissolving into book pages, and the ink still wet on both.

**Takeaway:** pour the front page into the book, and the piece should show the ink still wet.

## M14. The brand-name history factory

The collaboration with Gervaso and Cervi ran on the prevalence of the Montanelli "marchio", the brand: Gervaso did the research and prepared the draft, Montanelli did the definitive rewrite; Cervi, after Montanelli's death, said he wrote most of the later volumes himself, with Montanelli signing the prefaces and correcting the texts, drawing on Montanelli's newspaper production for the portraits. The factory is the mechanism, because one name on the cover hides two hands at the desk, and the two hands are the piece, and the piece must show a draft passing from one desk to another and coming back rewritten, and the cover name never changing.

**Takeaway:** show the two desks under one name, and the piece should show the passing.

## M15. The missing link

His stated conviction, repeated and rocky, was that Italian academic historiography lacked the connecting links between the Academy and the public, ignorant of Italian affairs remote and recent; he named only a few exceptions, Guglielmo Ferrero, Gioacchino Volpe, Roberto Ridolfi, Rosario Romeo, and set against the academy his love of Anglo-Saxon and French historiography. The missing link is the mechanism, because a bridge built where the academy left a gap is a public work, and the gap is the piece, and the piece must show two cliffs, the academy and the public, with a bridge being thrown across, and the bridge carrying the traffic.

**Takeaway:** throw the bridge across the gap, and the piece should show the traffic.

## M16. The missed train

In L'Italia della Controriforma he took the thesis of Max Weber and Werner Sombart and ran it backwards: "Ho accettato la tesi di due grandi maestri, Max Weber e Werner Sombart. Per loro la storia moderna comincia con la Riforma. Io percorro questa intuizione in senso inverso: non avendo noi italiani avuto la Riforma, abbiamo perso il treno della civilta moderna." The missed train is the mechanism, because a country that misses one train waits three centuries on the platform, and the waiting is the piece, and the piece must show a train leaving without Italy, and the platform filling with the centuries that follow.

**Takeaway:** show the train leaving, and the piece should show the waiting.

## M17. The closing image

He closes the Controriforma volume by evoking the burning of Giordano Bruno, because it "illumina di una luce piu pertinente lo squallido paesaggio italiano dell'Italia della Controriforma: un prete e un gendarme intenti ad arrostire un ribelle privo anche del conforto di una causa a cui intestare il proprio sacrificio", a priest and a gendarme roasting a rebel who lacks even the comfort of a cause to dedicate his sacrifice to. The closing image is the mechanism, because one burning body lights a whole squalid landscape, and the lighting is the piece, and the piece must show a dark landscape until one flame is struck, and the flame doing all the seeing.

**Takeaway:** strike one flame in the dark, and the piece should show the landscape by it.

## M18. The comic deflation of the Church

In the Secoli d'oro volume the corruption of the Church is handled in a comic key, above all the passage on the rudimentary "telephone" with which the future Boniface VIII makes the reigning pope, Celestine V, believe an angel sent by God orders him to renounce the throne. The telephone is the mechanism, because a sacred abdication staged as a prank call makes power look ridiculous, and the prank is the piece, and the piece must show a pope listening to a tube and hearing an angel, and the tube doing the talking.

**Takeaway:** let the tube do the talking, and the piece should show the pope listening.

## M19. The invented scoop

In August 1939, following 200 young fascists cycling from Venice to Berlin, he invented that the Italian cyclists had stopped in Austria to help the farmers harvest the wheat; all the rival papers reproached their own correspondents for having "missed" the story. The invention is the mechanism, because a made-up detail that every rival wishes it had filed exposes the whole trade's hunger for the scoop, and the exposure is the piece, and the piece must show a false dispatch racing ahead of the true ones, and the rivals chasing it.

**Takeaway:** let the false dispatch run ahead, and the piece should show the chase.

## M20. The refusal to write

In Greece and Albania in 1940-1941 he wrote almost nothing: a small reason was typhus, "a huge one because I refused to push as a glorious military campaign the quaking pummelling that we caught down there." The refusal is the mechanism, because a blank page where propaganda should be is itself a report, and the blank is the piece, and the piece must show a typewriter with no paper in it while a battle goes on outside, and the empty carriage doing the reporting.

**Takeaway:** leave the carriage empty, and the piece should show the blank doing the work.

## M21. The collection plate

From the command deck of Il Giornale he launched civic fundraising campaigns: Friuli earthquake 1976, three billion lire in a few weeks, entrusted to Egisto Corradi for the rebuilding of Vito d'Asio, Montenars and Sedilis; Irpinia 1980, more than five billion lire, handed to Castelnuovo di Conza in the presence of Sandro Pertini; Accademia della Crusca 1989, 700 million lire in three months; families of policemen killed in the years of lead, 1990, 2.5 billion lire from the legacy of Mario Borletti. The plate is the mechanism, because a newspaper that passes the plate turns readers into a public works department, and the passing is the piece, and the piece must show coins dropping into a plate until the plate becomes a village rebuilt, and the rebuilding visible.

**Takeaway:** pass the plate, and the piece should show the village rebuilt.

## M22. The candidate list

In the 1976 campaign he gave his readers a list of 40 DC candidates "non compromessi col malaffare", uncompromised by wrongdoing, to mark in the preference space; 33 were elected to Parliament, including the head of the list Massimo De Carolis. In 1992 he listed 457 names favourable to institutional reform across the parties; more than 150 "pattisti" were elected. The list is the mechanism, because a newspaper that prints a ballot paper stops reporting politics and starts conducting it, and the conducting is the piece, and the piece must show a printed list folding itself into a ballot, and the ballot coming back counted.

**Takeaway:** print the ballot, and the piece should show it coming back counted.

## M23. The gallery of calumnies

In the first years of Il Giornale he ran a column, Dicono di noi, They say about us, reprinting articles from other papers attributing the most fanciful secret owners and occult financiers to the paper: "Erano invenzioni talmente strampalate che, lette tutte insieme a distanza di tempo, risultano comiche", inventions so wild that, read together after some time, they turn out comic, including Fortebraccio's claim in L'Unita that he earned a million a minute. The gallery is the mechanism, because lies exhibited together cancel each other out, and the exhibiting is the piece, and the piece must show calumnies hung in a row like portraits, and the row becoming a joke.

**Takeaway:** hang the lies in a row, and the piece should show the joke.

## M24. The duel with the opposite pole

For years he carried on a long-distance duel in print with "Fortebraccio", Mario Melloni, columnist of L'Unita, two journalists of opposite ideologies but identical polemical force, masking under reciprocal attacks a personal relationship of friendship and esteem: Melloni declared in L'Unita that he wanted for himself the epitaph "Qui giace Fortebraccio / che segretamente / amo Indro Montanelli. / Passante, perdonalo / perche non ha mai cessato / di vergognarsene", Here lies Fortebraccio, who secretly loved Indro Montanelli; passer-by, forgive him, because he never stopped being ashamed of it; Montanelli replied in Il Giornale that he wanted to be buried beside his colleague, under the epitaph "Vedi / lapide / accanto", See / the stone / beside. The duel is the mechanism, because two men who insult each other daily and write each other's epitaphs are not enemies but a double act, and the double act is the piece, and the piece must show two tombstones side by side, and the insults turning into the epitaphs.

**Takeaway:** put the two stones side by side, and the piece should show the insults becoming epitaphs.

## M25. The owner is not the master

When Berlusconi took 12 percent of Il Giornale in 1977 and became majority shareholder in 1979, Montanelli told him, as Felice Froio records it, "Tu sei il proprietario, io sono il padrone almeno fino a che rimango direttore. [...] Io veramente la vocazione del servitore non ce l'ho", You are the owner, I am the master, at least as long as I remain director; I really do not have the vocation of the servant. The pact is the mechanism, because an owner who cannot give orders is a contradiction that holds until it breaks, and the breaking is the piece, and the piece must show a contract with two signatures pulling in opposite directions, and the paper tearing along the line in January 1994.

**Takeaway:** show the contract tearing, and the piece should show the two signatures pulling apart.

## M26. The walkout and the refounding

He left the Corriere della Sera in October 1973, resigned rather than be dismissed, and founded Il Giornale nuovo, first issue Tuesday 25 June 1974; he left Il Giornale on Tuesday 11 January 1994 and founded La Voce in March 1994, and 55 of the 77 journalists followed him. The walkout is the mechanism, because a man who leaves with his whole newsroom in tow is not resigning but transplanting, and the transplanting is the piece, and the piece must show a newsroom walking out of one door and into another, and the desks arriving in the new building.

**Takeaway:** move the whole newsroom, and the piece should show the desks arriving.

## M27. The self-written farewell

He wrote his own obituary while he knew he was living his last days, and the Corriere della Sera published it on its front page the day after his death, "Indro Montanelli's farewell to his readers"; in 1991 he had refused President Francesco Cossiga's offer of a life senatorship, to guarantee his complete independence. The farewell is the mechanism, because a man who writes his own goodbye controls the last word, and the last word is the piece, and the piece must show a front page that is a letter from the dead, and the letter arriving one day late.

**Takeaway:** let the dead write the front page, and the piece should show the letter arriving late.

## M28. The confession without remorse

In the 1969 episode of L'ora della verita he told Gianni Bisiach, on air, that he had bought and married a twelve-year-old girl in Ethiopia, and when Elvira Banotti, an Eritrean-Italian feminist in the audience, asked him how he could justify it, he answered "in Abissinia funziona cosi", in Abyssinia that is how it works; in 2000, in La Stanza di Montanelli, he reconstructed the episode minutely, naming her Desta; the Allegra Lab essay, read end to end in this leg, finds in the interviews and texts examined no sign of remorse, and no suggestion the relationship was based on reciprocal affection. The confession is the mechanism, because a crime told proudly on television, without remorse, turns the telling itself into the scandal, and the telling is the piece, and the piece must show a man confessing to a camera while the audience asks the question he will not answer, and the unanswered question hanging in the air.

**Takeaway:** let the confession hang unanswered, and the piece should show the question, not the answer.

## M29. The correction under evidence

For thirty years he denied the systematic use of chemical weapons, mustard gas, phosgene and arsine, by Italian aviation in Ethiopia, insisting Italian colonialism was mild and good-natured, carried out by a chivalrous army; in 1996, when Angelo Del Boca proved the use with documents in hand, he apologised. On the Vajont disaster of 1963 he called the journalists who denounced the dam "sciacallaggio", jackal work, and in 1998, answering a reader, he admitted the error, adding that perhaps, in similar circumstances, he would do it again. The correction is the mechanism, because a man who admits he was wrong and then says he would do it again has corrected the fact without correcting himself, and the half-correction is the piece, and the piece must show an eraser rubbing out a sentence and then writing it back in pencil, and the pencil line staying.

**Takeaway:** erase it, then write it back in pencil, and the piece should show the pencil line.

## M30. The forgiveness after the kneecapping

Ten years after the attack he met the two brigatisti who had shot him and who had dissociated themselves from the armed struggle, and on 19 March 1987 came the public gesture of reconciliation; in 1996, answering Lauro Azzolini, he recalled it. The forgiveness is the mechanism, because a man who was shot in the legs and then shakes the shooter's hand has closed a circle the state could not close, and the closing is the piece, and the piece must show two hands meeting across a decade, and the decade collapsing between them.

**Takeaway:** close the decade with a handshake, and the piece should show the collapsing.

## M31. The railing that saved his life

On Thursday 2 June 1977, shot at close range at the corner of via Daniele Manin and piazza Cavour, he grabbed the railing of the Giardini Pubblici and held on, shouting "Vigliacchi, vigliacchi!", cowards, cowards, at the attackers as they fled; he always said the gesture saved his life, because if he had fallen at once the last shots would have hit his stomach and chest, and he credited his childhood indoctrination in the Balilla fascist youth and its mantra, "to die on your feet". The railing is the mechanism, because a man who refuses to fall while being shot turns a piece of street furniture into a lifeline, and the holding-on is the piece, and the piece must show hands gripping a railing while legs give way, and the grip doing the saving.

**Takeaway:** grip the railing, and the piece should show the grip, not the fall.

## M32. The pseudonym as a second voice

In Il Borghese from 1954 he signed as Adolfo Coltano, after the prison camp at Coltano, and as Antonio Siberia; in Tempo from September 1942 to July 1943 he signed as Calandrino. The pseudonym is the mechanism, because a second name lets one man argue with himself in the same paper, and the argument is the piece, and the piece must show one typewriter producing two signatures, and the signatures disagreeing.

**Takeaway:** sign it twice, and the piece should show the disagreement.

## M33. The interview with the unlikely subject

His first celebrity interview was Henry Ford, who shocked him by admitting he had no driving licence, and who talked reverently of the Founding Fathers while Montanelli asked him how he felt about having destroyed their world with the automobile and the assembly line; in 1959 he gave the first interview ever granted by a pope to a secular daily, John XXIII, who picked him, through his secretary Loris Capovilla, exactly because he was an atheist and not a Catholic sympathiser. The unlikely subject is the mechanism, because the wrong interviewer asking the right question gets the answer no one else would get, and the mismatch is the piece, and the piece must show an atheist facing a pope and a car-maker without a licence, and the questions doing the work.

**Takeaway:** send the wrong interviewer, and the piece should show the question doing the work.

## M34. The three lives in one career

Fascist youth, anti-fascist resistance, liberal-conservative founder: the volte-face is the spine, from the boy who wrote for Il Selvaggio to the man who joined Giustizia e Liberta, from the admirer of Mussolini to the editor who called Berlusconi a liar, "He lies as he breathes", and who voted for L'Ulivo in 1996 and 2001 while calling the Italian bourgeoisie "the most cowardly in the whole West". The three lives are the mechanism, because a man who changes sides twice cannot be filed under one label, and the unfilability is the piece, and the piece must show one man walking through three doors, and coming out a different man each time, and the same man every time.

**Takeaway:** walk him through three doors, and the piece should show the same man each time.

## M35. The statue and the paint

The Milan commune named the Giardini di Porta Venezia after him and set up a statue by Vito Tongiani, Montanelli at work with his Lettera 22 typewriter on his knees, unveiled 2 July 2006; in March 2019 the feminist group Non Una Di Meno poured pink paint on it, in June 2020, in the Black Lives Matter protests, activists covered it in red paint and sprayed "racist" and "rapist" on its base, and the mayor, Giuseppe Sala, refused to remove it, saying lives must be judged in their complexity. The paint is the mechanism, because a statue that gets repainted every year is a trial that never ends, and the repainting is the piece, and the piece must show bronze turning pink, then red, then bronze again, and the paint never quite washing off.

**Takeaway:** repaint the bronze, and the piece should show the paint that never washes off.

## Evolution: five phases, and the afterlife

## Aesthetic logic

Montanelli's aesthetic is the plain sentence carrying a heavy load. The sentence is short, the anecdote does the arguing, the parallel does the explaining, the portrait does the judging. He distrusts systems and trusts scenes: a battle is a walk in the heat, a century is a face in half-light, a missed Reformation is a missed train, a political choice is a held nose. The newspaper page is his unit of measure, so his histories arrive in instalments and his books read like a year's worth of columns bound together; the reader is addressed directly, answered daily, and treated as a judge, not a pupil. The voice is the "anarchico conservatore" of the 2019 documentary title, the conservative anarchist: against the chorus from whichever side is singing it, against the academy from the side of the public, against the owner from the side of the reader. The logic breaks where the man breaks: the same plainness that makes the Santander dispatch unforgettable makes the madamato confession unbearable, because the plain sentence reports the purchase of a child with the same clarity it reports a battle, and the clarity does not flinch. The study does not resolve that tension; it records it, because the tension is the subject.

**Takeaway:** write the plain sentence, and the piece should carry the heavy load without flinching, the anecdote arguing, the parallel explaining, the portrait judging.
