# Kim J. Vicente: Study Notes

**Site:** Kim J. Vicente is a Canadian human-factors and cognitive-engineering researcher, best known for the books Cognitive Work Analysis (1999) and The Human Factor (2003), and for co-founding ecological interface design with Jens Rasmussen; Wikipedia's lead calls him "an inactive professor of Mechanical and Industrial Engineering at the University of Toronto", a wording its own body contradicts ("Currently, he is Professor of ..."), so no claim about his current status is asserted here. He took a B.A.Sc. at Toronto in 1985, an M.S. at Virginia Tech in 1987, and a Ph.D. in mechanical engineering at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign in 1991; spent a formative year in 1987-88 as a visiting scientist at Riso National Laboratory in Roskilde, Denmark, working with Rasmussen; was on the Georgia Tech faculty in 1991-92; then became professor at Toronto (Mechanical and Industrial Engineering, with cross-appointments in Biomaterials and Biomedical Engineering, Computer Science, and Electrical and Computer Engineering) and founding director of its Cognitive Engineering Laboratory; in 2002-03 he was Jerome Clarke Hunsaker Distinguished Visiting Professor at MIT. His birth date and birthplace were not established in any source read; do not assert them. [C37560]

**Relevance:** Vicente helps investigate what a display must contain so the person reading it can tell the record from the doubt: his ecological interface design, built on Rasmussen's abstraction hierarchy, is a worked-out answer to exactly that question, stated as a design obligation rather than a preference. The evidence is the 1992 EID paper's broken-constraint diagnosis rule (a fault is a broken constraint, so the display must show the complete set of goal-relevant constraints), the DURESS experiment in which a physical-plus-functional interface beat a physical-only interface at diagnosis, and the Three Mile Island pressurizer trap, in which a trusted cue stayed plausible while the state it described had already changed. [C37561]

**Doorway:** The 461st doorway. Polyhop (#840) left this open question: if the record is fixed and the doubt lives beside it, what must the display itself contain so the person reading it can tell the record from the doubt -- and who is responsible when the display does not show the difference? Vicente answers from the designer's side, and his answer is that the display must show the complete set of goal-relevant constraints at every level of the abstraction hierarchy, each mapped consistently onto a perceptual cue, with the boundary conditions under which each cue stays valid made visible beside it; a display that shows only physical values is exactly the failure mode, because it shows the record while hiding the functional relations that would let the reader judge whether the record can be trusted. On responsibility his answer is structural: the designer bears responsibility for the mapping (the one-to-one constraint-to-cue mapping is a design obligation in his second EID principle), while operators are held responsible for outcomes in practice and, under pressure for cost-effectiveness, migrate toward the boundary of acceptable performance until error is the arrival, not the cause. The open question carried forward is: no source read states an explicit rule for marking uncertainty or provenance on the display itself -- a visual code for "this value is trusted, this one is inferred, this sensor is suspect" -- so what must that code look like, and who decides which marks the reader is allowed to see? [C37562]

## M1 -- The Five-Level Stack: one system read at five levels at once

**Subject:** One simulated two-reservoir system shown simultaneously as five horizontal bands, from purpose at the top (keep the downstream reservoir in its safe band) down to physical form at the bottom (two vessels, valves, heaters, pipes) -- the abstraction hierarchy as a live display rather than a textbook figure.

**Visual principle:** Layering and scale, with the same flow drawn once per band so a change at the bottom visibly propagates upward and a purpose failure at the top visibly demands a search downward; the eye can read which level a mark belongs to because each band carries the same event in a different description.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the abstraction hierarchy, not Vicente's own display. Each level holds state variables derived from the level below by fixed rules (levels from flows, balances from levels, purpose error from balances); means-ends links are stored as edges between levels; breaking one edge greys out every dependent value above it. The five levels are Functional Purpose (the purposes the system was designed for), Abstract Function (the intended causal structure in terms of mass, energy, information, or value flows), Generalized Function (the basic functions the plant is designed to achieve, e.g. flow and storage of heat), Physical Function (the characteristics of the components and the connections between them), and Physical Form (the appearance and spatial location of those components). [C37564]

**Artistic opportunity:** The viewer watches one event become five different descriptions at once, so the piece is about description itself rather than about plumbing, and no other gallery piece stacks one system in five simultaneous readings. [C37565]

**Takeaway:** One system, five descriptions, and the fault is visible in all of them. [C37566]

## M2 -- Means-Ends Threading: failure as an unravelling

**Subject:** A field of small process nodes joined by threads that answer how below and why above, with one thread per means-ends link -- the cross-level relation that makes the hierarchy goal-oriented rather than merely layered.

**Visual principle:** Tension and occlusion, with taut threads for satisfied links and slack or snapped threads for links whose lower end no longer supports the upper end; the eye follows the slack to its source instead of being told where the fault is.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation. Each node has a demand value and a supply value; a link is taut only when supply meets demand within tolerance; a fault is injected by lowering one supply so the solver propagates slack upward level by level instead of jumping to the top. The means-ends relation is what distinguishes this hierarchy from part-whole or classification hierarchies: moving down a link answers how, moving up answers why, and that goal-orientation is what lets a problem solver prune search to the sub-tree relevant to current goals. [C37567]

**Artistic opportunity:** Failure arrives as a visible unravelling rather than a red alarm, and the viewer can trace the slack to its source instead of being told where it is. [C37568]

**Takeaway:** The thread, not the alarm, says where it broke. [C37569]

## M3 -- Balance as Shape: the configural display

**Subject:** A closed polygon whose sides are the inflows and outflows of the simulated system, drawn so the polygon closes exactly when mass and energy balance -- a configural display in which a higher-order property (balance) is visible as a shape rather than computed from numbers.

**Visual principle:** Symmetry and contrast, with a regular closed form for balance and a gap, bulge, or skew for imbalance, so the state of the system is read from shape before any number is read; the slow drift from closed to open gives the piece a visible pulse that numbers alone cannot give.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the configural-display idea. At each step inflow and outflow vectors are summed head to tail; the closure error is the vector from the last head back to the origin; the polygon is redrawn from those vectors with a fixed scale so a nonzero closure error is geometrically unavoidable. This is the display form EID principle 2 points at: a consistent mapping from domain constraints to perceptual cues, so a balance violation is seen, not calculated. [C37570]

**Artistic opportunity:** Balance becomes a felt geometry instead of an arithmetic check, and no gallery piece, as far as this study could establish, makes balance itself the picture. [C37571]

**Takeaway:** If it does not close, something is wrong -- and you can see it. [C37572]

## M4 -- The Valid Cue and the Procedural Trap: Three Mile Island

**Subject:** A single gauge-like cue (one rising level) that operators have learned to trust, beside the hidden condition (saturation) that decides whether the cue is valid -- the Three Mile Island pressurizer trap, Vicente's central example of a cue used past its boundary.

**Visual principle:** Occlusion and contrast, with the cue drawn large and calm while its validity boundary is drawn as a thin line that the system state crosses without changing the cue at all; the number stays right while the inference it invites goes wrong.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the TMI example. The cue value is computed from one sensor; validity is a Boolean computed from a second hidden variable (saturation); the simulation crosses the validity boundary on a schedule while the cue keeps rising, so the displayed value and the true state diverge by rule. At Three Mile Island, operators used pressurizer level as a cue for total primary inventory; under saturation conditions with void formation the cue became invalid, yet operators used it as normal and inferred the pressurizer was full of water when it was mainly steam. The EID remedy is a display of the Rankine cycle in temperature-entropy coordinates, so operators can directly see saturation conditions. [C37573]

**Artistic opportunity:** The piece stages trust itself, letting the viewer feel the trap of a familiar cue before any explanation, which is a different experience from watching a system merely fail. [C37574]

**Takeaway:** The number was right; the reading was wrong. [C37575]

## M5 -- Record and Doubt: the doorway mechanism

**Subject:** A fixed recorded trace (a single inked line that never moves once drawn) with a live band of doubt (sensor noise and model error) drawn beside it as a second semi-transparent layer -- the doorway question made visible: the record is fixed and the doubt lives beside it.

**Visual principle:** Density and layering, with the thin certain line over a widening translucent band, so certainty looks thin and doubt looks wide, reversing the usual visual hierarchy; the viewer must decide what to believe from the gap between the two, not from either alone.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the noisy-sensor limitation. The record is a stored series; the doubt band is computed at each step as the record plus or minus a noise term plus a model-error term that grows when the system is far from its calibrated regime; the band never overwrites the record. Vicente's own statement of the limit: sensor data are noisy and system models are never exact, so some deviation between expected normal behaviour and observed data occurs even under normal operation; his 2002 review flagged sensor failure as a possible "show stopper" for EID, and the 2013 sensor-noise study found the EID advantage survives industry-average noise but is compromised by large global increases. [C37576]

**Artistic opportunity:** No gallery piece, as far as this study could establish, separates a fixed record from a live doubt band as its whole subject -- and this is the piece the doorway asks for. [C37577]

**Takeaway:** The record stays wrong if it was wrong; the doubt is drawn beside it, never inside it. [C37578]

## M6 -- Three Ways of Reading: signals, signs, symbols

**Subject:** One moving field shown in three simultaneous registers: a continuous time-space signal, a set of sign-like cues, and a symbolic model of the same state -- the skills-rules-knowledge taxonomy as three ways of reading one picture.

**Visual principle:** Rhythm and scale, with the signal register moving fastest and smoothest, the sign register stepping in discrete jumps, and the symbol register changing slowest and in words or structure; the viewer experiences the cost of thinking as a change in the picture itself.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the SRK taxonomy. A single simulation state feeds three encoders (signal: direct position of a moving mark; sign: threshold crossings that light named cues; symbol: a recomputed abstraction-hierarchy summary), and the piece shifts which register is visually dominant when the state leaves its familiar regime. In Vicente and Rasmussen's terms: information interpreted as signals activates skill-based behaviour, as signs activates rule-based behaviour, as symbols activates knowledge-based behaviour; the perceptual levels are fast, parallel, and low-effort but only available in familiar situations, while knowledge-based behaviour is slow, serial, effortful, and error-prone -- and the only route for novelty. [C37579]

**Artistic opportunity:** The shift from smooth watching to rule following to slow reasoning becomes visible as a change in how the picture asks to be read, rather than as a caption about cognition. [C37580]

**Takeaway:** The same state, read three ways, costs three different kinds of attention. [C37581]

## M7 -- The Unanticipated Event: the blank where a procedure should be

**Subject:** A stable simulated process interrupted by events of three kinds -- familiar, unfamiliar but anticipated, and unfamiliar and unanticipated -- with no procedure supplied for the third kind.

**Visual principle:** Rhythm and density, with familiar events absorbed as small ripples, anticipated events met by a visible stored procedure, and unanticipated events leaving a blank space where a procedure should be; the most interesting moments are the ones the designer did not pre-plan.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the event taxonomy. An event generator draws from three classes with fixed probabilities; familiar and anticipated events trigger stored response rules; unanticipated events trigger no stored rule, forcing the display to fall back to the raw constraint view (balances and boundaries) until the state re-enters a known regime. Vicente and Rasmussen's classification: familiar events are routine, frequent, and trained for; unfamiliar but anticipated events are infrequent but have built-in procedures, decision support, or automatic controllers; unfamiliar and unanticipated events are rare, with no built-in solution, so operators must improvise one -- and unanticipated events cannot be designed away, since the design-basis set is representative classes judged likely and severe, not an exhaustive list. [C37582]

**Artistic opportunity:** The piece is about improvisation under no script, which fits the gallery goal of a working system rather than a scripted animation. [C37583]

**Takeaway:** The procedure is missing; the constraints are still there. [C37584]

## M8 -- Map, Not Directions: the territory survives the broken route

**Subject:** A work-domain map (all constraints and possible routes) overlaid with a rote set of directions (one fixed sequence of steps) for the same simulated system -- Vicente's contrast between knowing a territory and knowing a route.

**Visual principle:** Layering and occlusion, with the map drawn as a faint permanent field and the directions drawn as a bold single path that covers part of it, so the map is visible only where the path does not hide it; when the path breaks, the territory remains.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of Vicente's map-versus-directions contrast. The map is a graph of states and allowed transitions derived from the constraint set; the directions are one fixed path through that graph; a fault closes one edge on the path so the path breaks while the map still shows alternate routes. The underlying claim is the broken-constraint diagnosis rule: normal operation is a set of constraints holding, a fault is a broken constraint, and since it cannot be known beforehand which constraint will break, the display must show the complete set of goal-relevant constraints, not the information for a list of foreseen faults. [C37585]

**Artistic opportunity:** The viewer learns the difference between knowing a route and knowing a territory by watching the route fail and the territory remain, which is a visual argument no caption could make as directly. [C37586]

**Takeaway:** The route broke; the map did not. [C37587]

## M9 -- Nested Constraints: five layers, one feasible room

**Subject:** Five nested regions, one per phase of cognitive work analysis, each smaller than the last, with a moving point that must stay inside all five at once -- constraint as a visible shrinking room.

**Visual principle:** Scale and contrast, with each region drawn as a boundary that tightens the feasible space, so freedom is visible as area and each new constraint visibly shrinks it; the viewer can see exactly which layer is doing the squeezing at each moment.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the nested degrees-of-freedom model. Each layer contributes an inequality constraint on the state; the feasible set is the intersection of all five; the simulation steers the point by random drift plus a restoring pull that weakens as layers are added, so the point spends measurably less time feasible with five layers than with one. The five CWA phases, in Vicente's order: work domain analysis (the functional goals and field of possibilities of the work ecology, typically modelled with an abstraction hierarchy); control task analysis (controllability and observability constraints, stated independently of whether control is manual, automated, or mixed); strategies analysis (potential solutions to the control demands); social organisation and cooperation analysis (distribution of authority, information, and tasks among human and automated agents); worker competencies analysis (capabilities and limitations of the agents, in SRK terms). CWA is formative, not normative or descriptive: it identifies constraints that shape possible work practices without specifying one correct method, and the order of analysis is itself a prescription -- start with the work domain because its constraints are the most permanent. [C37588]

**Artistic opportunity:** Constraint becomes a visible shrinking room rather than an abstract rule, and the feasible set -- the intersection, not any one layer -- is the picture. [C37589]

**Takeaway:** Freedom is the area left after every layer has had its say. [C37590]

## M10 -- The Ladder: one incident at five scales

**Subject:** One incident in the simulated system read simultaneously at five Human-tech scales -- physical, psychological, team, organizational, political -- as five stacked strips, after The Human Factor.

**Visual principle:** Scale and density, with the physical strip dense and fast, the political strip sparse and slow, and the same incident appearing as a spike in the bottom strip and as a slow step in the top strip; a failure can be invisible at one scale and obvious at another.

**Computational mechanism:** Own formalisation of the Human-tech ladder. One incident time series is passed through five filters with increasing time constants and decreasing update rates; each strip plots its filtered series; a failure is counted at a level only when that level's filtered series crosses its own threshold. In the book's terms, the ladder runs physical, psychological, team, organizational, political; the argument is that technology is outrunning our ability to manage it, that design should start from a human or societal need and tailor technology to human nature at each level, and that the relationship between people and technology has been made invisible -- the book's remedy is to make the fit visible again. The Walkerton test of Rasmussen's risk framework is the same ladder read as a failure: the inquiry report's causes matched the framework's predictions across all levels, from physical factors to provincial budget cuts. [C37591]

**Artistic opportunity:** The piece gives the viewer a reason to keep all five strips in view at once, because the incident that matters may be the one only the top strip shows. [C37592]

**Takeaway:** The same incident is a spike at the bottom and a step at the top. [C37593]

## Mechanism note -- transparency and the field check (Harrison-led, Vicente co-authored)

The transparency strand is Harrison's programme with Vicente as co-author, and it adds a second, literal sense of the doorway question: a display can be transparent in the optical sense (layers visible through each other) while still hiding which layer a mark belongs to. Harrison, Ishii, Vicente and Buxton (CHI '95) measured exactly when that confusion sets in: for divided attention, substantial gains occur within the first 20-25% transparency with little gain above that, and levels of 5% or less are not usable; for focused attention, rapid degradation occurs between 5% and 50%, worst at 50%; and legibility depends on visual distinctiveness, not transparency alone -- the anti-interference outline (a border drawn in the maximally luminance-contrasting colour) is the concrete answer to how the reader tells which layer a mark belongs to. The field study (Vicente and Burns 1996) adds the operator's own check on the designer's mapping: instruments can lie (indicator bulbs burn out, leaving operators unsure whether a symptom is a true plant state or an instrument failure), while directly observed components cannot -- operators go into the field daily to look, listen, and feel, because "numbers and instruments are great but if you really want to understand what's going on, you have to go out there and see it and feel it"; and no display can be complete, because plant events map many-to-one onto instrument data. No prompt in this study is built from the transparency measurements alone; the layering principle survives in M5, where the doubt band is a second layer the reader must tell apart from the record. [C37594]

## The arc: from the reservoir to the whole society, the display stays the subject

Vicente's arc is a single question asked at widening scales. In the early phase (1988-1992) the question is theoretical and small: with Rasmussen at Riso and Georgia Tech, the SRK taxonomy, the abstraction hierarchy, and the first EID statements ask what a display must contain so that skill-, rule-, and knowledge-based behaviour are each supported. In the middle phase (1994-1999) the question is experimental: the DURESS studies test the display as an instrument -- P against P+F, over months, with reflection as the condition for deep knowledge -- while the parallel transparency strand with Harrison, Ishii, and Buxton tests how two layers can stay legible at once without losing either. In consolidation (1999-2003) the question widens from the control room to medicine, public health, and everyday technology: the CWA textbook, the TOCHI programme statement, the 2002 EID review, and The Human Factor ask who the design is for, at every level from physical to political. In the late phase (2005-2013) the question becomes robustness and accountability: sensor noise, network scale, and retrospective tests of Rasmussen's risk framework against Walkerton and BSE ask what happens when the data feeding the display cannot be trusted, and who is responsible when the system fails. [C37595]

Three constants run through all four phases. First, the display is always the artwork's subject in the same way a system is: the rules are the piece and the image is their residue -- here the rules are the constraints, and the image is the mapping from constraints to cues. Second, the operator is always given a real role, never a spectator's seat: diagnose the fault, trace the slack, check the instrument in the field, reshape the interface to make relevant information salient. Third, the unanticipated event is always the test: a display that only handles foreseen faults is a catalogue, and Vicente's whole programme is the argument that the display must show the constraints, because only the constraints cover the event nobody foresaw. [C37596]

## The aesthetic logic

Vicente's aesthetic is the legible constraint on a plain ground. The beauty is in a rule you can watch working: a polygon that will not close while the balance is off, a thread going slack level by level, a thin certain line with a wide translucent doubt beside it. Nothing is hidden in a preference panel or a confidence score; the system is on the surface, where the viewer can see it and act on it. The palette is restraint itself -- ink on paper white, one muted colour for the doubt, one for the break -- because the mark must be readable as a constraint's state, and decoration would break the reading. Slowness is not a mood here; it is the price of attention, paid in the seconds before a band widens enough to swallow the record, the minutes before a migration reaches its boundary, the years before a daily practice of checking becomes a body of knowledge -- and the honest piece shows the price rather than hiding it. [C37597]

## Rejections

Eleven candidate directions were considered and not queued. A live process-plant dashboard fed by real plant data was rejected: a gallery piece must use simulated internal data, and live plant data and real operators are outside a single-canvas web piece. A literal control-room replica with dials, pipes, and alarm lights was rejected: a viewpoint and a palette are not a computational mechanism, and the result would be illustration rather than a working system. A literal DURESS replica (two reservoirs, valves, heaters, a P vs P+F toggle) was rejected: a lab apparatus restaged, not a piece; the mechanism survives in VIC1 and VIC4 with the reservoirs abstracted to shape and bands. A Stroop-test transparency demo (colour words through coloured patches at 0-100% transparency) was rejected: a psychophysics experiment restaged, not a piece; the transparency trade-off survives as a layering principle in VIC3, not as a prompt. A literal abstraction-hierarchy diagram (five labelled boxes with arrows) was rejected: a textbook figure, not a system; the hierarchy survives in VIC4 only as a live derivation. An anti-interference font demo was rejected: a technique demonstration, not a piece. Click-to-reload as the primary interaction, or a piece that only reseeds on tap, was rejected: the brief forbids it as a primary interaction and it would make the system a slideshow. A multi-person control-room simulation with team coordination and handover was rejected: it needs several simultaneous operators, which a single-canvas single-viewer piece cannot provide without inventing fake teammates. A documentary narrative of the Walkerton E. coli tragedy or the Three Mile Island accident as a story was rejected: a narrative premise is not a computational mechanism, and a real tragedy retold as a gallery piece would be illustration, not a system. A "human error" blame piece was rejected: blame is a narrative premise, not a mechanism, and Vicente's own argument is that error is the arrival at a boundary, not a root cause in the individual. Repurposing ?seed= as a clock or scene index to step through scripted scenes was rejected: the brief forbids it, and scripted scenes would replace the simulated system with a storyboard. [C37598]
