# Vince Tempera: Study Notes

**Site:** Vince Tempera (b. Wednesday 18 September 1946, Milan, Italy; living), Italian pianist, composer, conductor and arranger, born Vincenzo Tempera [C29726]; keyboardist of the beat group La Nuova Era at twenty, co-founder of the avant-garde trio The Pleasure Machine, keyboardist of the progressive supergroup Il Volo, composer with Fabio Frizzi and Franco Bixio of Lucio Fulci's film scores, co-author of the two Grendizer themes that sold over a million and over 700,000 copies in 1978, conductor of the Sanremo orchestra across five decades and of Malta at Eurovision 1975, co-producer of the debut albums of Biagio Antonacci and Gianluca Grignani, and the pianist-narrator of the "Da Fellini a Tarantino" concert that opened a Milan theatre's season on Saturday 7 November 2026. His practice is the keyboard as infrastructure and the pseudonym as instrument: the same hands, the same two collaborators, the same rhythm section, reappearing under a dozen different names, while the name on the sleeve is a cartoon robot, a choir of stinging insects, or a band of buccaneers.

The first ten years are a keyboard in other people's bands. Born in Milan as Vincenzo Tempera, he graduated in piano and composition at a Conservatory no source read names -- do not name one -- and began his career in 1966 playing with the beat group La Nuova Era, touring Italy at twenty [C29730]. In 1969 he met drummer Ellade Bandini and bassist Ares Tavolazzi and the three founded The Pleasure Machine (La Macchina del Piacere), an avant-garde trio under a Durium contract: a 45 with a Byrds cover on the A-side and Tempera's own "The Pleasure Machine" on the B-side, issued as "Vince Tempera e la Macchina del Piacere", then the album Fourth Sensation (Dischi Ricordi, 1970, released under the name Fourth Sensation) and further 45s on EMI Italiana, including a Beatles cover and "Ultimo tango a Parigi" (1971). The group page carries an Italian Wikipedia "sources insufficient" banner, and the album title is spelled "Fouth Sensation" in the Bandini discography -- the study uses "Fourth Sensation" per the group page and the German Wikipedia. The trio was active 1969-1972, and the same three men are the spine of the next fifty-five years: they reappear together on Guccini, on I Giganti, on Actarus, and on Nomadi, and Bandini and Tavolazzi are still the first names attached to Tempera's work in every decade after.

The arranger years begin with the same trio. In 1970 Tempera arranged Francesco Guccini's L'isola non trovata (December 1970, EMI), playing piano and keyboards with Tavolazzi on bass and Bandini on drums -- the beginning of a collaboration Romics says continued "without interruption" until Guccini's farewell to music in 2013, forty-three years, the longest in Tempera's career [C29731]. The same year he arranged I Giganti's mafia-themed concept album Terra in bocca, and the sources disagree with themselves about the year: the Italian Wikipedia biography text places the arranging work in 1970 while its own arrangements list dates the album to 1971, and the study uses 1971 as the release year and carries the conflict. He arranged Guccini's Radici (1972), played uncredited on Lucio Battisti's Il mio canto libero ("Luci ah") after a quarrel with the singer-songwriter, and from the late 1970s joined Guccini's backing band, the Musici, accompanying him in concert mostly on keyboards. The mechanism is already visible: Tempera is the harmonic floor under someone else's narrative voice, and the floor is the same three men.

The seventies also hold his one prog-rock detour, and it is a supergroup in the literal sense. Il Volo (W05-W06, 1974-75) was founded on Mogol's initiative for Lucio Battisti's Numero Uno label: guitarists Alberto Radius and Mario Lavezzi, Tempera and Gabriele Lorenzi on keyboards, Bob Callero on bass, Gianni Dall'Aglio on drums and vocals -- six men, two LPs, Il Volo (1974, Numero Uno DZSLN 55667) and Essere o non essere? Essere! Essere! Essere! (1975, DZSLN 55679), eight tracks of group-composed music with lyrics by Mogol on the first, six tracks and 30:51 on the second, Tempera co-writing track 4, "Alcune scene" (6:14), with Lorenzi. The group was a studio project rather than a touring band and disbanded later in 1975. Not to be confused with the operatic-pop trio of the same name founded in 2010. The same year, 1974, he collaborated on Saint Just's La casa del lago and arranged Mina's "Caravel" (album Mina), and in 1975 he worked on Marcella Bella's live album L'anima dei matti and scored his first two Fulci films. [C29732]

The Fulci scores are the second career, and they are a trio credit, not a solo one. From 1975 to 1989 Tempera scored seven Lucio Fulci films -- Four of the Apocalypse and Dracula in the Provinces (1975), Sette note in nero (1977), Silver Saddle (1978), Manaos (1979), The House of Clocks and The Sweet House of Horrors (1989) -- and the scores were written with Fabio Frizzi and Franco Bixio as a named trio, "Bixio-Frizzi-Tempera", the three later forming the studio supergroup Magnetic System (one 7-inch, "Godzilla"/"Escape", 1977) [C29733]. The English Wikipedia attributes the Sette note in nero theme to Tempera alone; Discogs and aeonn.net credit "Bixio, Frizzi & Tempera", and the German Wikipedia says he wrote film scores "often together with Fabio Frizzi and Franco Bixio" -- the study credits the trio. The theme itself is the trio's purest device: a seven-note music-box tune, recorded 8-16 March 1977, a child's melody that turns threatening by repetition, for a film released Wednesday 10 August 1977. Fulci chose Tempera for the film, and kept him for fourteen years.

The anime year is 1978, and it is the biggest commercial success of his life. Ufo Robot Grendizer was the first anime broadcast in Italy (Rai 2, 4 April 1978), and Tempera wrote its themes twice over, under the pseudonym Actarus: the opening theme "Ufo Robot" (lyrics Luigi Albertelli; music and arrangement Tempera and Ares Tavolazzi) and the closing theme "Goldrake" (single Friday 15 December 1978; lyrics Albertelli; music and arrangement Tempera and Massimo Luca) [C29734]. "Ufo Robot" won the Gold Record, over a million copies sold, No. 4 in the singles chart, the 18th best-selling single in Italy in 1978; "Goldrake" sold over 700,000 copies, No. 7, the 37th best-selling single of 1979; the Actarus album Atlas UFO Robot was published 16 October 1978. The credit distinction matters and the sources are unanimous: Luca played on "Ufo Robot" but did not co-write it -- the opening theme is Albertelli/Tempera/Tavolazzi, the closing theme Albertelli/Tempera/Luca. The same year he arranged records for Loredana Berte and Mario Lavezzi, and scored Fulci's Silver Saddle. A television theme for a Japanese robot cartoon, written by a film-score trio's keyboardist and a session guitarist and a lyricist, outsold almost everything else in Italian music that year.

The pseudonym system is the mechanism that made the anime year possible, and it runs for two decades. The singles were released under many different group pseudonyms, often inspired by the anime title, with Tempera writing and playing the music under each name: Actarus (Grendizer, 1978), La banda dei bucanieri (Capitan Harlock, 1979), I ragazzi di Remi (Remi, 1979), I ragazzi dai capelli rossi (Anna dai capelli rossi, 1980), Gli Ypsilon (Astro Robot, 1980), Micronauti (Daitan III, 1980), Louisiana Group (Huck e Jim, 1980), I Pungiglioni (Ape Maia, 1981), Aiko and Company (Hello! Spank, 1982), Gli amici di Marco (Marco, 1982), I fratelli Grimm (Nils Holgersson, 1982), I Cuccioli (Il fedele Patrash, 1984), plus Gin and Tonic, I cavalieri di Silverland, and solo records under the pseudonym Andre Carr [C29735]. Tempera sang several themes himself (Hello Spank, Anna dai capelli rossi, others). The personnel lists are the giveaway: the same names keep reappearing under the masks -- Concato, Luca, Tavolazzi, Bandini, Albertelli -- a studio crew of perhaps a dozen people issuing records as a dozen fictional bands. The mask is the product; the crew is the constant.

The Sanremo podium is the role most Italians know him for. He conducted the festival orchestra in 1970-1972, 1990-1997, 1999, 2001-2003, 2005-2006 and 2016 -- the year list rests on the Italian Wikipedia alone, and no per-edition conductor roster was read, so the study reports it as the Italian Wikipedia gives it [C29736]. The host's announcement, "L'orchestra e diretta dal Maestro Vince Tempera", is, in the 1blog4u piece's words, "like a tattoo for most Italians collective memory". One dated instance is corroborated independently: at Sanremo 1990 he arranged and conducted Milva's entry "Sono felice". And the Eurovision count, the study's one pre-flagged dispute, resolves to one: he conducted Malta's entry at Stockholm on Saturday 22 March 1975 -- Renato's "Singing This Song", 10th in the running order, 12th of 19 with 32 points -- and no source lists him as conductor for any other Eurovision edition. Italy's own 1975 entry, "Era" by Wess and Dori Ghezzi, was conducted by Natale Massara, a close colleague with whom Tempera had co-arranged Wess and Dori's album Un corpo e un'anima. Prabook's "five editions" is a garbled scrape of the Wikipedia sentence, not an independent claim, and is not asserted anywhere in this study.

The producer years are the third career, and they are shared with Luca. On Monday 12 June 1989 Philips released Biagio Antonacci's debut Sono cose che capitano, produced by Ron, Luca and Tempera, with Tempera on oboe synth, synth strings, Hammond C3 and keyboards, Ellade Bandini on percussion and drums -- a Pleasure Machine reunion in the rhythm section of a debut album [C29737]. On Friday 24 February 1995 Mercury/PolyGram released Gianluca Grignani's debut Destinazione Paradiso, produced by Luca and Tempera, Tempera on keyboards and arrangements, mixed by Bruno Malasoma: about 650,000 copies in Italy during 1995, past 700,000 domestic later, about a million abroad in the Spanish edition Destino paraiso, peak No. 2 in Italy, Telegatto for Revelation of the Year. The two debuts are the same gesture eleven years apart in the same partnership: the keyboardist-arranger and the guitarist-producer, choosing a young singer-songwriter and building the room around him. Tempera also produced Fabio Concato's breakthrough self-titled album (1982), including "Domenica bestiale", and Giannutri (1990), and arranged Loredana Berte's first hit "Sei bellissima" (single, April 1975, No. 13 in Italy).

The afterlife is the strangest chapter, and the study corrects the record on it. In 2003 Quentin Tarantino used the main theme from Sette note in nero in Kill Bill: Volume 1 -- but he did not commission Tempera to play or compose for the film, and no new Tempera recording for Kill Bill is documented [C29738]. The facts are these: the 1977 theme "Seven Notes in Black", written by Bixio, Frizzi and Tempera and performed by Vince Tempera and Orchestra, plays in the film when the Bride wakes and fights off her attackers; RZA's "Ode to Oren Ishii", track 6 of the soundtrack album (released Tuesday 23 September 2003, A Band Apart/Maverick/Warner Bros.), samples it, with the Discogs credit "Performer [Includes A Sample Of '7 Notes In Black'] - Vince Tempera and Orchestra" and "Producer - Bixio, Frizzi and Tempera"; the full theme is not on the soundtrack album as a standalone track. Romics' wording -- "In 2003, Quentin Tarantino commissioned him to play the soundtrack for the film Kill Bill vol. 1" -- overstates a needle-drop and a sample into a commission, and the study does not repeat it. The mechanism is sampling, not commissioning: a twenty-six-year-old giallo theme, written by three men for a Fulci film, becomes the most internationally heard piece any of them ever wrote, because a director heard it and a producer cut it into a new track. The soundtrack album reached No. 45 on the Billboard 200, No. 1 on the Soundtracks chart, RIAA Gold.

The late career is the return of the conductor, now as narrator. He was orchestra director of Italia 1's Barracuda (1998), returned to RAI composing for the fiction series Raccontami 2 and Tutti i rumori del mondo (2009) and as orchestra director of La Corrida, was a jury member for San Marino TV choosing the country's Eurovision 2008 entry, received the Romics d'Oro Musicale in 2015, and in 2016 co-founded (with Devis Paganelli) and jury-presided the Sanremo Newtalent singing festival [C29739]. After Guccini's farewell to music in 2013 he began collaborating with the rest of the Musici, with Danilo Sacco (ex-Nomadi) on vocals. On 7 December 2025 he received an Attestato di Civica Benemerenza from the Comune di Milano at the traditional Ambrogini d'Oro ceremony -- Italian Wikipedia only, citing the Comune; the Comune page itself was not retrieved, so the award is single-source and the study says so. He was still arranging and performing in 2024, co-arranger and performer on "Telenovelas gassate" on Davide Matrisciano's album Libidal.

The last three years are a trio and a title. On 19 June 2024 the Vince Tempera trio -- Tempera at the piano, Sergio Bellotti on drums, Enrico Santarelli on bass -- played "From Fellini to Tarantino" at Blue Note Off, Milan, sold out, the second consecutive year, with a Burt Bacharach tribute sung by Marzia Bi, the singer Tempera discovered at a Milan charity concert and for whom he produced and arranged a Bacharach album [C29740]. On Saturday 7 November 2026 the same trio opened the Teatro della Quattordicesima's season in Milan with "Da Fellini a Tarantino" -- a journey through Oscar-winning Italian and international film music, with tributes to Fellini, Sergio Leone, Toto, Stelvio Cipriani, Luis Bacalov and Riz Ortolani, screenings of cult-film fragments (La Dolce Vita, La strada, Il Padrino, Giu la Testa, C'era una Volta il West, Metti una sera a cena, Profondo Rosso) and anecdotes told by Tempera from the piano -- then played it again the next day, Sunday 8 November 2026, at the Teatro Malatesta in Montefiore Conca, with a further Bologna date around 15 November. The theatre had been closed for almost fourteen years before reopening. The start time is uncertain -- the press release said 19:30, the poster 20:30 -- and the study states no time. The show is the career in miniature: the pianist who spent sixty years behind other people's names, now at the front of the stage, telling the stories himself, with the film clips playing behind him.

The evolution is a single arc with two hinges and no reversal, and it closes the same year it is written. Session keyboardist in the sixties, arranger of the cantautori in the seventies, prog-rock keyboardist and film-score composer in the same decade, cartoon-theme hitmaker in 1978, Sanremo conductor across five decades, debut-album producer in 1989 and 1995, and finally the narrator-pianist of his own retrospective -- the same hands, the same collaborators, the same rhythm section, changing costume as the decades pass [C29741]. The first hinge is 1970: the year he stops being only the keyboard in the room and becomes the arranger who decides what the room sounds like. The second is 1978: the year a television theme for a Japanese robot makes him, briefly, the best-selling composer in Italy. And the arc closes on a death this study must carry: Francesco Guccini, the cantautore Tempera arranged for at twenty-four and accompanied for forty-three years, died on Thursday 6 August 2026, age 86, at Pavana, the Apennine hamlet of his wartime childhood -- multiply corroborated, with the posthumous novel Calde le pere! (Giunti, 27 August 2026) and a Bologna tribute weekend, 29 October-1 November 2026, as its closing frame. Tempera played his retrospective three months later. The floor outlived the voice it carried.

## M1 -- The pseudonym ensemble: one musician, a dozen fictional bands

Tempera released singles under at least fourteen group pseudonyms between 1978 and 1986 -- Actarus, La banda dei bucanieri, I ragazzi di Remi, I ragazzi dai capelli rossi, Gli Ypsilon, Micronauti, Louisiana Group, I Pungiglioni, Aiko and Company, Gli amici di Marco, I fratelli Grimm, I Cuccioli, Gin and Tonic, I cavalieri di Silverland -- plus solo records as Andre Carr, each name taken from the anime it served [C29735]. The personnel lists give the game away: the same dozen names -- Concato, Luca, Tavolazzi, Bandini, Albertelli -- keep reappearing under the masks. The mechanism is a studio crew issuing records as fictional bands, the mask sized to the product: a robot hero gets a robot name, a bee gets stinging insects, a pirate captain gets buccaneers. The audience buys the mask; the crew stays the same. Two of those masks sold over a million and over 700,000 copies. [C29742]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be one small crew of figures issuing themselves under many changing masks -- the same hands, the same instruments, but each time the emblem above them changes: a robot, a bee, a pirate flag -- and the emblem is what the audience remembers, while the crew never changes size.

## M2 -- The two-man song factory: a television theme becomes a million-selling record

"Ufo Robot" and "Goldrake" were written by the same three-person cell in two configurations: Albertelli's words, Tempera's music, Tavolazzi on the opening theme, Luca on the closing theme [C29734]. The cell is the factory: a lyricist who had already won Sanremo in 1969 ("Zingara"), a keyboardist-arranger who conducted orchestras, and a session guitarist or bassist, writing a forty-second tune a child can hum to a thirty-second title sequence. The record then outgrew the television: the opening theme went gold, over a million copies, the 18th best-selling single in Italy in 1978; the closing theme sold over 700,000. The mechanism is scale without change of material -- the same forty seconds, first under a cartoon, then on a jukebox, then on a million turntables. [C29743]

**Takeaway:** the piece should start as a tiny tune under a moving picture, then let the same tune grow -- first the picture, then the room, then the whole town humming it -- without the tune itself ever changing a note.

## M3 -- The film-score afterlife: a 1977 giallo theme returns through a sample

The seven-note music-box theme of Sette note in nero was written by three men for a Lucio Fulci giallo in March 1977, played in the film that August, and then sat for twenty-six years [C29733]. In 2003 it came back without anyone writing a new note: Tarantino needle-dropped it into Kill Bill: Volume 1, and RZA sampled it into "Ode to Oren Ishii" on the soundtrack album [C29738]. The mechanism is the afterlife by reuse: the music does not need a revival, a remake, or a reunion -- it needs one listener with a sampler and one director with a needle. The theme is now the best-known piece any of its three authors ever wrote, and none of them touched it again. The same afterlife runs in the other direction through Frizzi, who still plays "Sette Note in Nero" live in 2026 as the set piece of his Frizzi 2 Fulci tour. [C29744]

**Takeaway:** the piece should let a small, quiet theme sleep for most of its runtime, then wake it unchanged in a completely new room -- sampled, re-lit, surrounded by strangers -- and the theme should be the loudest thing in the piece precisely because it never changed.

## M4 -- The Sanremo podium: conducting as a public instrument

Tempera conducted the Sanremo orchestra in 1970-1972, 1990-1997, 1999, 2001-2003, 2005-2006 and 2016, and the host's announcement -- "L'orchestra e diretta dal Maestro Vince Tempera" -- became, in the 1blog4u piece's words, "like a tattoo for most Italians collective memory" [C29736]. The mechanism is the downbeat as a public event: the conductor is the only musician in the festival the whole country sees, night after night, year after year, and the announcement of his name is part of the show itself. He is not the star -- the singers are -- but he is the metronome the country sets its watch by. The same podium logic in miniature at Eurovision 1975: one night, one Maltese entry, 12th of 19, and the record of it survives fifty years later in the contest's own tables. [C29745]

**Takeaway:** the piece should put one figure on a podium in the middle of the frame, conducting a crowd of louder figures -- the podium figure never sings, never takes the spotlight, but every entrance in the piece waits for his downbeat, and the audience learns his name from the announcement, not from the song.

## M5 -- The fifty-five-year trio: the rhythm section as a constant

Tempera met Bandini and Tavolazzi in 1969, founded The Pleasure Machine with them, and the three played together on Guccini's L'isola non trovata (1970), on I Giganti's Terra in bocca (1970/71), on the Actarus singles (1978), and on Nomadi's Ma noi no (1992) -- fifty-five years from the first meeting to the present, with Tavolazzi still recording in 2026 and Bandini still playing in 2025 [C29731]. The mechanism is the constant under the costumes: the bands change (beat group, avant-garde trio, prog supergroup, cartoon pseudonym, film-score trio), the names on the sleeve change, the genres change, and the same three men are underneath. The trio is not a band; it is a working condition, the thing Tempera's music stands on. [C29746]

**Takeaway:** the piece should hold three figures absolutely still at the centre of the frame while everything around them changes -- bands, costumes, genres, decades -- and the three figures should be the only thing that never moves, never changes size, never leaves.

## M6 -- The arranger as shadow author: forty-three years under one voice

Tempera arranged Guccini's L'isola non trovata at twenty-four and was still working with him in 2013, when the collaboration ended -- forty-three years, from Guccini's third album to his last working years, the longest collaboration in Tempera's career [C29731]. The mechanism is the harmonic floor: the piano and keyboards under a narrative voice, the arrangement that makes a song about a found island or a root system hold together, repeated across five decades while the voice above it ages and the floor stays level. The same floor under other voices -- Berte's first hit, Concato's breakthrough, Mina's "Caravel" -- and the arranger's name is never the one on the cover. When the voice died on 6 August 2026, the floor was still playing a retrospective three months later. [C29747]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be built as a floor and a voice: a quiet harmonic bed that never changes, and a single speaking figure walking above it for the whole runtime -- and when the voice stops, the floor should keep playing alone for a beat, because the floor was the piece all along.

## M7 -- The narrator-pianist concert: film music, anecdotes, and projections

In 2024 and again in 2026 Tempera staged "Da Fellini a Tarantino" as a trio -- himself at the piano, Bellotti on drums, Santarelli on bass -- playing Oscar-winning Italian and international film music while fragments of cult films (La Dolce Vita, La strada, Il Padrino, Giu la Testa, C'era una Volta il West, Metti una sera a cena, Profondo Rosso) played behind him and he told the stories between the pieces: the Sofia Coppola anecdote about how Tarantino heard the Sette note in nero theme, the Ray Charles encounter, the sixty years of sessions [C29740]. The mechanism is the concert as memoir: the pianist is also the narrator, the screen behind him is the evidence, and the trio is small enough that the stories fit between the notes. The theatre in Milan had been closed for almost fourteen years before reopening for him. [C29748]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be a small stage with a screen behind it: a pianist playing film themes while fragments of old films drift across the screen, and between the pieces the pianist stops and tells a short story -- and the story should change how the next piece sounds.

## M8 -- The three-composer score: one byline across six genres

Bixio, Frizzi and Tempera wrote together as one credit -- "Bixio-Frizzi-Tempera" -- across Fantozzi (1975, comedy), Febbre da cavallo (1976, comedy), Vai Gorilla (1976, poliziottesco), I quattro dell'apocalisse (1975, western), Sette note in nero (1977, giallo) and Silver Saddle (1978, western): six genres in three years under one shared byline [C29733]. The mechanism is the trio as a single author with three hands: Bixio the publisher's son and the businessman, Frizzi the melodist and the horror voice, Tempera the conductor-arranger and the keyboard colour, writing as one name. The same three formed the studio supergroup Magnetic System for one 7-inch in 1977. The byline outlived the partnership: the seven-note theme is still the trio's signature, still sampled, still played live by Frizzi alone in 2026. [C29749]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be written by three figures at once -- three hands on one instrument, or three figures passing a single melody between them -- and the piece should change genre every thirty seconds while the three hands stay the same, so the genre is the weather and the trio is the constant.

## Aesthetic logic

Put the eight mechanisms together and one logic holds them. Tempera does not make songs; he makes the conditions in which other people's songs become audible -- the keyboard line under a cantautore's voice, the arrangement that turns a television theme into a gold record, the conducted downbeat that holds a festival together, the pseudonym that lets a studio crew sell a million copies as a cartoon robot. The first-person voice is never his: he is the keyboardist in La Nuova Era, the arranger on Guccini's third album, the conductor the host announces before the singer appears, the co-writer whose name slides into the credit of a song he did not sing. The place is never decoration: the Milan studios, the Numero Uno offices, the Ariston podium, the Teatro della Quattordicesima reopened after fourteen dark years. Early he is the hired keyboard, the most adaptable hand of the seventies session world; late he is the narrator-pianist, the man who finally stands at the front of the stage and tells the stories himself -- and in between he is the man with a dozen names, the mask-maker whose own name is the one the audience learns last. His own programme, insofar as any source gives one, is the concert format itself: play the film music, show the fragments, tell the anecdote, and trust that the story changes how the next piece sounds. The work is the room, the floor, and the downbeat -- and the room is for someone else's voice, until the last decade, when it is finally for his. [C29750]
