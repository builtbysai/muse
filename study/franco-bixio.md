Franco Bixio was born in Rome on Sunday 29 January 1950, the youngest of three children in a house where music was the family trade -- his father, Cesare Andrea Bixio, had written the songs Italy sang in the streets, "Mamma" and "Parlami d'amore Mariu", and had founded the country's first music publishing house in Milan in 1920, thirty years before his youngest son was born. [C29840]

He began as a film composer, not a publisher. The filmography starts in 1966, when he was sixteen, and by 1968 he was scoring for Claude Chabrol and for the spaghetti westerns, and the solo scores of the early seventies are the genre pictures -- a Sahara heist, a women-in-prison cornerstone, a sex comedy played straight against Baroque court music -- each one given its own idiom, as if the young composer were trying on a new suit for every film. [C29841]

The trio is the first career, and it was his brother's idea. Carlo Bixio, who ran Cinevox in the seventies and believed in the "vivaio" -- the nursery -- had spotted the young men who would become Goblin, and after Fantozzi he proposed the partnership to Frizzi, who was twenty-three. With Vince Tempera the three became Bixio-Frizzi-Tempera, and Franco's place in it was the balance: the youngest child of an important patriarch, acting as the "bilancia" between two stronger characters, the clever ear that gathered their ideas and channelled them into a project. They worked without a script -- sometimes splitting scenes, sometimes each writing a version and voting in absolute democracy -- and they worked fast, a dozen scores a year, about seventy in five years. [C29842]

The breakthrough was a clerk. Fantozzi (1975) took its model from the slapstick cartoons -- Sylvester and Tweety, Tom and Jerry -- so a head-butt lands on violins, and its ballad came from Villaggio's love of Cat Stevens and the Harold and Maude soundtrack; the trio's references were Rota, Rustichelli, Trovajoli and Morricone, and the score they made was, as Frizzi says, the moment he first felt like a fortunate young professional. The same years hold the westerns and the poliziotteschi and the comedies that became national classics. [C29843]

Vai Gorilla (1975) took its model from the American films from Shaft on -- wah-wah guitars and a particular use of drums, the main theme built on melodies from the ARP Odyssey, a keyboard not yet widespread in Italy. Febbre da cavallo (1976) had its theme invented directly in the recording room -- during a break a chorister was amusing his colleagues humming a melody and beating his fists on his chest, and the trio stopped him and recorded the idea on the spot. Fulci, who sat silent through the sessions and then rose to demand precise explanations, wanted "schiaffi musicali" -- musical slaps, memorable enough to hit the audience on the nape of the neck. [C29844]

The giallo is the trio's purest device. For Sette note in nero (1977) they recorded a real carillon, note by note, and spliced the tape with adhesive where other composers would have reached for a celesta -- handcraft, in the Morricone sense -- and the tune has outlived the film by decades, coming back through Tarantino and RZA without a new note. The theme is not laid over the film; it is inside it, the chime of a wristwatch, heard first in a vision, then on the hour, then playing from inside a wall. [C29845]

The solo voice is documented separately, and it is the proof that Bixio could stand on his own. A pugni nudi (1974) is a pleasing mix of pop and R and B that hearkens back to Afro-American funk and urban jazz -- grouped strings over electric bass, piano, wah-wah guitar and drum kit, a trumpet theme clear as glass -- and the reviewer who heard it whole concluded that Bixio "can very much stand on his own as a film composer". Deserto di fuoco (1971) leans on exotic percussion and Edda dell'Orso's voice; Il tuo piacere e il mio (1973) is Baroque court music with a tuneful comedic twist, plus a song the composer sings himself. [C29846]

The trio also stepped out from behind the films. As Magnetic System they released one 7-inch in 1977, "Godzilla" / "Escape" -- a barrage of synths and funky basslines, "Escape" taken from the 1977 film Day of Violence, running at a simmer before exploding into a futuristic frenzy. The Finders Keepers history calls it the trio "bridging giallo jazz-bass-driven prog and the arrival of home studios and synthesizers... promoting melodic electronics to the forefront of Italian pop culture and preempting the first murmurs of Italo disco and synthpop". Under further masks, the Day-Glo Fruit Of The Gum and the Benjamin Franklyn Band, the same three sold the sound directly as pop. [C29847]

The hinge came in 1990, and it was not a new composition but an inheritance. Franco took over the Gruppo Editoriale Bixio and the Cinevox label his father had founded in the early sixties, and from then on the composer became the publisher: the catalogue -- a historic song list and more than a thousand film scores -- became the work, with the reissue of the catalogue and the production of television fiction scores taking the place of new film writing. He founded Musikstrasse in 1993 for classical repertoire nobody had recorded, and with Francesco Lotoro he put the music composed in the concentration camps, 1933-1945, on disc, and made the documentary Musica concentrationaria with the surviving musicians. From 1998 to 2004 he was president of the AFI, the Italian phonographic association. [C29848]

The trio came back once, for Febbre da cavallo - La mandrakata in 2002, and otherwise the seventies scores live on as reissues -- Quartet's two-disc Film Music of Franco Bixio, the 50th-anniversary vinyls of Fantozzi and Febbre da cavallo in 2026 -- while the man himself has moved into the room where the future of the trade is argued. The 2026 releases are the publisher reissuing his own past: Diario segreto da un carcere femminile on 30 May, Il Secondo Tragico Fantozzi on vinyl for the first time on 25 September, Febbre da cavallo's 50th anniversary on 30 October -- and the next generation, Marco Bixio, now art-directs Cinevox releases. [C29849]

The last two years are the proof of life. In October 2024 he shared a stage in Rome with Simonetti, Frizzi and Pivio to talk about horror scores; in October 2025 he was at Romics and the Festa del Cinema for the fiftieth anniversaries of the two great comedies. And on Thursday 22 October 2026 he opened a conference at the ICBSA in Rome -- Il compositore aumentato, the augmented composer -- which he had conceived himself, to ask whether traditional writing and generative AI can coexist in film music, and to insist that the Bixio Academy's young composers be taught to protect the originality of their work. [C29850]

The arc is a single line with one hinge and no reversal: the solo journeyman of the early seventies, the trio workhorse of the middle seventies, the publisher-custodian from 1990 on -- the same music, first written, then written three-handed, then kept alive, reissued, taught and argued over, by the man whose family owned the room it was written in. [C29851]

## M1 -- The balance: three heads, one vote, and the youngest child in the middle [C29856]

The trio's method, in their own words: no fixed script -- sometimes they split single scenes between them, sometimes each wrote one or more versions of a cue and they voted in "assoluta democrazia"; about seventy trio soundtracks in total, up to a dozen scores a year, drawing on the Unione Musicisti di Roma pool of about a hundred professionals. Bixio's place was the balance: Tempera says the trio was founded "in primis, sull'orecchio 'furbo' di Franco Bixio" -- on Franco's clever ear, which managed him and Frizzi, gathering their ideas -- and Bixio himself says he acted as the "bilancia" between the two stronger characters, the youngest of three children doing naturally what the family had taught him. The mechanism is the editor as peacemaker: three thinking heads, each with a different sensibility, no jealousy, and one man whose job is to hear all three and choose. [C29857]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be three voices writing the same cue at once -- three lines, three colours, one frame -- and a vote that picks one, the other two folding into it, the balance visible in the choosing. [C29858]

## M2 -- The publisher as gatekeeper: the commissions came through the house [C29859]

Frizzi is explicit about where the work came from: "Bixio was also my publisher. Through him we got the chance to work with Fulci." Carlo Bixio, the publisher brother, met the young Frizzi, paired him with his brother Franco and with Tempera -- "he knew I couldn't do too many things at once" -- and then proposed the group itself: "Why don't we try to do a group?" The division of labour inside the trio follows the same line: Frizzi wrote the music, Tempera helped him conduct it, and Franco helped him with the editing -- fitting the written music to the cut of the film, the post-production craft. The mechanism is the room that decides who works: the family firm owned the catalogue, the studio and the introductions, and the composer who also owned the firm never had to wait for a commission to arrive. [C29860]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be a room with a door -- work arrives through the door, not from the composer going out to find it -- and the man at the desk who opens it, edits what comes through, and sends it back out as a film. [C29861]

## M3 -- The carillon by hand: a real music box, note by note, tape spliced [C29862]

For Sette note in nero the carillon theme was recorded on a real music box, note by note, in the studio, the tape then spliced with adhesive where other scores would have used a celesta -- "un lavoro di vero artigianato musicale, un po' come quello di Ennio Morricone". The tune is a child's music box -- simple, elegant and gravely beautiful -- and it is inside the film the way a watch is inside a pocket: the chime of a wristwatch Gloria gives Virginia, heard first in a vision, then on the hour, then playing from inside a wall where her husband has entombed her. The mechanism is handcraft against the easy instrument: the same seven notes, played by a real box and cut by hand, so that the prettiness is physical before it is musical. [C29863]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be one small, pretty tune built by hand -- a real box, note by note, the tape visibly spliced -- repeated until the repetition itself turns it threatening. [C29864]

## M4 -- One thought per film: a distinct note of distinction for every score [C29865]

The trio's rule, in Frizzi's words from the Fantozzi DVD: "noi c'inventavamo un pensiero per ogni film" -- they invented a thought for every film. Bixio: they were "alla costante ricerca di una nota di 'distinzione' per ogni colonna sonora... cercavamo di fare del nostro meglio e di lasciare una 'cifra'". Fantozzi took slapstick cartoons as its model, so a head-butt lands on violins; Vai Gorilla took Shaft and the ARP Odyssey; Febbre da cavallo took a chorister humming in the recording room and beating his fists on his chest. The mechanism is the single distinguishing idea, found before the writing starts, that makes one score unlike the last -- the score as a signature, not a style. [C29866]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be one film, one thought -- a single distinguishing idea, found in the room, that everything else in the piece serves and nothing else repeats. [C29867]

## M5 -- The journeyman's idiom: one film, one style, and a voice of his own [C29868]

Before the trio and beside it, Bixio scored alone, and each solo score takes a different idiom: Deserto di fuoco leans on exotic percussion to represent the African setting, topped with Edda dell'Orso's voice; Diario segreto da un carcere femminile is a catchy, bloomy women-in-prison score; Il tuo piacere e il mio is Baroque court music with a tuneful comedic twist, plus a song the composer sings himself; Istantanea per un delitto reuses pop songs written for a 1971 Motowns LP, two of them co-written with Franco Micalizzi, augmented with new cues. The richest is A pugni nudi: pop and R and B, Afro-American funk and urban jazz, grouped strings over electric bass, piano, wah-wah guitar and drum kit, a trumpet theme "crystal clear", and a second orchestral main theme that returns on tremolo organ, accordion and fast piano groove. The mechanism is opportunism as craft: the journeyman takes the film's genre and plays it straight, in its own idiom, and the proof is that the idiom changes every time. [C29869]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be one film given exactly the idiom its genre asks for -- funk for the boxing picture, Baroque for the sex comedy, percussion for the desert -- and the composer disappearing into the idiom so completely that each piece sounds like a different man. [C29870]

## M6 -- The alias release: the trio steps out from behind the films [C29871]

In 1977 the three men stepped out from behind the films as Magnetic System and released one 7-inch, "Godzilla" / "Escape" -- a barrage of synths and funky basslines, "Escape" taken from the 1977 film Day of Violence, running at a simmer before exploding into a futuristic frenzy. The Finders Keepers history calls it the trio "bridging giallo jazz-bass-driven prog and the arrival of home studios and synthesizers... promoting melodic electronics to the forefront of Italian pop culture and preempting the first murmurs of Italo disco and synthpop". Under further masks, the Day-Glo Fruit Of The Gum and the Benjamin Franklyn Band, the same three sold the sound directly as pop. The mechanism is the alias as freedom: behind a false name the working band for hire could play the sound it actually liked, without a film to serve. [C29872]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be three film composers under a false name, playing the sound they actually like -- synths and funky basslines, no film in the room -- and the single going out as pop. [C29873]

## M7 -- The composer becomes the publisher: the catalogue as the second career [C29874]

In 1990 Franco took over the Gruppo Editoriale Bixio and the Cinevox label his father had founded in the early sixties, and the work changed its shape: the catalogue -- a historic song list and more than a thousand film scores -- became the job, with reissue and television fiction production taking the place of new film writing. He had founded Trafalgar Studios in the seventies and Musikstrasse in 1993, and with Francesco Lotoro he put the music composed in the concentration camps, 1933-1945, on disc. From 1998 to 2004 he was president of the AFI. The mechanism is stewardship as authorship: the 2026 releases -- Diario segreto on 30 May, Il Secondo Tragico Fantozzi on vinyl for the first time on 25 September, Febbre da cavallo's 50th anniversary on 30 October -- are Bixio the publisher reissuing the trio's 1970s catalogue, the same scores his former partner now tours live, with the next generation, Marco Bixio, art-directing the new editions. [C29875]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be a catalogue, not a composition -- a shelf of old scores, each one taken down, cleaned, and put back out in a new sleeve, the publisher's hands the only hands on it. [C29876]

## M8 -- The augmented composer: the past against the machines [C29877]

On Thursday 22 October 2026 Bixio opened a conference at the ICBSA in Rome -- Il compositore aumentato, the augmented composer -- which he had conceived himself, with Marco Testoni and Renato Marengo, to ask whether traditional writing and generative AI can coexist in film music. He gave the opening intervention, on the Bixio Academy's support for young talent, copyright protection and originality, while a film about his father Cesare Andrea Bixio played on the screen behind him. The panels held SIAE, FIMI, Nuovo IMAIE, FAPAV, AFI and the ACMF composers. The mechanism is the balance, one last time, in a new room: the boy who balanced two stronger characters now balances a whole industry's past against its machines, and the argument he makes is the one his career has always made -- that originality is the thing to protect, and that the catalogue is only worth keeping if the new work stays original. [C29878]

**Takeaway:** the piece should be a conference room, not a studio -- an old catalogue on one side, a machine on the other, and the composer in the middle, opening the argument and insisting the new work stay original. [C29879]

## Aesthetic logic

Put the eight mechanisms together and one logic holds them. Bixio is not a composer who became a publisher; he is a publisher's son who composed, and the composing was always the publisher's work done another way -- the clever ear gathering two stronger characters, the editor fitting music to picture, the man who owned the room deciding who worked in it. Early he is the journeyman, one idiom per film, disappearing into the genre; then he is the balance of the trio, the third hand that makes two hands into one name; late he is the custodian, and the catalogue is the composition -- a thousand scores kept alive, reissued, taught and argued over, the same music returning in new sleeves while his former partner tours it live. The place is never decoration: the family house in Rome, the Cinevox offices where the young Frizzi met the young Simonetti, Rocca di Papa where his father's piano stood, the ICBSA auditorium where he opened the argument about the machines. His own programme, insofar as any source gives one, is the one he states in the trio interview: a note of distinction for every score, a signature left on each one, and -- in 2026 -- the originality of the new work protected against the machine that would copy it. The work is the balance, the edit, and the keeping -- and the balance is the same at twenty-five and at seventy-six, only the room it holds keeps changing. [C29880]
