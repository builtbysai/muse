## M1. The break with Darmstadt serialism and what replaced it

The break is not a rejection of technique, it is a rejection of system-as-law, and Henze keeps twelve-tone material while refusing total serialism. Being Beauteous of 1963 bends twelve-tone technique toward "a certain cantabile quality, a sensual lyricism", in Chigiana's phrase, and the hinge works are Quattro poemi of 1955, which "made clear that he had moved far from the principles of the Darmstadt avant-garde", Konig Hirsch of 1956 with its lush textures, and Nachtstucke und Arien of 1957, of which Henze said "with this score I had reached a position that could scarcely have been further removed from that of the so-called Darmstadt School", and the Donaueschingen premiere of that work is the public rupture, Boulez, Nono and Stockhausen walking out after the first few bars. What replaces the system is meaning-driven form, and Chigiana, quoting the Henze poetics, says "he constructs his form according to meaning, even when he makes no use of words", and the music expresses "emotions, images, values and characters... drawing upon psychological symbols, mythical figures and ancient tales". In Der Prinz von Homburg the mechanism is literalised on stage, rigorously organised serialism, extended to rhythm and dynamics, for the world of orders and reason of state, and free tonality for the dreamer who transgresses. The mechanism is: keep the technique, refuse the law, and let meaning decide the form, because a system that cannot say yes or no is not a language, and a technique that can is a tool.

**Takeaway:** do not throw the technique away when you leave the school; keep the notes and refuse the law, and let meaning build the form, because a break that keeps its tools is a new language, and a break that burns them is only a gesture.

## M2. The Mediterranean turn: exile as a compositional resource

The move to Italy in 1953 is dated by every source to homophobia and the political climate in post-war Germany, plus Schott's advance, and the musical consequence is singability and sensuality. The Five Neapolitan Songs of 1956 carry "an Italianate singability unheard before in Henze's output", in the Guardian obituary's phrase, and Konig Hirsch has "lush, rich textures", and Undine is opulent and romantic, and the Mediterranean widens over the career, the Lieder von einer Insel of 1964 transforming "the Mediterranean landscape into an intimate poetic dimension", in Chigiana's phrase, and the late Arabic turn, long winter stays on Lamu in Kenya, the Sechs Gesange aus dem Arabischen of 1997-98 and L'Upupa of 2003, is the same mechanism extended eastward. The exile is not an escape from Germany, it is a new instrument, and the instrument is the climate, the light, the singing line, and the piece sounds like where it was written. The mechanism is: change the country, and let the country change the line, because a composer who writes in the same room writes the same piece, and a composer who moves writes the move.

**Takeaway:** do not treat exile as biography and music as something else; the move is the mechanism, and the singing line is the proof, because a new climate is a new instrument, and the piece should sound like where it was made.

## M3. Tonality as a dramatic code, serialism as another

Henze assigns musical languages to opposed worlds and lets them collide, so the listener can HEAR the argument, and this is the single most portable mechanism in the study. Boulevard Solitude sets tonality as the old world in decline and dodecaphony, learned from Rene Leibowitz, as the anti-bourgeois love of the two young protagonists, and the lack of communication between the two musical codes drives the finale. Der Prinz von Homburg sets serialism as orders and reason of state against free tonality as dream, freedom and transgression. The Bassarids sets rigid forms as Pentheus and rationality against unleashed sensual intoxication as Dionysus. We Come to the River runs "a wide variety of compositional styles, ranging from neoclassical harmony to atonal music" across three simultaneous stages. Symphony No. 6 draws on serialism and traditional tonality, microtonal intervals, amplified instruments and large percussion, in Britannica's account. The mechanism is: give each character, each world, its own musical language, and let the languages fail to understand each other, because the tragedy is not in the plot, it is in the non-communication, and the audience hears the argument before it understands it.

**Takeaway:** do not write one language and call it a style; write two languages and give one to each side, and let them collide, because a piece that argues in one language is a lecture, and a piece that argues in two is a drama.

## M4. Political music as communication, not illustration

Henze's stated poetics, quoted in Chigiana, is the mechanism in words: "Through words, music arrives at a confession it could not otherwise make. It becomes responsible, delineates the explicit meaning of yes and no, becomes full of participation, engaged, and makes itself a living part of our destiny", and "music becomes political when it aims to communicate, to inform and to inspire action". The mechanism in practice has three moves. First, real-world sound enters the score, and Voices of 1973 uses transistor radios, microphones and a tape collage of real-life sounds alongside 70 multi-ethnic instruments, and Tristan of 1972-73 carries on tape distorted fragments of the Tristan prelude, a heartbeat, the Florentine Lamento di Tristano and a child reciting Gottfried von Strassburg. Second, documentary text is set with spoken and sung extremes, and El Cimarron ranges from speech to improvised singing, Sprechgesang, countertenor sounds, whistling, howling and laughter. Third, the event itself becomes political, and the Das Floss der Medusa premiere of Monday 9 December 1968 was cancelled amid a riot, left-wing students hanging a large red flag bearing Che Guevara's image beneath the podium, the choir refusing to sing unless it was removed, police intervening violently, the librettist among those arrested, and the music was not heard that night, though it was due to be broadcast live by 22 European radio stations, and the event made Henze a pariah in Germany, and he turned his back on Germany for 15 years. The politics is in the fabric, and a Grove-derived biography calls it "political protest, deep-dyed into the very fabric of the music". The mechanism is: let the world into the score, let the text speak plainly, and let the performance itself be a political event, because music that only describes politics is a picture of a fire, and music that communicates is the fire.

**Takeaway:** do not illustrate the politics beside the music; put the world inside the score, the radio, the tape, the spoken word, the riot, because a piece about a fire is a description, and a piece that burns is a politics.

## M5. Two orchestras and three stages: conflict made spatial

Henze splits the performing forces so that conflict is spatial, not just harmonic. Symphony No. 6 of 1969 is written for two chamber orchestras that depict "sonic conflicts and, at times, an attempt at reconciliation between different musical languages", in Chigiana's phrase, with solo instruments from Cuban folklore and a quotation of Mikis Theodorakis's hymn to freedom, and it was premiered in Havana in 1969 by Henze himself. We Come to the River of 1974-76 is performed on three stages, each with its own orchestra, with 111 roles performed by more than 50 singers, and the audience must choose where to look, and the simultaneity IS the political argument, empire, war, many voices at once, and Wikipedia says "his political critique reached its high point" here. The mechanism is: do not resolve the conflict in the harmony, stage it in the room, and let the audience's attention be the battlefield, because a conflict that is only heard is an argument, and a conflict that is seen from three sides at once is a world.

**Takeaway:** do not mix the opposing forces into one texture; split them into two orchestras or three stages and let the room hold the argument, because harmony can reconcile what the eye cannot, and the eye is the honest witness.

## M6. Opera as the central form, and the librettist as co-author

Henze wrote about two dozen operas plus a dozen ballets and more than forty stage works, in the Guardian obituary's and EAMDC's counts, and the theatre is where every other mechanism is tested. The librettists are not suppliers but partners across decades, and each partnership has a distinct sound. Ingeborg Bachmann, 1952-64, is dream against order, Der Prinz von Homburg and Der junge Lord, and her poems carry the 1957 farewell to rigid serialism. W. H. Auden and Chester Kallman, 1961-66, are moral fable and myth, Elegy for Young Lovers and The Bassarids, and the farcical intermezzo that interrupts the long adagio between Pentheus and Dionysus. Hans Magnus Enzensberger, 1970-74, is documentary and revolution, El Cimarron and La Cubana, and poems in Voices. Edward Bond, 1976-83, is violence and the state, We Come to the River, Orpheus and The English Cat. Hans-Ulrich Treichel, 1989-97, is German memory, Das verratene Meer and Symphony No. 9. And finally Henze himself, L'Upupa of 2003, his only self-written opera libretto. The mechanism is: choose the writer first, and let the writer choose the sound, because the opera is not a composer with a text, it is a partnership with a voice, and the voice is half the music.

**Takeaway:** do not hire a librettist; marry one, for a decade, and let each marriage sound different, because an opera with a hired text is a setting, and an opera with a partner is a collaboration.

## M7. The recital for four musicians: a whole theatre from four players

El Cimarron of 1969-70, subtitled Biographie des geflohenen Sklaven Esteban Montejo, is a "recital for four musicians", baritone, flute, guitar and percussion, and the mechanism is scarcity of forces and maximality of means. The flautist plays piccolo, C flute, alto and bass flutes, plus the Japanese ryuteki and the Italian scacciapensieri, and uses Bartolozzi multiphonics, and beats percussion and sings. The guitarist plays the Afro-Cuban marimbula and drums. The percussionist commands Japanese prayer bells, log drums, boobams, a Trinidad steel drum and African drums. The baritone ranges from speech to improvised singing, Sprechgesang, countertenor sounds, whistling, howling and laughter, and all four play percussion. Fifteen brief scenes tell one life, slavery, escape, the jungle, the independence war, disillusionment with the republic, from Miguel Barnet's oral autobiography of Esteban Montejo, told in 1963, translated into German and adapted by Enzensberger, and Henze met Montejo himself in Havana. The premiere is disputed between the 1970 Berlin Festival and the 1970 Aldeburgh Festival, see C1, and the study does not assert a single venue. The mechanism is: four players, seventy sounds, one life, and the smallness of the forces is what makes the largeness of the story believable, because a big orchestra telling a slave's life is a spectacle, and four people telling it is a witness.

**Takeaway:** do not add players to make a story bigger; give four players seventy sounds and one life, because scarcity is the witness, and a theatre that fits in a room can hold a whole life.

## M8. Collage and quotation as political reinterpretation

Pre-existing music is quoted, distorted and set against itself, never as decoration. Tristan of 1972-73 quotes Wagner's Tristan chord and prelude, Brahms's First Symphony opening, Brahms the anti-Wagnerian, and Chopin's Funeral March, on tape and in the orchestra, and the work was written in the year of Pinochet's coup in Chile, 1973, and Wagner's desire for death becomes a contemporary lament. The Bassarids quotes Bach's St Matthew Passion. The Requiem of 1990-93 keeps traditional movement titles but no liturgy and no text, nine "sacred concertos" for solo piano, concertante trumpet and large chamber orchestra, in memory of Michael Vyner, who died of AIDS in 1989. Henze's Wesendonck-Lieder orchestration of 1976 shrinks Wagner to ten winds, harp and small strings for transparency. The point, per Chigiana, is that the quotations are "a genuine political and personal reinterpretation", navigating "attraction and repulsion towards German Romanticism". The mechanism is: quote the music you love and the music you refuse, in the same piece, and let the quotation argue with its source, because a quotation that only decorates is a postcard, and a quotation that reinterprets is a verdict.

**Takeaway:** do not quote to show you know the repertoire; quote to judge it, and let Wagner argue with Brahms in the same bar, because a quotation that agrees with its source is nostalgia, and a quotation that argues with it is a politics.

## M9. The guitar as a dramatic voice: Shakespeare as "sonic masks"

In Royal Winter Music, Sonata No. 1 of 1975-76 and Sonata No. 2 of 1979, for solo guitar, written at the suggestion of Julian Bream, who encouraged Henze to explore the guitar's timbre, Shakespeare's characters are not described but embodied, and each movement is a personality sounding through the guitar "as through a veil", in Henze's phrase via Chigiana. Sonata I gives Gloucester from Richard III, Romeo and Juliet, Ariel, Ophelia, Touchstone with Audrey and William, and Oberon. Sonata II gives Sir Andrew Aguecheek, Bottom's Dream and Mad Lady Macbeth. The guitar becomes "a dramatic voice capable of expressing passion, sadness, comedy and madness", bridging Elizabethan aesthetics and contemporary technique. The same mechanism runs in Kammermusik 1958, tenor, guitar and eight instruments on Holderlin, dedicated to Benjamin Britten and premiered by Peter Pears and Julian Bream, with textures "far harsher" than Undine, and in Memorias de 'El Cimarron' for solo guitar of 1970. The mechanism is: take one quiet instrument, and make it carry a whole cast, and let each character be a different way of touching the strings, because a guitar that plays notes is a guitar, and a guitar that plays people is a theatre.

**Takeaway:** do not write a character study for an orchestra; give one guitar six people and let the touch change, because a mask that is played is a person, and a mask that is described is a label.

## M10. The symphony as a kept faith

While his generation abandoned the symphony, Henze wrote ten, from 1947 to 2000, "alongside Dmitri Shostakovich, the most prolific and significant symphonic composer of his time", in Chigiana's phrase. The first five are short, about twenty minutes, three movements, the Fourth in one, drawn from Konig Hirsch. The Sixth breaks the mould after Cuba, two chamber orchestras, Cuban folklore, Theodorakis. The Seventh returns to German subjects, Holderlin, the third movement a meditation on Holderlin's medical tortures at Dr Autenrieth's clinic in Tubingen, the finale inspired by Halfte des Lebens, and Henze called it "a German symphony, and it deals with matters German", and the Guardian obituary says the January 1984 Berlin Philharmonic premiere under Gianluigi Gelmetti "at last saw a public reconciliation between Germany and the composer". The Eighth is wordless Shakespeare. The Ninth, 1995-97, is a choral anti-fascist memorial, seven movements, text by Treichel after Anna Seghers's The Seventh Cross, dedicated "to the heroes and martyrs of German anti-fascism", premiered 1997 by the Berlin Philharmonic under Ingo Metzmacher. The Tenth, 1997-2000, overturns the superstition that a composer dies after a Ninth, and Henze wrote that he completed it to "overturn" that superstition, and the last movement is a dream "in which all the instruments reassemble in order to sum up the dreams that had already begun to haunt me in my Eighth Symphony", and the work ends in "a questioning uncertainty, an elegiac decrescendo", in EAMDC's phrase, and it is also an In Memoriam for Paul Sacher, who commissioned it. The mechanism is: keep the classical genre as a vessel and refill it each time, and let the vessel remember what it held, because a form that is kept is a promise, and a form that is refilled is a history.

**Takeaway:** do not abandon the old form to prove you are new; keep it and refill it, and let the tenth symphony answer the first, because a discarded form is a lost argument, and a kept form is a witness.

## M11. Teaching and festival-building as composition by other means

Henze builds institutions the way he builds operas. The Cantiere Internazionale d'Arte at Montepulciano, founded 1976, is explicitly NOT a festival for consumers but a "workshop where young artists could invent and experiment with new forms of communication", turning a culturally dormant village into producers of art, and Pollicino of 1980 is an opera written for local children as an "intensive music course" in contemporary styles. The Munich Biennale, founded 1988, is a springboard for young composers of new music theatre. The Deutschlandsberg Youth Music Festival and the Murztal Workshops, from 1981 and 1984, do the same in Styria. The teaching posts run alongside, Mozarteum Salzburg 1962-67, Dartmouth 1967, Royal Academy of Music London 1972-96, Cologne Hochschule 1980-91, Chigiana Siena 1982, Tanglewood 1983 and 1988, Berlin Philharmonic composer in residence 1991. The mechanism is: if the institutions will not stage the work, build the institution, and if the village has no art, teach the village to make it, because a composer who only writes pieces is a tenant in someone else's house, and a composer who builds the house is at home.

**Takeaway:** do not wait for a stage; build one, and do not wait for an audience; teach a village to perform, because a piece needs a house, and a house you built is a piece.

## M12. The late style: return, farewell, self-authorship

After 1990 the music turns back to German subjects, Holderlin, Seghers, Treichel, Wagner orchestrations, AND eastward to Arabic culture, Lamu winters, the Sechs Gesange aus dem Arabischen of 1997-98 and L'Upupa of 2003, AND inward to self-written texts, the L'Upupa libretto, Henze's own libretto, his only one, the Latin text of Elogium Musicum, and five of the six Arabic songs are Henze's own poems, and he asked "Why look for suitable texts when you can write them yourself?" L'Upupa is a German comedy in eleven scenes, almost a modern Singspiel, based on a Syrian fairy tale, and Kasim seeks the magical hoopoe with golden feathers that escaped his father's garden, and finds love and rescues the young Jewish woman Badi'at from a tyrant, and the Muslim protagonist arrives two years after the Twin Towers attacks. The flow of original music "eventually slowed", in the Guardian obituary's phrase, and revisions of earlier works multiplied. The last operas form a leave-taking arc, L'Upupa, a journey home with the bird, Phaedra of 2007, myth, after he had said he was done, Gisela! of 2010, happiness, after he had said L'Upupa would be his last opera, and Elogium Musicum of 2008, a memorial for his partner of more than forty years, Fausto Moroni, who died in 2007 after a lengthy battle with cancer, shortly after Henze's sudden recovery from a nervous breakdown in which he barely spoke. The mechanism is: at the end, write your own words, return to your own country, and say farewell in your own voice, because a late style that borrows its texts is still waiting for permission, and a late style that writes its own has stopped asking.

**Takeaway:** do not end by repeating your best piece; end by writing your own words and going home, because a farewell in someone else's text is a quotation, and a farewell in your own is a signature.

## M13. The Hen-ze identity: the German who left and stayed away, then came back on his own terms

The biography IS the mechanism. Born into a Nazi household, father Franz wounded at Verdun and turned Nazi, Hitler Youth at 12, conscripted 1944, British prisoner-of-war camp, and Henze's whole career is a refusal, refusal of the father, of the Darmstadt system, of the German musical establishment after 1968, when he "turned his back on Germany for 15 years", in the Guardian obituary's phrase, and of Castro's Cuba after solidarity with dissident artists. The 1984 Seventh Symphony premiere is the reconciliation, on his terms, "a German symphony", and the Ninth Symphony of 1997 is the reckoning with the country of his youth, dedicated to the heroes and martyrs of German anti-fascism. The Berliner Festspiele names the through-line as a "lifelong aversion to authoritarian tendencies and his position on the left". The health decline frames the end, trouble speaking from 2005, a three-month coma around the Sebastian im Traum premiere of Thursday 22 December 2005, the last piece of his own he heard performed live, and he died in Dresden on Saturday 27 October 2012 while attending a Henze focus at the Semperoper, and he is buried in Marino Cemetery, in the Italian town he made his home. The mechanism is: let the life be the argument, and let each refusal be a compositional decision, because a biography that is only background is a programme note, and a biography that is the mechanism is the work.

**Takeaway:** do not separate the life from the music; the refusal is the composition, and the return is the coda, because a career that only sounds is a catalogue, and a career that refuses and returns is a life.

## Evolution

P1. Apprenticeship and serialism, 1946-1953. Darmstadt from 1946, twelve-tone study with Rene Leibowitz in 1948-49, Leibowitz a former pupil of Schoenberg and Webern, and the First Symphony and First Violin Concerto of 1947 as serial showpieces, and ballet-conductor jobs at Konstanz from 1948 to 1950 and Wiesbaden from 1950 to 1953 that made theatre his home, and the Kammerkonzert of 1946 bringing him to Schott's attention at Darmstadt, his publisher for the next six decades. Britannica records that Henze himself considered the early works up to Symphony No. 2 of 1949 "simple, or even primitive, as they depended greatly upon the effectiveness of his melodies". The phase ends with the move to Italy in 1953, enabled by Schott's advance on condition he give up conducting posts, and the move is the hinge of the whole career.

P2. The Italian turn and the break, 1953-1965. Ischia, then Naples from January 1956, then La Leprara at Marino from 1961. Mediterranean lyricism enters, Ode an den Westwind of 1953, the Five Neapolitan Songs of 1956, and Konig Hirsch of 1956 and Quattro poemi of 1955 shed serialism, and the last Darmstadt is 1955, and the Donaueschingen walkout is 1957. ## Aesthetic logic

The logic of Henze's work is that a refusal is a composition, and the career is a series of refusals that become forms. The refusal of the father becomes the refusal of Germany, and the refusal of Germany becomes the Italian line, and the refusal of Darmstadt becomes the dramatic code, two languages for two worlds, and the refusal of the German establishment becomes the political music, and the refusal of Castro becomes the persona-non-grata turn, and the return is not a surrender, it is the same refusal on new terms, a German symphony that deals with matters German, and an anti-fascist Ninth that names the country of his youth. The opera is the form because the opera is where refusals become people, and the librettist is the co-author because the refusal needs a voice, and the voice is half the music, and each partnership sounds different because each refusal is different. The symphony is kept because the kept form is a promise, and the promise is that the old vessel can hold the new argument, and the Tenth overturns the superstition that a composer dies after a Ninth, and the overturning is itself a refusal, of fate, of the number, of the grave. The collage is not decoration, it is a verdict, and Wagner argues with Brahms in the same bar because the German past argues with itself, and the quotation is the evidence. The guitar is a theatre because one quiet instrument can carry six people, and the mask is played, not described. The festival is a composition because a house you built is a piece, and the village that performs is the audience that was missing. The late style writes its own words because it has stopped asking for permission, and the farewell in your own text is a signature. The whole oeuvre is one long education in refusal, from the twelve-tone showpieces that already depended on melody, through the Mediterranean line that refused the system, through the political music that refused the establishment, through the three stages that refused to resolve the argument in the harmony, to the dream-summa that refused to die after the Ninth, and the lesson is that a career is not a catalogue, it is a life, and a life that refuses and returns is the work.
