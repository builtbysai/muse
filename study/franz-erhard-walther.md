Walther was born on 22 July 1939 in Fulda, and his youth is a bakery before it is an art school: a modelling course in the parental bakery business 1954-1956, evening drawing courses with Rudolf Kubesch in Fulda in 1955, a failed admission to the Werkkunstschule Offenbach in 1956 for not reaching the minimum age, and the Junge Kunstkreis in 1957. His education runs in sequence -- Werkkunstschule Offenbach 1957-1959, Hochschule fuer Bildende Kuenste Frankfurt (Staedelschule) 1959-1961, where he studied under Ferdinand Lammeyer before being expelled, and Kunstakademie Duesseldorf 1962-1964 under Karl Otto Goetz, alongside Chris Reinecke, Gerhard Richter and Sigmar Polke, taking part in Fluxus actions convened by Joseph Beuys, befriending Richter and Blinky Palermo and sharing a studio with Palermo. From the late 1950s he tried to involve spectators actively in producing the work, making "word" pictures to force the viewer to create their own image, and in the early 1960s he treated paper as the work itself, in stacks to leaf through or large books to interact with; his first action, Attempt to be a Sculpture, dates to 1958, when he was nineteen. [C27426]

In 1964 he married Johanna Walther, a trained clothing technician; since March 1963 she has sewn his stitched works in close collaboration with him, and he came to the craft of sewing through Johanna and her parents' tailor shop. The First Work Set (1. Werksatz), 1963-1969, is 58 activatable pieces of cotton fabric, foam, wood and other materials, described by the artist as "instruments for process", begun in Duesseldorf and completed in New York; the viewer is meant to use them by folding them, pulling them over the body, lying in them, or forming geometric shapes, placing the viewer in extraordinary interpersonal situations. Elements of the set were demonstrated in the auditorium of the Duesseldorf Academy in 1964 with Sigmar Polke participating and Blinky Palermo and Joseph Beuys present, and in 1966 activations took place in the flat of Joerg Immendorff and Chris Reinecke (show "Frisches") with Nam June Paik and Charlotte Moorman among others. [C27427]

He moved to New York with his wife Johanna in 1967 after finding Duesseldorf cramped and small-minded, conceived another seventeen pieces for the First Work Set there, and declared it complete in 1969; the set was shown in Spaces at the Museum of Modern Art, New York, 30 December 1969 to 1 March 1970, and in When Attitudes Become Form at Kunsthalle Bern in 1969. His work was included in four editions of documenta in Kassel: documenta 5 in 1972, documenta 6 in 1977, documenta 7 in 1982, and documenta 8 in 1987. He was guest professor at the Hochschule fuer bildende Kuenste Hamburg in 1970 and was appointed professor there in 1971 at the age of 32; the students named across the sources include Almut Linde, John Bock, Rebecca Horn, Santiago Sierra, Christian Jankowski, Lili Fischer, Martin Kippenberger and Jonathan Meese, and Tuzina's Hamburg study of 1971-1977 falls entirely inside Walther's professorship under either end-year. The New York end year splits 1971 (English and German Wikipedia, HKW, Goethe-Institut) vs 1973 (Peter Freeman, Drawing Room / e-flux), with Dia's "six years" pointing to 1973; this study writes 1967-1971 as the majority figure with the split carried. [C27428]

The later work groups keep the same grammar under new materials: the 2. Werksatz of 1971/1972, made of standing and walking paths in fabric; the Schreit- und Standstuecke (walking and standing pieces) 1973-1978, first in steel for outdoor space, then in wood and fabric for interiors; the New Alphabet, the 3. Werksatz of the 1990s, 26 body sculptures related to letters; and the Handlungsbahnen (Action Paths) 1997-2003, 55 multi-part sculptures of cotton ground paths of different lengths with action-related elements. His public-space works are Raumform fuer Fulda, made 1970/1971 and 1993/1994 in his home town, five steel plates with inscriptions laid at the Stadtschloss, Domplatz, Michaels-Kirche, Paulus-Tor and Kloster Frauenberg; and Sieben Orte fuer Hamburg (Seven Places for Hamburg), 1989, seven square steel plates set in concrete in Hamburg's inner city, engraved with the terms ORT, RICHTUNG, KOERPER, INNEN AUSSEN, BEWEGUNG, RAUM and ZEIT. Drawing is his life record: Dust of Stars. A Drawn Novel (2007/09) is composed of 524 pages of pencil drawings and handwritten texts, a visual and textual record of his life and work tracing his long engagement with drawing. [C27429]

Franz Erhard Walther: Work as Action opened at Dia:Beacon on 2 October 2010 and ran through 13 February 2012, his first solo museum exhibition in the United States since 1990, organised by Dia curator Yasmil Raymond; Dia had acquired the 1. Werksatz in 1978, and the show presented it in its totality for the first time in the United States since Spaces in 1970. The Body Decides at WIELS, Brussels, 21 February to 11 May 2014, his first show in Belgium and one of his largest retrospectives to date, travelled to CAPC musee d'art contemporain de Bordeaux, 13 November 2014 to 8 March 2015. Shifting Perspectives at the Haus der Kunst, Munich, 6 March to 2 August 2020 as first announced and extended to 29 November 2020, showed 250 works and numerous drawings tracing six decades, with daily work activations. In 2017 he was awarded the Golden Lion for the Best Artist in the Central Pavilion at the Venice Biennale; his other prizes run Guenter-Fruhtrunk-Preis 1985, Edwin Scharff Prize 1989, Piepenbrock Prize for Sculpture 1994, Kulturpreis des Kreises Pinneberg 1996, Ernst-Franz-Vogelmann-Preis fuer zeitgenoessische Skulptur 2011, Kulturpreis der Stadt Fulda 2014, Kunstpreis Aachen 2016. In 2008 he founded the Franz Erhard Walther Foundation, which manages the collection of his works, and in 2023 the Foundation and the City of Fulda opened the Villa Franz Erhard Walther as an exhibition and events space in Fulda; his wife Susanne Walther chairs the Foundation board. In 2023 he was commissioned by Performa for the Performa 23 biennial, creating Creation Needs Action, presented 16-19 November 2023 with the Judd Foundation at 101 Spring Street, New York. No death is reported in any source read; the latest listings show him alive and active, with Peter Freeman listing a 2026-2027 Villa Franz Erhard Walther show. [C27430]

## M1 -- Score and instruction

Every First Work Set element ships with a defined action, often fixed in a Work Drawing made before or after the object, so the viewer is a user following a score, not a free improviser. The watchable behaviour is the participant who reads or is told the move, then performs it in order, as in Weitergehen, where feet go into 28 pockets one after another against a borrowed watch.

**Takeaway:** give the viewer a score to follow, not a free improvisation; the instruction is the work. [C27431]

## M2 -- Body as plinth and material

The body carries, stretches and props the fabric, so the sculpture only stands up when a body is inside it. The watchable behaviour is heads through the two holes of For Two holding the green cloth up between them, and two bodies leaning into Body Weights tensioning the 650 cm length.

**Takeaway:** make the body the plinth; the sculpture stands only while someone is inside it. [C27432]

## M3 -- Pocket and insert sequence

Sewn pockets sized to feet, heads or whole bodies force the action into a counted order. The watchable behaviour is nine people filing into nine interlocking pockets in Compactness and Ambivalence, and a single walker working pocket to pocket in Weitergehen.

**Takeaway:** size the pocket to the body part and count the inserts; the order is the composition. [C27433]

## M4 -- Shared load and counterweight

Long lengths and multi-panel pieces only reach their form when two or more bodies pull or lean against each other. The watchable behaviour is two unequal weights balancing across Body Weights, and participants leaning into the four panels of Four Body Weights trying, and failing, to pull them into a square.

**Takeaway:** the form needs two bodies pulling against each other; one body alone cannot finish it. [C27434]

## M5 -- Hooding and shared sight

A long shared hood puts two heads under one cloth and couples what both people can see. The watchable behaviour is a couple placing Sehkanal over their heads together and having to move and look as a pair.

**Takeaway:** put two heads under one cloth; the shared sight is the sculpture. [C27435]

## M6 -- Repose and duration

Bedlike structures make lying still, waiting and lasting into the action, with time as the material. The watchable behaviour is participants lying down in the two and four-section maroon beds of Time Place Duration Direction Relation, and the divided boards marking how long and where a body lies.

**Takeaway:** lying still is an action too; let duration be the material. [C27436]

## M7 -- Scored walking

Stride Paths and steel pedestals score the floor: step here, step over this, follow the pockets along the seam. The watchable behaviour is a walker following a bordeaux-red path pocket by pocket, or stepping on and over a steel ring or pedestal on a walk-on tour.

**Takeaway:** score the floor like a page; the walk is the reading. [C27437]

## M8 -- Storage form vs action form

Every piece has two states, folded storage (Lagerform) and unfolded action (Handlungsform), and the stored pile is itself a display state. The watchable behaviour is the set lying like stock in a textile factory or struck camp tents, then unfolding into use; from 1974 to 1997 the First Work Set was shown only in storage form, with demonstrations resuming in Geneva in 1997.

**Takeaway:** keep both states visible; the folded pile is a form, not a failure to show. [C27438]

## M9 -- Wall as field

In the 1980s the fabric moves to the wall, where colour, wood and architecture carry the action instead of the wearer's body. The watchable behaviour is a viewer manipulating a Wall Formation such as Memory Base, and the large Yellow Modeling of 1980-81, sewn dyed canvas and wood at 500 x 1100 x 60 cm, holding the room like a painted relief.

**Takeaway:** move the fabric to the wall and let the room carry the action the body used to. [C27439]

## M10 -- Drawing as action record and drawn novel

Work Drawings record, prescribe and extend each action, and in Dust of Stars the drawing itself becomes the work: 524 sheets of pencil and handwriting. The watchable behaviour is a reader turning sheets one by one, or listening to a public reading, while Action Paths drawings from 1997-2003, pencil and gouache on paper at 73 x 103 cm each, map walks that the body then performs.

**Takeaway:** draw the action first and last; the drawing is the score, the record and finally the work itself. [C27440]

## Aesthetic logic

Walther's logic is the sculpture that refuses to exist without a body inside it. The work is an instrument, not an object: the fabric has no form until a participant folds it, wears it, walks it or lies in it, and the form it takes is the record of that use -- a pocket counted, a weight leaned, a hood shared, a path walked. Where Tuzina left the hand in the line, Walther leaves the body in the sculpture: the viewer is not a spectator but a user, and the piece is finished only while it is being used. The storage form is the honest admission that the sculpture spends most of its life waiting -- folded, stacked, inert -- and the action form is the brief moment when it is alive. Against Tuzina's dark rectangle that holds the gaze, Walther's answer is a length of cloth that holds nothing until two people pull it taut between them. [C27441]
