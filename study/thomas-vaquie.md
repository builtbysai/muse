# Thomas Vaquie: Study Notes

**Site:** Thomas Vaquie (b. Toulouse, 1982), French composer and sound designer, lives and works in Brussels; the composer of the AntiVJ label -- not a founder, but the man who has scored most of its works since 2008, and the first release on its Antivj Recordings imprint (Ecume, 2016). He splits his life in two halves, stated as a deliberate economy: most of his time goes to composition and post-production for European cinema, and that work pays for the other half -- experimental, often large-scale projects in public space, scored with and for the visual artists of the label. He founded the Brussels post-production studio Chocolat-Noisette with Jeff Levillain in 2007, and in 2018 co-founded the composer collective NONE with Laurent Delforge and Simon Thieree. His own account of the method, repeated verbatim across the press kit and the album notes: every piece is "a response to place and space, to light and architecture, to code and motion", written from "a capacity to project an image sonically", "consistently trying to extend visual experiences into physical ones". The Beursschouwburg bio coins the term for it: he has created "'sonic images' for projects around the globe". The sound is not laid over the image. The image's geometry, the room's acoustics, the site's history and the code's behaviour are the score's inputs.

## The life: a listener who learned to score buildings

Vaquie was born in Toulouse in 1982 -- on the evidence of a single source chain, the 2016 Ecume press kit and the bios derived from it (C3104) -- and lives and works in Brussels. Nothing in any source read names a school, a conservatory, a teacher or a degree. Contrast his NONE co-founder Laurent Delforge, whose bio states he is "a graduate in electroacoustic music of the Royal Conservatory of Belgium". For Vaquie there is silence, and the study states the gap rather than inventing a training (C3101). His biography, everywhere it is written, starts not with a school but with a studio: "In 2007, he founded Chocolat-Noisette with Jeff Levillain, a post production studio based in Brussels. they have been working on sound recording, sound editing and mixing for European movies industry." The lowercase "they" is the source's own. The founding date rests on that one About line alone (C3114).

The earliest dated work on his site is not a building at all but a student film, and it states the whole method in miniature. Machu Picchu Post (2008), a short 3D film made at Supinfocom Arles, carries his own note: "This soundtrack is maybe a part of myself at 6 years old playing Lego, making sound effects and music of my imaginary story with my mouth." Voice-made foley as score: sound effects and music are the same act, made with the mouth. The body is the first instrument, and the mouth noises return -- as the "storm of voices" in Mutis (2016) and the human voice inside the machine in Out of Control (2014).

He is not a founder of AntiVJ. "Antivj was originally created by Yannick Jacquet, Joanie Lemercier, Olivier Ratsi and Romain Tardy around 2006" (3Destruct presentation PDF). He is the label's main composer -- the press kit calls him "Antivj's main composer" -- and the first release on its Antivj Recordings imprint was his album Ecume. When he started is a three-way spread in the sources: the press kit says "Since 2007 he has specialised in music experimentation for audiovisual work with the Antivj visual label", the label's own page says he has composed "for most projects since 2008", and his own About says "Since 2009". The documentary evidence favours 2007-2008: 3Destruct was "originally designed specifically for the Biennale d'art contemporain of Louvain-la-Neuve, Belgium in 2007" with "Music composed by: Thomas Vaquie", and the 4 October 2008 Nuit Blanche performance at Mont des Arts, Brussels, already credits his sound design (C3120, search-result level). His own "since 2009" is the outlier -- possibly counting from when the collaboration became his specialisation rather than his first credit. This study uses the label's "since 2008" in the doorway and carries the spread (C3102, C3151, C3201).

The label he joined worked as a label, not a collective -- solo projects, collective projects and external commissions, like a music label. Lemercier's 2011 interview gives the working method: "We work in a very organic way. We can all do a bit of 3D, photography, graphic design; and we really like swapping roles, and experimenting with new things." That role-swapping is the context for Vaquie repeatedly stepping beyond "music" into co-direction. The credits show the climb: 3Destruct (2007) -- "Music composed by: Thomas Vaquie" under Jacquet and Peeters' visuals; Omicron (2011/2012) -- "Direction: Romain Tardy and Thomas Vaquie", his only co-direction credit in the catalogue; Paleodictyon (2012) -- "A project by Simon Geilfus, Yannick Jacquet and Thomas Vaquie" with artistic direction by all three (Vimeo description, search-result level -- C3116); 3Destruct | Oaxaca (2013) -- installation "by Yannick Jacquet, Thomas Vaquie and Jeremie Peeters"; Replica (2013) -- "by Laurent Delforge and Thomas Vaquie". His About confirms it in prose: "he has co-directed certain projects with Antivj visual directors". The study presents him as co-author of the AntiVJ image, not as its soundtrack.

The career runs in four phases. Phase 1, cinema post-production, Brussels, from 2007: Chocolat-Noisette, sound recording, editing and mixing for European movies -- the economic base for everything after. Phase 2, AntiVJ, the site-specific decade, 2007/2008-2016: music for most of the label's projects -- 3Destruct, Light Sculpture v3 (2010, Dijon), Clair Obscure (2011, Neuchatel), the Chateau des ducs de Bretagne (2010, Nantes), Omicron (2011/2012, Wroclaw), Paleodictyon (2012, Centre Pompidou-Metz), Onion Skin, 3Destruct | Oaxaca and Replica (March 2013, Oaxaca), Out of Control (2014, Atomium), Last Breath of a Hanged Man (August 2014, Berlin Atonal, mixed on 4D Sound's 48.9 system), Elytre (2017, Paris). Phase 3, the album as object, 2016: Ecume, first release on Antivj Recordings -- 8 tracks, 51'46", double gatefold 180g vinyl (500 copies, June 2016), plus a limited resin-cast boxset edition of 25, each a unique hand-crafted piece cast from the concrete mould of the album artwork. Phase 4, NONE and spatial sound, 2018 to the present: "In 2018, with Laurent Delforge and Simon Thieree, he founded None, a music composer collective based in Brussels" -- the founding date rests on the About line alone (C3115) -- and the collective's most recent dated activity is "space is the place", a two-evening microfestival dedicated to 3D sound and modular synths at Beursschouwburg, Brussels, 21-22 March 2025, inspired by Pauline Oliveros's Deep Listening: "the skill to play music not just 'in' a space, but 'with' a space". Meanwhile the Atomium run -- the Visual System collaboration that began with Infinity (2012/2013) -- has become a home: Talk (2016), A Circular Journey (2018), Symbol (2021), Restart (2023), Central (2023), Look Up (2024), Crossing (2025), Ciels (2025, Grand Palais), Nimbus (opening 14 February 2026, running to 7 March 2027). The through-line from 2007 to 2026 is unbroken: music written from a space, then music played with a space.

## The program, in his own words

Three sentences carry the program, and they are all about direction -- the sound goes out from the image, not down onto it. First, the formula, repeated verbatim in the press kit and in Dezeen: each Ecume track was composed as "a response to place and space, to light and architecture, to code and motion". Second, the ambition, from his About: "a capacity to project an image sonically, Thomas produces striking, emotional and physical pieces, consistently trying to extend visual experiences into physical ones". Third, the Oaxaca statement, the fullest account of method found in any source read: "We approached this idea of a garden trail as a dream. Even though each piece could work separately, it was important for us to build the trail as a journey, so that people might enter and exit with a sense of continuity. To give them the impression that they never left the dream." The trail's last piece, Replica, "acts as a recollection, using sonic textures and musical parts previously heard along the trail" -- recapitulation as memory, a musical device doing the trail's narrative work. And the 5.1 surround set-up in Onion Skin "accentuates the physical dimension and creates volume to this perspective". Sound here is architecture: it builds the rooms, the corridor, and the exit.

The recurring move, stated three times in three projects, is to adopt the subject's point of view and let the music be what that subject would hear. The peeling's axis: the Onion Skin score is computed from the peelings (M1). The building's megalomania: "We can listen to Omicron's music as the megalomaniac phantasm of [Max] Berg. It is orchestral and arrogant with a desire to occupy the space and to last in time. It is inspired by noise music, which is made of sound from object and machines and anchored in the futuristic movement at the beginning of the century. It is also tinged with electronic sounds, including a Theremin, the first electronic instrument created 6 years after the construction of this building and symbol of future times at that time." Take the building's date (1913), take the instruments invented just after it (theremin, 1919-20), take the art movement of its moment (futurism, noise music), and write the building's own ambition as orchestral arrogance. The machine's elevator: "The music of Out of Control is an elevator music. An elevator with a human voice, accompanied by ersatz jazz and baroque. It's the music of a machine that becomes aware of the protocols that impose its condition, the music of a machine that discovers the impermanence of the body that constitutes it."

The Ecume track notes -- eight miniature poetics, each his own one-paragraph description -- are the closest thing to a statement of his musical imagination: Crabe -- "about madness. About that animal we all have inside ourselves, that we keep an eye on, channel and contain. About a creature that could grow at any moment, invading us"; Ether -- the Greek divinity above the skies, "that feeling of dizziness, the asphyxiation that we feel when faced with the immensity of the world"; Ecume -- "like an oil stain lost in the middle of the sea waves, left by a boat slowly finding its way to the depths of the ocean"; Foret Blanche -- "the seemingly motionless struggle between a tree and the stalactites crushing it"; Corde -- "the last breaths of a hanged man"; Peigne -- "the sound of a stick bouncing off a fence. The sound of the seconds growing slower or quicker depending on whether we walk or run"; Otarie -- "a Danse Macabre. One of the ocean's depths. One of a school of fish dancing around a sea lion"; Diese -- "the sound of light... four visions of this energy. Sometimes warm and comforting, sometimes cold and blinding, sometimes colorful and joyful, sometimes twinkling and piercing." Time, pressure, drowning, slowness, light-as-sound: the recurring materials are duration, weight, and the body under constraint. Dedication, on his own site: "Ecume est pour la personne qui m'est si chere et qui aimait tant la mer." -- "Ecume is for the person so dear to me who loved the sea so much."

## The turn: from the body and the building to the voice and the archive, 2008-2026

The early manner is documentary and site-bound. Songdo (2009) is the first large-scale outdoor mapping score, and the method is fixed there: three elements, deliberately mismatched -- purely electronic sounds, samples of traditional Korean music, and percussion and drones recorded ON the building itself. "I auscultated the building with my microphones and I collected a multitude of samples: impacts on the metal of the wall, friction and twisting of sheets, sounds of wind rushing into the asperities of the building still under construction." Mixed in 6.1, the music surrounded the square. The auscultation -- listening to a structure with microphones as a doctor listens to a body -- becomes his signature site method: the same move returns in Cityscape 2095 (balcony recordings) and in Perche (post-war field recordings). Tour des Convoyeurs (2009) is the same method turned to memory: "Mainly made with sounds of boats and harbors, the music of this piece is like an echo of a time when the tower was still in activity." The building hears what it used to hear. Grote Kerk (2009) asks the room's question out loud: "How does it sound to explode a violin and an organ in that huge reverb?" St Gervais (2010) answers with measurement: the music "was composed inside the church, including the very special acoustics of this place in the creative process (about 8 seconds of reverberation time)". The reverb is not an effect, it is the form.

The middle years, 2011-2013, are the system years -- the AntiVJ canon, one sonic idea per piece, stated as a rule. 3Destruct: from basic sound material (sinusoids and white noises), "I built the major part of the elements that made up this soundtrack", composed for 6 speakers and 1 sub, "adapted each time, arranged according to the place of exhibition and mixed on site". Omicron: orchestra as megalomania plus theremin. Le Bestiaire: a Ravel sample, Mickey-Moused -- every visual event gets a musical gesture, cartoon-style, "a dance macabre, the one of the skeletons dancing on a phantom boat from Europe to America". DELTA: a Shepard tone for an infinite dive. Fragment in Space: 44 different sounds played and processed by VVVV, one sound for each fragment, "played randomly according to several principles of triggering" -- the composition is a set of compatibility rules, every possible combination must work. Cityscape 2095: 24 hours of skyline ambience compressed into ten minutes, "a lot of sounds recorded on my balcony in Brussels at different periods of the day and the year". Onion Skin: three viewpoints, three note-series, every peeling sounding according to its colour, speed and repetition count. Paleodictyon: two parts written in mirror, "the idea of sound masses, the idea of representing musically the movement of a multitude of fish, plankton, and sea creatures, all these animals interacting together like liquids masses" -- Plancton, the abyss; Otarie, just below the surface, "a sea lion playing in a school of fishes".

The years 2014-2018 give the sound a character and then an object. Out of Control is the first character-score: the music is not about a place but about a consciousness, a machine discovering it has a body. Last Breath of a Hanged Man (2014) stages a condemned man's last walk as a walk through sound itself, in 4D Sound: sound "can move infinitely far away or come intimately close: it moves around, as well as above, beneath, in between or right through you... You can move between blocks of sound, touch lines of sound and walk through walls of sound". Dry Lights (2015) is one sixteenth-note pulse, growing until it eats the whole soundscape: "a simple sixteenth note that accompanies all the images. As it goes along, it gets bigger, enriches its timbre and ends up absorbing all the other sounds of nature." Flux Europe (2018) is the opposite growth: "a part of the European musical heritage carved, mixed, scattered, recomposed. It's a glitch of a classic piece lost in the flow of communication networks." Then the retrospective turn: Ecume (2016) gives installation music an independent life and a physical body in concrete -- Jacquet used Geilfus's visualisation tool to turn the waveforms into topographical imagery, 3D-printed it, made a silicone mould, cast it in concrete, and photographed it under light: "a photography of Ecume". The Ether video (2018) makes sound visible through a home-made micro lens, travelling inside physical representations of the composition, "from a concrete mountain built from the spectrogram to eruptions of metal powder caused by rhythmic impulses".

The recent arc, 2019-2026, is warmer, more melodic and more vocal than the early work. The Atomium scores get texts (Stephane Beauverger, all three of Talk, A Circular Journey, Symbol), voices (Mellow Levy; Alice George Perez; Valerian Meunier and Harry Pane) and fanfares: Talk "starts with a few tears, the kind that make the skin slip from an imprisoning shed. Then it's the release of energy... Finally, exaltation, a burst of light that burns the belly and electrifies the limbs"; A Circular Journey's music "is like a game, a song for the hands, the beginning of a fanfare of hope and joy"; Symbol is "as many voices as it is questions about the right direction to take. It's as many rhythms and melodies as there are paths to take." Restart (2023) stacks three eras of popular music at once -- "the accordion of the fairground shows of yesteryear, the psychedelic rock of the hallucinatory wanderings of the '70s, it's a space-opera". Central (2023) is "a series of waiting-room paintings. Familiar, soothing, gentle, colorful, empty, solitary, and strange." Look Up (2024): "Rest assured, your trip will go smoothly, in color and in major." Crossing (2025) is "a work for cymbals and cello. The string serves as its backbone, the bronze as the shimmering reflections of its shell, and the bow breathes life into this creature of light." Ciels (2025) matches metals to metals: "the sound of the steel lattice framing the building to blend with the brass of the trombones, French horns, and saxophones, the bronze of the bells, and the aluminum of the snare drum." Nimbus (2026) builds from a text drawn from a 1957 documentary on the Atomium's construction, "adapted and recited in several languages". Sarabande in Time of Crisis (2025) returns to the sacred register of St Gervais and Clair Obscure as choral music in space: "early sacred compositions meet contemporary writing... from Hildegard von Bingen to Johan Duijck... Voices resonate within a spatial sound design". The method is unchanged -- match the sound to the material, the place, the structure -- but the materials are now instruments and voices rather than noise and sine tones. And the late albums re-hear the whole catalogue as archive: Faune and 5 Bagatelles is "a series of small sound landscapes populated by hysterical animals and damaged instruments", three tracks reworked from installation scores; Slight Echo "tells the story of our humanity which gradually blends into its own inventions, sometimes becoming one, sometimes interdependent entities, suspicious and concerned about each other. Slight Echo is a song, a mantra, a line of code, a protocol."

## M1 -- The translation table: visual parameters in, sound out

The mechanism that names the whole practice. Onion Skin's score is not written to accompany the peeling; it is computed from it. Three perspectives -- front, left, right -- each correspond to a series of notes. Each peeling sounds depending on its colour, speed and number of repetitions. The 5.1 surround set-up "accentuates the physical dimension of the installation, strengthening and playing with the impression of volume and space". Ratsi's own statement gives the full six-term rule: shape, speed, appearance, interaction, decomposition on the line-of-sight axis. DELTA is the same mechanism reduced to one effect: a Shepard tone -- a stack of octaves that seems to rise forever without ever getting higher -- is the sonic twin of the anamorphic triangle, both illusions of infinity built from a fixed structure. The sound dives while the image recedes; neither arrives anywhere. Fragment in Space is the same mechanism made generative: 44 sounds, one per fragment, triggered randomly by VVVV under compatibility rules, so the piece is never played the same way twice and always holds together. The translation table is a contract: pick the visual parameters, bind each one to a sonic parameter, and let the image play the score.

**Takeaway:** a score computed from the image's own parameters cannot drift out of sync with it; the translation table is the strongest form of synchronisation, because there is nothing to synchronise.

## M2 -- Auscultation: record the site, compose from what it gives back

The site method, and the one that never leaves the practice. Songdo: microphones on the building's metal, friction and twisting of sheets, wind in the asperities of a building still under construction. Tour des Convoyeurs: boats and harbour ambience, composed into an echo of the tower's working past. Grote Kerk: a violin and an organ exploded inside seconds of church decay. St Gervais: the 8-second reverb measured and composed with, electronics set against the organ inside the room's own tail. Cityscape 2095: the balcony outside his own flat, at different hours and seasons, standing in for a whole imaginary city. Perche: post-war French countryside field recordings -- conversations, horse hitches, tractor engines -- used as the landscape the instruments play in. The composer is a listener first: he goes to the site with microphones before he goes with instruments, and the site's own sounds become the material. The auscultation is also an ethics: the building is played as an instrument before it is finished, and its past is allowed to answer.

**Takeaway:** a place recorded honestly will give you a score no invention can match; put the microphones on the building before you put the notes on it.

## M3 -- One idea, one system: the rule stated, then obeyed

The AntiVJ canon is a shelf of single-idea scores, each one a rule stated and then obeyed for the whole piece. 3Destruct: sinusoids and white noise, moved through 6.1, re-mixed on site for every room. DELTA: one Shepard tone. Fragment in Space: 44 sounds under generative triggering rules. Dry Lights: one sixteenth-note pulse that grows until it absorbs every other sound in the landscape. Flux Europe: one classical fragment, glitched and scattered through the network. L'Orage: six channels of forest, trees, insects and birds that begin as separate sounds and gradually start to converse, then to narrate. The rule is always small enough to state in one sentence and strict enough to generate the whole piece; the variation comes from the system running, not from the composer adding more ideas. This is why the scores travel: a rule re-tuned to a new room is still the same piece, and 3Destruct could tour from Louvain-la-Neuve in 2007 to Berlin Atonal in 2013 without being rewritten.

**Takeaway:** one strict rule, run to its limit, outlasts ten loose ideas; the system is the composition, and the room is its second performance.

## M4 -- The building's biography as score: write what the place would hear

The method for the grand commissions: take the place's own history and write the music it would hear. Omicron takes the Hala Stulecia's date (1913), its architect's ambition (Max Berg's dome, the largest reinforced-concrete structure in the world when it opened), the art movement of its moment (futurism, noise music) and the instrument invented just after it (the theremin, 1919-20), and writes the building's megalomania as orchestral arrogance. The Jay Z x Barneys installation takes New York's vertical journey -- "a sound trail from the depths of the New York's suburbs to the heights of the city center" -- and climbs it in quadriphonic field recordings: empty metro station, desert suburb, downtown street, skyscraper ambiances, DJ Mustard beats mixed in as the trip's cargo, the whole soundtrack mixed on site on a 9.1 surround system. Nimbus takes a text from a 1957 documentary on the Atomium's construction and recites it in several languages, the building remembering its own making. The Chateau des ducs de Bretagne takes the forests that surrounded the castle: "the spirit of trees telling their memories... the seemingly motionless struggle between a tree and the stalactites crushing it". In every case the score is not about the place; it is the place's own story, told in the place's own materials.

**Takeaway:** a building's biography is a readymade score; date it, name its ambition, find the instrument invented just after it, and the music writes itself.

## M5 -- The machine's point of view: give the subject a voice

The character scores, where the music belongs to a consciousness rather than a place. Out of Control is the purest form: elevator music with a human voice, ersatz jazz and baroque, for a machine that "becomes aware of the protocols that impose its condition" and "discovers the impermanence of the body that constitutes it". The joke is the concept -- the Singularity scored as lift music -- and the human voice inside the Muzak is the machine's first symptom of selfhood. Last Breath of a Hanged Man gives the condemned man three movements -- the crowd, the scaffold, the soul rising -- and stages them as a walk through blocks and walls of sound in 4D Sound, the listener moving between them. L'Orage gives the forest a voice: trees, insects and birds that "seem to communicate gradually, starting to tell a story little by little", the anti-spectacle stated in the project text itself ("Architectural projection is often connected to the idea of being spectacular -- but not this time"). Talk gives the shedding body three acts -- tears, release, exaltation. The move is always the same: adopt the subject's point of view, find the music that subject would hear, and let the banality of that music -- lift Muzak, a pulse, a forest -- carry the strangeness.

**Takeaway:** a machine's Muzak is stranger than any noise; adopt the subject's point of view and the most ordinary music becomes the most uncanny.

## M6 -- Sound made physical: the waveform as an object

The reversal at the centre of the practice: instead of music responding to an image, the sound becomes the image. Ecume is the proof. Geilfus's visualisation tool analyses the waveforms into topographical imagery; Jacquet explores circular and linear visualisations; the sonic landscapes are 3D-printed, silicone-moulded, cast in concrete, and photographed under the right light. The album cover -- black concrete, a blazing white circular sunburst of radiating ridged lines -- is the waveform made topography. The Ether video does the same at the other scale: a camera with a home-made micro lens travels inside physical representations of the composition, from a concrete mountain built from the spectrogram to eruptions of metal powder caused by rhythmic impulses. Last Breath of a Hanged Man does it in space rather than in matter: in 4D Sound the listener walks through walls of sound, touches lines of sound, moves between blocks of sound -- the sound field is navigable architecture. The label's statement is the aesthetic in one sentence: sound's "capacity... to feed the imagination" without visual justification. The sound does not need the image; the image needs the sound, to have something to be made of.

**Takeaway:** when the sound is strong enough to be cast in concrete, the image stops being the work's illustration and becomes its photograph.

## M7 -- The suite of portraits: one energy, several characters

The recurring form: one energy, several characters, each given its own movement. Enghien (2009): "4 musical paintings for 4 visual artists" -- one movement per artist, the score a suite of four portraits. Light Sculpture: "the sound of light... four visions of this energy. Sometimes warm and comforting, sometimes cold and blinding, sometimes colorful and joyful, sometimes twinkling and piercing." Onion Skin: three perspectives, three note-series, the chapters revealing first the geometry of the module, then the geometry of the room, then a third perspective of two interlocking triangles. Ecume: eight pieces, each a derivation of a site-specific composition, each with its own poetics -- the crab, the ether, the foam, the white forest, the rope, the comb, the sea lion, the sharp. Faune and 5 Bagatelles: "a series of small sound landscapes populated by hysterical animals and damaged instruments. It's the patrol of the elephants, the raft of the medusa, the ugly little duck, the dance of the mice between dog and wolf, the faune one afternoon in the meadows and a few bagatelles in addition." The suite is how he handles plurality without losing unity: one energy (light, the peeling, the sea, the Atomium) and as many characters as it has temperatures, viewpoints, animals or movements. The composer is a portraitist of energies.

**Takeaway:** one energy with several characters is a stronger form than one idea with several variations; give each temperature of the light its own movement.

## M8 -- The archive as work: old scores re-heard as songs, mantras, protocols

The late form, and the one that closes the practice into a loop. Ecume (2016) is the first retrospective album: installation music given an independent life as an album, eight pieces that "were originally composed for site specific and installation work, always in close relationship with the images and the setting where the projects were taking place", then adapted to stand alone. Faune and 5 Bagatelles reworks three installation scores as animal fables -- "Peindre avec les chats" from Enghien, "Chien pirate" from Le Bestiaire, "Le Phasme et l'orage" from L'Orage -- plus "Mouton crecelle" from a documentary on Mecaniques Discursives. Slight Echo gathers ten years of Atomium collaborations into one album-length argument: "tells the story of our humanity which gradually blends into its own inventions, sometimes becoming one, sometimes interdependent entities, suspicious and concerned about each other. Slight Echo is a song, a mantra, a line of code, a protocol." The Atomium run itself is an archive in slow motion: fourteen years in one building, the same team, the same writer, the scores returning as tracks -- Act and Leave from Out of Control, Return from A Circular Journey, Learn from Symbol. The mechanism is recapitulation as method: the old work is not repeated, it is re-heard, and the re-hearing is the new work. The Oaxaca trail already knew this: Replica, its last piece, "acts as a recollection, using sonic textures and musical parts previously heard along the trail".

**Takeaway:** an archive re-heard is a new work; let the old scores return as songs, mantras and protocols, and the catalogue becomes the composition.

## The aesthetic logic: the sound is the image, played backwards

The whole practice is one question, asked in eight different materials: what does this place, this image, this machine sound like -- and what happens when the sound is allowed to answer back? The answer is always a translation, never an accompaniment. In the early work the translation is documentary: the building is auscultated, the harbour is recorded, the reverb is measured. In the system years the translation is a rule: visual parameters in, notes out, one idea per piece. In the character years the translation is a point of view: the machine's elevator, the condemned man's walk, the forest's conversation. In the object years the translation is reversed: the sound becomes the image, cast in concrete. In the late years the translation is archival: the old scores return as songs and protocols.

Two palettes carry the arc, and they are sonic before they are visual. Noise and sine tones from 2007 to 2013 -- the documentary recordings, the sinusoids and white noise of 3Destruct, the Shepard tone, the generative rules -- a palette of materials, of the sound as a physical substance. Then notes and voices from 2014: the elevator's ersatz jazz, the cello and cymbals of Crossing, the brass matched to the steel of Ciels, the major key of Look Up, the choral voices of Sarabande -- melody and the human voice as the content itself. The break is not decorative; it is the moment the sound stops being a substance to be measured and becomes a character to be heard.

Three rules govern the work, and they are the rules a piece built from this study must obey. First, the sound is never decoration: it must be computed from the image, recorded from the site, or adopted from the subject -- a score that could belong to any image belongs to none. Second, the listener is a component, not an audience: the music is unfinished until a body stands at the aligned viewpoint, walks through the sound field, or passes through the fog of the mix. Third, the mechanism must be honest about its own support: the microphone must admit it is a microphone, the sine tone must admit it is a sine tone, the concrete must admit it is concrete -- the sound appears "as if it existed without any other support" only when the support has been designed out of the way, not when it has been hidden. A piece that plays music over an image has missed the point; a piece that lets the image play the music has found it.
