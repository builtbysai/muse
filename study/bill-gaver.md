# Bill Gaver: Study Notes

**Site:** William (Bill) Gaver is Professor of Design and co-leader of the Interaction Research Studio at Northumbria University, London, with Andy Boucher; the studio moved from Goldsmiths, University of London, in late 2021, where Gaver had been Professor of Design since 2005. Earlier he was Professor of Interaction Research at the Royal College of Art, London, 1994-2004 (the exact dates and title rest on Wikipedia alone). He took his PhD in experimental psychology at the University of California, San Diego, in 1988, for the dissertation "Everyday Listening and Auditory Icons", and applied the work as auditory icons for Apple Computer and Xerox EuroPARC. He was elected to the ACM SIGCHI CHI Academy in 2009. He is the researcher behind auditory icons and the SonicFinder (1989), Cultural Probes (1999), "Ambiguity as a Resource for Design" (CHI 2003), the Drift Table (2004) and the History Tablecloth (2006), and the later batch-deployed ludic devices: Indoor Weather Stations, Energy Babble, the Datacatcher, and the My Naturewatch Camera. Birth date and birthplace: not established -- no source read this run states them. [C38820]

**Relevance:** (1) Gaver helps investigate what happens when a tool's interpretation of a gesture differs from what the hand meant -- when ambiguity is a resource to be designed with, not an error to be corrected. (2) The evidence is specific technique, not biography: his CHI 2003 paper "Ambiguity as a Resource for Design" (with Beaver and Benford) frames ambiguity explicitly as a design resource, and his ludic artefacts -- the Drift Table, the History Tablecloth, the Key Table -- embody it as pictures whose meaning is deliberately underdetermined. [C38821]

**Doorway:** The 467th doorway. Takes up the open question from the Brad Myers study (#846), quoted verbatim: "the system's first guess is right about 80 percent of the time, which means it is wrong about 20 percent of the time, and the correction is always the user's job -- so what happens when the tool's interpretation of a gesture differs from what the hand meant, when the misreading is not an error to be corrected but something the piece is built to hold open, and who gets to decide what the gesture meant?" Gaver answers it, and his answer is a reversal of Myers's. Peridot asks whether its guess is right and waits for the correction; Gaver's artefacts never ask, never correct, and never resolve. The Drift Table cannot be steered to a destination, the History Tablecloth's tracking errors are read by the household as character, the Key Table's force-to-tilt mapping was never communicated to its volunteers at all -- and in each case the misreading is not a failure to be repaired but the thing the piece is for. The decision about what a gesture meant is not taken by the system, and it is not handed back to the user as a correction task either: it is left open, and the piece is built to stay open. The open question carried forward is the one the open reading leaves standing: when ambiguity must resolve into a single usable action -- when the playful reading meets a tool that has to be learned, used, and trusted -- what survives of the openness, and who does the resolving, the hand or the tool? [C38822]

## M1 -- A sound that means by analogy: everyday listening as mapping

**Subject:** A file being selected, dragged, opened, or thrown away, and the everyday sound that stands for it.

**Visual principle:** Analogy made visible. Each event is a small mark whose shape echoes a real-world action (a tap, a slide, a drop into a bin); the correspondence is the composition, not decoration. Rhythm comes from the sequence of actions: a busy sequence is a busy soundscape, a sparse one is nearly silent.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from the SonicFinder and auditory icons (PhD 1988; SonicFinder 1989; "Synthesizing Auditory Icons" 1993). Everyday sounds are mapped onto computer events by analogy, so selecting, dragging, opening, or binning a file makes a corresponding real-world sound, and later work parameterised the sounds so one icon could carry dimensional information. The executable rule is a mapping table plus a parameter rule: event type selects the sound family (impact, scrape, pour, thud); event parameters (object size, distance moved, speed) scale pitch, duration, and loudness by fixed functions; simultaneous events are mixed, never queued. This study's formalisation: a field of 12 draggable tokens, each drag emitting its analogy sound and leaving a fading trace whose length encodes the drag distance; the mapping table and the parameter functions are fixed, the sequence is the viewer's.

**Artistic opportunity:** The piece is heard as much as seen, and the hearing is the meaning: the interface stops being silent machinery and becomes a small room of real things. Distinctive because the sound is not an effect laid over a picture but the picture's own logic, stated in sound.

**Takeaway:** The sound is the meaning -- an action sounds like what it is. [C38824]

## M2 -- Drifting, not navigating: weight steers a slow view

**Subject:** A small round window onto a vast aerial landscape, moved only by shifting weight across a table.

**Visual principle:** Scale contrast and slowness. A tiny porthole against a large quiet surface; the landscape inside it moves slowly and continuously, never jumping. The eye stays inside the small circle while the world slides past it, so the composition is a still table with a moving world trapped in a hole.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from the Drift Table (CHI 2004). A coffee table about a metre square carries a small porthole viewport showing slowly scrolling aerial photography of England and Wales (about a terabyte donated by getmapping.com), steered by the distribution of weight on its surface, with a place-name display on the side; it was deliberately impossible to direct to a typed destination, so the only activity it supports is drifting. The executable rule: weight sensors under the four corners produce a two-dimensional weight vector; the viewport centre moves in the direction of the vector at a speed proportional to its magnitude, capped at a slow maximum, with heavy damping so the view glides and never snaps; a place-name label updates only when the centre crosses a named region boundary. This study's formalisation: the weight vector comes from drag position and pressure on the canvas (the viewer's hand is the weight); the damping constant, the speed cap, and the aerial tile set are fixed.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture that refuses the destination. The pleasure is the drift itself -- the long, unhurried slide over fields, roads, and coasts -- and the smallness of the window makes the vastness felt rather than shown.

**Takeaway:** You cannot steer to anywhere; you can only lean, and the land slides by. [C38825]

## M3 -- Six hours of light: dwell as accumulation

**Subject:** Objects left on a table, and the halo of light each one slowly earns.

**Visual principle:** Density by duration. Brightness is time made visible: a newly placed object is dark, a long-sitting one glows, and the table becomes a map of attention and neglect. Layering is temporal: halos overlap where objects sat near each other, and the brightest patches are the places nothing disturbed for longest.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from the History Tablecloth (DIS 2006). A flexible substrate screen-printed with electroluminescent material in a lace-like grid of 95 independently controllable cells sits over a table whose legs rest on load cells; objects left on it grow a halo of light over about six hours, and removing them fades the halo in about 30 seconds. The executable rule: each cell holds a charge value 0-1; charge rises linearly while weight is present above that cell (full in about 6 hours) and decays exponentially on removal (near zero in about 30 seconds); cell brightness is charge, with a small spread to neighbouring cells so halos are soft, not square. This study's formalisation: time is compressed (full charge in 90 seconds of dwell, fade in 10 seconds) so the mechanism is visible in one sitting; the 95-cell grid, the rise and decay rates, and the neighbour spread are fixed; the viewer places and removes the objects.

**Artistic opportunity:** A still life that remembers. The glow is a record of staying, and the table quietly accuses or honours whatever was left there -- decorative objects unmoved for years, in the deployment, glowed longest. It is a picture of time, drawn in light.

**Takeaway:** Light is how long something stayed. [C38826]

## M4 -- The misreading is the character: error kept, not fixed

**Subject:** A system that misreads its input -- a missed lift-off, a floor vibration, a glass that lights only when filled -- and a household that treats the misreading as personality.

**Visual principle:** Tension between intent and result. The picture shows both layers at once: the objects as placed, and the system's reading of them, slightly offset, slightly wrong, in a different weight of line. The gap between the two layers is the composition; when they agree the picture is calm, when they disagree it is alive.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from the History Tablecloth deployment and the Key Table. In the Tablecloth's four-month deployment, tracking errors (missed lift-offs, floor vibration, cells stuck lit) were read by the householders as interactive richness, not failure, and a wine glass missed on placement lit up once wine was poured in; the Key Table's force-to-tilt mapping was inadvertently never communicated to volunteers, who developed their own interpretations of the frame's behaviour, which the later papers treat as a happy accident in favour of interpretative appropriation. The executable rule: a sensor reading passes through a noisy channel (a fixed miss rate, a vibration threshold, a hysteresis band) before it drives the display; the display state is a function of the noisy reading, never of the true state; no correction step exists. This study's formalisation: the miss rate (about 1 in 8 placements), the hysteresis band, and the display mapping are fixed parameters; the viewer places objects and watches the system's reading diverge from what they did.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture with a temperament. The system's small wrongnesses are the only thing that makes it feel alive rather than instrumented, and the viewer starts to play with the misreading itself -- placing things to see what the table thinks happened.

**Takeaway:** The table is wrong sometimes, and that is why it is a cat, not a machine. [C38827]

## M5 -- The confident wrong reading: over-interpretation as a tactic

**Subject:** A home's sensor readings -- light, temperature, door state, hairbrush strokes, condensation -- turned into a daily horoscope for the household.

**Visual principle:** Confidence against thin evidence. A single bold sentence sits over a field of faint, fragmentary sensor traces; the sentence claims far more than the traces support, and the contrast between the assertive text and the sparse evidence is the picture. Rhythm comes from the daily cycle: one new sentence a day, the old one fading.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from the Home Health Monitor / Superstitious Home (Equator workbook, c. 2001). Light, temperature, and door sensors, plus idiosyncratic ones (hairbrush stroke rate, condensation on kitchen windows, toilet-seat state), are mapped through a state space onto sentences drawn from published horoscopes, producing a bespoke daily horoscope for the household; it is Gaver's clearest case of over-interpretation as a tactic, where the system's confident claims invite scepticism and through that scepticism reflection. The executable rule: each sensor contributes a categorical value (high/low, open/shut, rising/falling) to a small state vector; the state vector indexes a fixed sentence bank by a deterministic hash, so the same state always yields the same sentence and a small state change yields a wholly different sentence; no confidence score is shown. This study's formalisation: five virtual sensors driven by the viewer's activity and by a slow ambient drift; the sentence bank (about 40 sentences) and the hash rule are fixed; the viewer cannot ask for a re-reading, only live with the one given.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture that lies to you on purpose, and knows you know. The pleasure is the scepticism it provokes: the viewer argues with the sentence, checks it against the traces, and in arguing starts to notice the home itself. It is the only mechanism in this set where the artwork's job is to be doubted.

**Takeaway:** The house says something grand about very little, and you argue back. [C38828]

## M6 -- Three kinds of not-knowing: ambiguity of information, context, relationship

**Subject:** One object that is two things at once -- a radio and a pillow, a display and a glow -- and a viewer who cannot settle which.

**Visual principle:** Layering and occlusion as undecidability. A soft, translucent surface sits over a sharper display, so the image is always partly veiled; the same form reads as furniture from one angle and as instrument from another. Contrast is low on purpose: nothing in the picture insists on a single reading.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from "Ambiguity as a Resource for Design" (CHI 2003) and the Pillow. Ambiguity is a property of the interpretative relationship between person and artefact, not of the artefact itself, in three kinds: ambiguity of information (source: the artefact), of context (source: the sociocultural discourses used to interpret it), and of relationship (source: the individual's evaluative stance). The Pillow (Dunne, 1997-1999) is a clear inflatable pillow enclosing a translucent block over an LCD screen, where light suffuses the object and softens the display into imprecise patterns of colour indicating passing electromagnetic information, and Gaver uses it as his standing example of all three kinds at once. The executable rule: one input stream (a drifting signal field) drives two simultaneous outputs -- a pattern layer and a sound layer -- through two different, fixed mappings that never agree exactly; a blur/veil parameter attenuates the pattern layer so detail is always partly lost; the viewer's framing choice (which layer they attend to) is the only selector, and the system never resolves it. This study's formalisation: the signal field is a slow sum of three sine fields; the two mappings and the veil strength are fixed; the viewer drags the veil thicker or thinner but can never remove it.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture that refuses to be one thing. The softness is not a failure of resolution but the point: the viewer supplies the meaning, and the same screen is a radio, a lamp, and a pillow in the same minute.

**Takeaway:** The picture stays open; the viewer finishes it, differently each time. [C38829]

## M7 -- The second window: a view displaced from its source

**Subject:** A flat screen hung beside a real window, showing the same outside from a camera on a pole -- watched more than the window itself.

**Visual principle:** Doubling and framing. Two rectangles side by side, one real and one mediated, showing nearly the same scene with small differences (a different angle, a different slice of sky, a bird the real window missed). The composition is the comparison: the eye moves between the two frames and the picture is the gap between them.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from The Video Window (2006/2009). A camera on a 6.7-metre telescopic fishing-pole mast outside Gaver's bedroom window feeds a flat screen hung beside the real window, always on, "for" nothing; the household watches it mostly mornings and evenings and often prefers it to the real window, which is partly shaded for privacy and gives each side of the bed a different view; four cameras were tried before settling on one of about 70 degrees. The executable rule: a single camera state (position, angle, zoom) renders a second view of one shared scene; the camera state drifts slowly on its own (pan, tilt, and focal length each follow slow independent cycles) and is never synchronised with the primary view; the two views share the same underlying scene state (time of day, weather, passing birds) so events appear in both, offset in framing and timing. This study's formalisation: the scene is a procedural skyline (sun path, cloud drift, birds on fixed flight paths); the camera cycles are fixed periods (pan 7 minutes, tilt 11 minutes, zoom 13 minutes); the viewer cannot steer the camera, only choose which frame to enlarge.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture about looking itself. The mediated view is preferred not because it is better but because it is framed, always on, and slightly other -- and the small differences between the two windows are where the attention lives.

**Takeaway:** The same outside, twice, and the copy is the one you watch. [C38830]

## M8 -- Upwind: direction as a query

**Subject:** The wind outside a house, and the advertisements arriving from the direction it blows from.

**Visual principle:** Direction as composition. A compass rose anchors the picture; marks arrive from the upwind sector and drift downwind across the field, so the picture's flow always has a weather-given direction. Density gathers upwind and thins downwind; the wind arrow is the only fixed element.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from the Local Barometer (c. 2007-2008). An anemometer in a volunteer household's back garden measures wind speed and direction, and an algorithm searches for online advertisements originating upwind of the home, so the household sees ads from the direction the wind is blowing from. The executable rule: a wind vector (direction, speed) is sampled on a slow cycle; the vector selects a sector of a fixed catalogue of short texts/images (each tagged with a compass bearing); selected items enter at the upwind edge with a speed proportional to wind speed and drift across the field, fading as they cross; when the wind shifts, the sector shifts and the flow re-orients. This study's formalisation: the wind follows a slow procedural weather model (direction wanders, speed gusts on a 40-second cycle); the catalogue is about 60 short found-text fragments, each tagged with a bearing; the viewer cannot choose the wind, only watch the sector turn.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture whose content is weather. The absurdity -- ads as weather vanes -- is the charm: the commercial stream becomes a natural phenomenon, and the viewer reads the wind by reading what it brings.

**Takeaway:** The wind decides what arrives; the picture is a weather vane for other people's messages. [C38831]

## M9 -- The babble: many voices mixed into one talk-radio stream

**Subject:** A tabletop appliance that talks all day about energy, in many synthesised voices, with jingles -- never telling anyone what to do.

**Visual principle:** Polyphony as texture. Many small text fragments and voice-lines overlap, interrupt, and fade, like a crowded room heard through a wall; no single voice holds the centre for long. Rhythm is the mix: a jingle marks a change of source, a pause marks a change of topic, and the density of overlap is the picture's pulse.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from Energy Babble (CHI 2015) and the Prayer Companion (CHI 2010). Energy Babble is automated talk-radio obsessed with energy, mixing Twitter feeds, government departments, and National Grid data into synthesised voices with jingles and sound effects; 21 Babbles went to UK energy communities for about six months. The Prayer Companion streams RSS headlines interleaved with social-networking excerpts to suggest prayer topics for cloistered nuns, who used it enthusiastically for ten months. The executable rule: several fixed source feeds (each a slow generator of short sentences) are sampled by a weighted round-robin mixer; the mixer emits one sentence at a time into a spoken and a written stream, inserts a jingle every N sentences, and lets sentences overlap only when two feeds fire in the same tick; the viewer can add one sentence (typed or spoken) to a single feed, which the mixer then treats like any other source. This study's formalisation: four feeds, fixed weights, a jingle every 7 sentences; the written stream is the visible picture (fragments scrolling and fading), the spoken stream is optional sound.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture made of other people's words, mixed until no one owns the message. The babble provokes discussion precisely because it never instructs; the viewer supplies the judgement the machine withholds.

**Takeaway:** Many voices, one stream, no instructions -- and the room starts talking. [C38832]

## M10 -- The place talks back: location-anchored data as a stream

**Subject:** A handheld device that, wherever its carrier stands, speaks about that place -- its politics, its history, its data.

**Visual principle:** Place as a moving frame. The picture is a strip of statements scrolling past a fixed marker for "here"; as the location moves, the statements change, so the composition is a journey through text. Contrast comes from the statements themselves: some are dry statistics, some are provocations, and the mix is the texture.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from the Datacatcher (CHI 2016). Datacatchers are custom-built, location-aware devices streaming messages about the area they are in, drawn from many data sources and speaking to sociopolitical issues; the paper reports a field trial of 130 bespoke devices, the studio's largest deployment. The executable rule: a location value indexes a spatial database of short statements (each tagged with a place and a topic); as location moves, the active set is recomputed by distance; statements are emitted one at a time in a fixed rotation (nearest first, then by topic round-robin), each displayed for a fixed dwell before the next; the location itself is the only input. This study's formalisation: the "map" is a procedural terrain of about 40 named districts with fixed statement sets; the carrier (the viewer's drag) moves the location marker, and the stream follows; dwell is 6 seconds per statement.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture that makes data local. The same stream in a different district is a different picture, and the viewer learns a place by what the device chooses to say about it -- including what it chooses not to say.

**Takeaway:** Where you stand decides what the device says. [C38833]

## M11 -- Returns, not data: the probe kit as a fragment machine

**Subject:** A kit of maps, postcards, and a disposable camera, sent out and returned by post as fragments -- a dream in ten seconds, a photo of something red, a map of friends drawn on Dante's heaven and hell.

**Visual principle:** Fragmentation as composition. Small, mismatched pieces -- a torn map edge, a postcard sentence, a photo caption -- are scattered across the field with gaps between them; the gaps are part of the picture. No piece explains another; the eye jumps between them and supplies the connections.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from Cultural Probes (1999) and the Domestic Probes kit (2003-2004). Cultural Probes were packages of maps, postcards, cameras, and booklets given to elderly people in Oslo, the Bijlmer, and Peccioli to provoke inspirational responses, not information; returns were fragmentary clues, not data, and Gaver insists probe returns are not analysed, counted, or turned into personas or scenarios, because analysis replaces the raw material with a representation. The Domestic Probes kit sent a repackaged disposable camera with absurd requests, a friends-and-family map drawn on Dante's heaven and hell, and a Dream Recorder giving ten seconds to describe a dream on waking, with no playback or editing. The executable rule: a fixed set of prompt cards (about 12) is dealt one at a time; each prompt accepts exactly one short response (text, or a placed mark, or a ten second recording window that then closes); responses are stored verbatim and displayed as fragments in arrival order, never counted, summarised, or clustered; the display shuffles fragment positions on each new arrival but never edits a fragment. This study's formalisation: the 12 prompts are this study's own, written in Gaver's manner (absurd, personal, unanswerable by data); the viewer answers three or four, and the piece is the fragments they left.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture that is mostly other people's words, kept raw. The refusal to analyse is the art: the fragments stay stubbornly themselves, and the viewer reads a life in them that no summary could hold.

**Takeaway:** The returns are the piece; nothing is done to them. [C38834]

## M12 -- Sporadic affection: a device that disappoints, then lingers

**Subject:** Three small devices that reveal a home's microclimate -- a gust, a colour of light, a temperature difference -- and are first judged disappointing, then kept with affection.

**Visual principle:** Smallness and patience. Three modest instruments, each showing one thin trace (a flicker, a colour shift, a slow gradient), against a large quiet field. The traces are barely there most of the time; the picture rewards looking slowly, and the devices' modesty is the composition.

**Computational mechanism:** Gaver's own, from Indoor Weather Stations (CHI 2013). Three batch-produced devices (Wind Tunnel, Temperature Tape, Light Collector) make the home's microclimate perceptible -- small gusts of wind, the colour of ambient light, temperature differentials across a five-metre span; twenty households lived with them for several months, first finding them disappointing against either a tool or a toy, then keeping a lingering affection, a pattern the authors call sporadic interaction. The executable rule: three slow ambient signals (wind gust, light colour temperature, temperature gradient) are sampled continuously; each drives one minimal display (a ribbon that stirs, a patch that shifts hue, a bar that tilts) with heavy smoothing, so the display changes only when the signal genuinely moves; a "sporadic" timer fires a small event (a flutter, a blink) at random intervals of 2-9 minutes, independent of the signals. This study's formalisation: the three signals are procedural (gusts on a 50-second cycle, light following a day curve, gradient drifting with a slow random walk); the smoothing constants and the sporadic timer are fixed; the viewer does nothing but watch, and the piece is built to be left running.

**Artistic opportunity:** A picture that asks for almost nothing and gives back almost nothing -- and is loved for it. The sporadic flutter is the whole drama: a small sign that the house is breathing, noticed only because the viewer happened to be looking.

**Takeaway:** Almost nothing happens, and that is the point; the flutter is the event. [C38835]

## The arc: from the ear, to the living room, to the street

Gaver's career reads as one long move across three questions, and the move is the through-line. First, "how do we hear what things mean" (1986-1993): the PhD on everyday listening and auditory icons, the SonicFinder, EuroPARC media spaces -- the ear as an interface, analogy as the mapping. Second, "how does a thing mean in a home" (1999-2010): the move to the RCA replaces measurement with provocation -- Cultural Probes, the Alternatives value fictions, the Presence Project, then the ludic furniture, one-off and highly finished, loaned to single households for months and evaluated by ethnography and documentary film rather than hypothesis testing, with Gaver's own home as a laboratory in the Video Window. The theory of this period -- ambiguity (2003), staying open to interpretation (2006), cultural commentators as polyphonic assessment (2007) -- is a sustained refusal of the single authoritative interpretation. Third, "how does a place mean, at scale" (2013-2024): batch production replaces one-offs (20 Indoor Weather Stations, 21 Energy Babbles, 130 Datacatchers), the topics turn outward to environment, energy, and sociopolitical data, and dissemination turns DIY (My Naturewatch cameras built by strangers, ProbeTools/TaskCam open-source probe cameras, Yo-Yo Machines as self-build pandemic communication devices). The 2024 "Designing with Data: An Annotated Portfolio" looks back over two decades of the studio's data-driven designs -- a retrospective turn the early work never attempted. What changed technically: from sound, to weight and light, to data streams and batch deployment. What did not change: interpretation handed to the user. The SonicFinder lets a sound mean something by analogy; the Drift Table lets a landscape mean something by drifting; the Datacatcher lets a place mean something by talking about it. The mechanism changes; the move -- refusing to dictate the meaning, and building the device so the user must supply it -- does not. This arc paragraph is an interpretation built from legs 1-2, not Gaver's own words. [C38837]

## The aesthetic logic

Gaver's aesthetic is the object that withholds its meaning on purpose, and the withholding is the design. The Drift Table is a coffee table with a hole in it; the History Tablecloth is a lace grid that glows where things sat; the Key Table is an entrance-hall table that tilts a picture frame for reasons nobody explained; the Video Window is a screen beside a window showing the same outside, always on, for nothing. None of them explains itself, and that is the point: a device that explained itself would be a tool, and these are provocations. The most Gaver-like visual in the whole study is not any one object: it is the gap between what the household did and what the device did back -- the glass that lights only when the wine is poured, the frame that swings for a reason the volunteers invented themselves, the slogan hidden by the person sitting on the bench. And the honest boundary is stated on the surface too: ambiguity is a property of the interpretative relationship, not of the artefact, so the same device is a radio and a pillow and a voyeur's instrument in the same minute, and no reading is the right one.
