**Site:** Tomas Saraceno (b. 1973, San Miguel de Tucuman, Argentina; living), transdisciplinary artist working across architecture, science and Traditional Ecological Knowledge, whose projects are floating sculptures, international collaborations and interactive installations -- Cloud Cities (2002-), Museo Aero Solar (2007-), Aerocene (2015-) and Arachnophilia, the spider/web research community -- built with spiders, scientists and Indigenous communities as co-authors rather than subjects; lives and works in Berlin. **Doorway:** Hop from the Olafur Eliasson study: Eliasson builds the weather in the room and lets the viewer walk inside it, so the work exists only while a body is standing under it -- the sun, the fog tunnel, the rainbow that only appears at the angle of your own eyes. Tomas Saraceno was born in 1973 in San Miguel de Tucuman, Argentina. He studied architecture at the Universidad Nacional de Buenos Aires, 1992-1999, then took postgraduate degrees from the Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes de la Nacion Ernesto de la Carcova, Buenos Aires (2000), and the Staatliche Hochschule fuer Bildende Kuenste -- Staedelschule, Frankfurt am Main (2003), where he studied under Thomas Bayrle and Ben van Berkel under director Daniel Birnbaum. In 2003-2004 he took the course "Progettazione e Produzione delle Arti Visive" (Design and Production of Visual Arts) at the Instituto Universitario di Architettura di Venezia (IUAV), held by Hans Ulrich Obrist and Olafur Eliasson -- the documented teacher-student contact that anchors this study's doorway. He showed first at the Venice Biennale of Architecture in 2002 and the Venice Arte Biennale in 2003, founded his studio in Frankfurt in 2005, took the International Space Studies Program at NASA Ames in 2009 (the year he won the Calder Prize), and relocated the studio to Berlin in 2012, to the former AGFA administrative building in Berlin-Rummelsburg, a chemically contaminated former photographic film and dye factory site whose soil is the subject of his "Silent Spring" (2022), pressed poppies from the studio grounds. From 2014 to 2016 he directed the Institute of Architecture-related Art (IAK) at Braunschweig University of Technology; he was artist in residence at CNES, the French space agency's Observatoire de l'Espace, in 2014-2015, and has been MIT CAST Visiting Artist since 2012. [C29144]

The studio today is a team of twenty or more -- designers, architects, anthropologists, biologists, engineers, art historians, writers, musicians -- working in a hybrid studio environment, and it runs three standing communities rather than a catalogue of objects: Museo Aero Solar (open-source, community-built, since 2007), the Aerocene Foundation (non-profit, 2015) and Arachnophilia (the spider/web research community, with its Arachnophilia.net portal, Arachnomancy App and Mapping Against Extinction project). The career runs in long strands, not discrete objects: Cloud Cities since 2002, Museo Aero Solar since 2007, Aerocene since 2015, Arachnophilia ongoing. The honours along the way are the Calder Prize (2009), the 1822 Kunstpreis (2010), the Design Prize Golden Madonnina (2019) and the Platinum Konex Award (2022). His works are in the collections of MoMA New York, SFMOMA, the Walker Art Center, the Museum fuer Moderne Kunst Frankfurt, the Nationalgalerie Berlin, Hamburger Bahnhof, Mudam Luxembourg and Bonniers Konsthall, among others. He lives and works in Berlin. [C29145]

## M1 -- Solar-thermal lift: the sun is the engine, and there is no other engine

Every flying thing Saraceno makes runs on the same physics, and the physics is the point. A dark envelope absorbs solar radiation, the air inside warms, the warm air is less dense than the air outside, and the thing rises -- no burner, no helium, no hydrogen, no fuel, no solar panels, no batteries. The first version is deliberately crude: 59 Steps to Be on Air by Sun Power (2003) is a DIY solar balloon built from black garbage bags, tape, two bicycle wheel rims, an inner tube, a child's chair, string, a car radiator ventilator, a 12-volt battery and a safety harness, published as a 59-step instruction set so anyone could build one -- two people working for 24 hours, the sources say -- and the first tethered human flight in this lineage (the balloon dates to 2003; one source dates the first tethered human flight to 2006, and the discrepancy is unresolved). Museo Aero Solar (2007-) scales the same lift into a community practice: volunteers in each city bring used plastic bags, the bags are taped into an envelope, the sun heats the air inside, a ventilator helps inflation, and the balloon flies -- more than 20,000 bags collected from Colombia, Cuba, France, Germany, Italy, Austria, Palestine, Switzerland, the UAE and the USA, growing bigger at every stop because a new section is added at each city. Aerocene (2015-) is the same lift made rigorous: the D-0 AEC aerosolar sculpture, tested at White Sands Dunes, New Mexico, on 8 November 2015, lifted seven people for about 2 hours 15 minutes with a net lift of more than 250 kg, tethered, on sunlight alone -- the longest and most sustainable certified manned solar flight then registered -- and the project grew out of Saraceno's CNES residency studying the MIR (Montgolfiere Infrarouge) solar balloon, with MIT's Lodovica Illari. The mechanism is lift as ethics: the balloon proves, by flying, that the atmosphere will carry you if you stop burning things to get up there. The watchable behaviour is the inflation: the black envelope lying flat, the fan, the slow filling, the moment the sun takes over and the thing stands up by itself. Seen in W02, W03 and W11. [C29147]

**Takeaway:** heat the air with sunlight alone, and let the balloon prove the atmosphere will carry you. [C29147]

## M2 -- The visitor as load and disturbance: you are the weather in the web

Saraceno's walkable structures are not floors with a view; they are webs, and the visitor is the disturbance a spider would feel. Cloud Cities (2002-) states it first: the pods are linked, so if one sphere wobbles the movement ripples through the others, and the studio quotes the artist -- one sphere wobbling "reverberates through the others in a ripple of stress that mimics the way we react as social groups" -- so a visitor's weight in one pod affects the person in another, two people at a time in a transparent cocoon in Berlin in 2011, bouncing on an air-filled cushion and looking down through the foil. On Space Time Foam (2012) makes it architectural: three levels of thick clear plastic film, 400 square metres per level, suspended at 24 metres in HangarBicocca, each level at a different air pressure, and every step and breath changes the shape of the membrane, the levels coupled so movement on one deforms the others -- the film billows and wrinkles under bodies "like a living organism" that breathes through the people crossing it, and the artist calls it 99.9 percent air. In Orbit (2013-2024) makes it permanent: three levels of 3 mm stainless steel cable net, 2,500 square metres under the K21 glass cupola, five air-inflated PVC spheres 3.5 to 8 metres across holding the levels apart, and when several people enter at once the whole structure is set in motion -- their weight alters the tension of the cables and the distances between the levels, so the web vibrates and sags under the group, ball joints absorbing the vibration and bifurcating "branch tree" cables distributing the forces to the dome. Free the Air (2022) makes it auditory: two wire-mesh nets inside a 95-foot ovoid, one at 12 feet and one at 40 feet, and the visitor lies down while shakers and low-frequency effects send spider vibrations through the net into the body, the visitor's own presence and movement feeding back into the vibrations. The mechanism is tension as a shared medium: the structure has no rigid floor, so every body in it is both load and signal, and the web reports the group to itself. The watchable behaviour is the ripple: one person shifts, and somewhere else in the structure someone else feels it. Seen in W01, W06, W09 and W18. [C29148]

**Takeaway:** build a floor out of tension, and let every visitor feel every other visitor move. [C29148]

## M3 -- The web as instrument: pluck it, sound it, let it play you

A spider's web is a tuned structure, and Saraceno treats it as one. The pluck is demonstrated live at the Baltimore Museum of Art in 2017: in Entangled Orbits, iridescent Plexiglas clusters hang in a two-storey net of black polyester rope from twelve anchors, and the artist plucks one cord -- the vibration travels through the net and sets remote threads and crystalline forms trembling, accommodating and dispersing the disturbance, the whole web as a single vibrating system. Algo-r(h)i(y)thms (2018, first iteration as Galaxies Forming... in Venice 2009) is the instrument formalised: 3.4 kilometres of 1-2.5 mm black cord arranged in complex clusters, and the visitor plays it as a spider might, touching, sliding and stridulating -- gently plucking -- within a system of amplified strings, where each converging node is a net whose diameter encodes a reverberation frequency of a micro- or macroscopic phenomenon: a 25 cm net at 58-178 Hz, the peak courtship-signal frequencies of the orb-weaving spider Argiope keyserlingi; a 140 cm net at 0.61 GHz, the radio halo generated by ultrarelativistic electrons around a distant galaxy cloud. The name is a pun that is also a definition: Spanish "algo" (something) plus "rhythm" from Greek "rhythmos" (movement marked by regulated succession). Sounding the Air (2018) is the instrument played by the wind: five long threads of Nephila spider silk float on currents of warm air from Dedolights underneath, on the radiant heat of visitors' bodies, their breath, dust and electrostatic force, and a camera captures the threads' movements in real time and a computer translates them into fluctuating sound frequencies -- the sounds in turn influencing the pulsating lights on the Webs of At-tent(s)ion in the next room. The Cosmic Dust Spider Web Orchestra (2017) is the instrument played by everything at once: a live Nephila clavipes spider plucks its web into tiny microphones while a light beam makes cosmic dust visible and a live 3D video tracks 25 dust particles and translates their movement into sound, and the dust, the wandering visitors, the spider's plucking, the air moved by the speakers and the visitors' breath combine into a low-frequency collective composition -- "a jam session for a more-than-human world". The mechanism is vibration as a common language: the web, the spider, the dust, the visitor and the galaxy all report in frequency, and the instrument is the room that lets them hear each other. The watchable behaviour is the pluck that arrives somewhere else: touch one thread and watch a far thread answer. Seen in W14, W16 and W13. [C29149]

**Takeaway:** tune the web, pluck one thread, and let the far thread answer. [C29149]

## M4 -- Scanning the web: a laser sheet, half a millimetre at a time

Before Saraceno, nobody had a precise three-dimensional model of a complex spider web, because the fibres are too thin for CT, ultrasound or confocal microscopy. With the Photogrammetric Institute at TU Darmstadt he invented the Spider/Web Scan, a laser-supported tomographic technique, and the invention is what makes the whole middle practice possible. A web is built by spiders in a carbon Spider Web Frame; a sliding sheet laser -- originally red, 650 nm, later green, 532 nm in the MIT iteration with Markus Buehler's lab -- illuminates vertical slices of the web, 0.5 mm thick in the newer setup; one or two high-resolution cameras capture stereoscopic images of each illuminated slice; the 2D images are processed, with manual reconstruction of dense sections, into a precise 3D data model. The first successful deployment was in 2009, on the black widow web behind 14 Billions: 110 pairs of stereoscopic photogrammetric images of a Latrodectus mactans web, processed into the model that was then rebuilt by hand at sixteen or seventeen times life size -- the sources disagree, 16x per MIT Arts, 17x per MIT CEE -- in 8,000 black strings joined by over 23,000 individually tied knots, taking two years to develop, spanning 350 or 400 cubic metres (the BALTIC guide says 350, the press release 400). The scan is also the science: with Buehler's lab the models became computer simulations of web mechanics, joint papers on web structural properties, sonifications of silk proteins, a 3D web archive and a VR simulation, published in the Journal of the Royal Society Interface in 2018. The mechanism is measurement as making: the scan does not describe the web and then stop; the scan becomes the web, rebuilt at human scale, played as an instrument, modelled as a structure, sonified as music. The watchable behaviour is the slice: a sheet of light moving through darkness, each pass revealing one cross-section of something too fine to see whole. Seen in W08 and W05. [C29150]

**Takeaway:** slice the invisible with light, and rebuild what the slice reveals at human scale. [C29150]

## M5 -- Spiders as co-authors: the frame is turned, the spider decides

The Hybrid Webs are the practice's most radical move, because the artist stops making the work and starts housing the makers. Spiders of different species -- solitary, social and semi-social, which would not normally share a web -- are housed in the same carbon-fibre frame or Plexiglas case in sequence, each building on the previous spider's web, and during the building period the frame is turned onto each side, so gravity changes direction and "confuses" the spiders, and the silk interweaves in three dimensions into a hybrid architecture no single species would make, described as "a unique, geometric galaxy floating in infinite space". The spiders are then removed; what remains is the silk, presented in a glass vitrine, suspended by near-invisible wire in a darkened room and spot-lit. The titles are the labour record: "Hybrid Dark solitary semi-social Cluster BD-15 3966 built by: a duet of Nephila edulis -- six weeks, a quintet of Cyrtophora citricola -- eight weeks, rotated 180 degrees"; "Hybrid solitary semi-social semi-social SAO 90734 built by: a solo Nephila senegalensis -- one week, a duet of Cyrtophora citricola -- three weeks, a quartet of Cyrtophora citricola juvenile -- two weeks". The scale of the collaboration peaks in Buenos Aires in 2017, where about 7,000 Parawixia bistriata -- a quasi-social species, native to northern Argentina, that lives together in a single web for a period before maturity -- were housed in a gallery with only a minimal rope framework and over six months built a single vast web covering more than 190 square metres, described as the largest three-dimensional spider web ever exhibited, framed by the artist as a musical instrument with the web as instrument and the spiders as players. At ON AIR in Paris in 2018-2019, 76 hybrid webs hung spot-lit in a near-pitch-black room (450 living spiders took part in the exhibition as a whole), and the visitor's job was attention: to consider the aesthetic contributions of non-human producers. The mechanism is authorship distributed by design: the frame, the rotation and the sequence are the artist's, and everything else -- the geometry, the density, the decisions -- is the spiders'. The watchable behaviour is the title read as a timesheet: six weeks, eight weeks, rotated 180 degrees, and a web no human hand drew. Seen in W08, W13 and W16. [C29151]

**Takeaway:** build the frame, turn it, and let the spider decide the geometry. [C29151]

## M6 -- The cosmic web: galaxies strung like droplets on silk

Saraceno's founding analogy is astrophysical, and he builds it at room scale rather than stating it. Astrophysicists describe the large-scale structure of the universe as filaments with galaxies strung along them like droplets on a spider's web, and in Galaxies Forming along Filaments, like Droplets along the Strands of a Spider's Web (2008, shown at the 53rd Venice Biennale 2009) he builds that image as a room you must stoop and twist to pass through: thin elastic cables radiating from floor, walls and ceiling, criss-crossing in space and meeting in the centre to form polyhedron-shaped nuclei, clusters of bubble-vessels, thousands of strands filling the room, the elastic giving the whole thing a slight sag and tremble. 14 Billions (Working Title) (2010) is the same analogy made exact: the title is the approximate known age of the universe, about 14 billion years, and the work is a scaled-up reconstruction of a real black widow web -- scanned by laser tomography, rebuilt by hand at sixteen or seventeen times life size -- so the visitor walks through a monstrous black widow web that is also a model of the cosmic web, the two structures sharing the same filament-and-node geometry at scales separated by twenty orders of magnitude. The mechanism is analogy as architecture: the web is not a metaphor for the universe, it is the same structure, and walking through it is the argument. The watchable behaviour is the stoop: the visitor who has to duck under a cable to get through a model of the universe, and finds the model is in the way. Seen in W04 and W05. [C29152]

**Takeaway:** build the universe as a room, and make the visitor stoop to walk through it. [C29152]

## M7 -- The kit and the forecast: anyone can float, and the wind has a timetable

Aerocene is not only a sculpture; it is a kit, a forecast and an open-source community, and the kit is the mechanism by which the practice escapes the museum. The Aerocene Backpack, also called the Aerocene Explorer (2016-), is a portable floating kit: a dark triangular aerosolar sculpture, a backpack, and open-source hardware carrying sensors for aerial photography, temperature, humidity and altitude. The user inflates the black sculpture by running with its mouth open, lets it sit in the sun, and the dark envelope absorbs solar radiation, the air inside warms, and the sculpture rises by itself, tethered, lifting its sensor payload -- Aerocene.org calls it "a kit that teaches you how to float", thinking-through-making, with the data collected uploadable to the open-source online community. The Float Predictor (2012, from an idea by Saraceno in conversation with MIT's Lodovica Illari, Glenn Flierl and Bill McKenna, launched at the World Economic Forum, Davos, 2017) is the navigation companion: a global forecasting system using open meteorological data -- NOAA GFS, 16-day wind forecasts at different altitudes -- to predict the flight path of an aerosolar sculpture as it drifts with the winds, so a user can plan a journey around the world without CO2 emissions, across national borders, developed with Cambridge University Space Flight, Ingo Randolf and Roham Fayazy, among others. The mechanism is distribution as the work: the sculpture is not the object in the gallery but the kit in the backpack and the forecast on the screen, and the flight is planned by anyone, anywhere, from public weather data. The watchable behaviour is the run: a person running across a field with a black envelope streaming behind them, and the envelope deciding, in sunlight, to stay up. Seen in W12 and W11. [C29153]

**Takeaway:** put the balloon in a backpack, put the wind on a screen, and let anyone plan a flight. [C29153]

## M8 -- Air made visible: dust, particles, and who gets to breathe

The late work turns from the air as lift to the air as evidence -- what is in it, and who breathes it. Particular Matter(s) (2020, The Shed 2022) is a light beam at eye level aimed at the doorway of a pitch-black room, illuminating millions of tiny particles -- cosmic dust and PM2.5 pollution -- floating in the air, making the invisible air visible, the beam briefly overwhelming the eye; the show's title is a pun on particulate matter, the hazardous airborne particles at the centre of environmental-justice questions about who breathes clean air. The Cosmic Dust Spider Web Orchestra (2017) makes the same air audible: a light beam makes cosmic dust visible while a live 3D video tracks 25 dust particles and translates their movement into sound. "We do not all breathe the same air" (2018-, noted in the works survey but not a numbered entry) makes it documentary: a Beta Attenuation Mass Monitor pushes air hourly through a glass-fibre strip, producing lines of dots on paper whose colours vary with the quantity and type of particles in the air. At the Serpentine in 2023 the show included particle-pollution samples from Mumbai and radio waves from First Nations Argentina; at MAAT in 2018, A Thermodynamic Imaginary filled the Oval Gallery with aerosolar orbs, video and shadow play, and at The Shed in 2022 its iteration was a black-and-white scenography of large silver spheres in which visitors "confuse their shadows with their neighbours' as gestures overlap, mirror, and intersect". The mechanism is the beam and the filter: a light beam or a paper strip that makes the air's contents legible, and the legibility is the politics -- the same air is not the same air for everyone. The watchable behaviour is the doorway: stepping into darkness and finding the air full of things you were breathing all along. Seen in W18, W13 and W15. [C29154]

**Takeaway:** aim a beam at the doorway, and let the visitor see what they have been breathing. [C29154]

## M9 -- Multispecies habitat and Traditional Ecological Knowledge: the cloud has other tenants

The most recent turn folds the whole practice back into the knowledge systems it learned from: the cloud stops being a human sculpture and becomes a habitat, and the monument stops being an artwork and becomes a community's property. Cloud Cities: Species of Spaces and Other Pieces (2023-) is one cloud with cohabitational spaces for 24 species -- compartments designed for birds, insects, dogs, hedgehogs, foxes and other animals of Kensington Gardens, plus a children's area, "Cloud Imagination", entered through an aperture too small for most adults -- and at the Serpentine in 2023 the whole building was altered for multispecies use: climate control switched off, doorways opened to the park for natural ventilation and the movement of living organisms, indoor artworks powered by newly installed roof solar panels, visitors invited to surrender mobile phones at the entrance, and bicycles at the entrance that visitors pedal to activate an audio recording of the "Manifesto for an Ecosocial Energy Transition from the Peoples of the South". Cloud Cities Barcelona (2022), the most complex Cloud Cities realisation to date and the first permanent walk-through one, hangs 113 pods formed by 1,200 panels on 6 km of tensile cable with 5,000 nodes and 267 anchor points, suspended over 130 metres up in the Torre Glories, visitors climbing and sliding through pods that hang "like droplets of water condensing along the strands of a three-dimensional spider's web", with cushions and books placed in some pods and the artist proposing that one day entry could be free to anyone who shows a book. The Sanctuary of Water (2026-, opening late March 2027 per the later reports) is the centre of both 2026 shows and the practice's furthest step: conceived following a direct request from the Indigenous Communities of Salinas Grandes and Laguna de Guayatayoc for a permanent sculpture to facilitate community-based rural tourism, it consists of five salt apachetas -- traditional Andean stone mounds built over time as places of offering, gratitude and safe passage -- reimagined at monumental scale, 2-24 metres in diameter and up to 12 metres high per the Haus der Kunst (other sources give 2-30 metres and up to 15 metres), built from locally harvested salt around a circular reflecting pool, and when the pool is replenished by rare rainfall each semicircle is completed by its reflection into a full rounded figure; the five forms take names from Andean cosmology -- Inti, Killa, Ch'aska, Hawcha and Tiqsimuyu -- and the site will be owned and managed by the communities themselves, with admission income retained by them. The mechanism is legal and economic as much as sculptural: a monument to water and a protection zone that orients towards futures sustaining the communities defending the salt flat against lithium extraction, which depletes the groundwater of the endorheic basin. The title of Ancestral Futures follows the Brazilian Indigenous philosopher Ailton Krenak: "The future is nothing we need to invent -- it is already here, woven into the landscape like a river". The watchable behaviour is the reflection completing the semicircle: a mound that is only half a shape until the rain comes and the pool finishes it. Seen in W19 and W20. [C29155]

**Takeaway:** build the cloud for twenty-four species, and give the monument to the people who asked for it. [C29155]

## M10 -- The work that grows: a museum that gets bigger every time it flies

Saraceno's works do not finish; they accumulate, and the accumulation is the system. Museo Aero Solar grows with each reassembly: a new section of bags is added at every city, so the museum literally gets bigger at every stop, more than 20,000 bags and counting, and at the Haus der Kunst from September 2026 the museum's Open Atelier becomes a workspace where bags collected during the run are folded into the next expansion of the sculpture -- an artwork whose authorship is structurally distributed, the visitors' waste becoming the next wing. The Hybrid Webs grow by sequence: each spider builds on the last spider's web, and the frame is turned so the geometry compounds in three dimensions. Fly with Aerocene Pacha (28 January 2020, Salinas Grandes, Jujuy, Argentina) grows the practice from a sculpture into a record and a protest: pilot Leticia Noemi Marques flew a fuel-free aerosolar balloon of hybrid SKYTEX fabric, 86 kg, carrying up to 237 kg, inflated with a pedal-powered fan, free and untethered -- the world's first manned solar-powered free flight -- 272 to 275.5 metres above the ground, 667.85 to 668 metres, 16 minutes, setting 32 world records certified by the Federation Aeronautique Internationale in Class A (free balloons), subclass AT (solar balloons), the inaugural entries in that subclass; the white salt flat reflected light upward from below, amplifying the heating; and the flight was developed with 33 Indigenous communities of Salinas Grandes, who opened the launch with ritual ceremonies to bless Pachamama and protested lithium extraction, the balloon flying bearing their slogan "Water and life are worth more than lithium". The mechanism is growth as the medium: the work is never the object on the day it opens, it is the object plus everything that has been added to it since, by spiders, by visitors, by communities, by the sun. The watchable behaviour is the return visit that finds a bigger work: the balloon with a new section, the web with a new layer, the flight that became a record. Seen in W03, W08, W17 and W20. [C29156]

**Takeaway:** let the work grow after it opens, and count the additions as the work. [C29156]

## Aesthetic logic

Saraceno's logic is the system with the instructions left on and the author stepping back. Against Eliasson, whose installation knows it is a machine and shows you the machine -- the hose, the fan, the sodium lamp, the scaffolding behind the sun -- Saraceno's sculpture knows it is a system and shows you the system: the plastic bags, the fan, the black envelope, the carbon frame, the laser sheet, the salt bricks. The work is always the interval -- between the sun and the air inside the envelope, between the spider and the silk, between the visitor's step and the ripple in the net, between the salt flat and the lithium mine -- and the interval is where someone else has to stand, with their own body, their own web, their own community as the last component. What changes across twenty-four years is not the question but who is allowed to answer it: first the artist proposes the floating city, then the spider builds the web, then the sun lifts the balloon, then the community owns the monument and keeps the admission money. He refuses the digital fix, and the refusal is the point: the balloon is made with garbage bags and sunlight, the web with silk and a laser sheet, the flight with a pedal-powered fan and a salt flat, and none of it needs a screen -- the Float Predictor is a forecast, not a simulation, and the Arachnomancy App is a map of real webs, not a game. The meaning is the making, and the making is shared: the visible bag, the counted knot, the titled week of spider labour, the named community, the slogan on the balloon -- because the work is only honest about what it cost when the cost is on view, and only just when the people who paid it own it. [C29157]
