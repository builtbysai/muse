# Pilar Boliver: Study Notes

**Site:** Maria Angelica del Pilar Boliver Jimenez (born Thursday 25 July 1963, Coyoacan, Mexico City), Mexican actress of theatre, television and film, director, costume designer and television host, studied Literatura Dramatica y Teatro at the UNAM. She has more than eighty stagings as an actress by the Teatro UNAM count, more than fifty by the Spanish Wikipedia count, and more than ninety costume-design projects beside them, a double career the record keeps splitting apart: the actress on stage and the designer who dresses other actors. She is La Veros, the parody of Veronica Castro, born in 1993 at El Habito beside Dario T. Pie's La Ro~na, and she is also the actress who played Emma Goldman and Coco Chanel in back-to-back monologues at the Teatro La Capilla, the doctor who cannot save in Un corazon normal, one of the eight founders of Los monologos de la vagina in 2000 and still in its cast twenty-five years later, and since 2018 the director who strips the folklore off Frida Kahlo. She is not a cabaret founder in the sense of Rodriguez, Hadad, Pie, Vasconcelos or Orozco, and that is the point of studying her here: she is the first stage partner, the woman who shared the room where the founding character was born, and then spent thirty years carrying the cabaret's methods into the commercial theatre, the television studio and the director's chair. Her full name and her birth date are carried from the Spanish Wikipedia, and her birthplace, Coyoacan, from her own words in a 2018 interview, because the Wikipedia gives no birthplace. This file is pure ASCII, so every Spanish accent is dropped and the tilde is written as "~" after the n, "Do~na", "Ro~na", following the #353 convention.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Eugenia Leon study: Leon was a guest on Desde Gayola, listed among the show's "Invitados Relevantes", and Boliver was a protagonist of the same show from 2008 to 2013 as La Veros Castro, host of the sketch "Mala Nacha, si!"; and La Veros was born in 1993 at El Habito, Jesusa Rodriguez's room, beside Pie's La Ro~na, in a show the two of them called "La Ro~na y La Veros". Leon is the singer the cabaret circle keeps borrowing; Boliver is the actress the cabaret circle was built beside, the woman who was in the room at the birth and then went on to do the thing the founders did not do, which is to stay in the commercial theatre for twenty-five years in the same play, and to become a director. The doorway is the guest list and the birth certificate together: Leon guested where Boliver starred, and Boliver starred where Pie was born. Where Leon's line grows because the same voice keeps coming back in a different genre, Boliver's grows because the same actress keeps coming back in a different job: the parodist, the monologuist, the television critic, the costume designer, the director, and the woman underneath never changes her mind about what the stage is for, which is to make the room look at a woman it thought it knew.

## M1. The twin birth at El Habito

The Spanish Wikipedia article on Desde Gayola states the mechanism in one paragraph, and the paragraph is the whole doorway: "El personaje de La Ro~na, parodia de Maria Felix realizada por Dario T. Pie, surge en 1993 en el centro de espectaculos teatrales y cabaret El Habito. Alli tambien surge el personaje de La Veros, realizado por Pilar Boliver. Ambas tenian un espectaculo llamado La Ro~na y La Veros. Curiosamente, ambos actores no fueron contemporaneos en el programa." La Ro~na, the parody of Maria Felix by Dario T. Pie, arises in 1993 at the theatre and cabaret centre El Habito. There also arises the character La Veros, performed by Pilar Boliver. Both had a show called "La Ro~na y La Veros". Curiously, the two actors were not contemporaries on the program. The mechanism is the twin birth: two parodies of two divas, La Do~na degraded to La Ro~na, Veronica Castro degraded to La Veros, born on the same stage in the same year, in the same room, as a pair. The degradation is the joke and the method together: take the most untouchable woman in Mexican culture, the Do~na, and rename her after a scab; take the most beloved television star, Veronica Castro, and rename her after a woman who knows nothing. The pair is the unit, not the single parody: the show was called by both names, and the two characters needed each other to make the room, because one degraded diva is a joke and two degraded divas are a world. The second half of the paragraph is the mechanism's strange coda: the twins were not contemporaries on television. Pie played La Ro~na on Desde Gayola from 2002 to 2006; Boliver played La Veros from 2008 to 2013. The pair was born together and then lived apart, each carrying the other one's name in the show's memory. The title variant matters and is carried, not smoothed over: Boliver's own Wikipedia theatre list gives the show as "La Do~na y La Vero", not "La Ro~na y La Veros", and whether that is the same show under a different billing or a different show is unresolved in the sources read. Watch the pairing: when two degraded divas share one stage and one billing, and the room understands the second one because it already knows the first, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** degrade the diva twice, in a pair. One scab is a joke; two scabs are a world. Against the chain: Pie (#353 M1) also built his career on the single degraded diva, La Do~na turned into La Ro~na, but his unit was the one mask worn for thirty years; Boliver's unit is the pair, which is why her version is a duet and his is a solo, and why the pair could be split across two television eras without breaking.

## M2. The ignorant interviewer

On Desde Gayola from 2008 to 2012, La Veros hosted the sketch "Mala Nacha, si!", and the sketch's rule is stated plainly in the article: "La Veros entrevista a personalidades reales, presumiendo su ignorancia y su poca cultura", La Veros interviews real personalities, showing off her ignorance and her poor culture, and she "saca los trapitos al sol", she airs the dirty laundry, with her guests and vice versa. The mechanism is the interviewer who knows nothing and wins anyway: the guest arrives with a reputation, and the host undoes it by not knowing it, by asking the wrong question, by being proud of the ignorance. The guest cannot correct her without looking like a snob, and cannot play along without looking like a fool, so the reputation leaks out in the gap between the question and the answer, and the dirty laundry airs itself. The name is the joke's engine: "Mala Nacha, si!" is a pun on "mala nacha" and on Nacha Guevara, the Argentine singer, so the sketch announces itself as a bad interview before it starts. La Veros is the same mechanism as the twin birth, turned outward: the degraded diva is now the one holding the microphone, and the degradation spreads from the host to the guest. She also carried the character to Nocturninos on 52MX from 2008 to 2013, billed "Pilar Boliver (La Veros)", so the ignorant interviewer worked on two channels at once, the sketch show and the late-night talk show, which is the measure of how portable the mechanism is: it needs only a chair, a guest and a host who knows nothing. Watch the ignorance: when the host's wrong question makes the guest confess something the guest would never have said to a competent interviewer, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** host with ignorance, not knowledge. The wrong question is the interview. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M5) also interviewed the guest, but her interview ended in a duet, the host proving she had heard the guest by singing with them; Boliver's interview ends in a confession, the host proving she had not heard the guest by asking the wrong thing, which is why hers is an ambush and Leon's is a duet. Pie (#353 M3) also brought the guest on stage to be undone, but his undoing was a corpse with an albur; Boliver's is a host with no culture, which is why hers needs no prop, only a question.

## M3. The disappearing actress

The entretenia review of Coco, Mademoiselle Gabrielle states the rule that governs Boliver's monologues, and it states it as a criticism: her biggest enemy is herself, when her own personality shows through in the role of Chanel in search of the comedy in which she is also an expert, and "En la medida en que Boliver desaparezca por completo para dar paso por completo a tan mitica figura de la historia su trabajo alcanzara los mismos niveles a los que llego en su monologo anterior Emma, la Mujer mas Peligrosa". To the extent that Boliver disappears completely to give way completely to such a mythical figure of history, her work will reach the same levels it reached in her previous monologue, Emma. The mechanism is the disappearance as the performance: the actress is the obstacle, and the work is the removal of the actress. Emma (2014) and Coco (2016) are the two halves of the proof, both at the Teatro La Capilla in Coyoacan, both on Mondays at 20:00, both seventy minutes, both one woman alone on a small stage with almost nothing: Emma's set is a blackboard, a bench and a table; Coco's set is pink brand panels, a divan and a table with thread and needle. The photographs show what the disappearance looks like: pb-05, Boliver as Emma, face lit from above, eyes nearly closed, a patterned orange-brown headscarf, no trace of the laughing red-haired woman in pb-02; pb-06, Boliver as Emma seated, white blouse with puffed sleeves, tan vest, red scarf, glasses, reading a letter, an old leather trunk beside her, the actress gone and the anarchist in her place. Coco's mechanism adds the reinvention: an old woman giving herself a morphine injection becomes a girl inventing a better reality before her misery, then a powerful businesswoman, and the reviewer's line for it is Chanel's own, "Me reinvente donde otros se dieron por vencido", I reinvented myself where others gave up. The actress who disappears is also the woman who reinvents herself, and the two are the same motion: leave the self behind, become the next one. Watch the vanishing: when the comedian's timing stops showing through the tragic woman's lines, and the room forgets who is speaking, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** disappear completely, or the comedy shows through. The actress is the obstacle. Against the chain: Pie (#353 M9) also changed bodies while keeping the voice, voicing Edna Moda in a cartoon, but his change was a dubbing booth, the voice leaving the body behind; Boliver's change is a stage, the whole body leaving itself behind, which is why hers is a disappearance and his is a ventriloquism.

## M4. The second Emma, or the letter to her mother

Emma "La mujer mas peligrosa" is a monologue by Carlos Pascual, written at the request of his very close friend and protagonist, and the entretenia review gives the mechanism in its first lines: the play is "una carta de amor a su madre, Pilar Jimenez" and to all exiles, a love letter to her mother, Pilar Jimenez, and to all exiles. Pilar Jimenez fled the Spanish Civil War at twelve and settled in Mexico. The mechanism is the personal stake as the engine: the actress does not play Emma Goldman because the role is famous, she plays her because the role is a letter to her own mother, and the anarchist's exile is her mother's exile, and the play's politics are her family's history. The review quotes the lines that carry it: "Nadie entiende una sola palabra de lo que yo digo", nobody understands a single word of what I say; "Yo he venido a esta tierra para tener la libertad de crear mi propia libertad", I have come to this land to have the freedom to create my own freedom; "Nos condenan al exilio por haber dicho la verdad", they condemn us to exile for having told the truth. The phrase that unlocks the mechanism is "su segunda Emma", her second Emma: the first Emma was Dr. Emma Brookner in Un corazon normal in 2013, the doctor in Larry Kramer's play about AIDS, and the second Emma is Emma Goldman the anarchist, so the name Emma is itself a relay, one woman handing the name to the next, and the actress playing both is the wire between them. The set is the proof of the mechanism's economy: a blackboard, a bench, a table, seventy minutes, Mondays at 20:00, $150, and nothing else, because a letter to your mother needs no scenery. Watch the letter: when the historical woman's words become the actress's family history, and the room hears a daughter talking to her mother through an anarchist, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** play the woman who is a letter to your mother. The exile is the inheritance. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M6) also sang the canon one composer at a time, but her canon was other men's songs; Boliver's canon is other women's lives, and the difference is that her women are letters, not homages, which is why hers is a correspondence and Leon's is a library.

## M5. The doctor who cannot save

In Un corazon normal, Larry Kramer's The Normal Heart, Boliver played Dr. Emma Brookner at the Teatro San Jeronimo in 2013, and the entretenia Emma review names the mechanism in passing: it was "el homenaje que Boliver hace a tantos amigos que ha perdido a causa del SIDA", the tribute Boliver makes to so many friends she has lost because of AIDS. The mechanism is the doctor as witness: Dr. Brookner is the one character in Kramer's play who cannot save anyone, the doctor who diagnoses the plague and then watches her patients die, and the actress plays her as a tribute, so the performance is a memorial and the doctor's helplessness is the actress's grief. The name is the relay again: the doctor is Emma, and the anarchist is Emma, and the actress played the doctor first and the anarchist second, so the two Emmas are one woman's two ways of standing beside the dying, once as a doctor who cannot cure and once as an exile who cannot go home. No photograph of the production was viewed in this leg, and no review of it was read end to end, so the mechanism is stated from the single sentence that names it, which is why it is carried as a tribute, not as a seen performance. Watch the helplessness: when the doctor's lines stop being a diagnosis and start being a list of the dead, and the room understands the actress is naming her own friends, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** play the doctor who cannot save. The helplessness is the tribute. Against the chain: Pie (#353 M16) also earned his mask with twelve years first, but his apprenticeship was a straight actor's training before a single character; Boliver's apprenticeship is the friends she lost, which is why her version is a memorial and his is a curriculum.

## M6. The rotating cast

Los monologos de la vagina premiered in Mexico on Thursday 19 October 2000 at the Sala Chopin, directed by Jaime Matarredona and produced by Morris Gilbert, and Boliver was one of the eight original cast: Andrea Legarreta, Stephanie Salas, Lilia Aragon, Sofia Alvarez, Adriana Roel, Pilar Boliver, Ana Karina Guevara and Dr. Anabel Ochoa. Twenty-five years later, on Tuesday 14 October 2025, she sat beside the author V, formerly Eve Ensler, and Ana Karina Guevara at the Universum conversatorio as one of the two founding actresses still in the cast, and on Thursday 16 October 2025 the gala at the Nuevo Teatro Libanes put eighteen actresses on stage at once. The mechanism is the rotating cast: the play is built to be interpreted by three actresses per function, and over twenty-five years 158 women have performed it, so the same words pass through a different body every night, and the body is the variable and the text is the constant. The production sold out every function for the first five years, at its peak played Monday to Sunday and in two theatres simultaneously in Mexico City, toured nationally and internationally, and became the first Spanish-language play with an open run on Broadway. The mechanism's other half is the activism that rides on it: more than fifteen years of collections for Casa Amiga in Ciudad Juarez, a march with Jane Fonda, Sally Field and V against the feminicides, the Dia V and Un Billon de Pie campaigns, a 2023 visit to the women's federal prison in Morelos with hygiene products for 1,200 women. The play is a relay and a movement at once, and the actress who stays in it for twenty-five years is the proof that the relay works: she is one of 158, and she is also one of the two founders still there. Watch the rotation: when the same monologue is spoken by a different woman on a different night, and the room hears the text change because the body changed, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the cast rotate and the text stay. The body is the variable. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M2) also let the room build the show, but her room was one voice taking requests; Boliver's room is 158 voices taking turns over twenty-five years, which is why hers is a relay and Orozco's is a jukebox. Leon (#354 M1) also became a new singer every time, but her newness was a new genre; Boliver's newness is a new body saying the same words, which is why hers is a rotation and Leon's is a reinvention.

## M7. The costume as the character

The Teatro UNAM semblanza files Boliver under costume design, not acting, and that is the mechanism's first proof: she is a member of the Programa de Creadores Escenicos con Trayectoria of the FONCA, with more than ninety projects in costume design and stage characterization, from the classical to the new avant-gardes. The highlights are a catalogue of the Mexican theatre's directors: "Mural Tres Siglos de Teatro Mexicano" for the Compania Nacional de Teatro, directed by Jose Caballero; ten productions with Los Endebles, directed by Boris Schoemann, El regreso al desierto, Los endebles, El puente de piedra, La piel de imagenes, Sensacional de maricones, Alphonse, Telenovela, Kiwi, Un campo and La oca; Rinoceronte and Bichos de viaje for the Compania de Teatro de Jalapa, directed by Alberto Lomnitz; Andromaca by Calderon, directed by Claudia Rios; El Avaro by Moliere, directed by Jose Sole; Puerto infinito, Pessoa poetry, directed by Mauricio Jimenez; Temporal by Flavio Gonzalez Mello; El Quijote by Bulgakov and Noche de estio by Usigli, both directed by German Castillo. The mechanism is the character built from the outside in: the actress who dresses other actors knows that the costume is not decoration but dramaturgy, that the turtleneck and the red pants are the character before the character speaks, and her own directing proves it, because in Las hermanas Vals the costume defines the sisters before a word is said. The Wikipedia omits the design career entirely, which is why the study carries it from the Teatro UNAM semblanza and names it as a second career, not a footnote: the woman who played Emma Goldman in a headscarf and Coco Chanel in a pink room also built the clothes for ninety other productions, and the two jobs are one job, the making of a woman out of cloth. Watch the wardrobe: when the costume tells the room who the character is before she opens her mouth, and the actress only has to wear what the designer already decided, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** build the character from the outside in. The costume is the dramaturgy. Against the chain: Hadad (#351 M1) also wore the scenery, but her scenery was sewn into costumes she carried on her own body, up to fourteen per show; Boliver's scenery is sewn for other actors, which is why her version is a design career and Hadad's is a wardrobe, and why Boliver can direct a play by dressing it.

## M8. The de-folklorized Frida

Kahlo, viva la vida, Humberto Robles's monologue, is the one Frida in this study that Boliver did not play: she directed it, Ana Karina Guevara played Frida, and Boliver designed the wardrobe, the hair and the makeup herself, at the Foro Bellescene in Colonia Narvarte from Saturday 10 November to Saturday 15 December 2018, six Saturday functions, and then at the Foro 4 Espacio Alternativo of the Centro Cultural Helenico on Saturdays at 21:00 until Saturday 20 July 2019, sixty minutes, $300. The Cartelera interview gives the method in her own words: "mi biblia es este texto, soy muy respetuosa de los autores", my bible is this text, I am very respectful of the authors; and the de-folklorization is the program, "desmarcarla de los folcklorismos exacerbados", to unmark her from the exacerbated folklorisms, "desenmascarar este folcklorismo... quitandole todo este mercantilismo exacerbado", to unmask this folklorism, taking away all this exacerbated merchandising. The mechanism is the director as restorer: strip the icon of the folklore and the merchandise until the woman is visible again, and work from the childhood sensations of Coyoacan instead, because Boliver was born there and her father was a muralist and her mother learned to wear rebozos from Frida herself. The staging is the proof: no wheelchair, no brushes, no easels, no crutches, artisanal elements instead, a bench with human-feet legs showing "El Venado Herido", a crinoline headdress, a dance with a prosthesis, Guevara covering her mouth when she smiles because Kahlo hid her dark teeth, and the show as a rollercoaster, "brinco, brinco, no hay manera de que la gente se baje de la montana rusa", jump, jump, there is no way for people to get off the rollercoaster. The photographs show the restoration as an image: pb-03, Guevara as Frida against a flat bright blue background, braided hair with red ribbons, unibrow, red rebozo, hand at chin; pb-07, Guevara seated on the stage floor, gold-embroidered black blouse, white skirt, wide red sash; pb-08, Guevara standing with arms raised wide, a huge white pleated ruff collar worn like a tehuana headdress, long red rebozo strips in her hands. Watch the stripping: when the wheelchair and the easel disappear from the stage, and the woman appears instead of the souvenir, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** strip the folklore off the icon. The restoration is the direction. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M8) also played a historical singer, Tona la Negra, but her mechanism was the citation, the singer playing the singer she already was; Boliver's mechanism is the restoration, the director removing the icon's costume so the woman can be seen, which is why hers is a stripping and Leon's is a wearing.

## M9. Three versions of one accident

Las hermanas Vals, "Falsedad", Lot Vekemans's play, directed by Boliver at the Foro La Gruta, is built on the mechanism its own announcement states: "Dos hermanas, un testigo y tres versiones de un mismo accidente", two sisters, a witness and three versions of the same accident. The mechanism is the accident told three times, and the truth is the difference between the versions: Denise, the television actress, vibrant, image-obsessed, turtleneck, red pants, blue blazer; Cat, the theatre actress, sober, anxious about missing her performance, green blouse and pants, gold sweater; and the third character, a disheveled man with visible wounds, the witness, who monologues and is shown images of his dead dog. The costume is the character again, M7 turned into a directing choice: the sisters are defined by what they wear before they speak, and the lines say it out loud, "El teatro pone las cosas en movimiento, la TV idiotiza al publico", theatre sets things in motion, television makes the public stupid, says Cat, and "Es facil aferrarse a los principios cuando no tienes nada que perder", it is easy to cling to principles when you have nothing to lose, says Denise. The cast conflict is carried, not smoothed over: the Cartelera review of c. 2024 gives Talia Marcela, Ana Karina Guevara and Constantino Moran, with the sisters called Denise and Cat, while the October 2026 season gives Talia Marcela, Maria Fernanda Garcia and Gaston Yanes, with the sisters called Kat and Sis and the witness Ge, a biologist, so the three versions of the accident are also three versions of the production, and the play keeps changing its own cast the way it changes its own story. The premiere of the 2026 season was Thursday 8 October 2026 at 19:30, and the run was 8 to 25 October 2026. Watch the versions: when the same accident is told three times and the room stops asking which one is true and starts watching what each version protects, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** tell the accident three times. The truth is the difference. Against the chain: Pie (#353 M11) also kept an archive of other women's lives, but his archive was a museum of divas worn one at a time; Boliver's archive is a single accident told three ways, which is why hers is a courtroom and his is a museum.

## M10. The critic on the couch

Farandula 40 premiered on Wednesday 20 January 2010 at 21:30, forty-five minutes, with Horacio Villalobos, Boliver and Mario Loria, and it ran for ten years, until the last show on Saturday 19 December 2020, ended by the losses of COVID-19, with a podcast successor, Farandula 021, from Thursday 4 February 2021, and a return, Extra40, from Saturday 12 August 2023 with Boliver and Alejandro Brofft. The mechanism is the actress who becomes the critic: the woman who played La Veros, the ignorant interviewer, now sits on a couch and judges the culture for real, with the cabaret cast beside her, Daniel Vives "La Supermana", Carlos Rangel "Maniguis", Javier Yepez "Mama Mela Rico", who died on 27 February 2019, and Mauricio Valle from 2012. The annual special is the mechanism's purest form: "Los Premios Tarantula 40", the Tarantula Awards, a parody prize for the worst of the year, the critic's chair turned into a joke that still judges, because awarding the worst is still awarding, and the room that laughs at the worst has still agreed on what the worst is. The farewell is the mechanism's proof: on the final show Boliver told Villalobos he was "casi un hermano" for her, almost a brother, and she adored him, so the critic's chair was also a family table, and the ten years of judgment were ten years of sitting beside the same people. Watch the award: when the worst of the year is given a prize, and the room laughs and agrees, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** award the worst of the year. The joke still judges. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M5) also sat in the host's chair, but her chair ended in a duet, the host joining the guest; Boliver's chair ends in a prize for the worst, the host judging the guest, which is why hers is a court and Leon's is a duet.

## M11. The alternating monologue

La Rubia, la Triguena y la Pelirroja Vengadora, Robert Hewett's play, was presented to the press on Monday 23 March 2009 and premiered in April 2009, with Raquel Garza starring and Boliver alternating functions, "alternando funciones", and the play is eight characters with melodrama tints in one actress's body. The mechanism is the role as a relay: one part, several actresses, and the audience's job is to compare the versions, so the monologue is not a performance but a set of performances, and the character is the sum of the women who play her. La senorita Delfina, la educastradora, Roberto Athayde's black-humor monologue, translated into twenty-two languages and staged in thirty countries, is the same mechanism fifteen years later, directed by Boliver and premiered on Monday 10 June 2024 at the MarkeTeatro, with three actresses alternating the role, Libertad Palomo, Aleyda Gallardo and Ariane Pellicer, produced by Alvaro Arriola, on Thursdays. The review adds the mechanism's risk: "La decision de apoyar el monologo de la actriz con la presencia de publico en el escenario, es un acierto que implica el riesgo de lo inesperado", the decision to support the actress's monologue with the presence of members of the public on the stage is a success that implies the risk of the unexpected, so the alternating monologue is also an open monologue, with the audience on stage and the unexpected built in. The first Mexican staging was more than forty years earlier, directed by Gerald Huillier and starring Virma Gonzalez, so the relay is also a history: the same monologue, handed from one actress to the next across four decades. Watch the alternation: when the same monologue is played by a different woman on a different night, and the room compares the versions instead of judging one, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** alternate the role. The character is the sum of its players. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M7) also sang as a trio, three complete voices becoming one, but her trio sang together; Boliver's trio plays apart, one at a time, which is why hers is a relay and Leon's is a chord.

## M12. The archive of historic women

Emma Goldman, Coco Chanel, Sarah Bernhardt, Frida Kahlo: Boliver's shelf of historic women is the mechanism that holds the monologues together, and the four are four different jobs. Emma (2014) is the anarchist as a love letter to her mother, M4. Coco (2016) is the fashion empress as a reinvention, M3. Sarah Bernhardt, in La divina ilusion by Michel Marc Bouchard, directed by Boris Schoemann at the Teatro Julio Castillo, Mondays and Tuesdays at 19:00 until Tuesday 4 June 2019, in its fourth season, is the divine actress herself, the woman who invented the modern actress, played by an actress who has spent her life becoming other women. Frida (2018-2019) is the one she did not play but directed, M8, the icon stripped of her folklore. The mechanism is the monologue as a portrait gallery: one actress, one woman at a time, each famous, each dangerous, each dead, and the gallery's argument is that the dangerous woman is the only woman worth a monologue. The reviewer's line for Coco is the gallery's rule: "Esconder es acentuar", to hide is to accentuate, and "Dime como te vistes y te dire quien eres", tell me how you dress and I will tell you who you are, so the portrait is the costume and the costume is the portrait, and the actress who designed ninety productions knows it. Watch the gallery: when the same actress returns with a different dead woman, and the room recognizes the actress by the woman she has become, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** keep a gallery of dangerous women. One at a time, each dead, each worth a monologue. Against the chain: Pie (#353 M11) also kept an archive of other women's lives, but his archive was divas worn as masks, La Do~na and her sisters; Boliver's archive is historic women worn as monologues, which is why hers is a gallery and his is a wardrobe, and why her women are dead and his are alive in the gossip.

## M13. The Coyoacan childhood as method

"I was born in Coyoacan, my father was a Mexican muralist painter, Frida taught my mother to wear rebozos to carry the children." Boliver said it in the 2018 Cartelera interview, and the sentence is the mechanism: "Yo naci en Coyoacan, mi papa era pintor muralista mexicano, Frida le enseno a mi mama a ponerse los rebozos para cargar a los chamacos." The mechanism is the biography as a tool: the childhood is not a story, it is the directing instrument, the sensations of Coyoacan as the way to reach Frida, the rebozo as the way to carry a child, the muralist father's studio as the way to understand a painter's house. The correction matters and is carried: the entretenia Kahlo review says Boliver "en su propia vida personal tuvo contacto directo con Frida Kahlo", had direct contact with Frida Kahlo in her own personal life, which is chronologically impossible, Boliver born in 1963, Kahlo dead in 1954, and the contact was her parents', her father an assistant on Diego Rivera's murals, her mother taught by Frida to wear rebozos, so the reviewer's compression is the mechanism's proof in reverse, that the family's contact is so close it looks like hers. The mother is the other half: Pilar Jimenez fled the Spanish Civil War at twelve and settled in Mexico, and Emma Goldman is the love letter to her, M4, so the childhood is also the exile, and the actress's method is her family's history. The likely sister is carried as converging evidence only: Rocio Boliver, "La Congelada de Uva", born 1956, whose article gives the same father, Angel Boliver, painter and muralist, and the same mother, Pilar Jimenez, and the same maternal surname, Jimenez, but no source read states outright that they are sisters. Watch the childhood: when the director reaches for a sensation from Coyoacan instead of a fact from a book, and the room feels the street rather than the lecture, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** direct from the childhood, not the book. The rebozo is the method. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M9) also let her voice cook slowly over fifty years, but her cooking was the career, album by album; Boliver's cooking is the childhood, the rebozo and the muralist's studio, which is why hers is a place and Leon's is a time.

## M14. The late turn to directing

The directing career begins in 2018, when Boliver is fifty-five, and the mechanism is the actress who becomes the director without leaving the stage. Kahlo, viva la vida (2018-2019) is the first, M8. Los chicos de la banda, Mart Crowley's The Boys in the Band, at the Teatro Xola on Friday 4 October 2019, with Horacio Villalobos, Juan Rios Cantu, Juan Carlos Martin del Campo and Aldo Guerra, is the second, and its history matters: the Mexican premiere in 1974, by Nancy Cardenas, was the first openly gay play in Mexico, so Boliver's second production as a director is a landmark play staged forty-five years after its first Mexican staging, by a director who had played the cabaret rooms where that history was made. Lavar, peinar y enterrar, JuanMa Pina's text, at the Foro La Gruta from Saturday 27 May to Sunday 30 July 2023, weekends at 18:00, with Shiky and Ignacio Sucedo, after two sold-out seasons in alternative spaces, is the third, and its tagline is the mechanism's question: "A los fantasmas del pasado: los entierras o los enfrentas?", to the ghosts of the past: do you bury them or face them? La senorita Delfina (2024) is the fourth, M11. Las hermanas Vals (c. 2024 and 2026) is the fifth, M9. The image design for Network in 2022, credited "IMAGEN: PILAR BOLIVER", is the hinge between the two careers: the costume designer and the actress meeting in the director's chair, the image as the job. The mechanism's rule is the one she states in the Kahlo interview: "mi biblia es este texto, soy muy respetuosa de los autores", the text is my bible, I am very respectful of the authors, so the late director is not a new author but a faithful reader, and the direction is the restoration, the stripping, the dressing, the three versions, all the mechanisms of the acting career turned into staging. Watch the turn: when the actress stops playing the woman and starts dressing the room, and the room recognizes her hand in the costumes before it recognizes her name in the program, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** direct at fifty-five, from the text. The acting career is the directing school. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M12) also made her late turn, but hers changed the job itself, from interpreter to author, composing into her phone at sixty-nine; Boliver's late turn keeps the job and changes the chair, from actress to director, which is why hers is a promotion and Leon's is a second career.

## Evolution

The early years are the cabaret room and the small parts: Mago de Oz on television in 1985, the El Habito birth in 1993, the short film Tierra 1 in 1993, El amor de tu vida S.A. in 1995 or 1996, a Televicine comedy whose cast holds Pie, Orozco and Boliver together, the whole circle in one film. The middle years are the long runs and the television: Los monologos de la vagina from Thursday 19 October 2000, twenty-five years and counting, the longest commitment in the study; Desde Gayola and Nocturninos from 2008, the ignorant interviewer on two channels; La Rubia in 2009, eight characters in one body; Farandula 40 from Wednesday 20 January 2010, ten years as the critic. The monologue years are 2013 to 2016: Un corazon normal in 2013, Emma in 2014, Coco in 2016, three historic women in three years, the gallery filling up. The directing years begin in 2018 and have not stopped: Kahlo in 2018-2019, Los chicos de la banda in 2019, Lavar, peinar y enterrar in 2023, La senorita Delfina in 2024, Las hermanas Vals in 2024 and 2026, and a film credit, Papa o mama in 2023, as the lawyer, missing from the Wikipedia filmography. The evolution's shape is a widening: the actress starts in a cabaret room with one other person, becomes a television host with a whole cast, becomes a monologuist alone on a stage with a bench and a table, and then becomes a director who dresses the room and tells the accident three times. The through-line is the woman: the parody, the monologue, the direction, all of them are ways of putting a woman on stage and watching what she does, and the actress who disappears into Emma Goldman is the same director who strips the folklore off Frida Kahlo, because both jobs are the removal of what is in the way.

## Aesthetic logic

Boliver's aesthetic is the removal of what is in the way. The set is a blackboard, a bench and a table. The costume is a headscarf and a vest. The direction is the stripping of the folklore and the merchandising. The interview is a question asked by someone who knows nothing. The award is for the worst of the year. The play is the same accident told three times. The logic is subtraction, and the subtraction is the point: the actress disappears so the woman can appear, the folklore disappears so Frida can appear, the knowledge disappears so the guest can confess, the scenery disappears so the letter to the mother can be heard. Against the chain, this is the opposite of Hadad's addition, the fourteen costumes, and the opposite of Orozco's accumulation, the room shouting requests: Boliver's room is empty, and the emptiness is the stage. The color logic follows from the images viewed: the black-and-white portrait, the black turtleneck, the dark-rimmed glasses; the red of the off-shoulder top and the red rebozo; the orange-brown of Emma's headscarf; the flat bright blue behind Frida; the gold embroidery on the black blouse; the white of the pleated ruff collar. The palette is a dark room with one red thing in it, and the red thing is the woman.
