# Yannick Jacquet: Study Notes

**Site:** Yannick Jacquet (b. 1980), French-Swiss visual artist, scenographer and art director, based in Brussels since 2005; co-founder of the visual label AntiVJ; the practice is moving image located in space -- "a desire to step outside of traditional projection formats and locate video in space." Born in Geneva; graphic designer by training, self-taught in scenography and video at the Zoo, the electronic-music room inside Geneva's alternative cultural centre L'Usine, working as VJ Legoman in the early 2000s (C2601). No formal art-school degree is evidenced in any source read -- the project-published monograph calls him self-taught, and the only claim of formal schooling (Ecole Bellecour 2002, Ecole superieure des beaux-arts de Geneve 2003) rests on a French Wikipedia article that carries an AI-generated-content banner and a deletion request (C2601, C2603). The arc runs 2005-2026: the move to Brussels, 3Destruct in 2007, the AntiVJ label, the decade of Mecaniques Discursives with wood engraver Fred Penelle (2011-2020), the sensor-driven permanent Elytre (2016), the slowness works, and the Interval studio founded with producer Nicolas Boritch in 2024. His stated program, assembled from project texts and his own site: video belongs in space, not on screens; resilience and "a discourse on the end of time" haunt every undertaking; slowness is the response to the attention economy -- "creating temporary spaces of deconditioning where attention can be cultivated and valued."

## The life: a VJ who wanted the image off the screen

Jacquet was born in Geneva in 1980 and has lived and worked in Brussels since 2005. The training on the record is graphic design, then self-teaching: "Autodidacte, il se forme a la scenographie et a la creation video au Zoo de l'Usine a Geneve" -- self-taught, he trained in scenography and video creation at the Zoo at L'Usine in Geneva (2022 monograph press dossier). In the early 2000s he worked as VJ Legoman in the electronic-music scene; the "resident VJ" wording is Wikipedia-only (C2617). His first solo exhibition, Fragments urbains, was at Mediaruimte in Brussels in 2006, per his own site (C2610).

The move to Brussels in 2005 is the hinge. In 2007 he made 3Destruct with graphic designer and screen printer Jeremie Peeters and composer Thomas Vaquie for the Biennale d'art contemporain de Louvain-la-Neuve: translucent volumes crossed by luminous projections and a sound environment, "an immersive experience in a non-linear world, where spatial coherence is lost." It was recreated and adapted again and again -- Mapping Festival Geneva 2007, Yota Space Saint Petersburg 2009, Le Lieu Unique Nantes 2011, Berlin Atonal 2013, MUTEK Mexico 2013 -- and it is the template for everything that follows: image taken off the screen and fitted to a volume, a material, a building.

AntiVJ is the second hinge, and its founding year is the record's second fault line. The label's own page says it "was originally created by Yannick Jacquet, Joanie Lemercier, Olivier Ratsi and Romain Tardy around 2006." Jacquet's own site says he "co-founded the international visual label Antivj in 2007"; MadMapper, Hopscotch and the Mecaniques Discursives site also say 2007; Sotheby's, artnet, CLOT and the Mapping Festival say 2008; Lemercier himself said "around 2005" in a 2025 interview. The honest spread is 2005-2008, with 2007 the best-attested year on Jacquet's own side of the record (C2602, C2515). What the sources agree on: the four founders all came out of VJing and set out to move the image off screens and conventional projection surfaces and onto architecture; the label works as a label, not a collective -- solo projects, collective projects and external commissions, like a music label. Producer Nicolas Boritch was involved from the start in direction and production; composer Thomas Vaquie has scored most works since 2008; visual artist and creative coder Simon Geilfus joined in 2009. The label was co-directed by Lemercier and Boritch until 2010 and has since been managed by Boritch alone. Under the label Jacquet took part in the large architectural projections that defined its early years -- Songdo (2009, a new city in South Korea), Saint-Gervais (2010, Mapping Festival Geneva, with Vaquie and organist Diego Innocenzi), Clair Obscur (2011, Collegiale de Neuchatel, with Geilfus and Vaquie), Paleodictyon (2012, Centre Pompidou-Metz, co-directed with Geilfus and Vaquie) -- and in the live-performance strand Intangible States (2010-2012) with the duo Stray Dogs, and Cityscape 2095 (2011, with French cartoonist Mandril's drawings; Mapping Festival Geneva, then Sundance 2013), a digital dystopian city.

The label as a producing entity looks dormant, not formally closed (C2518): the antivj.com events listings stop at October 2014, no new label project was found for 2015-2025, and no closure announcement was found either. No evidence Jacquet left AntiVJ -- his artist page still lists him as a founding member, his works still carry the "Label: Antivj" credit, and Boritch still produces his work (Sarabande 2025). There is no named "Jacquet studio" and no co-director, unlike Lemercier's Studio Lemercier (C2519) -- until 2024, when he founded the studio Interval with Boritch.

The third hinge is Fred Penelle. Jacquet met the Belgian wood engraver in Brussels in 2008, and in 2011 they began Mecaniques Discursives, the project that would dominate the next decade of his life. The premise, in their own words: an encounter between the oldest form of image reproduction (woodcutting) and the most recent digital technologies, "a machinery of the absurd and the poetic, unfolding across walls like some exquisite skeleton using the principle of a chain reaction as its starting point." Penelle's ferocious woodcuts -- chimeras, machines, pop-culture heroes, fragments of architecture -- are printed, cut out, pinned to walls with cardboard and welded metal structures, and Jacquet's projections animate them: vector impulses, data-design graphics, sonar, alarms, radio frequencies, figures that climb the walls and seem about to greet the viewer. The first showing was at the Mapping Festival in Geneva in May 2012. Some sources date the project's creation to 2013 (C2605); 2011 is the consistent date across the project site, the monograph and the exhibition lists.

Mecaniques Discursives toured relentlessly: iMAL residency in Brussels and the Art Collector's Prize at Slick Art Fair Brussels in 2013; the Milano Design Week Tech Award in 2014; STRP Eindhoven, MACRO Rome, the Nemo Biennale at CENTQUATRE-Paris, Lille3000, L.E.V. Gijon, the Jerusalem International Design Week, the Cairo Video Festival, Shenzhen Design Week, the National Taiwan Museum of Fine Arts -- by the project's own count "nearly 80 installations" (MadMapper) or "nearly a hundred exhibitions" (project site) or "plus de 70 creations" (2022 monograph); the counts drift by source and date, and no single number should be stated without attribution (C2612; leg 2's duplicate, C2655). In 2021 the project reached its monumental form at LABoral Centro de Arte in Gijon, commissioned by the L.E.V. Festival: 700 square metres, 12 metres high, more than 15 simultaneous projections, over 250 narrative elements -- the largest indoor configuration it ever reached, and, because Penelle had died in 2020, the last joint work and a tribute. The same year the monographic touring exhibition Ne suivez pas le guide ! (Don't Follow the Guide!) opened at LaVallee in Brussels, the book Mecaniques Discursives was published by CFC-Editions (144 pages), and the City of Brussels commissioned a permanent large-scale version for 21 Boulevard Anspach, a daytime fresco with animations projected after nightfall (on view 2021-2026). The project continues posthumously under Jacquet's direction (C2676): in 2025 he transposed to the stage Sarabande en temps de crise, a line of research begun with Penelle in 2019, performed at the Halle aux Draps in Tournai with the vocal ensemble Les Bruxelloises and Thomas Vaquie -- concept credited to Penelle and Jacquet, direction to Jacquet alone.

Parallel to the Penelle collaboration, Jacquet built a personal strand organised around slowness, attention and cycles. The key works: Elytre (2016), a forty-metre permanent generative work for Le Flow, a floating building moored at the foot of the Pont Alexandre III in Paris; Remote Memories (2016-2018), a panoramic video-and-sound polyptych with composer Laurent Delforge (Before Tigers); Entropy (2017, Sonar Barcelona), a live art-science performance staging the origin and end of the universe, with lectures by cosmologist Dr Katarina (Dida) Markovic and stellar astrophysicist Dr Zazralt Magic, a live electronic score by Dopplereffekt, and real astronomical data visualised in real time; Ressac (2016), a panoramic scenography for the Quatuor Voce playing Ravel's string quartet; Suite (2017-2019); Minakami (2019-2020), a choreographic piece with Jonathan Schatz and Thomas Vaquie; Burning (2019), video creation for Julien Fournier's theatre piece on work and burnout; Tokyo no oto (2021-2022), scenography and video creation for Jerome Combier on the soundscapes of Tokyo; Monochromes (since 2023, with double bassist Otto Lindholm), which rests on French Wikipedia alone (C2608); and Still Loading (first version January-February 2024, Maison des cultures de Tournai), his most personal recent statement, described in his own words on his site.

The most recent turn is institutional: in 2024 Jacquet founded with Boritch the studio Interval (interval.ooo), "initiating artist-led projects focused on the dialogue between handcrafted graphic arts and contemporary digital creation... in the space between ink and pixels, between the fixed and moving image" (PRINT Magazine, 16 September 2024). Its first major outing was the scenography and timeline design for The Masters Series: Stefan Sagmeister at the SVA Chelsea Gallery, New York (29 August - 12 October 2024). Further Interval projects named only on French Wikipedia -- Morphosis with Rocio Alvarez (KIKK Festival) and an installation with illustrator Jean Dalin for the Metal Hurlant fiftieth-anniversary exhibition -- are unverified (C2609). In the same period he made Futur(s) parallele(s), a site-specific installation in the Botanique's greenhouses in Brussels -- a diorama joining the imaginary of the romantic garden to cyberpunk, video games and science fiction -- dated 2024 by French Wikipedia and listed under 2025 on his own site (C2616). He is active into 2026: an immersive conference at CERN during the Antigel Festival, Geneva, with astrophysicist Laurent Chevalier, is announced for February 2026 (single-sourced, third-party events page).

## The turn: from the cold white line to the engraved wall, 2007-2012

His work has always run on one sentence -- video located in space -- but the image inside that sentence changes completely between 2007 and 2012, and the change has a date, a collaborator, and a quote. 3Destruct is the purest statement of the early AntiVJ manner: light as architecture you enter, geometry as the only image, a penetrable volume where spatial coherence is lost. Jacquet later said the piece was "assez froide" (quite cold) and that he felt a saturation with that minimal white-line digital aesthetic. The escape came through drawing. Cityscape 2095 (2011) pairs his 3D-modelled city with Mandril's ink, watercolour and wash drawn over it: "a constitue pour moi une forme d'echappee, hors de cette esthetique" -- an escape from the white-line aesthetic. The spectator stands "at the summit of a tower facing the horizon" and watches a day pass in fast-forward over an imaginary city, the drawn layer accumulating signs until the skyline overloads. Art21's reading: a "world city" where each country's visual identity clashes with the others, read through Lev Manovich's augmented space and Baudrillard's hyperreal -- the physical and virtual as "a contiguous, layered, and dynamic reality."

Mecaniques Discursives (2011) makes the escape a system: the hand-drawn layer is now a wood engraving, the digital model is now a wall, and the two are held together by projection. The working method, in Jacquet's own words: "We work very spontaneously, a bit like exquisite corpse... We create a bit like a jazz band. We know some little elements that work well together but the overall composition is improvised." The concept, in the artists' words: "Mecaniques Discursives is like a parenthesis between two epochs: Gutenberg's and Big Data's. By contrasting the oldest form of image reproduction (woodcutting) with the most recent digital technologies, the installation straddles centuries and contracts time." The press called it "a magical installation combines Rube Goldberg with shadow puppets" and "between Marcel Duchamp's The Large Glass and a ghost train." Fast Company, 2015: "futuristic and nostalgic... modern and anachronistic at the same time"; Penelle's personal mythology; "accidentally invented the new steampunk."

The same years produce the two most opposite works in the catalogue: Paleodictyon (2012), the monumental architecture piece in which the Pompidou-Metz's own hexagonal woven-timber roof becomes the screen for a work about scale -- tiny hexagonal burrows on the seabed and the museum's own hexagonal frame as the same pattern at two sizes -- and Ecume (2016), a hand-held concrete object, the album artwork for Vaquie's album, in which sound waves become a topographical relief you can hold. Between them sits the whole practice: the building as screen, the object as landscape.

## The practice: slowness, sensors, and the end of times

Three load-bearing ideas organise everything he makes after 2011, all in his own words or in the words of the people who write about him.

First, video belongs in space, not on screens. "His work is characterised by a desire to step outside of traditional projection formats and locate video in space" is the sentence every biography of him repeats, and it is the founding gesture of both AntiVJ and his practice. The refusal never changes: the flat screen is never the surface; the building, the wall, the volume, the barge, the greenhouse is.

Second, resilience and the end of times. "While the precise stratagem may vary, from the spectacular to the intimate, each undertaking is always rooted in the concept of resilience. This is art haunted by a discourse on the end of time. Jacquet makes no mystery of it" (Elytre press PDF). He names his parallels himself: Berlinde de Bruyckere's work on mutations in living matter, Ryoichi Kurokawa's stellar visions, Jean Tinguely's sardonic laugh and his 1960s machines designed to self-destruct. Entropy (2017) stages the idea literally -- the origin and end of the universe, real astronomical data from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey and the Dark Energy Survey visualised in real time, a team that had been working together since 2011 under the working title "Neutrino" (C2611, search-text only).

Third, slowness as a political response to the attention economy. In the early 2000s he believed in the utopian promise of networked technologies and free exchange; as personal data became commodified he shifted to "creating temporary spaces of deconditioning where attention can be cultivated and valued," and to "slowness, as a means of restoring sensitivity in our lives" (MadMapper trainer bio). Elytre is "designed less as an invitation to a journey as an order to slow down. To take the time for contemplation." Mecaniques Discursives offers "a pause, a suspension, a breath" against a world in which "attention... measurable and quantifiable, becomes a merchandise." Still Loading makes the same point from the other side, turning the humble loading spinner -- the emblem of our intolerance for waiting -- into a contemporary vanity, a memento mori for the digital age: "Ces loader sont le miroir de notre societe de l'information, qui produit et consomme des donnees a un rythme effrene" (these loaders are the mirror of our information society, which produces and consumes data at a frantic pace). "J'aimerais que mon projet soit vu comme une sorte de vanite contemporaine" -- I would like my project to be seen as a kind of contemporary vanity. And: "J'aimerais approfondir mes recherches sur la vanite et les liens entre le numerique et son histoire, en explorant la lenteur comme reponse a l'acceleration du monde induite par le numerique" -- exploring slowness as a response to the acceleration of the world induced by digital technology. The epigraph of the Mecaniques Discursives site is Bruno Munari, 1937 -- "A useless machine that does not represent anything is the perfect device through which we can easily revive our imagination, afflicted daily by useful machines" -- quoted by the project, not Jacquet's own words (C2619).

One negative finding belongs in the practice section, because it corrects a plausible assumption: Murcof is NOT a direct Jacquet collaborator (C2516). Murcof's documented AntiVJ partner is Simon Geilfus; Lemercier's 2011 "our collaboration with Murcof" is label-level only, and no Jacquet-named Murcof work was found. Do not claim one.

## The works: twenty entries, 2007-2025, plus a coda

- **W01 AntiVJ group exhibition, Centre of Contemporary Art, Geneva (10-20 April 2008):** the label's first public self-presentation as a group: Jacquet (Legoman), Romain Tardy (Aalto), Olivier Ratsi (Emovie) and Joanie Lemercier (Crustea), with music by D'incise. Four VJs presenting themselves not as club visuals but as a visual label, with mapping as the shared technique. No single Jacquet work is isolated in the record of this show; it is the collective origin point. Rests on a Vimeo compilation description (C2670). Note: W02 (2007) predates W01; the catalogue keeps leg 2's numbering.
- **W02 3Destruct (2007; toured 2007-2014):** audiovisual installation by Jacquet, Jeremie Peeters and Thomas Vaquie. "A space of visual and sonic deconstruction. Half way between light sculpture and 'penetrable' the installation offers an immersive experience in a non-linear world, where spatial coherence is lost." Designed for the Biennale d'art contemporain of Louvain-la-Neuve, 2007, then adapted to many sites: the ethnobotanical garden in Oaxaca (Proyecta, 2013), Yota Space Saint Petersburg, the Lieu Unique Nantes (Scopitone, October 2011), Kraftwerk Berlin (Berlin Atonal, 2013), Nuit Blanche Brussels (2014), Milano Design Week (2014), MUTEK Mexico (2013). The viewer walks inside the piece rather than facing it: a penetrable volume of projected light and sound in which the room's geometry stops reading coherently. 2007 is the creation year; 2011 and 2014 listings are tour dates (C2653). No image obtained: the AntiVJ press-photo directory returned HTTP 403 (C2663).
- **W03 Nuits Sonores, stage design + live visuals (23 May 2009, Lyon):** stage design and live visuals for the Nuits Sonores festival, by all four AntiVJ founders. The label's stage work at its most literal: a festival stage rebuilt as a mapping surface, with live visuals played in real time against the music. Rests on a Vimeo description (C2670).
- **W04 Songdo (August 2009, New Songdo City, South Korea):** audiovisual installation / architectural mapping. Art direction by Romain Tardy in collaboration with Jacquet, Ratsi and Lemercier; visuals by Jacquet and Lemercier; production Nabi Art Center, Seoul. The first-ever mapping installation in New Songdo City -- a city built from scratch on reclaimed land, sold as a "u-City" of ubiquitous computing. The inspected image shows a huge curved white facade above a glass frontage, washed with projected blocky white geometry -- stacked slabs and voids that re-cut the building's own lines -- while a seated audience watches from tables below. The subject is the city itself: "the importance of technology, the coexistence of the ancient and the new and the presence of the sea." The smart-city critique first appears here and returns, transformed, in Cityscape 2095.
- **W05 Grote Kerk, Breda (2009):** architectural mapping on the 25-metre-high baroque organ of the Grote Kerk, Netherlands, for the cultural night. AntiVJ collective project. The inspected image shows the organ from below in a dark vaulted church: tall gilded pipes rising into gothic ribs, warm light catching the gold -- the projection restrained, working with the instrument's own ornament rather than overwriting it. Jacquet's individual contribution is not pinned in any source read; catalogued as a collective work he stood inside, not as a solo piece (C2657).
- **W06 Intangible States (20 October 2010, Film Fest Gent, Vooruit, Ghent):** audiovisual performance by post-rock electronica duo Stray Dogs and Jacquet (Legoman). The two musicians sit on stage between 100 cardboard boxes that serve as TV screens; Jacquet projects images "that seem taken from a road movie" onto the boxes, and the visuals "actually interact with the music and the musicians." The mechanism is the cheap-surface trick at its most literal: a hundred identical throwaway boxes become a hundred small screens. Rests on a single 16-line festival preview; the 100-box mechanism is the programmer's description, not the artist's (C2671). No image obtained.
- **W07 Cityscape 2095 (first shown 19-29 May 2011, Mapping Festival, Geneva; Sundance New Frontier 2013):** audiovisual installation by Legoman (Jacquet, 3D animation), Mandril (Marc Ferrario, drawing) and Thomas Vaquie (sound design). The spectator stands "at the summit of a tower facing the horizon" and watches a day pass in fast-forward over an imaginary city. The method is the two-layer method in its first figurative form: Jacquet builds the city as 3D modelling; Mandril draws over it in ink, watercolour and wash, enhancing and masking the model. The inspected images show both layers at once: visitors before a tall screen filled with a dense blue-white futuristic skyline; a triptych of details showing the day's arc -- early hours sparse and line-based, a pagoda and towers accumulating signs, then full urban semiotic overload. The hinge of the whole career: the first figurative work, the first hand-drawn layer over the digital model, and the direct prototype for the Mecaniques Discursives method.
- **W08 Mecaniques Discursives (2011-2020; with Fred Penelle):** the core work of Jacquet's career. Penelle's woodcuts -- chimeras, owls in suits, plague doctors, tanks, hooded children, checkered trees, pseudo-data -- are printed, cut out, nailed and arranged across walls, floors and freestanding structures; Jacquet maps a bank of short animated videos onto them with (in the early specification) three ceiling-mounted projectors, 2,500 lumens each with 0.6 lenses, driven by a Mac mini through a Matrox TripleHead2Go. The projected light animates the still engravings: numbers flicker ("400", "800", "28%", "88%"), arrows and dotted lines connect figures into false itineraries, halos and rings pulse, small scenes play inside the printed frames. The inspected images show the range: LABoral, Gijon 2021 -- the largest indoor configuration, 700 m2, 12 m high, 15+ simultaneous projections, 250+ narrative elements, two visitors dwarfed by a wall of owl, plague-doctor, tanks and checkered trees; Perte de Signal, Montreal 2016 -- the first use of digital 3D optical illusions after Renaissance perspective; Centquatre, Paris 2015-16 -- the first complete spatial environment, cut-out figures standing on the floor among cubes. Creation 2011 vs "created in 2013" in two press PDFs (C2654); first showing 12 May 2012, Mapping Festival Geneva. Scale counts differ by source (C2612, C2655).
- **W09 Paleodictyon (6 October 2012, Centre Pompidou-Metz):** architectural projection mapping by Simon Geilfus, Jacquet and Thomas Vaquie; visual content by Geilfus, Jacquet and Romain Tardy; music Vaquie. Commissioned by Nuit Blanche Metz and Centre Pompidou-Metz. The Pompidou-Metz's hexagonal woven-timber roof becomes the screen for a piece about scale: loosely inspired by deep-sea expert Peter A. Rona's research into Paleodictyon nodosum -- unknown creatures that leave hexagonal marks on the seabed -- the piece "abolishes notions of scale by contrasting micro-architecture with human construction." A digital counterpart, Paleodictyon -- The App, was released alongside. Videos not watched; no image downloaded (C2662).
- **W10 Collective architectural mappings (2009-2012): Enghien, Saint Gervais, Square, Scopitone 2010:** a run of AntiVJ collective mappings, named in the label's project lists but not individually documented in any source read end to end. One is partly pinned: the Scopitone 2010 mapping in Nantes, a large-scale projection on the restored castle, with 2D/3D animation by Romain Tardy in collaboration with Jacquet, Lemercier and Simon Geilfus. Catalogued as one entry because the record treats them as one list; do not split without new sources (C2672). No image obtained.
- **W11 Ecume (2016; AntiVJ Recordings):** album artwork / object for Thomas Vaquie's album Ecume -- the first release on AntiVJ Recordings. Jacquet analysed Vaquie's tracks with a custom digital visualisation tool, turning each song's waveform into a "sonic landscape" -- a topographical relief -- then 3D-printed it and cast it in concrete via a silicone mould (limited edition of 25 dark resin versions from the same mould). The inspected images: the cover, a dark concrete disc with a bright white centre and fine radial ridges fanning out like a lunar surface; the physical object, dark resin slabs with fine grooves, one sliding out of a sleeve. The album's tracks were themselves composed for AntiVJ site-specific installations, each "a response to place and space, to light and architecture, to code and motion" -- so the object closes a loop: installation music becomes a landscape you can hold. The only object-work in the catalogue.
- **W12 Elytre (also called Flow) (2016; three years in the making; permanent):** permanent generative installation, 40 metres long, on Le Flow -- a floating building moored at the foot of the Pont Alexandre III, Paris, on the Seine. Concept, design, animation: Jacquet; software: Eric Renaud-Houde, Simon Geilfus; music: Thomas Vaquie; production Nicolas Boritch. A metal mesh with its own unique structure is fastened over the cladding from roof to hull, creating a pointillist effect from "an infinite palette of pixels"; LEDs are pushed beyond their usual capacities to get nuance and shading of colour. The work runs on dedicated custom software and a battery of sensors -- time of year, season, atmospheric pressure, wind speed, temperature -- so it "varies according to" the weather and the calendar and is "in a constant state of flux, permanently subject to imperceptible shifts." The inspected image shows the barge at night from across the river: long horizontal bands of white, pink and blue light across the dark hull, shimmering and doubling on the Seine. Name variance: the Vimeo pages call the Le Flow work Elytre; the Elytre PDF text calls the 2016 generative work "Flow" (C2677). Treat 2016-2017 as the window.
- **W13 Remote Memories (2017):** audiovisual installation; polyptych in panoramic format, a large canvas of video and sound. Concept, design, animation: Jacquet; music: Before Tigers (Laurent Delforge). A wide, low panoramic canvas in a dark industrial room -- the inspected image shows it as a long band of vertical slats glowing blue-grey, textured like weather or static, with a single bench set before it in an empty concrete hall. The image "seems fixed at first" but is "criss-crossed by almost imperceptible waves, like a brownian movement that shakes a gas's particles": textures superimposed and interlaced, "a sort of 'sfumato video'" with neither line nor contour. The soundtrack runs through 11 vibration speakers that use the canvas's wooden panels as a sound box. Crucially, there is "no question of audio-reactivity here": image and sound "evolve at their own rhythm, mixing, losing ground, drifting, letting chance and coincidence create new interactions endlessly." ## M1: The penetrable volume -- light as architecture you enter

The founding mechanism, stated in 3Destruct and never abandoned: build a volume of projected light that the viewer walks inside, so the room's geometry stops reading coherently. Translucent volumes crossed by luminous projections and a sound environment; "half way between light sculpture and 'penetrable'"; spatial coherence lost. The viewer is not before the image but in it, and the disorientation is the work -- the building is the screen, the body is the camera. Songdo scales it to a city; Paleodictyon scales it to a museum roof; Elytre scales it to a 40-metre barge flank. The mechanism is visible in the work: you can always tell the projection is doing the architecture's job, and the pleasure is the building misbehaving.

**Takeaway:** a volume of light the viewer enters, where the projection is the architecture and disorientation is the point; the room stops being a room.

## M2: The two-layer method -- hand drawing over the digital model

The hinge mechanism, first in Cityscape 2095, then the system of Mecaniques Discursives: make the picture twice. First a digital model -- a 3D city, a wall of engraved figures. Then a hand-drawn layer over it -- Mandril's ink and wash over the skyline, Penelle's woodcuts over the wall. Then a projected light layer that animates the drawn layer: numbers flicker, arrows connect figures into false itineraries, halos pulse, small scenes play inside printed frames. The three layers are independent: the model is the place, the drawing is the story, the light is the weather. The escape from the cold white-line minimalism of 3Destruct is the point: the hand is what warms the machine. Retable compresses the method into a single altarpiece; PLAY breaks it into portable boxes; Sarabande puts singers inside it.

**Takeaway:** a digital base, a hand-drawn layer, a projected animation -- three pictures at once, where the drawing is the escape from the machine's coldness and the projection is the life in the drawing.

## M3: The chain-reaction machine -- Gutenberg against Big Data

The narrative mechanism of Mecaniques Discursives: a Rube Goldberg chain reaction that never resolves, "an anti-machine" whose stagnant objects imply movement through their shadows. Engraved figures -- owls in suits, plague doctors, tanks, hooded children, checkered trees -- are arranged like an exquisite corpse, improvised like a jazz band: "We know some little elements that work well together but the overall composition is improvised." The projections supply the false logic: percentages, arrows, dotted itineraries, sonar rings, alarms -- data-design graphics that pretend the absurd machinery means something. The concept is the parenthesis: "Mecaniques Discursives is like a parenthesis between two epochs: Gutenberg's and Big Data's." The oldest image reproduction against the newest digital technology, the machine that produces nothing but its own narration. The exhibition's only instruction -- "Don't follow the guide" -- is the mechanism stated as a rule: there is no single narrative, and following one is the mistake.

**Takeaway:** a wall of still engravings animated by false data -- percentages, arrows, itineraries -- into a machine that narrates without resolving; the chain reaction is the story, and the story never ends.

## M4: The sensor-driven permanent -- weather as the timeline

The generative mechanism of Elytre: replace the timeline with sensors. A 40-metre skin of light on a barge's flank, running on custom software and a battery of sensors -- time of year, season, atmospheric pressure, wind speed, temperature -- so the work "varies according to" the weather and the calendar and is "in a constant state of flux, permanently subject to imperceptible shifts." A metal mesh over the cladding creates a pointillist effect from "an infinite palette of pixels"; LEDs are pushed beyond their usual capacities for nuance of colour. The work never repeats and never leaves: a building that breathes with the weather instead of a show that tours. The artist's framing: the piece reverses "the overall structural inertia of the barge's four hundred tons of steel"; the dark mass of the barge between sky and river, the instability and permanence of flowing water. It is the direct ancestor of the slowness language that dominates the later practice, and the first time the sensor, not the timeline, drives the image.

**Takeaway:** a permanent work with no timeline -- sensors and seasons drive the image, so the piece never repeats; the weather is the animator and the building is the screen.

## M5: Slowness as programme -- the sfumato video

The contemplative mechanism, stated in Remote Memories and named in the program: a panoramic band of video that "seems fixed at first" but is "criss-crossed by almost imperceptible waves, like a brownian movement that shakes a gas's particles" -- textures superimposed and interlaced, "a sort of 'sfumato video'" with neither line nor contour, glimmers and colours emerging and disappearing "as if glimpsed through thick fog." The soundtrack runs through 11 vibration speakers that use the canvas's wooden panels as a sound box. And the rule that makes it a mechanism rather than a mood: "There is no question of audio-reactivity here since the images and sounds evolve at their own rhythm, mixing, losing ground, drifting, letting chance and coincidence create new interactions endlessly." No sync, no trigger, no climax: two independent rhythms drifting against each other, and the viewer's job is to sit on the bench and let the drift happen. The invitation is explicit: "to pause and apprehend the impossibility of immobility in an age where data is overabundant." Elytre is the monumental version of the same rule -- an order to slow down -- and Still Loading is the polemical version: the spinner as a demand for patience.

**Takeaway:** an image that barely moves, a sound that never syncs to it, and a bench -- two rhythms drifting by chance, where the viewer's attention is the interaction and waiting is the work.

## M6: Sound made into matter -- the waveform as landscape

The one object-work in the catalogue, and the material opposite of everything else he makes. For Vaquie's album Ecume, Jacquet analysed the tracks with a custom digital visualisation tool, turning each song's waveform into a "sonic landscape" -- a topographical relief -- then 3D-printed it and cast it in concrete via a silicone mould. The cover is a dark concrete disc with a bright white centre and fine radial ridges fanning out like a lunar surface or a fingerprint. The loop it closes: the album's tracks were composed for AntiVJ site-specific installations, each "a response to place and space, to light and architecture, to code and motion" -- so installation music becomes a landscape you can hold. The label's stated position: against music "forced... to be augmented or justified by visual impact", the release reaffirms "the capacity held within the medium of sound itself to feed the imagination" -- in a physical object. Where the light works are immaterial, this one is concrete; where they vanish when the power is cut, this one stays.

**Takeaway:** a song's waveform turned into a topographical relief and cast in concrete -- sound as a landscape you can hold, the one work that survives the power cut.

## M7: The machine becomes theatre -- wall, altarpiece, boxes, choir

The compression mechanism of the late MD strand: the same system, smaller and more social at each step. The wall-filling machine of Mecaniques Discursives becomes Retable (2019), a secular altarpiece -- a stepped triptych of black-framed boxes, the bestiary inside, projected rings rippling across the wall behind like a halo. Then PLAY (from 2019), the system made tourable: modular lit boxes, each a diorama, the figures now drawn from interviews with local residents, so each stop re-authors the cast. Then Don't Follow the Guide! (since 2021), the whole universe as a 360-degree narrative labyrinth you walk through. Then Sarabande (2019 work, 2025 performance): the figures step off the wall entirely and the audience's place is taken by a choir -- three singers in black geometric armour, early sacred music against contemporary writing, "a space where past and future overlap, where folklore meets science fiction." Same scenographic instinct as the festival stages of 2009, now with live voices instead of live visuals: the machine that began as club visuals ends as theatre.

**Takeaway:** one system compressed step by step -- wall, altarpiece, travelling boxes, choir -- where each compression adds a social layer and the machine ends up staging people, not pictures.

## M8: The loading spinner as vanity -- waiting as the subject

The polemical mechanism of Still Loading (first version 2024): collect the loading animations of the digital age -- spinners, progress bars, percentages -- and present them as a contemporary vanity, a memento mori. The inspected image is a black field tiled with roughly 400 tiny spinners and progress bars (5%, 18%, 56%), with a few gold coins and rings, an hourglass, a wine glass, a sword and a diamond scattered among them, each cell labelled with a small code -- the vanity symbols (skull, watch, candle, shell, coins, hourglass, globe) translated into interface elements. "Ces loader sont le miroir de notre societe de l'information" -- these loaders are the mirror of our information society. The mechanism is the wait itself: the spinner promises the thing is coming, and the piece never delivers it; the intolerance for waiting is the subject, and slowness is the answer -- "exploring slowness as a response to the acceleration of the world induced by digital technology." The first version was three vertical screens, a contemporary retable; the future developments named on his site are low-tech means -- printing, drawing on paper, Arduino, plotter, traditional or dynamic lighting. The vanity, like the machine in M3, does nothing useful -- and that is the point.

**Takeaway:** four hundred loading spinners as a vanity painting -- the wait is the image, the promise that never resolves is the mechanism, and patience is the interaction.

## The aesthetic logic: the deconditioning room

Jacquet's aesthetic is a twenty-year refusal of the flat screen, and it has three terms. First, the surface is always something else: a volume you enter, a building, a barge, a wall of engravings, a greenhouse, a bench-facing band of fog -- never a rectangle that shows a picture. The image is located in space, and the space is the work. Second, the image is always double: a digital base and a hand-drawn layer, an old reproduction and a new machine, a song and a concrete cast, a sacred repertoire and a speculative future -- the two terms never merge and never leave, and the seam between them is where the meaning sits. Third, the energy is always the ethics: the attention economy is the enemy, slowness the response, and the work's job is to make a room where attention can be cultivated -- a temporary space of deconditioning. The work is at its best when all three terms are present at once -- a real place, a doubled image, an honest slowness -- as in Elytre, where the barge breathes with the weather and the order is to slow down, or in Remote Memories, where the bench and the barely-moving band ask for nothing but time. It is at its thinnest when the technique runs ahead of the subject, as in the early club mappings, which are precise and cold -- his own word for 3Destruct -- or the collective mapping lists, which nobody wrote up. The evolution, stated as one sentence: he spent the first years learning to put video on buildings, the middle decade learning to put a hand-drawn image inside the machine, and the last years learning to make rooms that ask you to sit down and wait -- until only the light, the weather, and the attention are left.
