## M1. The readymade: choice over making

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. Sixty years FAILS: documented Johns engagement with Duchamp and the Large Glass runs 1958 to 1986, 28 years. See the Doorway. C2. "Myself Torn to Pieces" is Johns's tentative memory, not a confirmed Duchamp title. See the Doorway. C3. Why Not Sneeze, Rose Selavy? contains 152 marble cubes, not a couple of dozen small cubes of marble as the main Wikipedia article says. Confirmed by the PMA object page, the MoMA 2026 extended labels, and the dedicated article, all of which say 152. C4. The MoMA 2026 extended labels give the Society of Independent Artists exhibition opening as April 9, 1917. The standard account, and The Blind Man No. 1 dated 10 April 1917, give the opening as Tuesday 10 April 1917. The culturedmag account, snippet-sourced, says 9 April was the day before the opening, the delivery day. Use Tuesday 10 April 1917 for the opening. C5. Britannica says the Salon des Independants committee refused to hang Nude Descending a Staircase No. 2 in February 1912. Wikipedia and Cabanne say there was no jury, Albert Gleizes asked the brothers to withdraw it voluntarily, and Duchamp took it home himself in a taxi. Use the voluntary withdrawal version. C6. Tate Papers, line 92, dates Etant donnes 1944 to 64. Wrong on both ends. The standard dating, per the Philadelphia Museum of Art and Taylor, is 1946 to 1966. See also C28. C7. The Box of 1914: Tate says 16 notes plus 1 drawing, To Have the Apprentice in the Sun, the MoMA 2026 labels say fifteen mat boards. The two counts conflict and neither is corroborated by the other source. State the conflict rather than picking a number. C8. The Boite-en-valise: Britannica says 68 miniature reproductions, The Art Story, Yale and the MoMA labels say 69. Use 69, the majority figure, and note the discrepancy. C9. Britannica's FAQ says He moved to New York in 1942. Misleading. Duchamp first moved to New York in 1915, arriving Tuesday 15 June 1915, and settled in Greenwich Village in 1942. Use the two-date version. C10. The Philadelphia Museum of Art page "Marcel Duchamp, Curator" misprints his dates as 1889 to 1967. Correct dates: born 1887, died 1968. C11. The Tate Large Glass diagram lists the Malic Moulds as 11a, 11b, 11d to 11i. Number 11c is missing: eight are named of the nine Bachelors. The Tate diagram names the cuirassier, gendarme, delivery boy, bus boy, priest, undertaker, station master and policeman. Do not present the diagram as a complete list of nine. C12. Maria Martins: the Wikipedia infobox gives the affair as 1946 to 1951, Taylor says the affair lasted until 1950. The end date is disputed. Use 1946 to about 1950 and note the dispute. C13. The MoMA Floor 5 text says the readymade term first appeared in a letter Duchamp wrote in 1915 to his sister Suzanne. The letter is dated 15 January 1916. Use 1916. C14. Dennis Young says the Large Glass was placed in its final position at the Philadelphia Museum of Art in 1954. The PMA says Duchamp placed it in the gallery in 1952, 1953 to 1954 was the installation of the Arensberg collection. Use 1952. C15. Wikipedia's main article says the Bicycle Wheel was an idea of Elsa von Freytag-Loringhoven. This is a contentious, unattributed claim. The Art Story notes the 2014 Fountain authorship claim for the Baroness has been disproven. Do not repeat the Bicycle Wheel attribution as fact. C16. The Cabanne Dialogues chronology as reproduced on archive.org, OCR text, says Duchamp died October 1, 1968. The standard account is that he died in the early morning of Wednesday 2 October 1968, having retired at 1:05 am. Use 2 October 1968. C17. The Art Story Duchamp says the Large Glass theory was called the Retinal Shudder. The wording is not verified against Duchamp's own terms, Duchamp's term is retinal art, as opposed to art that serves the mind. Treat Retinal Shudder as The Art Story's wording, not Duchamp's. C18. A Zenodo paper says Etant donnes took 23 years to build. From 1946 to 1966 is 20 years. Use 20 years, and see C28 on the start date. C19. The size of the 1917 Society of Independent Artists show: Cabinet says 2,125 works of art by 1,200 artists, a Boston article of 25 April 1917 says 2500 paintings and sculptures submitted. The two figures conflict. State both or neither, not one as settled. C20. Fountain delivery: culturedmag says Monday 9 April 1917, the day before the opening, Cabinet says Two days before the scheduled opening, which would be Sunday 8 April. The delivery date is disputed by one day. Use 9 April with the Cabinet variant noted. C21. Rrose Selavy's emergence: The Art Story says Fresh Widow, 1920, marks the debut, Wikipedia says Selavy emerged in 1921 in a series of photographs by Man Ray, Duchamp himself said she was born in 1920. Reconcile as: the name first appears on Fresh Widow in 1920, the Man Ray photographs follow in 1921. C22. A snippet-sourced claim says Duchamp was 28 in 1915 when he arrived in New York. He was 27: born 28 July 1887, arrived 15 June 1915. C23. Britannica says In 1918 he sold The Large Glass to Walter Arensberg. Wikipedia frames it as the Arensbergs being his landlords for two years, with the payment being the Large Glass. The two framings differ, the rent-payment version is the better sourced one. Do not present it as a simple sale. C24. The Boite-en-valise: the MoMA labels say more than three hundred in seven series, A through G, The Art Story says one of twenty-four editions. Reconcile: 24 is the number of deluxe Series A boxes made through 1949, more than 300 is the total across all seven series. Use both figures with the distinction. C25. First Papers of Surrealism, 1942: sixteen miles of string was claimed, the actual amount was far less. The installation is commonly called a mile of string or His Twine. Use claimed as sixteen miles, in fact far less. C26. The Large Glass work dates: 1915 to 1923 is the standard dating for the glass itself, Britannica's Between 1913 and 1923 covers the studies as well as the glass, since the Glass was imagined and drawn in 1913 and 1914. Use 1915 to 1923 for the glass, 1913 to 1923 for the project including studies. C27. CHECKED, CONSISTENT, NOT A CORRECTION: the Etant donnes dimensions, 242.6 x 124.5 x 177.8 cm, Taylor, are the same numbers Dennis Young lists, in a different order. No discrepancy. C28. Etant donnes start date is given three ways: the MoMA 2026 labels say the work was developed between 1944 and 1966, the Wikipedia list of works says 1949 to 1966, the Philadelphia Museum of Art and Taylor give 1946 to 1966. Use 1946 to 1966, the museum's own dating. Treat 1944 as the start of the early studies and 1949 as an outlier. C29. To Be Looked at, from the Other Side of the Glass, with One Eye, Close to, for Almost an Hour: the MoMA 2026 labels date it to Buenos Aires, 1918, the Wikipedia list of works says 1920. Use 1918, the MoMA dating, and note the list's variant. C30. 50 cc of Paris Air: the title names 50 cc, but the ampoule actually contains more than twice that volume of air, MoMA labels. The title was conceived in 1937, when a photograph of the work was published in a set of Surrealist postcards, the work itself was made in 1919. Do not present the title as a measurement. C31. L.H.O.O.Q.: the 1919 original is in a private collection, per the Wikipedia list of works. The version most often reproduced is the 1930 collotype held by the Museum Boijmans Van Beuningen. Do not conflate the two. C32. Rotary Demisphere, 1925: the Wikipedia list of works places it at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, not MoMA, although the MoMA 2026 labels discuss it, the exhibition presumably shows a loan or a replica. State the Met as the holding. C33. Comb, 1916: the Wikipedia list of works locates it at the Philadelphia Museum of Art. Some references place a Comb at the Metropolitan Museum of Art. The list's claim is used here, the holding should be confirmed against a second source before it is stated as settled in the study file. C34. In Advance of the Broken Arm: the snow shovel purchased in early 1945 for the Yale exhibition was a replica bought from a New York hardware store, not the 1915 original. Do not date the Yale replica as 1915. C35. Fountain replicas: the first 1:1 replica was authorised in 1950 at Sidney Janis's request, and the Philadelphia Museum of Art holds that 1950 replica. The edition of eight dates to 1964. Do not conflate the 1950 replica with the 1964 edition. C36. Bicycle Wheel replica: the 1951 replica at MoMA was made at Sidney Janis's request, Carroll Janis recalled the wheel was sent from Europe and the kitchen stool was bought by his father in Brooklyn. The MoMA object is the 1951 replica, Sidney and Harriet Janis Collection, entered 1967. Do not describe it as the 1913 original.
