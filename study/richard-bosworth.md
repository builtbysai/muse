## M1. The pretence of great power

Italy the Least of the Great Powers: Italy "pretending to be a Great Power in Europe, despite poor geography and resources", and "this pretence has taken its toll" (first book, 1973, p. 2, carried into the 1979 thesis book). The pretence is the mechanism, because a country that acts a part it cannot play pays for the acting, and the paying is the piece, and the piece must show a small country wearing a uniform three sizes too big, the seams splitting as it marches, because the pretence is not a lie told to others but a cost borne at home.

**Takeaway:** show the uniform, not the country, and the piece should let the seams split.

## M2. The figurehead king

Victor Emmanuel III "was merely a figurehead" (Least of the Great Powers, p. 17). The figurehead is the mechanism, because a crowned figure who signs whatever is put in front of him lets the real decisions happen in the next room, and the next room is the piece, and the piece must show a throne that is a desk and a signature that is a rubber stamp, the king present and the power absent.

**Takeaway:** put the crown on the empty chair, and the piece should show the signing, not the ruling.

## M3. The one-man foreign policy

Italy and the Approach of the First World War: Foreign Minister Antonio di San Giuliano "single-handedly controlled Italian foreign policy" and rejected Italian responsibility to honour the Triple Alliance, with decision makers largely uninfluenced by external pressures, including a public who wanted peace. The single hand is the mechanism, because a whole country's foreign relations narrowed to one desk and one pen means the state has no face of its own, and the narrowing is the piece, and the piece must show one minister, one pen, and a map of Europe folded to fit the desk.

**Takeaway:** narrow the whole country to one desk, and the piece should show the one hand.

## M4. Legal Italy vs real Italy

Italy and the Wider World: the discrepancy between "legal" Italy, the bureaucracy, and "real" Italy, the population, "especially concerning 'dreams of empire'". The discrepancy is the mechanism, because two maps of the same country that do not line up means the official country and the lived country are different places, and the difference is the piece, and the piece must show the two maps laid over each other, the borders sliding apart.

**Takeaway:** lay the two maps over each other, and the piece should show the gap between them.

## M5. The paese as a world

"Each paese can be seen as 'a world-ranging community' or simply a village" (Wider World, p. 3). The paese is the mechanism, because a village that opens into a whole world when you look closely, and shrinks to a dot when you step back, is the same place at two scales at once, and the two scales are the piece, and the piece must zoom into a village until it becomes a world, then zoom out until it is a dot again, without ever changing the place.

**Takeaway:** zoom in until the village is a world, and the piece should hold both scales at once.

## M6. Many Italies

"Contradictory images from Italy's past and Italy's present, [which] jostles for attention" (Wider World, p. 2); "there are many Italies to be pondered, both in the scholarship of historian or of other experts and in the popular mind". The crowd is the mechanism, because a country that is many countries at once, each demanding to be the real one, can never be drawn as one thing, and the jostling is the piece, and the piece must show a crowd of Italies in one frame, each elbowing the others for the title.

**Takeaway:** let the Italies jostle, and the piece should show the crowd, not the one.

## M7. History as a debate without end

Explaining Auschwitz and Hiroshima: history is "a debate without end", and societies historicise the war in "comfortable" or "mad" ways. The debate is the mechanism, because the same event retold by six countries, six different stories, none of them closing, means the past is not settled by being written but kept open by being argued, and the openness is the piece, and the piece must show one event told six ways, the tellings overlapping and contradicting, and no version winning.

**Takeaway:** tell it six ways and close none of them, and the piece should keep the argument open.

## M8. The long Second World War

The crisis of confidence in liberalism and the nation-state that by the end of 1938 had left only Britain, France, the Low Countries and Scandinavia preserving liberal freedoms (History and Theory, 1999). The long war is the mechanism, because a war that does not end in 1945 but keeps running, underground, for forty-five years, means the dates on the treaty are not the dates of the experience, and the running-on is the piece, and the piece must show a war whose end is a rumour, the fighting continuing in the footnotes and the memories long after the surrender.

**Takeaway:** let the war outlive its ending, and the piece should show the running-on.

## M9. The anti-anti-Fascist charge

The Italian Dictatorship, p. 23: Renzo De Felice and Emilio Gentile are "anti-anti-Fascist", failing to condemn Italian Fascism. The charge is the mechanism, because a historian pointing at two colleagues and naming their silence divides the room, and the division is the piece, and the piece must show the pointing finger and the silence it names, the room splitting into those who condemn and those who will not.

**Takeaway:** name the silence, and the piece should show the room dividing.

## M10. The moral duty of scholarship

Recent scholarship "deflected the field away from its moral and political purpose, which is to be vigilant against renewed fascism and protective of anti-fascism", blaming cultural relativism and postmodernism (The Italian Dictatorship). The duty is the mechanism, because a scholar who treats neutrality as a failure puts a duty on the desk beside the books, and the duty is the piece, and the piece must show a desk with a duty attached to it, the historian writing with one eye on the past and one on the present danger.

**Takeaway:** put the duty on the desk, and the piece should show the writing as a watch.

## M11. The culturalist warning

Culturalist historians "in their determination to be apolitical and to treat Fascism on its own terms... often credulously report what Fascism said rather than critically exploring what it meant" (quoted in Nelis 2006 from The Italian Dictatorship, p. 27). The warning is the mechanism, because a poster quoted as evidence and then interrogated is two different objects, what it said and what it meant, and the difference is the piece, and the piece must show the poster first as Fascism presented it, then as a historian reads it, the same image changing meaning between the two viewings.

**Takeaway:** quote the poster, then interrogate it, and the piece should show the meaning change.

## M12. Biography against the great man

Mussolini (2002) is critical of "the great man in history" and intentionalist approaches; Mussolini was "more driven by, and adaptive to, events than driven by them" (Australian Book Review). The tide is the mechanism, because a leader being pushed by events instead of pushing them reverses the usual biography, and the reversal is the piece, and the piece must show the tide moving the man, the dictator surfing the wave he claims to command.

**Takeaway:** let the tide move the man, and the piece should show the surfing, not the steering.

## M13. The opportunist, not the ideologue

"Unlike Hitler, the Duce was impelled by no credo, his Fascism was vague, undefined and opportunistic, his politics governed not by ideology but by compromises and deals, by short-term tactics not long-term strategies and goals" (Australian Book Review). The shapelessness is the mechanism, because a man with no fixed shape changes colour with each room he enters, and the changing is the piece, and the piece must show a figure who is a different colour in every room, the same man and no man at all.

**Takeaway:** give him no fixed shape, and the piece should show the colour changing.

## M14. Opening at the end

Mussolini (2002) "begins with Mussolini's final two years, then explains how he got there" (Wikipedia). The rewind is the mechanism, because a story that starts at the corpse on the lakeshore and rewinds makes the ending the question and the life the answer, and the rewinding is the piece, and the piece must start at the end and walk backwards, each step explaining the step after it.

**Takeaway:** start at the corpse and rewind, and the piece should make the ending the question.

## M15. The counterfactual hinge

Mussolini "might have entered history as a figure of some light and some darkness", had he retired in 1932 (Mussolini, p. 264). The hinge is the mechanism, because a door in 1932, one step through it, and the whole life changes colour, and the door is the piece, and the piece must show the door standing open in 1932, the two histories on either side of it, the light one and the dark one, and the man pausing at the threshold.

**Takeaway:** put a door in 1932, and the piece should show both histories at once.

## M16. The impossible total history

Mussolini's Italy: "Aspiring to write the total history of a totalitarian society is a delusion"; the book is an "'impossible project': 'to unveil the lives of Italians' from all walks of life 'under a generation of dictatorship'". The impossibility is the mechanism, because a historian admitting the map cannot cover the territory, and drawing it anyway, makes the attempt itself the subject, and the attempt is the piece, and the piece must show the map with its holes in it, the territory spilling out at the edges, and the drawing going on regardless.

**Takeaway:** draw the map with its holes in it, and the piece should show the attempt, not the coverage.

## M17. The everyday under dictatorship

Statistics and anecdotes of poverty: illiteracy pervasive "even into the families of future leaders"; the functional air force "about a tenth of its strength on paper" (Frumious Consortium). The dissolving is the mechanism, because a paper strength that dissolves into a tenth of itself when counted shows the regime as a ledger that does not add up, and the not-adding-up is the piece, and the piece must show the numbers on the page melting as they are counted, the air force shrinking in the counting.

**Takeaway:** count the paper strength and watch it shrink, and the piece should show the ledger failing.

## M18. Rule by ringing pronouncement

Mussolini ruled "by issuing aggressive pronouncements, leaving his underlings 'to interpret just what the ringing words "really" meant'" (Mussolini's Italy, p. 354). The shattering is the mechanism, because a loud sentence that leaves the dictator's mouth and shatters into a dozen different orders in a dozen offices means the command is not one thing but many, and the shattering is the piece, and the piece must show the sentence leaving the mouth whole and arriving in the offices broken, each office holding a different shard.

**Takeaway:** let the sentence shatter on arrival, and the piece should show the shards.

## M19. The beehive and its capture

The Fascist image of the beehive, "everyone knowing their place and all working together in harmony", and the beekeepers' association arguing that its importance to the nation deserved "special subsidies and promotions" (Frumious Consortium). The capture is the mechanism, because a slogan designed for the nation is immediately claimed by a lobby for its own subsidy, and the claiming is the piece, and the piece must show the beehive on the poster and the beekeepers at the ministry door, the same image serving the nation and the subsidy at once.

**Takeaway:** show the poster and the queue at the ministry, and the piece should let the lobby claim the slogan.

## M20. The gulf between rhetoric and reality

"The Fascist revolution was never anywhere near as complete as the rulers liked to claim"; the conclusion: "every one of the great slogans of Fascism had turned out to be false" (Mussolini's Italy, pp. 571-72). The marking-false is the mechanism, because a row of grand slogans, each one quietly marked false, one by one, is the whole regime judged in a list, and the list is the piece, and the piece must show the slogans standing in a row and the marks appearing beside them, false, false, false, until the row is a ruin.

**Takeaway:** line the slogans up and mark them false, and the piece should show the row becoming a ruin.

## M21. Charisma as a thin principle

"Mussolini's charisma was a sufficient principle for seizing power, but as the Fascist years wore on, it became an increasingly thin one for organizing public life" (Frumious Consortium); and the charisma passage (pp. 354-55) connects Mussolini to Berlusconi and TV celebrities: "Celebrities, beneath the glitter, are often people of no account. Only their dazzle matters." The thinning is the mechanism, because a bright light that is enough to take a stage and not enough to run a country fades as the work piles up, and the fading is the piece, and the piece must show the dazzle holding at the moment of seizure and thinning to nothing in the years of rule, the same light, two thicknesses.

**Takeaway:** show the dazzle thinning, and the piece should measure the light, not the man.

## M22. The first modern dictator as measure

"As I explore Mussolini's personality, his power, its effects and its limitations, I became convinced that the Duce was not just the first modern dictator but also, far better than Hitler, the personage against whom to measure the very many tyrants who dominated so many countries in Europe between the wars" (Mussolini, 2nd edition, quoted in Wikiquote). The yardstick is the mechanism, because a man in the shape of a ruler, held up against every later tyrant, turns a biography into a measuring instrument, and the measuring is the piece, and the piece must show the yardstick laid against one tyrant after another, each one coming up short or long against the first.

**Takeaway:** hold the yardstick up, and the piece should show the measuring, not the man.

## M23. The city as palimpsest of competing pasts

Whispering City: the city's "conflicting pasts... continuously rearranging themselves as one or other faction achieved control over the narrative". The rearranging is the mechanism, because layers of a city peeling and re-sticking as each new regime rewrites the street means the stones stay and the story changes, and the changing is the piece, and the piece must show the layers of Rome peeling and re-sticking, the same stones carrying a different past each time a faction takes the narrative.

**Takeaway:** peel the layers and re-stick them, and the piece should show the stones staying and the story changing.

## M24. The overlooked site as doorway

Whispering City: "each chapter beginning by drawing the reader's attention to a site most people overlook (the Napoleonic Museum is the first stop) and drawing out its significance" (Times Higher Education); each chapter opens with a map of the area. The small door is the mechanism, because a small, ignored museum door opening onto two hundred years means the history is hiding in the places nobody visits, and the opening is the piece, and the piece must start at the overlooked site, the quiet room, the museum nobody enters, and let the whole city unfold from it.

**Takeaway:** start at the door nobody opens, and the piece should let the city unfold from it.

## M25. The oscillation of church and state

The Holy Year of 1933 was "timed in order to reclaim the limelight after the 10th-anniversary festivities of 1932 in honour of the 'Fascist Revolution'"; government and church were "competing to upstage each other through spectacular popular entertainment" (Times Higher Education). The oscillation is the mechanism, because two spotlights swinging back and forth across the same square, each trying to outshine the other, means the city is a stage fought over in light, and the swinging is the piece, and the piece must show the two lights trading the square, the church's festival answering the regime's anniversary, each brighter than the last.

**Takeaway:** swing the two spotlights, and the piece should show the square fought over in light.

## M26. The victorious history vs the warts-and-all history

Italian Venice: a "history war" between "the prettifiers of Venice's history and those who seek to illuminate it warts and all"; "The victorious history has much to be said for it. It portrays a shiningly beautiful Venice that glitters in the sunshine... Married thus indissolubly to beauty, this history allures and sells." The war is the mechanism, because two Venices on the same canal, one glittering for sale and one grubby and real, means the same city is two books, and the two books are the piece, and the piece must show the glittering Venice and the warts-and-all Venice side by side, the same canal carrying both.

**Takeaway:** put the two Venices on one canal, and the piece should show the history war.

## M27. Two cities in one

"Two Venices co-exist throughout this book: the Venice of partying along the Grand Canal and the Venice of an underemployed local population locked in poor housing" (Blue Guides); 40 percent of the population supplementing their diet with molluscs picked at low tide from polluted rocks in 1933. The two floors are the mechanism, because a palazzo party above and a family eating shellfish from polluted mud below is the same city, two floors apart, and the apartness is the piece, and the piece must show the party above and the mud below in the same frame, the music from the palazzo reaching the family at the waterline.

**Takeaway:** show the party above and the mud below, and the piece should hold both floors in one frame.

## M28. The family as prism

Claretta: the Petaccis as "a bourgeois family on the make, using Claretta's connections to the leader to gain favour, promotion, riches and the trappings of fame"; the Amendolas: "Through the prism of a single, exceptional family, the Amendolas, R.J.B. Bosworth reveals the heart of twentieth-century Italian politics" (Cambridge University Press). The prism is the mechanism, because a whole century of politics refracted through one family's dining table means the private is the political seen close up, and the refraction is the piece, and the piece must show the century arriving at the dining table, the family's quarrels and favours bending the light of the whole age.

**Takeaway:** refract the century through one table, and the piece should show the private bending the light.

## M29. The diary as ego-document

Claretta's diary: lovemaking recorded with an underlined "si"; prim about "whore" and "bum" (first letter only); Mussolini after "famously brief and brutal acts of copulation" listening to Beethoven's Seventh and mulling over family life (Moorehead). The notebook is the mechanism, because a private notebook in which a dictator becomes a husband, briefly, between performances, is the only place the performance stops, and the stopping is the piece, and the piece must show the diary open, the underlined "si", the Beethoven playing, the dictator briefly a husband before the performance resumes.

**Takeaway:** open the diary between performances, and the piece should show the stopping.

## M30. Gossip as entry

"The vicious gossip surrounding the couple provides a nice entree into discussing the fine-grained textures of Roman haute bourgeois society" (Gregor, Times Higher Education). The whisper is the mechanism, because whispers in a salon opening a door into how a whole class lived means the gossip is not the subject but the key, and the key is the piece, and the piece must start with the whisper and follow it through the salon door, the fine grain of a class arriving through the thing nobody says aloud.

**Takeaway:** follow the whisper through the door, and the piece should let the gossip open the class.

## M31. Populism as reaction

Eclipse: as Hitler rose, Mussolini's decline in ideological pre-eminence led him to "a more populist approach: angry and bellicose words at home, violent aggression abroad, and a more extreme emphasis on charisma"; his was "a weak regime that went to its collapse broadcasting fake news about itself" (Gilbert). The turning-up is the mechanism, because a dictator turning up the volume as his power drains away, louder words, emptier state, means the noise and the power move in opposite directions, and the opposite directions are the piece, and the piece must show the volume rising as the state empties, the broadcast getting louder while the country it describes gets thinner.

**Takeaway:** turn the volume up as the power drains, and the piece should show the noise and the emptiness moving apart.

## M32. The street name as history lesson

Amendolas blog: the Amendola Fiera station (opened 1964), Piazza Giovanni Amendola: "In any Italian city, it is easy to brush up on your modern Italian history... there's always a Piazza Garibaldi or a Via Cavour"; "By contrast, presently, there are no Piazza Mussolinis". The sign is the mechanism, because a metro station name doing the work of a history book, if you read it, means the city teaches whoever walks it, and the teaching is the piece, and the piece must show the station sign, the piazza name, the street doing the lesson, the history legible in the names people walk past without reading.

**Takeaway:** read the station sign as a history book, and the piece should show the city teaching.

## M33. The gulch

"After all the stairs into darkness, into what looks like a gulch, might warn us of the fact that Italy did experience a 'ventennio' (twenty dark years of Fascist rule between 1922 and 1943, 1945), which led to its utter defeat in World War II... A national gulch indeed." (Amendolas blog). The descent is the mechanism, because descending a station staircase into a dark gulch, a whole country's twenty dark years as a hole in the ground, means the history is a place you walk down into, and the walking-down is the piece, and the piece must show the stairs going down into darkness, each step a year of the ventennio, the bottom of the gulch the defeat.

**Takeaway:** walk the stairs down into the gulch, and the piece should make the twenty years a descent.

## M34. The "indipendente di sinistra" stance

The signing is the mechanism, because a historian signing his own position on the page instead of hiding behind neutrality makes the stance part of the evidence, and the signing is the piece, and the piece must show the signature on the page, the historian standing where he stands, the position declared rather than disguised.

## Evolution: five phases, and the afterlife

PHASE 1 - THE DIPLOMATIC HISTORIAN, 1971-1983: PhD at St John's College, Cambridge (1971); first book 1973, a teaching overview for teachers and students, anti-Fascist in tone from the beginning; then the thesis book, Italy the Least of the Great Powers (1979), and Italy and the Approach of the First World War (1983). The signature moves are set here: the pretence of great power, the figurehead king, the single minister making foreign policy alone, the state that cannot present a unified face. He is a lecturer at Sydney throughout, becoming associate professor in 1981; deputy or acting director of the Frederick May Foundation for Italian Studies, 1981-1986.

PHASE 2 - THE HISTORIOGRAPHER OF MEMORY, 1993-1999: after the move to Perth, University of Western Australia professor 1987, the work turns from diplomacy to how societies remember: Explaining Auschwitz and Hiroshima (1993) compares six countries' historicisation of the war and proposes history as "a debate without end"; Italy and the Wider World (1996) finds many Italies, legal and real; The Italian Dictatorship (1998) turns polemical, accusing De Felice and Gentile of "anti-anti-Fascism" and insisting scholarship has a moral duty of vigilance. The cost: he is charged with politicising scholarship and dismissing twenty years of culturalist work. The stance never softens; it becomes his trademark.

PHASE 3 - THE BIOGRAPHER OF THE DUCE, 2002-2006: the twin peak. Mussolini (2002) is the first major biography since the Cold War and wins the Western Australian Premier's Prize and Non-Fiction Prize, announced 13 June 2003 by Premier Geoff Gallop; Mussolini's Italy (2005 UK / 2006 US) wins the Western Australian Premier's non-fiction prize and the New South Wales Premier's general history prize. Together the two books win five of Australia's major history prizes and are short-listed for five others (Australian Academy of the Humanities). The argument hardens into its final form: the weak, opportunistic, event-driven dictator; the revolution that never happened; the slogans that all turned out false. The Australian Book Review's verdict, "the culmination of a thirty-year career researching, teaching and writing on post-Risorgimento Italy", is also a eulogy for a social-democratic witness watching his values disintegrate, moral indignation "muted by an under-thread of elegiac sadness".

PHASE 4 - THE CITY HISTORIAN, 2007-2015: Nationalism (2007) and The Oxford Handbook of Fascism (2009), 31 new essays in five parts, are the pivot: from the dictator to the idea and the field. Then the two city books, Whispering City (2011) and Italian Venice (2014), written from the new base at Jesus College, Oxford (senior research fellow from 2012) and Reading part-time (2007-2011). The method is the same in both cities: start at an overlooked site, read the competing pasts off the stones, and the tone is more personal and playful than the polemics of the 1990s. The Cambridge History of the Second World War, volume II (Politics and ideology), edited with Joe Maiolo (2015), caps the phase as an editor's book.

PHASE 5 - THE LATE INTIMATE HISTORIAN, 2017-2025: Claretta (2017) turns from the dictator to the lover's diary; Mussolini and the Eclipse of Italian Fascism (2021) turns from the dictator to populism and the present; the Jesus College history (2021) is college service; Politics, Murder and Love in an Italian Family (2023) is, in his own words, "my new and probably last book", one family as the prism for a century, the private as the political. Then the coda: "Christmas in Somalia" (The Cricket Monthly, 27 February 2025), a wartime cricket story he is, per the Australian Academy of the Humanities profile, proud of "in his old age": Lieutenant Douglas Tatham Collins, 21, posted to Mogadishu in summer 1941, teaches cricket to Somali askaris on Christmas Day 1942 at Alula, the match ties 57-57, the locals complaining of "a torture called 'kricket'".

LIVING AFTERLIFE. He is living, in Oxford, still writing; there is no obituary to write and no death to record. The afterlife is his influence: the standard Anglophone biography of Mussolini, the "weak dictator" thesis, the city-palimsest method, and the anti-anti-Fascist stance that has never softened. The arc: from the pretence of a great power, through the memory wars, to the dictator deflated, the city read off its stones, and finally the century refracted through one family's dining table.

## Aesthetic logic

Bosworth's work is beautiful in the way a deflation is beautiful, and that is the point. Mack Smith's mechanism is the sentence landing like a verdict and the scene freezing on it; Gramsci's is the fragment arriving out of order and the idea returning in a different notebook; Duggan's is the archive opening and a person stepping out of it; Bosworth's is the grand thing being measured and found thin, the uniform splitting, the slogan marked false, the air force shrinking in the counting, the charisma thinning to a dazzle. His aesthetic is the paper strength, the ringing pronouncement, the beehive, the wedding-cake monument, the glittering Venice, the diary with the underlined "si", the station sign, the stairs into the gulch. He begins by saying a country is pretending, and ends by showing the pretence from the inside, the people who lived it, the lover who kept the diary, the family who paid the price. The elegiac sadness is the through-line: the social democrat watching his values disintegrate, writing the history of a country that wanted to be great and was not, and finding, in the small places, the overlooked museum, the village that is a world, the cricket match on Christmas Day, the history that survives the grand claims.

**Takeaway:** measure the grand thing and find it thin, and the piece should be deflationary, documentary, and elegiac, the big claims shrinking as the small places open.
