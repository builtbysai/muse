# Guadalupe Pineda: Study Notes

**Site:** Maria Guadalupe Pineda Aguilar (born Wednesday 23 February 1955, Guadalajara, Jalisco), Mexican singer, mezzosoprano, with a career counted from 1974 in the infobox and from three different years in the anniversary counts, more than thirty discs by the Wikipedia count and thirty-two by Sony's count for the 2018 album, sales counted four ways from three million to more than fourteen million, the one figure every source agrees on being the million and a half copies of "Te amo" in Mexico alone. She won the Latin Grammy Award for Musical Excellence on Thursday 16 November 2017, she is the only Mexican artist to have received the Grand Prix of the Societe des Auteurs, Compositeurs et Editeurs de Musique (SACEM) in Paris in December 2009, she was named Woman of the Year by UNESCO and the European Parliament at Cannes in 2007 and Embajadora de la Francofonia, and she has sung in the Palacio de Bellas Artes, the Sala Nezahualcotl, the Auditorio Nacional, the Teatro Metropolitan, the Teatro Colon in Buenos Aires, the Cathedral of Notre Dame in Paris, the Louvre oratorio, the Harris Theater in Chicago, the Santa Barbara Bowl and the Teatro Degollado. She is not a cabaret artist in the sense of Hadad, Orozco or Pie, and she is not the border-crossing singer Libertad is either: she is the singer who stayed in one country and made the whole world come to her voice instead, the Guadalajara sociology student who sang the forbidden trova until the whole country was singing it with her. ## M1. The ten-year apprenticeship in the pen~as

She did not arrive famous and start again, the way Libertad did in Mexico in 1980. She arrived unknown and stayed unknown for ten years, and the ten years are the mechanism. The El Financiero interview of 2016 gives it in her own words, and the biography pages repeat it in the flat third person: she began singing in small forums, pen~as and cafes, interpreting "el canto joven de una America Latina que queria ser escuchada", the young song of a Latin America that wanted to be heard, while she studied sociology at the UNAM. In those years she founded the groups La Propuesta and Sanampay, with which she recorded two discs, and then she began her solo career. The interview fills in the mechanism: she started "volando bajo", flying low, singing for groups so tiny they could hardly be called audiences, "con los ojos cerrados por la verguenza", with her eyes closed from shame. She took small parts in her uncle Antonio Aguilar's films, and a few lines were forced into the scripts so she could sing a song, and those weekly contracts let her save a little money. She sang under contract for the IMSS and the ISSSTE and in prisons and preventive detention centres. The ten years are stated as a number in every biography: "Despues de 10 anos de intensa lucha, llevando su voz a universidades, plazas publicas, reclusorios, oficinas y parques, logra llegar al publico masivo con el tema 'Yolanda'". After ten years of intense struggle, carrying her voice to universities, public plazas, prisons, offices and parks, she reaches the mass public. The mechanism is the apprenticeship as the career: the pen~a is not the waiting room before the career, it is the school, "una escuela maravillosa para mi canto", a wonderful school for my singing, and the singer who graduates from it has already sung in every room that matters, because she has sung in the smallest ones. Watch the apprenticeship: when the singer is singing with her eyes closed from shame to a handful of people in a cafe, and ten years later she is singing the same song to a full Auditorio Nacional, and the song has not changed, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** serve the ten years in the small rooms. The pen~a is the school. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M1) also started again from below, but her below was a disappearance, six years out of the media after arriving already famous; Pineda's below was the beginning itself, ten years before anyone knew her name, which is why her version is an apprenticeship and Libertad's is a restart.

## M2. The refusal

A record company offered her a contract on three conditions: dye herself blonde, wear miniskirts, and stop singing what was then hers, the new Cuban trova and the Latin American music of protest. The El Financiero interview gives the offer and the answer in two sentences: "En alguna disquera le ofrecieron contratarla siempre y cuando se tin~era de rubia, se vistiera con minifaldas y dejara de cantar lo que entonces era lo suyo: la nueva trova cubana y la musica latinoamericanas de protesta." And then: "No cedi. Toque puertas y pique piedra. Hasta aprendi mecanica para no gastar en la afinacion o en los arreglos de mi coche, que me dejo tirada mas de una vez saliendo de madrugada de las pen~as. Era una experta en arrancarlo en segunda." I did not give in. I knocked on doors and broke stone. I even learned mechanics so as not to spend on tuning or repairing my car, which left me stranded more than once coming out of the pen~as at dawn. I was an expert at starting it in second gear. The mechanism is the refusal as the career: the price of the contract was the voice itself, blonde hair and a miniskirt and no more trova, and she paid the opposite price instead, ten years of knocking on doors and fixing her own car at dawn, and the car that has to be started in second gear is the career, still running. The refusal has a second half that the interview states plainly: with Sanampay she sang out of register and nodules began to grow on her vocal cords, so becoming a soloist was also a way of taking care of the voice, "cuidarse las cuerdas vocales". The refusal is therefore not only moral but physical: the wrong register grows nodules, the wrong contract grows a different singer, and she refused both. Watch the refusal: when the singer is offered the contract and the contract asks for her hair, her skirt and her songs, and she walks out and learns to fix her own car instead, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** refuse the contract that asks for the voice. The car started in second gear is the career. Against the chain: Leon (#354) also fought the industry, but her fight was the long discography, album by album, in public; Pineda's fight was a single no said to a single offer, and then ten years of paying for it, which is why her version is a refusal and Leon's is a career.

## M3. The forbidden song made mass

"Yolanda" is a song by Pablo Milanes, and Pineda recorded it in 1984 under the title "Te amo", and the recording did what the trova was not supposed to do: it sold a million and a half copies in Mexico alone and it was heard everywhere. The mechanism is stated by Pineda herself in the El Financiero interview, and it is the sharpest sentence in this study: "La trova solo se oia en ciertos lugarcitos elitistas, como los cafe-concert; era patrimonio de la clase academica e intelectual, hasta que la grabe en 1984 y se masifico. Hubo colegas que incursionaron despues en los medios y aprovecharon el camino que yo abri con mucho dolor. La trova cubana habia estado prohibida en programas como Siempre en domingo, y de pronto, pacatelas. Se escuchaba en todas partes." The trova was only heard in certain little elitist places, like the cafe-concerts; it was the heritage of the academic and intellectual class, until I recorded it in 1984 and it became mass. There were colleagues who came into the media afterwards and took advantage of the road I opened with much pain. Cuban trova had been banned on programs like Siempre en Domingo, and suddenly, wham. It was heard everywhere. The mechanism is the forbidden genre made mass by one recording: the song was banned from the biggest television program in the country, and the singer recorded it anyway, and the ban broke, not because anyone lifted it but because a million and a half people bought the disc. The production was shelved for a year, the interview says, because the label Nuestro Gran Suen~o gave priority to artists "de moda", artists in fashion, and the shelving is part of the mechanism: the song waited a year in a can and then sold anyway, and the waiting is what proves the ban was the only thing holding it back. The same interview adds the sting: popularizing the trova was a "pecado" that the left never forgave her, "'pecado' que nunca le perdono 'la izquierda'", so she was punished from both sides, by the television that banned the song and by the left that resented its success. Watch the massification: when the song that was banned from television is suddenly heard everywhere, and the singer who recorded it is blamed by the people who banned it and by the people who loved it, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** record the banned song and let the sales lift the ban. The million and a half is the argument. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M4) also sang a forbidden song, but hers was forbidden by one teacher, Chabuca Granda, and she waited thirty-six years to sing it; Pineda's was forbidden by a whole television program, and she recorded it at once, which is why her version is a massification and Libertad's is a debt.

## M4. The voice that will not be labelled

"A Guadalupe no se le puede etiquetar." Guadalupe cannot be labelled. The toletras biography states it flatly, and then lists what the label would have to contain: "ha recreado acertadamente en su voz, de igual forma el bolero que el tango, la cancion ranchera que la opera, la nueva cancion y la balada, al igual que temas de su propia autoria." She has recreated successfully in her voice, in equal measure the bolero and the tango, the ranchera song and the opera, the new song and the ballad, as well as songs of her own authorship. The Wikipedia infobox gives the voice a name, "Mezzosoprano", and the genres a list, "Nueva cancion, folclore, cancion ranchera, bolero", and the list is already too short, because the discography adds the tango, the opera, the French chanson and the lullaby. The mechanism is the refusal of the label as a method: she does not change genre to prove she can, she changes genre because the song asks for it, and the voice is the constant that makes the change believable. The poet Ali Chumacero gave the voice its best description, quoted in the older biographies: "una voz de metal iluminado", a voice of illuminated metal, and the phrase is the mechanism stated as a material, a voice that is hard like metal and lit like a lamp, so it can carry the bolero and the aria in the same night without breaking. The El Financiero interview states the range plainly: "La trova no lo es todo: canta opera, tangos, baladas y sus propias composiciones. Canta en mas idiomas de los que habla." The trova is not everything: she sings opera, tangos, ballads and her own compositions. She sings in more languages than she speaks. Watch the label fail: when the singer who sang the trova last year is now singing an aria, and the year after that a tango, and the year after that a song in French, and the room accepts her in every genre without asking what happened to the last one, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the label fail. The voice is the constant; the genre is the change. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M1) also became a new singer every time, but her newness was a new genre per album, a new singer per record; Pineda's newness is the same voice refusing to be filed, which is why her version is a refusal and Leon's is a reinvention.

## M5. The opera album

In 2002, by the discography list, or 2003, by the press, Pineda recorded "Mi corazon se abre a tu voz: Arias de opera", with the Camerata de las Americas, and in 2004 she received a double platinum disc for sales of more than 150,000 copies in Mexico. The La Jornada report of 28 February 2003 gives the return: "Regresa Guadalupe Pineda con disco de arias de opera, tras dos anos de silencio." She returns with a disc of opera arias, after two years of silence. The mechanism is the popular singer taking the opera, and it is Libertad's M3 seen from the other side of the same year: Libertad recorded "Arias de opera... Y, por que no?" in 2001 and took a platinum disc and a gold disc from Carlos Slim and Felipe Gonzalez, and Pineda recorded her arias the next year and took a double platinum disc, so the two voices of the future Tres Grandes chord crossed into the opera house one after the other, a year apart, and both were bought by the audiences that had bought their boleros. The difference is the instrument: Libertad is a soprano and her album is a question, "and why not?", while Pineda is a mezzosoprano and her album is an opening, "Mi corazon se abre a tu voz", my heart opens to your voice, so her crossing is warmer and lower, the heart opening where Libertad's voice climbs. The silence before it matters too: two years without a disc, and then the opera, so the return is not a new album but a new room, and the room is the hardest one. Watch the crossing: when the singer who sold a million and a half copies of a trova song is now singing an aria with a chamber orchestra, and the audience that bought the trova buys the aria too, because they are buying the voice and not the genre, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the heart open to the aria. The opera is the next room, not a different singer. Against the chain: this is Libertad's M3 from the lower voice, and the difference is the title: Libertad asks "why not?" and Pineda opens her heart, which is why her version is an opening and Libertad's is a question.

## M6. The nine languages

One of her discs, dedicated to the children of the world, includes pieces in nine languages. The El Financiero interview states it in one sentence: "Uno de sus discos, dedicado a los nin~os del mundo, incluye piezas en nueve idiomas." The disc is "Un mundo de arrullos" (2005), a world of lullabies, and the TVyNovelas interview of 2018 gives the mechanism its second half, thirteen years later, in the grandmother's house: her daughter plays the disc for her grandchildren, "oyen cantar a su abuela en nueve idiomas", they hear their grandmother singing in nine languages, and the two boys, one of them four years old and taking piano and singing lessons, have "un oido musical increible", an incredible musical ear. The mechanism is the lullaby as the world map: she does not learn nine languages to show off, she sings in more languages than she speaks, and the lullaby is the genre that lets her do it, because a lullaby does not need the singer to speak the language, it needs her to mean it. The disc is the gentlest version of the same refusal as M4: the singer who will not be labelled will not be labelled by language either, and the proof is not a concert but a child falling asleep. The grandmother's house is where the mechanism closes: she sang to her daughter, she composed songs with her daughter, and now she sings to her grandchildren and dances with them, "ahora canto y bailo con mis nietos", and the nine languages are the inheritance, carried in a lullaby instead of a will. Watch the lullaby: when the singer who sang the forbidden trova is now singing a child to sleep in a language she does not speak, and the child falls asleep anyway, and thirteen years later the grandchild hears the same voice on the same disc, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** sing the child to sleep in nine languages. The lullaby is the map. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M12) also left a name as her legacy, the girls named Tania Libertad; Pineda's legacy is a voice on a disc, heard by her own grandchildren in nine languages, which is why her version is a lullaby and Libertad's is a christening.

## M7. The French turn

In 2009 she released "Francia con sabor latino", France with a Latin flavour, and in December 2009 she received in Paris the Grand Prix of the Societe des Auteurs, Compositeurs et Editeurs de Musique (SACEM), the only Mexican artist ever to receive it. The disc sings the French chanson in French and the Mexican song in French: "Mourir d'aimer" (Morir de amor), "La vie en rose" (La vida en rosa), "Besame mucho" in French, "Les feuilles mortes" (Las hojas muertas), and eight more themes entirely in French. The mechanism is the translation as a conquest: she does not sing French songs to sound French, she takes the Mexican song, "Besame mucho", and sings it back to Paris in French, so the conquest runs in both directions, and Paris answers with a prize no other Mexican has received. The prize has a sequel: she was named Embajadora de la Francofonia, and the disc stayed eight weeks at number one in sales in Mexico, so the French turn was not a detour for the French audience but a triumph at home. The turn has a prehistory: in 2002 she was the only Mexican present in the Buddha Bar compilation of France, volume IV, edited all over the world, and in the Italian Monte Carlo collection, so Paris had already heard her before she sang to it in French. The mechanism's sharpest detail is the venue: she sang in the Cathedral of Notre Dame in Paris in 2009, and returned in December 2015 for the Man~anitas to the Virgin of Guadalupe and then gave a recital in the oratory of the Louvre museum accompanied by mariachi, so the French turn ends with a mariachi in the Louvre, the Mexican instrument inside the French museum, which is the translation completed. Watch the turn: when the singer who sang the trova in a cafe is now singing "Besame mucho" in French in Paris, and Paris gives her a prize no Mexican has received, and she brings the mariachi into the Louvre, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** translate the song and let Paris answer. The mariachi in the Louvre is the proof. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M6) also drew a map, but hers was the Black Coast, from Zana to Paris to Senegal; Pineda's map is a single city, Paris, taken twice, which is why her version is a conquest and Libertad's is a coastline.

## M8. The cathedral voice

She has sung in Notre Dame de Paris twice, in 2009 and in December 2015, and in the Teatro Colon in Buenos Aires in November 2006, invited by Mercedes Sosa, and in the Harris Theater in Chicago and the Theatre du Rond Point in Paris in 2009, and the mechanism is the sacred room as the stage. The Wikipedia article gives the Notre Dame photograph its caption, "Guadalupe Pineda cantando en Notre Dame, en Francia", and the image itself gives the mechanism: Pineda centre in a long red robe, singing into a microphone on a stand, priests in white albs at her sides, rose windows and stone arches behind her, the singer small inside the stone and the voice filling it. The Teatro Colon invitation is the mechanism's other half: Mercedes Sosa, the great Argentine singer, invited her, and she sang in the Colon and then in the New York Theater and the UIC Pavilion in Chicago in 2007, so the invitation came from a singer, not from a promoter, and the room was the reward. The mechanism is the voice as the instrument that fits the sacred room: the mezzosoprano, lower and warmer than Libertad's soprano, fills a cathedral the way it fills a pen~a, by meaning the song, and the room, whether it is a cafe or a cathedral, does the rest. The Man~anitas of December 2015 add the detail that makes the mechanism visible: she sang the morning songs to the Virgin of Guadalupe in Notre Dame, the Mexican Virgin honoured in the French cathedral, so the cathedral held a Mexican devotion, and then she sang with mariachi in the Louvre, so the sacred and the popular shared the same week. Watch the cathedral: when the singer who sang with her eyes closed in a cafe is now singing in a red robe in Notre Dame, and the priests stand beside her in white, and the rose windows light the stone, and the voice is the same voice, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the cathedral hold the song. The voice fits the stone. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M11) also sang one sacred song, the Ave Maria in Zana for a pope; Pineda's sacred song is the Man~anitas in Paris, a Mexican devotion in a French cathedral, which is why her version is a cathedral and Libertad's is a homecoming.

## M9. The chord of three

From Pineda's chair the chord looks different from Libertad's M7, because Pineda is the third voice and the one who brings the trova and the ranchera into it. In June 2015, in the Estudios Churubusco in Mexico City, Sony Music recorded for CD and DVD a project reuniting for the first time in a recording the three best exponents of Latin American folklore of their generation, "Las Tres Grandes" in the Primera Fila format, where the artists interpreted several of their greatest hits, plus new songs in their voice, in solo, duo and trio, with special guests Paquita la del Barrio, Lila Downs, Ely Guerra and Maria Leon, under the musical direction of Aureo Baqueiro. On Wednesday 25 November 2015 the three arrived at the Auditorio Nacional with their show, a full house of almost ten thousand people, and the El Universal report of the night gives the staging: the three in passion red adorned with traditional Mexican details, each singing her most representative songs, then joining voices until the three became one voice, "las voces de cada una de ellas se volvian una misma", with a mariachi finale with the hats and the instruments. The Iztapalapa report of 26 May 2019 gives Pineda's own turn in the chord, at the concert for more than ten thousand people in the Parque Cuitlahuac on Saturday 25 May 2019: after the trio opened with "Cantares" and "Gracias a la vida", and after Leon sang her songs and Libertad sang hers, "finalmente toco el turno de solista a Guadalupe Pineda, quien hizo vibrar a toda la gente con 'Tu recuerdo y yo' de Jose Alfredo Jimenez; 'Un Pun~o de Tierra, Como Fue', un popurri con 'Mi Unicornio Azul' y 'Todo Cambia', asi como 'Coincidir'". Finally it was Pineda's turn as soloist, and she made the whole crowd vibrate with her ranchera and her trova and her ballad, and then the three closed together with "La Muralla" and "Si nos dejan". The mechanism is the trio as a chord in which each voice keeps its own colour: Leon brings the Mexican song, Libertad brings the Peruvian song, Pineda brings the trova and the ranchera, and the chord is the argument that the three belong in the same sentence. The project has lasted: the three closed the Quimera festival in Metepec on Sunday 20 October 2019, played the Expo 2020 Dubai Mexico pavilion on Wednesday 26 January and Sunday 30 January 2022, and opened Cumbre Tajin on Wednesday 19 March 2025, still billed as Las Tres Grandes, ten years on. The photographs give the mechanism its colours: gp-02, the three with their right arms raised, Pineda in red on the right; gp-03, the three with the mariachi behind them, Pineda in gold and white with a purple sash; gp-07, the giant screen at Dubai showing the three portraits above the script "Las tres GRANDES". Watch the chord: when the three stop taking turns and start singing together, and the room hears one voice made of three, and the trova is inside the chord without losing its accent, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** bring the trova into the chord. Three complete voices are the argument. Against the chain: this is Libertad's M7 from the third chair, and the difference is the cargo: Libertad's chair carries Peru, Leon's carries Mexico, and Pineda's carries the forbidden song that became mass, which is why her version is a cargo and theirs are a border and a reunion.

## M10. The family album

In November 2018 she released "Homenaje a los Grandes Compositores", her thirty-second album by Sony's count, and it went to number one in sales in Mexico. The Sony press release gives the mechanism in its tracklist: fourteen songs, and almost every one is a duet, and the duets are a family tree. Armando Manzanero sings "Nos hizo falta tiempo" and "Contigo aprendi" with her. Oscar Chavez sings "Por Ti". Maria Leon sings "El tiempo de ti". Edgar Oceransky sings "Aqui estoy", the representative of the singer-songwriters of today. Raul Di Blasio plays the piano on "Procuro olvidarte", with only that instrument and a cello, an arrangement the TVyNovelas interview calls "una delicia". La Sonora Santanera plays the Rafael Hernandez "El Jibarito" medley, "Lamento Borincano", "Perfume de gardenias" and "Preciosa". Pablo Milanes sings "Yolanda (Te amo)" with her again, thirty-four years after the song changed her life. Pepe Aguilar sings "Amor de los dos" with her, and then Pepe, Angela and Leonardo Aguilar join her for the ranchero medley "El cantador" and "Tristes recuerdos", the songs her uncle Antonio Aguilar made known internationally. The TVyNovelas interview gives the mechanism its sentence: the album "muestra de donde vengo", it shows where I come from, and "si no siento las canciones, no las puedo interpretar", if I do not feel the songs, I cannot interpret them, so every duet is a song she chose because it gave her something, "se me ponga la piel chinita", gooseflesh, or a deep joy or sadness. The mechanism is the duet as genealogy: she does not duet with stars, she duets with her family and her teachers, the composer who wrote the song that changed her destiny, the canto nuevo peer who sang beside her in the 1970s, the cousin and the niece and nephew who carry her uncle's songs, and the album is the family tree sung aloud. The interview adds the price of singing in the family: "Rendirle un homenaje a mi tio, don Antonio Aguilar, con El cantador y Tristes recuerdos, al lado de mis sobrinos, fue una gran responsabilidad, porque habia que dar todo de si cantando en familia." Paying homage to my uncle with those songs, beside my nephews, was a great responsibility, because you had to give everything of yourself singing in the family. Watch the family: when the singer who refused the blonde contract is now singing with her cousin and her niece and nephew, and the songs are her uncle's, and the room hears three generations in one medley, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** sing the family tree. The duet is the genealogy. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M5) also made the duet a conversation, trading verses with Manzanero across four albums; Pineda's duet is not a conversation but a tree, fourteen branches in one album, which is why her version is a genealogy and Libertad's is a friendship.

## M11. The song that changed her destiny

"Te amo, me cambio el destino." Te amo changed my destiny. Pineda said it to TVyNovelas in November 2018, and the sentence is the mechanism: "Si, Te amo, me cambio el destino, y ahora cantarla junto con Pablo Milanes es un agradecimiento eterno a la vida. Ese tema, de venir de cantar a publicos pequen~itos, me permitio que en 1984 se vendieran un millon y medio de discos. Grabarla ahora en el 2018 con Pablo Milanes en una version que me hace llorar, es algo muy especial." Yes, Te amo changed my destiny, and now singing it together with Pablo Milanes is an eternal gratitude to life. That theme, coming from singing to tiny publics, allowed a million and a half discs to be sold in 1984. Recording it now in 2018 with Pablo Milanes in a version that makes me cry is something very special. The mechanism is the one song as the spine of the career: the song is recorded in 1984 and sells a million and a half copies, it is sung for thirty-four years, and then in 2018 the composer himself comes back to sing it with her, and the new version makes her cry, so the song has not aged, it has ripened. The song's double name is part of the mechanism: "Yolanda" is the composer's title, "Te amo" is the singer's title, and the retitling is the appropriation, the song becoming hers by being renamed, the way a singer takes a song by singing it until it belongs to her. The song appears everywhere the career is told: it is the breakthrough in every biography, the first track of the 2018 album's emotional centre, the song she sings in the Iztapalapa medley as "Todo cambia" beside it, and the song the 2018 interview returns to three times. Watch the spine: when the singer who sang to tiny publics is now singing the same song with its composer, thirty-four years later, and the version makes her cry, and the room hears the thirty-four years in it, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** keep the one song and let it ripen. The retitling is the appropriation. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M4) also carried one forbidden song for thirty-six years, but hers waited unsung until 2019; Pineda's was sung from the first year and never stopped, which is why her version is a spine and Libertad's is a debt.

## M12. The fifty years

She celebrated fifty years of career in 2024 and 2025, not with a museum but with a tour. The tomaticket listing gives the first date: "50 Aniversario Guadalupe Pineda en Mexico", Friday 10 May 2024, 21:00, Teatro Metropolitan, Avenida Independencia 90, Colonia Centro. The mezcalent note gives the second: Thursday 5 December 2024 at La Maraka, with the Sonora Santanera and the Trio Los Panchos as special guests. The marcandotrayectoria note gives the third: Saturday 3 May 2025, 21:00, Sala TLAQNA in Xalapa, after twenty-seven years away from Veracruz, "un magno concierto celebrando 50 anos de trayectoria". The mechanism is the anniversary as a tour, not a retrospective: she does not stop to look back, she keeps singing, and the fifty years are counted in rooms, the Metropolitan and La Maraka and the Sala TLAQNA, the same way the ten years were counted in pen~as. The tour has a soundtrack that is still being written: the singles keep coming, "Besos y Copas" in 2021, "Tu, Solo Tu" in 2022, "La Llorona" in 2024, "El Camino de la Vida" in 2024, "Veinte Anos", "Las Man~anitas" and "Boleros Hoy y Siempre" in 2025, "Yo Ya Me Voy De Mi Tierra" in 2026, and the La Chicuela podcast description says she has just recorded "La llorona" and recorded with Bronco, so the fifty-year singer is still recording new songs, not reissuing old ones. The anniversary count itself is part of the mechanism: fifty years from 1974, thirty-five years in September 2012, forty-one years in August 2016, and the counts disagree, but the tour does not, because the tour is the count that matters, the rooms filled one after another. Watch the tour: when the singer who sang with her eyes closed in a cafe is now celebrating fifty years by singing in Xalapa after twenty-seven years away, and the room is full, and she has just recorded a new song, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** celebrate by touring, not by looking back. The rooms are the count. Against the chain: Leon (#354) also counted her anniversaries in albums, and Libertad (#356 M9) sang her whole life in one night in 2019; Pineda's anniversary is neither an album nor a night, it is a tour, which is why her version is a road and theirs are a discography and a set list.

## M13. The gypsy

"Guadalupe Pineda se define como 'gitana'." Guadalupe Pineda defines herself as a gypsy. The El Financiero interview states it, and then gives the reason: "'Cuando pasa un mes que no me subo a un avion me siento extran~a'. Es raro, pero disfruta la estancia en el aeropuerto." When a month passes without getting on a plane I feel strange. It is odd, but she enjoys being in the airport. The TVyNovelas interview says the same thing from the other side: "Guadalupe Pineda ama los aeropuertos", she loves airports, because "casi siempre voy a algo bonito: a trabajar, a subirme a un escenario, a enfrentar a un nuevo publico, a estrenar una cancion o un disco", almost always I am going to something lovely: to work, to climb onto a stage, to face a new public, to premiere a song or a disc. The mechanism is the road as home: the singer who left Guadalajara for Mexico City in 1964, who sang in pen~as and prisons and plazas, who toured the United States, Ireland, Spain, Italy, France, Colombia, Puerto Rico, Ecuador, Venezuela, Argentina and Central and South America, who sang in Rimini and Madrid and Las Vegas and Puerto Rico and Cannes and Chicago and Paris, has no home but the moving, and the airport is the room where she feels normal. The mechanism has a discipline: she loves the airport because she is always going to something lovely, to work, to a stage, to a new public, to a premiere, so the travel is never empty, it is always toward a song. Watch the gypsy: when the singer who has been everywhere says she feels strange after a month without a plane, and the airport is where she feels at home, and the next flight is always to a stage, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** live on the road and love the airport. The flight is the home. Against the chain: Libertad (#356) also travelled the world, from Lima to Mexico to Paris to Senegal, but her travel was a map with a home at the end, Zana, and the return; Pineda's travel has no return, only the next flight, which is why her version is a road and Libertad's is a circle.

## M14. The devil's advocate

"Siempre he sido un abogado del diablo; es decir, siempre estoy viendo en que falle, o en que puedo mejorar, por que no salio una cosa bien... Siempre busco la superacion." I have always been a devil's advocate; that is, I am always looking at where I failed, or where I can improve, why something did not come out well. I always seek to surpass myself. Pineda said it to TVyNovelas in November 2018, the week after receiving the Latin Grammy Award for Musical Excellence, and the sentence is the mechanism: the prize does not close the career, it raises the demand. "Lo que si me dio ese reconocimiento fue un aire nuevo, ganas de seguir luchando y seguir adelante. Recibirlo fue para mi un enorme honor que voy a agradecer toda mi vida." What that recognition did give me was a new air, the desire to keep fighting and keep going forward. Receiving it was an enormous honour I will thank all my life. And then the price: "Recibir el Grammy es una responsabilidad de hacer cada vez las cosas mas excelentes. En cierto sentido me exigio mas de mi misma para seguir siendo mejor cantante." Receiving the Grammy is a responsibility to make things more excellent each time. In a certain sense it demanded more of me, to keep being a better singer. The mechanism is the prize as a demand, not a reward: the Latin Grammy for Musical Excellence, won on Thursday 16 November 2017 at the Mandalay Bay in Las Vegas, voted by the board of the Latin Recording Academy for creative contributions of exceptional artistic importance, is carried as a debt, and the debt is paid by singing better, by choosing songs that give her gooseflesh, by producing her own discs, by refusing the contract that asks for the voice. The same interview gives the mechanism its proof: "si no siento las canciones, no las puedo interpretar", if I do not feel the songs I cannot interpret them, so the devil's advocate is not self-punishment but selection, the refusal of every song that does not move her. Watch the advocate: when the singer who has just won the highest honour says it only means she must sing better, and she goes on choosing songs by whether they give her gooseflesh, and she keeps recording new singles at seventy, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** take the prize as a debt. The gooseflesh is the test. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M10) also made her late turn at seventy-three, commissioning songs from a machine in lando and candombe; Pineda's late turn is to keep choosing songs by the gooseflesh and keep singing them herself, which is why her version is a discipline and Libertad's is a commission.

## Evolution

1964-1974, the move and the school: born in Guadalajara, the fourth of six children of the lawyer Agustin Pineda and Josefina Aguilar, goddaughter of the writer and ex-governor of Jalisco Agustin Yan~ez, the family moves to Mexico City in 1964 when Yan~ez is named Secretary of Public Education; she enters sociology at the UNAM, marries briefly, leaves the family home for a boarding house in Colonia Napoles and a modest flat shared with three students, joins a Marxist study circle in her last year of preparatory school at the Academia Hispano Mexicana, reads El Capital and Trotsky with republican exiles from Spain, and falls in love with Violeta Parra, Mercedes Sosa, Alfredo Zitarrosa, Amparo Ochoa, Oscar Chavez and the folklorists. 1974-1984, the pen~as: she sings in small forums, pen~as and cafes, founds La Propuesta and then Sanampay ("estar presente", to be present, in Quechua), records two discs with Sanampay, sings for the IMSS and the ISSSTE and in prisons, learns mechanics to fix her own car, refuses the blonde-and-miniskirt contract, and in 1980 records an album with Carlos Diaz "Caito" and in 1981 launches herself as a soloist. 1984-1998, the breakthrough and the boleros: "Te amo" in 1984 sells a million and a half copies in Mexico alone and makes the trova mass; "Un poco mas" in 1985 takes a gold disc for more than 500,000 copies; "20 Boleros de Siempre" (1990) and "Costumbres" (1991) take gold discs; "De nuevo sola" (1993), "Enamorarse asi" (1994), "Asi como tu" (1997) and "Vestida de besos" (1998), with the hit "Coincidir" by Alberto Escobar. 2000-2009, the opera and the French turn: "Con los trios del siglo" (2000); the opera album in 2002/2003, double platinum in 2004; "Canciones de mi tierra" (2004); "Un mundo de arrullos" (2005), the lullabies in nine languages; "Gracias a la vida" (2006); the Teatro Colon with Mercedes Sosa in November 2006; "La voz en vivo" at the Metropolitan, recorded March 2007, released 2008; "Francia con sabor latino" (2009), the SACEM Grand Prix in December 2009, eight weeks at number one in Mexico, Notre Dame in 2009, "Coincidencias" with Filippa Giordano at the Auditorio Nacional in 2009. 2010-2015, the anniversaries and the chord: L.A. Live and the Mexican Consulate in Los Angeles for the Bicentennial on 15 and 16 September 2010; "A flor de piel" (2011); thirty-five years of career with two sold-out concerts at the Palacio de Bellas Artes in September 2012, recorded for CD and DVD; the Auditorio Nacional in early 2013 and as guest of Placido Domingo for the Auditorio's sixty years; "En Bellas Artes, Vol. 1 y 2" at the end of 2013; "Tiempo de amar" (2015), singing Mana, Cafe Tacvba, Ricardo Montaner and Gian Marco; Las Tres Grandes in Primera Fila, recorded June 2015, the Auditorio Nacional on Wednesday 25 November 2015, the gold disc in 2016, the 17th Latin Grammy nomination on Thursday 17 November 2016, Notre Dame and the Louvre in December 2015. 2016-2026, the late years: the Latin Grammy Award for Musical Excellence on Thursday 16 November 2017; "Homenaje a los grandes compositores" on Friday 9 November 2018, number one in Mexico; the Metropolitan Mother's Day concert on Friday 10 May 2019; the Quimera closing on Sunday 20 October 2019; El Consorcio at the Auditorio Nacional and the Arena Monterrey in February 2020; the Billboard Latin Music Awards Juan Gabriel tribute with Yuri and Ana Barbara in September 2021; the Expo 2020 Dubai concerts on Wednesday 26 January and Sunday 30 January 2022; "Guadalupe Canta a Manzanero" (2023); the fifty years tour, the Metropolitan on Friday 10 May 2024, La Maraka on Thursday 5 December 2024, Xalapa on Saturday 3 May 2025; the singles into 2026, "La Llorona" in 2024 and "Yo Ya Me Voy De Mi Tierra" in 2026. The arc is a road with no return: she left Guadalajara for the pen~as, the pen~as for the mass public, the mass public for the opera and Paris, Paris for the chord of three, and the chord for the fifty-year tour, and she is still recording.

## Aesthetic logic

Pineda's aesthetic is the voice as illuminated metal, sung in every genre the song asks for and in more languages than she speaks: the trova, the bolero, the ranchera, the tango, the ballad, the opera aria, the French chanson, the lullaby, and the scarcity is the argument, because a singer with no costume, no mask and no character has only the voice to hold the room, and the voice has to be hard enough and lit enough to be believed in every genre. The materials are a mezzosoprano voice, a pen~a, a refused contract, a car started in second gear, a forbidden song made mass, a heart opened to the aria, nine lullabies, a French prize, a cathedral, a chord of three, a family tree of duets, one song that changed her destiny, a fifty-year tour, an airport, and a devil's advocate who tests every song by the gooseflesh. The palette is the one the photographs give: the red robe of gp-01 against the stone and the rose windows of Notre Dame; the red blouse and wide sheer sleeves of gp-02 against the blue spotlights, with Leon's pale blue-green and gold and Libertad's black and gold beside her; the gold blouse and white floral skirt and purple sash of gp-03 against the mariachi hats; the fuchsia-magenta dress with white cuffs of gp-04 against the dark stage and the orchestra; the black dress with red floral embroidery and green sash of gp-05 against black; the pale peach sheer-sleeved gown of gp-06 against teal light and pink roses; the purple light and the red dress of gp-07 against the giant screen at Dubai; the red lace gown of gp-08 against the dark cover. Where Leon's logic was the whole country sung by one voice in many genres, and Libertad's was two countries sung by one voice in one night, Pineda's is one country sung by one voice in every genre, and the difference is the refusal: Leon changes genre per album, Libertad changes country per song, and Pineda refuses to be labelled at all, which is why her version is a road and theirs are a discography and a set list.
