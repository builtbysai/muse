# Study #229: Zimoun

Expansion series, cron run 2026-10-31. @studiozimoun, public, not verified, 374,067 followers, 197 accessible posts (2019-03-06 to 2026-10-30; 2020-2022 a gap). 20 frames visually inspected (videos as thumbnails only; no audio observed).

## Account facts
- Zimoun, born 1977 in Bern, Switzerland. Sound artist working with kinetic sound installations. Bio: "Exploring Complexity through Simplicity. Recycling Loop." That bio IS the thesis: simplicity of means, complexity of outcome, materials in perpetual reuse.
- Handles correction: the brief's "@zimoun" resolves to Najwa_K (Morocco, 8 followers, 0 posts), a different person. The artist's real handle is @studiozimoun, confirmed via his Vimeo bio.
- The account is a video documentation channel: 179 of 197 posts are video, 18 carousel, almost no static photography. Captions are a bilingual formula: sound-on marker, title/year/materials, exhibition list, and a hashtag cluster that is his vocabulary of intent: #microsound #chaos #order #multiplication #mass #individualism #texture.
- His titles are material inventories: "658 prepared dc-motors, cotton balls, cardboard boxes 70x70x70cm". The title is the recipe and the parameter list.

## Core engine
Hundreds of identical cheap units (a recycled DC motor plus one raw material: cardboard box, cotton ball, wood stick, string) are set to rotate or oscillate in a shared space, and their unsynchronized microscopic differences in timing, friction, and wear sum into one large emergent sound-mass that breathes, pulses, and never repeats.

## Evolution
- 2019 (earliest accessible): site-specific gallery swarms. Floor scatter of stick-trailing boxes (MBAL Le Locle 2016, f00), the floor-to-ceiling cardboard wall with a lone viewer dwarfed before it (Le Centquatre Paris 2017, f01). The recycling doctrine stated outright: "95% of the used materials are recycled and are getting recycled/re-used afterwards again and again and again." Viewer as witness walking among units.
- 2023: the studio-documentary turn. Closer camera, the atomic unit revealed at macro scale (one black DC motor on a cardboard box, f02). Material substitution keeps the mechanism constant: wood sticks with motor feet (Taipei 2016, f03). The old bio-acoustic work "25 woodworms" (2009, f04) resurfaces: amplified live wood-boring insects, the no-motors exception. The recordings strand begins: "Zimoun | Modular" album.
- 2024: compilation and repurposing. "Sound Sculptures & Soundinstallations" montages (f07, amber/black, moodier than the usual white gallery). The 12k relationship deepens into releases: "ModularGuitarFields I-VI" (12k, mastered by Taylor Deupree). Strict grid rows (f05, Kunst(Zeug)Haus) versus floor scatter (f00): same parts, two spatial grammars, the order/chaos poles.
- 2025: first-person method statements. The laboratory image (f09): one motor bolted to a wood block, a bent wire arm, a pipe-cleaner looping from a gear, overlaid "SOUND SCULPTURE, EARLY PROTOTYPE". Caption: "many steps of prototyping and testing are often required to achieve this simplicity... solely about initial observations of its behavior." The named concept "Primitive Complexity" is pinned to a concrete unit: "1 prepared dc-motor, cotton ball, cardboard box 60x20x20 cm, 2013", Edition 6 + 1 AP (f11). November 2025: the instrument strand breaks through with the double 12k release "Wind Dynamic Organ, One & Two" (solo) and "Wind Dynamic Organ, Deviations" (with Taylor Deupree), recorded over many sessions across years on one unique instrument (Prototype III) in Bern (f12, f13).
- 2026: the instrument strand takes over. "97 prepared dc-motors, 11 kg wood, 2026" (f17, artist seated inside his own swarm). Newest material in the set: metal ball chain rattling on mesh (f16, Rueschlikon). Black garbage bags as resonating membranes, the most political caption in the set ("throwaway society", f15). The practice turns inward and playful: "beat box" (f19), a wearable headband rig with two cardboard boxes at the ears, two motors playing for the wearer. The 2009 "25 woodworms" reposted again as a formal Sound Sculpture (f18): canonization of the bio-acoustic lineage.
- Arc: anonymous material swarms filling museums -> process transparency and recordings -> named unique instruments and body-scale self-played devices, plus canon re-releases of 2009/2013 landmarks. The mechanism never changed; the framing moved from installation-documentation to instrument-portraits.

## Key mechanisms (the observed systems)
1. Primitive Complexity (f11): one prepared DC motor, one raw material, one cardboard box. The whole practice reduces to this trio. Multiplication is the compositional act: prototype one unit's behavior, then deploy hundreds. Watchable behavior: one unit tested, then a field of identical clones whose timings disagree.
2. The unsynchronized sum (his method quotes): "Technically, the pieces are usually very straightforward: motors interacting with materials through a steady power supply... The complexity then arises from the interaction between rotation and material properties." Microscopic differences in timing, friction, and wear sum into a breathing mass that never repeats. Watchable behavior: a pulse that swells and thins with no repeating cycle.
3. The controlled-frame out-of-control ("The systems usually get out of control within a controlled framework"): strict spatial frameworks (grid rows, walls) whose internal phases are free. Watchable behavior: a perfect grid whose cells drift out of phase, order visibly leaking into chaos.
4. Order pole vs chaos pole (f00 vs f05): floor scatter and strict grid rows, same parts, two spatial grammars. Watchable behavior: one swarm breathing between a grid and a scatter, the composition as a state machine.
5. The recycling loop (f01 caption): materials reused exhibition to exhibition, 95% recycled, "again and again and again". Watchable behavior: after one movement the units disassemble into parts, the parts stream and reassemble into the next formation; nothing new is ever introduced.
6. Hear what you see (his directness doctrine): "In my work you hear what you see and you see what you hear... The sounds are generated in real time, through the combination and physical interplay of materials in motion." No speakers, no playback: the visible motion is the only instrument. Watchable behavior: a visible tick is always paired with its sound mark; removing units removes their contribution.
7. The bio-acoustic exception (f04, f18: "25 woodworms, wood, microphone, sound system", 2009): no motors at all, just live insects plus amplification. Proves the mechanism is broader than motors: any unsynchronized micro-agents summed through one listening channel. Watchable behavior: 25 agents working a form, one listening point, no mechanical parts.
8. The inventory title ("658 prepared dc-motors, cotton balls, cardboard boxes 70x70x70cm"): the title is a live parameter list. Watchable behavior: the piece's label reads as its counts, and the counts are the composition; units die and respawn per the list.
9. The named instrument (Wind Dynamic Organ, Prototype III; two 12k albums Dec 5 2025; "many sessions over a few years"): from anonymous swarms to one unique machine explored longform, like folk repertoire on a single instrument. Watchable behavior: one large device played in sessions, session marks accumulating quietly over time.
10. Body scale (f19 "beat box"): two cardboard boxes worn as headphones, motors at the ears, the room-filling swarm compressed onto the head. Watchable behavior: the mass collapses from architecture to the personal; the listener's position becomes the mix.

## Network (evidenced)
- Taylor Deupree <-> Zimoun (strongest, bidirectional): co-composed "Wind Dynamic Organ, Deviations" (12k2062, Dec 2025); Deupree mastered "ModularGuitarFields I-VI" (12k2057, 2023). IG evidence: @12k_label posts f12/f13. Also study #228.
- 12k label: the recordings strand (Modular, ModularGuitarFields, both Wind Dynamic Organ albums). The label explicitly separates his two strands: installative site-specific works vs purely acoustic studio compositions.
- Daniel Glaus: organist/composer who developed the Wind Dynamic Organ Prototype III (Bern Minster, HKB/SNSF research) that Zimoun recorded over years for both 2025 albums.
- Marc Beekhuis: co-founded the Leerraum label with Zimoun in 2003 (platform for reductive principles and radical material use).
- Lawrence English: Room40 founder, long-form book interview with Zimoun for "Guitar Studies I-III".
- FM3: "Live 19.06.2004" on Leerraum.
- Hannes Zweifel: architect, 329-motor toluene-tank permanent installation (Dottikon, 2013).
- Jason Cook / Digitalarti Lab Paris: control systems for the ventilator works (Lugano 2014, STRP 2015).
- Commissioning venues (caption-evidenced): MBAL Le Locle (f00), Le Centquatre Paris (f01, f02), Muxin Art Museum Wuzhen (f01), Museum of Fine Arts Taipei (f03), Kunst(Zeug)Haus (f05), Klangraum Krems (f14).
