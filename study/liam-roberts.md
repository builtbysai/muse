## The question: what does a fixed frame do to the form that does not obey it?

Roberts's pictures are built from two things that do not agree with each other: a field cut by straight lines, and one curve laid across the cut. In "Tidal" the field is two flat colours divided by a horizontal band, and the circle centred on the band takes its colour from whichever side of the line each of its points falls on -- cream above, navy below, so the circle is half one colour and half the other and belongs to neither side. In "Aperture" the field is four flat zones cut by a diagonal, and one large white circle sits across the cut, its colour belonging to none of the zones. The straight geometry makes the field; the curve refuses it. The question the pictures ask is how large the foreign body has to be, and how exactly it has to sit on the dividing line, before the field reads as its background rather than its equal. [C36119]

The second question is the series question, and it is the one Roberts himself names: a fixed grid of 36, "which varies in both size and material", examined through the idea of "Lusus Naturae" -- a freak of nature, a sport, a one-off variation thrown up by a fixed system. Each painting is supposed to be one possible outcome explored "until one emerges as unique and becomes a part of the series". The grid is the control; the anomaly is the experiment. But no source read states what the grid is mechanically -- 36 works, 36 cells, or 36 outcomes -- and no source states a selection rule beyond the eye deciding that a composition "emerges as unique". The study therefore has to do two things at once: take the pictures seriously as visible rules, and refuse to pretend the artist's practice is a computation when every source says it is judgement. [C36120]

**Takeaway:** Do not build "a Roberts" as a grid picture. Build the disagreement -- a straight field against one curve that does not belong to it -- and, where the series is concerned, build the frame (36, fixed) with the variation inside it, not a series machine with no rule to run. [C36121]

## The 36 Series: what it is and what it is not

The series is described, in identical wording on the Saatchi "Clarity" page and the Artfinder "Eventide" page, as "an ongoing painted exploration into a geometric grid of 36, which varies in both size and material", examining "the idea of 'Lusus Naturae' within the realms of geometry", where "each possible outcome is explored within the grid until one emerges as unique and becomes a part of the series" -- and there the sentence is truncated in every crawl of the Saatchi page; the ending is not available from Saatchi's text. (Artfinder's version adds "/Sport" after "Lusus Naturae".) "Lusus Naturae" is Latin for a freak or sport of nature: the series idea is that each painting is a one-off variation thrown up by a fixed system. [C36128]

Only two members are confirmed by listings that name the series: "Clarity" (2024, acrylic on canvas, 60 x 60 cm) and "Eventide" (acrylic on canvas, 60 x 60 cm, sold, GBP 560). "Aperture" and "Tidal" are the same format (square acrylic on canvas, 60 x 60 cm and 50 x 50 cm) but their captions do not mention the series, so their membership is unconfirmed; the other square acrylics (Coalesce, Notion, Grounded, Array, Embody, Optic, Serenity, Origin) are plausible members on format alone, and no listing consulted labels them as such. The series is described as "painted", so the wood reliefs are presumably not members. What stays fixed across the two confirmed members: square canvas, acrylic, flat hard-edge shapes, a small palette (2-4 colours plus a cream ground), and a vocabulary of circles, semicircles, triangles and diagonals. What varies: the palette, which shapes are used, and their arrangement. "Clarity" is three stacked offset squares in dark navy, turquoise and white; "Eventide" is three overlapping circles in turquoise, dark navy, coral red and cream, with no straight lines at all except the frame. They are not 36 similar paintings: the system throws up genuinely different compositions. But the visible rule is loose -- no source states a cell structure, a shape inventory, or a selection rule beyond "one emerges as unique". [C36129]

Two things must not be asserted. First, no 6 x 6 cell structure: none of the paintings viewed shows a grid of 36 cells; the visible paintings are single compositions, not grids. "A geometric grid of 36" may mean 36 works, 36 cells, or 36 outcomes, and no source read settles it. Second, the wording drift across runs is corrected here: study #823 called the series "an exhaustive painted grid", while #824 and #825 called it "an ongoing painted exploration of a geometric grid of 36, confirmed via Saatchi". "Exhaustive" implies a completed set; "ongoing" implies the series continues. The Saatchi text itself says "ongoing", and "ongoing" is carried; the #823 wording is wrong and must not be reused. [C36130]

**Takeaway:** The 36 Series is a frame with two free dimensions (size, material) and no stated algorithm. Treat 36 as a constant to build inside, never as a series machine to run -- there is no order, no path, and no stopping rule to execute. [C36131]

## Aperture and Tidal: the two saves

"Aperture" was posted on Friday 3 December 2021: acrylic on canvas, 60 x 60 cm, sold. A white triangle fills the top left; a mustard-yellow triangle fills the top right; a dark navy-blue field fills the right side; a teal field fills the bottom left; a large white circle sits bottom right, overlapping the navy field. One diagonal runs from upper left to lower right across the whole square. Four flat colours plus white, hard edges throughout. The circle's white is also a zone colour in the source picture -- the white triangle at top left -- which is why the prompt built on it (LRO2) tightens the rule to a fifth colour absent from the zone set, so the disc can never be confused with a zone. [C36132]

"Tidal" was posted on Monday 11 April 2022: acrylic on canvas, 50 x 50 cm. The whole painting is two colours only, deep navy blue and cream. A horizontal band boundary runs across the middle: navy above, cream below. One large circle is centred on that boundary, so its upper semicircle is cream against navy and its lower semicircle is navy against cream. The circle is the only motif. The local saved file is cropped tight at the top edge by the frame; the Instagram summary of the same post describes the same two-colour circle-and-band design. Tidal is the cleanest statement of Roberts's central move: a figure with no colour of its own, whose colour is a pure function of position relative to a divider. [C36133]

**Takeaway:** Hans saved the two pictures that state the rule most plainly. Tidal is one rule, fully visible; Aperture is the same rule with the field made complicated. Build from Tidal first. [C36134]

## The reliefs: light is the material

The middle of the arc is the single-colour wood relief, where shadow does the work colour used to do. "Worlds Apart" (2023, wood relief, 50 x 50 x 3 cm, limited edition of 5) is all white: two raised circles, one upper left and one lower right, cut by a single thin diagonal line running from top centre to bottom centre-right. Saatchi's text calls it "a study of light, exploring how the sun dances and creates moving shadows throughout the day", painted in a single colour "to show the purity of light and shadow", with the frame "an integral part of the relief". "Vella" (wood relief, size not stated in any source consulted) is the same mode at its purest: an all-white relief in a white frame against a terracotta wall, a 2 x 2 arrangement of raised quarter-circles, leaf or petal shapes and squares interlocking around a small central square, readable only through the shadows the raised shapes cast. "After Dark" (wood relief, 50 x 50 cm, edition of 10) is a single warm cream throughout, a pinwheel of four curved petals radiating from a centre point just left of middle, crossed by one thin diagonal; despite the title the work is pale, not dark. "Spark" and "Dark Celestial" (both relief, 50 x 50 cm) are the dark mirror: a single very dark navy or teal throughout, raised circles and arcs readable only by shadow. [C36135]

Colour returns to the relief format at larger scale in 2024-2025. "Apricity" (wood relief, 76 x 76 cm, edition of 5) is dark navy ground with four large overlapping circles in a 2 x 2 grid, each circle split into two semicircles of different colours -- steel blue, sage green, cream -- and the raised discs cast soft shadows, so the grid reads in depth as well as in colour; it was named Artfinder Sculpture of the Year 2024 (Saatchi only, unconfirmed independently). "Daybreak" (2025, wood relief, 76 x 50 cm, edition of 3, the only landscape-format work viewed) is a large dark-navy semicircle top left, a cream circle top right against a sky-blue field, cream semicircles along the bottom edge, and a terracotta-pink strip along the very bottom. Saatchi's text says the piece "explores the relationship between light, form and surface" and that "as natural light changes throughout the day, the piece casts evolving shadows that bring subtle movement and depth to its surface". "Causeway" (wood relief, 50 x 50 cm, edition of 3) is cream ground with a wide pale-lavender diagonal band, a large raised circle centre left split by the diagonal into a dark-navy upper half and a slate-blue lower half, and a dark-navy rectangle in the bottom-right corner. [C36136]

**Takeaway:** The reliefs prove the same rule as the paintings, with the paint removed: the drawing is not on the surface, it is cast onto it. A piece built on Daybreak should compute the shadow, never paint it. [C36137]

## Evolution: flat colour, then shadow, then colour again

Early (about 2021; People of Print images dated March 2021; "Aperture" December 2021; "Tidal" April 2022): flat-colour paintings and giclee prints in the PRESS series, plus linocuts. Colourful, multi-colour, square and portrait formats. The linocuts ("Scope Blue", an edition of 10, steel-blue ground with white radiating lines from a small central disc; and an untitled red starburst print, a repeating 3 x 4 grid of white radial starbursts) are the most line-based work he makes and do not reappear in later work viewed. Middle (2023): the single-colour wood relief arrives, and in the October 2023 interview he says the majority of his wood reliefs "have been single colour over the past few years". Recent (2024-2025): colour returns to the relief format -- "Apricity", "Daybreak", "Causeway" -- at larger scale, 76 x 76 cm and 80 x 80 cm reliefs where 50 x 50 cm was the early standard, while the 36 Series paintings continue alongside ("Clarity", 2024). The circle and the diagonal survive the whole arc unchanged. [C36138]

**Takeaway:** The arc is not a break but a subtraction and a return: colour removed so that shadow can be seen, then colour put back once the shadow is understood. The mechanisms below follow the same order -- first the colour rule, then the shadow rule. [C36139]

## Mechanisms

The flagged doubt is tested here and the flag stands: no source found states a rule, a procedure, a parameter walk, or a generative method for The 36 Series or any other work. "A geometric grid of 36, which varies in both size and material" names a fixed frame (36) and two free dimensions (size, material); it does not name an algorithm. "Lusus Naturae" names a theme, not a rule. His interview describes an eye-led method -- note shapes and colours, experiment until a composition "emerges as unique", choose the format after -- which is judgement, not computation. What follows is therefore not his procedure made executable. It is formalisation from the pictures: rules the pictures themselves state visibly, written in executable terms. Every mechanism is labelled OBSERVED (the picture itself states the rule) or FORMALISATION (this study's construction, built on the picture, not his documented procedure). [C36140]

## M1 -- Side-of-the-line colouring (OBSERVED, from "Tidal")

Subject: one circle crossed by one straight band. Visual principle: a figure has no colour of its own; its colour is a pure function of position relative to a divider. The line, not the figure, decides. Computational mechanism: plane divided by line L (angle theta, offset d). Figure F (a disc, centre c, radius r). Colour(p) = A if p is on one side of L, B if on the other, evaluated per point of F. Parameters: theta, d, c, r, colours A and B. Constraints: the line always crosses the figure (else the rule is invisible); A and B are fixed per picture; the figure never moves when L moves. State transition: moving L repaints F continuously; the repaint boundary sweeps across the disc. What varies: theta, d, the proportion of F in each colour. What is fixed: the disc, the two colours, the rule itself. Watchable behaviour: drag the line and the disc visibly repaints in real time; at the moment the line passes the disc's centre, the majority colour flips. Distinction from MAN6 (Mangold, #820): MAN6 holds an ellipse fixed while a colour boundary slides through it and the reading flips between one ellipse and two arcs -- a figure/ground reading test in which the ellipse keeps its own line and colour. M1 repaints the figure itself by rule; there is no reading flip, only a colour function. Different rule, different observable. [C36141]

**Takeaway:** The line is the author; the circle is the witness. Build the draggable line, not the circle. [C36142]

## M2 -- Diagonal field split with a counter-circle (OBSERVED, from "Aperture")

Subject: a square field cut by a diagonal into colour zones, with one large circle laid across the cut. Visual principle: straight geometry makes the field; one curved form refuses it. The circle's colour belongs to none of the zones, so it reads as a foreign body, not as a zone. Computational mechanism: square divided by two lines (one diagonal, one cross-cut) into four zones, each assigned a colour from a fixed set of four. One disc, radius about 0.35 of canvas width, centre near the canvas centre, coloured with a fifth colour absent from the zone set. Parameters: diagonal angle, cross-cut position, the four zone colours, disc radius and centre. Constraints: the disc always crosses at least one zone boundary; its colour never equals a zone colour; the zones always tile the square exactly. What varies: the diagonal angle, the zone colours, the disc's size and position. What is fixed: four zones, one disc, the colour-exclusion rule. Watchable behaviour: rotate the diagonal and the zones re-derive around the unmoving disc; move the disc and its intersections with the zone boundaries change; the disc is the only thing that never belongs. [C36143]

**Takeaway:** The disc that belongs to no zone is the picture. The colour-exclusion rule is the whole mechanism; without it this is just four coloured triangles. [C36144]

## M3 -- The intruding organic form / Lusus Naturae (FORMALISATION, built on the 36 Series theme and on M2)

Subject: a strict grid of 36 cells, and one form inside it that is not a cell. Visual principle: regularity makes the anomaly legible. The grid is the control; the intrusion is the experiment. The anomaly is a different kind of object (a disc, a blob, a diagonal) rather than a corrupted cell. Computational mechanism: fixed 6 x 6 grid of equal cells. One intruder: a disc of radius R centred at grid position p, growing from R = 0 to R covering about 3 cells. Cells overlapped by the disc are not recoloured -- they are occluded, pushed aside, or clipped by it, by a stated accommodation rule (occlusion, where the disc paints over cells; or displacement, where cells near the disc shrink away from it). Parameters: p, R, growth rate, accommodation rule. Constraints: exactly one intruder; the grid never changes its own structure; the intruder never becomes a cell (it keeps its own geometry at every size). What varies: the intruder's position, size, and growth. What is fixed: the 36-cell grid, the one-intruder rule, the accommodation rule. Watchable behaviour: the intruder grows and the grid visibly gives way around it; at full size the grid reads as a frame around a foreign body. Distinction from KFR7 "The Disobedient Square" (#821): KFR7 is a search game -- exactly one cell of the grid itself misbehaves (rotated 45 degrees, stretched about 1.07x, replaced by a rectangle), and the piece is judged by whether the viewer can find it in seconds. M3's intruder is not a cell and is not hidden; it is openly a foreign body, and the observable is the grid accommodating it, not the viewer finding it. Different object, different observable, different test. [C36145]

**Takeaway:** The anomaly is not a mistake in the grid. It is a living thing that grew there, and the grid's job is to make that visible by giving way. [C36146]

## M4 -- The fixed 36-frame (OBSERVED as a frame; the series-machine reading is REJECTED)

Subject: the number 36 as a container. Visual principle: a fixed count is a constraint the hand works against -- 36 identical slots, each filled differently, the count never negotiable. Computational mechanism as a frame: N = 36 is a constant, never a parameter. Each of the 36 slots holds one small picture; the slot contents vary by size and material (the two dimensions Roberts names), while the frame -- 6 x 6, equal cells, one picture per cell -- never varies. Parameters: per-slot size and material only. What varies: what is inside the slots. What is fixed: the count, the grid, the one-per-cell rule. Watchable behaviour: none on its own -- a frame is not a behaviour. That is the verdict: M4 survives only as a parameter (N = 36) used inside other mechanisms, never as a piece by itself. As a standalone series machine it is rejected as a duplicate (see the dedupe section below): MAN7 "The Series Machine" (Mangold #820, PP5733) enumerates a variation space with a visited set and terminates on a checkable exhaustion rule, and KFR6 "The Series Machine (Fritz)" (Fritz #821, PP5740) walks a parameter vector in Gray-code order with one change per step and the observable is causality. Roberts's version has no enumeration rule, no path, and no termination condition -- the series is explicitly "ongoing" -- so there is nothing to build that is not already built better by MAN7 (if order matters) or KFR6 (if stepwise causality matters). The Roberts version differs from both by rule (fixed N = 36, no path, no exhaustion), but the difference is a subtraction, not a mechanism: a series without a series rule. The build leg should build MAN7 and KFR6 first and not build a third series machine from Roberts. What survives from M4 is only the constant N = 36, used as a frame inside LRO3 and LRO6. [C36147]

**Takeaway:** A fixed count is a frame, not a machine. Do not mistake the number 36 for a system. [C36148]

## M5 -- Moving-sun relief shadows (OBSERVED, from "Daybreak")

Subject: a fixed geometric wood relief under a moving sun. Visual principle: the drawing is not painted on the surface; it is cast onto it. The relief is constant, the light moves, and the shadow is the picture -- so the picture changes all day while nothing in the work changes. Computational mechanism: a relief height field h(x, y) over a rectangle, built from a small set of flat-topped geometric prisms (bars, discs, triangles) at fixed positions and heights. A directional light at azimuth theta(t) and elevation phi(t), driven by a time variable t running over one day. Shadow length and direction at each point are computed from h and the light vector; shadowed area is darkened by a fixed factor. Parameters: t (time of day), the prism layout, the relief heights. Constraints: the relief never moves; the light path is a fixed arc; shadows are computed, never painted. What varies: t, hence every shadow's length and direction. What is fixed: the relief, the light's arc, the shadow rule. Watchable behaviour: scrub time and the shadows sweep across the relief, lengthening toward morning and evening, shortest at noon; a prism's shadow visibly crosses its neighbour at a reproducible time. Interaction earned: a time scrubber, because time is the parameter. [C36149]

**Takeaway:** The relief as a sundial is the picture. If a shadow is painted rather than computed, the piece is a picture of a relief, not a relief. [C36150]

## M6 -- Montage search / the composition that locks (FORMALISATION, built on the interview method)

Subject: a handful of noted shapes and colours, rearranged until the arrangement is "unique". Visual principle: a composition is found, not drawn. Fragments from different places and moments are tried against each other until one arrangement holds together; the search itself is visible. Computational mechanism: a fixed set of k fragments (shapes with assigned colours, k about 6-9). A scoring function S over an arrangement: balance (the colour-mass centroid within the central third), separation (no two fragments of the same colour adjacent), dominance (exactly one fragment larger than all others by at least 1.5x). A stochastic search: at each step, move one fragment by a small random displacement or swap two fragments; keep the move if S improves, else keep it with small probability (simulated annealing). The arrangement locks when S exceeds a fixed threshold. Parameters: the fragment set, the annealing temperature schedule, the lock threshold. Constraints: the fragment set never changes mid-search; the lock threshold is fixed at authoring; once locked, the picture stays locked until the viewer unlocks it. What varies: fragment positions during the search. What is fixed: the fragments, the scoring function, the threshold. Watchable behaviour: fragments jitter and settle, visibly converging; the lock is an event (motion stops, one arrangement holds). Honest flag: the scoring function is this study's invention -- Roberts's "emerges as unique" is his eye, and S is a stand-in for it, stated as such. This is the weakest surviving mechanism and the most dependent on the scoring function being genuinely discriminating rather than a rationalisation; the build leg should judge the piece on whether the locked arrangements actually read as compositions. [C36151]

**Takeaway:** The search is the picture; the lock is the event. If the lock does not read as a composition, the scoring function is wrong, not the idea. [C36152]

## Rejections

Six candidates were considered and dropped, and are recorded so they are not re-proposed. The Roberts series machine (M4 as a machine): no series rule exists to execute; MAN7 and KFR6 already occupy the form better. Colour relativity / Albers interaction (M7): a perceptual demonstration, not a system with state or evolution -- a palette-adjacency rule, which this project's own rule counts as not-a-mechanism on its own. Format follows composition (M8): a framing rule; the only variation is the aspect ratio, which is not watchable as behaviour. One disobedient cell in the grid (M9): KFR7 "The Disobedient Square" by rule, not by resemblance -- same object, same deviation types, same observable. Colour-by-plane relief as its own prompt (M10): folded into LRO4 as a variant; alone it is a palette on a height field. A second Tidal variant with multiple figures: not genuinely different from LRO1 -- same rule, more discs -- so not offered; LRO6 is the multi-instance piece instead, and it differs by rule (a field of instances, one shared phase). A "grid of 36 Apertures": a grid of variants with no shared parameter is a series machine in disguise; rejected under the M4 verdict. [C36153]

## Aesthetic logic

Roberts's logic is a designer's logic: a small vocabulary, a flat surface, and one decision made visible. The vocabulary never grows -- circle, semicircle, triangle, diagonal, the 2 x 2 quadrant -- and the palette never grows past four colours plus a cream ground, with dark navy present in about two-thirds of the works viewed. What grows is the format: from 50 x 50 cm paintings to 76 x 76 cm and 80 x 80 cm reliefs, and from flat paint to raised wood, so that the same shapes are first read in colour and then read in shadow. The discipline is subtraction: Tidal is two colours and one circle; "Notion" is two colours and one star; "Vella" is no colour at all. A piece built on Roberts should feel like a decision held still -- the line placed, the disc laid across it, the light doing the rest -- and it should never add a second decision to decorate the first. The most Roberts-like thing a generated piece can do is refuse to add anything. [C36154]
