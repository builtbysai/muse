## M1. One box, three voices

The cajon is a drum kit in one box, and the one box is the mechanism. The palm flat on the middle of the tapa, the front face, gives the low bass, and the fingers on the top edge give the high slap, and the fingertips give the soft ghost tap, and the three strokes are the kit, because the bass is the kick drum and the slap is the snare and the tap is the ghost note, and the kit is one box, and the one box is the player sitting on it, and the sitting is the mechanism, because the player is astride the drum the way a rider is astride a horse, and the riding is the posture, and the posture is the sound. What is Caitro-specific is the touch, and the touch is the proof: Chabuca told him "Carajo negro haces llorar el cajon", damn black man, you make the cajon weep, and the weeping is the mechanism, because the bass is not struck, it is drawn out, and the drawing-out is the hand staying on the wood a fraction longer than a strike, and the staying is the warmth, and the warmth is the weeping, and the weeping is the voice, and the voice is the box. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the world: the box leaves Peru in one man's hands and becomes the heartbeat of the flamenco tablao, and the heartbeat is the same three strokes, and the three strokes are the proof that the kit was always in the box, and the box was always a kit, and the kit was always one man sitting on it. Watch the box: when the palm lands in the middle, and the bass blooms low, and the fingers answer at the edge, and the slap cuts high, and the fingertips whisper in between, and one seated man is the whole low end of the band, and the box weeps instead of banging, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** sit on one box, and let three strokes be the kit. The box is the band. Against the chain: Casaverde (#365 M1) also reduced a crowd to one instrument, but his was a guitar that had to be bass and harmony and melody and rhythm at once, and the guitar was the crowd; Caitro is a box that is the drum kit, and the box is the crowd, which is why his version is a reduction of the percussion and Casaverde's is a reduction of everything. The Kipus (#364 M2) also built their sound from a weave, but theirs was a weave of two guitars that answer each other; Caitro is a weave of three strokes from one pair of hands, which is why their version is a dialogue and his is a monologue that contains the dialogue.

## M2. Cajonero, not cajoneador

He draws a line between two words, and the line is the mechanism. Caitro's own distinction, recorded in "De Cajon" p. 54 and quoted by Carazas: the cajoneador "golpea fuerte sin 'respetar al cantante y a los musicos acompan~antes'", he hits hard without respecting the singer and the accompanying musicians, and the cajonero "toca armoniosamente el instrumento e inventa nuevos golpes", he plays the instrument harmoniously and invents new strokes, and the inventing is the mechanism, because mastery is restraint plus invention, and the restraint is the respect, and the respect is the singer staying audible, and the invention is the new stroke appearing inside the accompaniment rather than on top of it, and the inside is the harmony, and the harmony is the cajonero, and the cajonero is a synonym for "creador", creator, and the creator is the man. The distinction is the ethics of the instrument, and the ethics is the proof: the box is loud enough to cover a voice, and the loudness is the temptation, and the temptation is the cajoneador, and the refusing is the cajonero, and the refusing is the art, and the art is the accompaniment that never covers what it accompanies, and the never-covering is the mechanism. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the reputation: Chabuca keeps him beside her for twenty-six years, from the Plaza de Acho meeting in 1957 to her death in 1983, and the keeping is the proof, because a singer who is drowned by her percussionist does not keep him for twenty-six years, and the twenty-six years are the respect, and the respect is the cajonero, and the cajonero is the creator. Watch the distinction: when the voice sings, and the cajon stays under it, and a new stroke appears inside the pattern, and the pattern does not get louder, and the singer does not have to fight the box, and the box invents without interrupting, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** respect the voice, and invent inside the pattern. The restraint is the creation. Against the chain: Casaverde (#365 M10) also refused to accompany in the ordinary sense, but his was a guitar that made room for the voice to become itself, and the room was the guitar; Caitro is a box that makes room by staying quiet enough to invent, which is why his version is a restraint and Casaverde's is a room. Baca (#363) also built her work from witnesses and fragments, but hers was a harvest; Caitro is a harvest of strokes, which is why her version is an archive and his is a technique.

## M3. The lando as a slow machine

The lando is slow, heavy and strophic, and the slowness is the mechanism. Per Tompkins via Carazas, the song opens with an instrumental prelude, and an instrumental interlude can divide the sections, and the melody runs in two or three sections in a general strophic form, and the text is not bound to a fixed poetic form, and the repetitions follow the melody, and the following is the mechanism, because the lando is a machine that turns slowly, and the turning is the verse, and the verse is the step, and the step is the cajon marking a heavy tread, and the tread is the guitar answering in the gaps, and the answering is the prelude, and the prelude is the machine warming up, and the warming-up is the song. Caitro's "Toro mata" is a lando WITH a fuga, and the with is the mechanism in M4, and the machine is slow until it is not, and the not is the gear-change, and the gear-change is the song, and the song is the lando. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the life: the boy who starts work at 7:30 in the morning in the Can~ete fields and ends when the mail plane passes, and the man who drives a taxi for nine years after Chabuca dies, and the old man who comes back to play in 1992, are all the same slow machine, and the slowness is the patience, and the patience is the lando, and the lando is the man. Watch the machine: when the verses hold long, and the cajon marks a heavy step, and the guitar answers in the gaps, and the prelude returns between the sections, and the song turns slowly, and the turning is the tread, and the tread is the lando, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the song turn slowly, and let the cajon mark the step. The tread is the machine. Against the chain: Casaverde (#365 M6) also made the lando slow, but his was a slowness of 60 to 70 beats per minute so the poetry could run like speech, and the speech was the guitar; Caitro is a slowness of the step, and the step is the cajon, which is why his version is a tread and Casaverde's is a voice. Eva (#362) also sang the lando, but hers was a voice that carried three bloods; Caitro is a box that carries the tread, which is why her version is a throat and his is a footstep.

## M4. The fuga as the release valve

Every Afro-Peruvian song-form ends in a fuga, and the ending is the mechanism. The fuga is a faster closing section that lets the tension out, and the letting-out is the mechanism, because the slow lando holds the tension for minutes, and the holding is the tread, and the tread is the weight, and the weight needs a release, and the release is the fuga, and the fuga is the gear-change, and the gear-change is the song changing speed without changing song, and the changing is the chorus taking over, and the taking-over is the joy, and the joy is the valve, and the valve is the piece. In "Toro mata" the fuga is a borrowed old Can~ete festejo, "Lima 'ta hablar y Can~ete 'ta ponde", Lima is talking and Can~ete is answering, and the borrowing is the mechanism too, because the fuga is not composed, it is found, and the finding is the recopilacion in M5, and the recopilacion is the song, and the song is the assembly, and the assembly is the man. Tompkins via Carazas gives the rule in general: a complete festejo "suele tener uno o mas estribillos y algun tipo de coro que suele tomar la forma de una fuga", it usually has one or more refrains and some kind of chorus that usually takes the form of a fuga, and the usually is the proof that the valve is the form, and the form is the release, and the release is the fuga. Watch the valve: when the slow lando has held for minutes, and the chorus suddenly quickens, and the cajon runs faster, and the guitar runs with it, and the song is the same song at twice the speed, and the tension leaves through the fuga, and the fuga closes the song, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** build the fast ending into the slow song, and let the chorus take it. The gear-change is the release. Against the chain: Casaverde (#365 M12) also built an escape valve into the suite, but his was a festejo of 28 bars of 6/8 at 130 beats per minute inside the lando, and the valve was a divertimento the lando retook to close; Caitro is a fuga that IS the closing, and the closing is the form, which is why his version is a structure and Casaverde's is a visit. The Kipus (#364) also had a breakthrough that came from a contest, but theirs was a prize that opened doors; Caitro is a gear-change that opens the tempo, which is why their version is a door and his is a valve.

## M5. Recopilacion as composition

He does not write "Toro mata", he assembles it, and the assembling is the mechanism. Caitro's own words, in "De Cajon" p. 81, quoted by Carazas and in the blog: "El Toro mata viene de lo que me contaba mi abuela, mi bisabuela. La cancion la arme y la hice yo con cosas que me narraban mis ancestros", the Toro mata comes from what my grandmother, my great-grandmother told me, I put the song together and made it myself with things my ancestors narrated to me, and the putting-together is the mechanism, because the song is not invented, it is gathered, and the gathering is the composition, and the composition is the seams, and the seams are the ages, and the ages are the fragments, and the fragments are a 1912 text published in the booklet of Nicomedes's "Cumanana" album of 1964, and a grandmother's story, and a festejo fuga borrowed from Ernesta Laguna Indalecio of San Luis, and the borrowing is the composing, and the composing is the assembly, and the assembly is the man. He distinguishes his version from the earlier one, and the distinguishing is the proof: Rosa Mercedes Ayarza de Morales collected a version in the 1930s that is a lamento in which the bull kills the bullfighter, "ya lo llevan a enterrar, el toro mata", they are already carrying him to bury him, the bull kills, and his is a lando con fuga in which the bull is "rumbambero", shiny and dancing, and the dancing is the difference, and the difference is the assembly, and the assembly is the song. And the correction that matters most: "Toro mata" is NOT Nicomedes Santa Cruz's song, and the not is the mechanism at the level of the record, because Nicomedes recorded extracts in "Ahi viene mi caporal" in 1964 and published a 1912 text in that album's booklet, and he is a transmitter and a fellow revivalist, not the author, and the author is the assembly, and the assembly is Caitro, and the Caitro is the grandmothers, and the grandmothers are the song. Watch the assembly: when the 1912 text appears, and the grandmother's story appears, and the festejo fuga appears, and the three fragments are stitched into one song, and the seams are visible, and the visible seams are the composition, and the bull dances instead of killing, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** gather the fragments, and let the seams be the song. The assembly is the authorship. Against the chain: Baca (#363) also built her work from witnesses and fragments, but hers was a harvest of testimonies across the Caribbean and Latin America, and the harvest was an archive; Caitro is a harvest of fragments from one town, San Luis de Can~ete, which is why her version is a diaspora and his is a household. Chabuca (#361) also gathered, but hers was a gathering of forms, vals and marinera and lando, into a repertoire; Caitro is a gathering of fragments into one song, which is why her version is a catalogue and his is a stitching.

## M6. The song as a three-stranded story

One cheerful chorus carries three stories, and the carrying is the mechanism. Carazas reads "Toro mata" as three themes braided: first, the bullfight and the Black bullfighter whom "la color no le permite hacer el quite", the colour does not allow him to do the quite, the pass, so race decides who gets the credit for the faena; second, hunger, "Toro viejo se murio, man~ana comemos caine", the old bull died, tomorrow we eat meat, beef the poor could only eat when the bull died in the ring; third, the runaway from Acari who "no se habia enterado de la libertad", had not heard about freedom, hid among the cane, arrived starving at Laponde, and was recognised as a stranger by the brand, the carimba, burned into his body, a nineteenth-century slavery memory carried in a dance song, and the carrying is the mechanism, because the same chorus that jokes about the bull also remembers the brand, and the remembering is the braid, and the braid is the song, and the song is the history, and the history is the dance, and the dance is the man. The verses name Pititi, Laponde and Acari like places on a map, and the naming is the proof: Pititi is Eusebio Sirio, the fellow cajonero and dancer whose name Caitro added to the song after seeing him dance it, and Laponde is the hacienda, and Acari is the town, and the map is the song, and the song is the coast, and the coast is the memory. Watch the braid: when the chorus sounds cheerful, and the verse names the bullfighter who gets no credit, and the next verse names the meat that only comes when the bull dies, and the next verse names the runaway recognised by his brand, and the three stories run in one song, and the song dances while it remembers, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** braid the joke, the hunger and the brand into one chorus. The braid is the history. Against the chain: Casaverde (#365 M9) also argued about the label, but his was an argument with Nicomedes that ended in a concession at SICLA in 1986, and the concession was the proof; Caitro is an argument carried inside a dance song, which is why his version is a debate and Caitro's is a chorus. Nicomedes Santa Cruz, the next candidate in the chain after Ronaldo, is the man who preserved the decimas Caitro only half-remembers, and the half-remembering is the doorway to him, because the chain's next study is the other side of this one.

## M7. The guitar answers the cajon

In Chabuca's late lando the two instruments are one mechanism, and the one mechanism is the doorway. Carazas states it in a footnote: "el arreglo musical de la guitarra es de Felix Casaverde y del cajon de Caitro Soto", the musical arrangement of the guitar is Felix Casaverde's and of the cajon is Caitro Soto's, and the stating is the mechanism, because the lando is not arranged for guitar plus cajon, it is arranged as guitar and cajon together, and the together is the arrangement, and the arrangement is the duet, and the duet is the sound, and Elias Llanos hears "Tarimba Negra" the same way, as Chabuca, Felix and Caitro together, "todo... ban~ado de guitarra y cajon", everything bathed in guitar and cajon, with string and wind arrangements and a piano on top, and the bathing is the mechanism, because the guitar states the lando's harmony and the cajon states its step, and neither leads, and the not-leading is the duet, and the duet is the stage, and the stage is the voice riding between them, and the riding is the song. This is the doorway mechanism from #365, where Casaverde's single guitar had to be the whole band, and the whole band was the reduction, and here the guitar has the cajon beside it, and the beside is the sharing, and the sharing is the band, and the band is two, and the two are one, and the one is the sound. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the working life: the two men are the rhythm section of Chabuca's Afro-Peruvian stage from 1974 to 1977, and the section is the proof that the duet was not a record, it was a job, and the job was the stage, and the stage was Madrid in 1977 and 1978, and Madrid was the OTI and "300 millones" and "Tarimba Negra", and the record is the duet, and the duet is the mechanism. Watch the duet: when the guitar states the harmony, and the cajon states the step, and the voice rides between them, and neither instrument leads, and the two share the band, and the sharing is the sound, and the sound is bathed in guitar and cajon, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the guitar answer the cajon, and let neither lead. The answering is the band. Against the chain: Casaverde (#365 M1) also made one guitar be the band, but his was a monologue that contained the dialogue, and the monologue was the reduction; Caitro is the dialogue itself, and the dialogue is the duet, which is why his version is a conversation and Casaverde's is a soliloquy. The Kipus (#364 M2) also had two guitars that answer each other, but theirs was a weave of strings; Caitro is a weave of string and wood, which is why their version is a harmony and his is a rhythm.

## M8. The reduced percussion lattice

Peru Negro's kit is small and interlocked, and the interlocking is the mechanism. Two cajones, Ronaldo Campos and Pititi Sirio, and a tumba, conga and quinto, Guillermo "El Nin~o" Nicasio, and a bongo, Julio "Chocolate" Algendones, and a quijada and a cencerro, Orlando Soto, and a first and a second guitar, Linder Gongora and Isidoro Izquierdo, and two voices, Lucila Campos and Caitro Soto, and the list is the mechanism, because there is no drum kit and no bass guitar, and the no-drum-kit is the lattice, and the lattice is the weave, and the weave is the groove, and the groove is five or six players each holding one thread, and the holding is the pattern, and the pattern is the song, and the low end is the cajon, and the rattle is a donkey's jawbone, and the bell is a cowbell, and the jawbone and the cowbell are the proof that the kit is made of what the farm left behind, and the farm is the origin, and the origin is the lattice. Remove one thread and the pattern sags, and the sagging is the mechanism made visible, because the lattice has no redundancy, and the no-redundancy is the interlocking, and the interlocking is the discipline, and the discipline is Peru Negro, and Peru Negro is the company, and the company is the sound. Watch the lattice: when the two cajones hold the low end, and the tumba answers, and the bongo ticks, and the jawbone rattles, and the cowbell rings, and the two guitars weave, and the two voices ride, and one thread goes quiet, and the pattern sags, and the sagging is the proof that every thread matters, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** weave a few threads tight, and let the sag show the weave. The lattice is the groove. Against the chain: Casaverde (#365 M7) also cleared the space, but his was a clearing of the big Latin percussion so the guitar and the cajon could be one body, and the one body was the sensuality; Caitro is a clearing that keeps five small threads, which is why his version is a lattice and Casaverde's is a unity. Chabuca (#361) also reduced the orchestra to a small group, but hers was a chamber; Caitro is a lattice, which is why her version is a room and his is a weave.

## M9. Zapateo as percussion and as duel

He dances with his feet as well as his hands, and the feet are the mechanism. Caitro is a zapateador as well as a cajonero, and he distinguishes three zapateos, in "De Cajon" p. 50, quoted by Carazas: first, zapateo de chacra, barefoot, flat foot on the earth, in the haciendas; second, zapateo with shoes in church for Christmas and the Danza de Negritos, with the violin macho; third, zapateo for the stage, "punta y el taco, con redobles que no usan todo el pie", toe and heel, with rolls that do not use the whole foot, and the three are the mechanism, because the same dance changes its foot for the field, the church and the stage, and the changing is the adaptation, and the adaptation is the dance, and the dance is the man. The form is a competition, and the competition is the mechanism: "Siempre hay un rival y uno tiene que superar... En su turno el zapateador es libre y siempre zapatea en contrapunto. Cada uno hace su figura, uno por uno, y siempre tiene que hacerla mejor que el anterior. El zapateo tiene sus amarres. Como comienza, asi tiene que terminar", there is always a rival and one has to surpass him, in his turn the zapateador is free and always zapateas in counterpoint, each one makes his figure, one by one, and always has to make it better than the previous one, the zapateo has its ties, as it begins, so it has to end, and the ties are the mechanism, because the duel is free inside a frame, and the frame is the beginning and the end, and the beginning and the end are the same, and the sameness is the discipline, and the discipline is the dance. Watch the duel: when the first dancer makes his figure, and the second dancer answers it, and the answer tops it, and the feet are a second drum answering the cajon, and each figure is better than the last, and the dance ends the way it began, and the ending is the tie, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** dance the duel, and tie the ending to the beginning. The counterpoint is the competition. Against the chain: the Kipus (#364) also had a stage, but theirs was a stage that launched caravans; Caitro is a stage that holds a duel, which is why their version is a road and his is a ring. Eva (#362) also danced, but hers was a voice that danced; Caitro is feet that dance, which is why her version is a throat and his is a floor.

## M10. The lyric as spoken language

The songs are not written in standard Spanish and then sung; the singing IS the spelling, and the spelling is the mechanism. Seven of the twelve songs in "De Cajon" are written in the phonetic spelling of Afro-coastal speech, and Carazas lists the rules: elision of final /s/, "te comite a mi currun~ao"; elision of /r/, "a saca' camote", "zapatea'"; /r/ for /l/, "afroja'"; /r/ for /rr/, "boriquita"; elision of /d/, "llevao"; reinforced vowels, "caine" for carne, "goido" for gordo, and the listing is the mechanism, because the words on the page look misspelled until you hear them, and then the spelling is the sound, and the sound is the speech, and the speech is the coast, and the coast is the song, and the song is the spelling, and the spelling is the proof that the lyric was never written, it was heard, and the hearing is the recopilacion, and the recopilacion is M5, and the recopilacion is the man.
