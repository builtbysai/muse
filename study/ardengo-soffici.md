## M1. The bridge: Paris carried to Florence in a person

Soffici is not a theorist of the avant-garde; he is its courier. He went to Paris in 1900 for the Exposition Universelle with companions from the Scuola libera del nudo, Umberto Brunelleschi, Giovanni Costetti, Gino Melis, and he alone stayed seven years, living by drawing for the Paris satirical reviews, Le Rire, Gil Blas, L'Assiette au beurre, Le Frou-Frou, and he learned the new art by living inside it, not by reading about it: at the offices of La Plume he met Charles Guerin, who made him appreciate Henri Rousseau, "il Doganiere"; he frequented the Musee du Luxembourg and the Louvre, approaching Manet, Degas, Renoir, Monet, Cezanne, Pissarro, Sisley, Odilon Redon; he joined the Societe des artistes independants in 1902 and exhibited seven works at the Salon des independants in March 1902, marked by primitivism and symbolism, and Tristan Klingsor wrote favourably in La Plume; he approached Impressionism and Post-Impressionism through the Durand-Ruel gallery and the dealer-writer Ambroise Vollard; he lived in the Bateau-Lavoir circle, Braque, Derain, Picasso, Juan Gris, Apollinaire. Then he carried it home: he signed the first Italian article on Paul Cezanne, in Vita d'arte, Siena, 1908; he published articles on Rosso, on Impressionism, and on the Futurists in La Voce; in 1910 he was sent to Paris by La Voce to find Impressionist works for a Florence show, and he met Rosso personally and decided to include a room of his sculptures, see M10; when Papini visited Paris, Soffici guided him and introduced him to his circle, Picasso, Juan Gris, Max Jacob, Apollinaire, and the correspondence between the two Tuscans became constant and their friendship intimate. The mechanism is that the bridge is a person who has lived on both banks, and the ideas arrive as friendships and as rooms full of pictures, not as manifestos. Rosai's education runs through this bridge: Soffici pointed him to Cezanne first among the French, then to Picasso, the Impressionists, Rousseau, see D1.

**Takeaway:** let the bridge be a person who lived on both banks, and the piece should carry Paris to Florence as a friendship, not as a manifesto.

## M2. Plane decomposition: one lamp, many viewpoints

The key Cubist work is an ordinary lamp. Decomposizione dei piani di una lampada, also titled Scomposizione dei piani di un lume, 1912 to 1913, oil on fibreboard, 45 by 35 cm, Estorick Collection, London: muted tones, a static subject, multiple perspectives, and to the left stands an oil lamp surmounted by a cluster of triangular forms offering different views of its shade. The Estorick's description is the whole mechanism in one sentence: one object, faceted into simultaneous viewpoints, and the facets are triangles, and the triangles are the shade seen from several places at once. The painting was shown at a Futurist group show in Rome in early 1913, and Marinetti bought it from Soffici in 1913 and wrote thanking him "for having given me the pleasure of seeing in my living room a painting which impressed me from the first day I saw it", and it stayed in the family collection until 1950, when Eric Estorick bought it from Benedetta Marinetti. The same decomposition runs through the other 1912 to 1913 works: Piani e linee di una donna che si pettina, dal vero, 1912, planes and lines of a woman combing her hair, from life, shown in the 2013 Lacerba 1913 exhibition; Le Pont, Scomposizione dei piani di un ponte, 1913, the bridge, its planes decomposed; Linee di una strada, 1913, lines of a street; Sintesi di un paesaggio autunnale, 1913, synthesis of an autumn landscape, the Futurist programme in miniature, a landscape reduced to its lines. Soffici tried the first plastic decompositions of landscapes, figures and still lifes in the summer of 1912 at Poggio a Caiano, after meeting the Kiev painter Alexandra Exter, "Aissa", in Paris and approaching Cubism through her. The watchable behaviour is a still, humble object, a lamp, a bridge, a woman combing her hair, that refuses to sit still in one viewpoint, and the facets multiply without the object moving, because the painter walked around it and the picture keeps all the walks at once.

**Takeaway:** take one lamp and show it from everywhere at once, and the piece should facet the still object, not chase the moving one.

## M3. The muted Cubist palette

Grey, ochre, brown: analysis instead of colour. The lamp painting is in muted tones, and the muted tones are the point: Soffici's Cubism is not the Fauve colour he knew in Paris, and it is not the Futurist noise he joined in 1913, it is a grey, ochre and brown analysis of a still subject, and the analysis is what the colour is for. The early Paris works already show the temper: Mendiants, 1907, exhibited at the Salon d'automne in Paris in October 1907, beggars as subject, shown the year he left Paris for good; Il bagno, 1905, oil on canvas, shown in the Uffizi 2016 exhibition "Scoperte e massacri" as an early work; Figura, 1903, a figure recalling the first Paris experiences in a Post-Impressionist key. The 1911 works show the same restraint under Impressionist light: Trasporto funebre, 1910, a funeral procession, "dalle spiccate suggestioni impressioniste", of strong Impressionist suggestions; Margherite, 1911, daisies; La route, 1911, a road; Bagnanti, 1911, bathers, "le famose Bagnanti del 1911". Even the joyful exception proves the rule: the Composizioni di fiori of 1914 and 1917 are "una vera esplosione gioiosa che la pittura esprime compiutamente nel colore", a joyful explosion that painting expresses fully in colour, and the explosion is news precisely because the palette is usually held back. The watchable behaviour is a picture that analyses before it colours, and the colour arrives late and sparingly, and the restraint is the style, because the painter is a critic first and the criticism is done in grey.

**Takeaway:** analyse in grey and ochre and let the colour arrive late, and the piece should be restrained before it is bright.

## M4. Neutral subjects under radical theory

Landscapes, still lifes and figures carry Futurism; never machines or speed. Soffici's Futurist season, 1913 to 1914, produced its most significant works dialoguing with Cezanne and the Cubists, and the subjects are a lamp, a bridge, a street, an autumn landscape, a woman combing her hair, a still life with a measuring tape, Il metro, 1911, oil on cardboard, a humble measuring tool as a Cubist-era still life. He published in Lacerba "Teoria del movimento del futurismo plastico", Theory of the movement of plastic Futurism, accepting that Futurism had reconciled what had seemed irreconcilable, Impressionism and Cubism, and the reconciliation is visible in the subjects: the theory is radical, the subject is neutral, and the neutrality is what lets the theory be seen, because a lamp faceted into triangles shows the faceting, and a speeding car would only show the speed. His 1913 paintings, Linee di una strada and Sintesi di un paesaggio autunnale, showed the Futurist influence in method and title, per en.wikipedia, and the method is the decomposition and the synthesis, and the title announces it, and the street and the landscape stay a street and a landscape. The contrast is with the Milanese Futurists, whose subjects were the machine and the crowd, and Soffici's Florentine Futurism is a Futurism of still things, and that is why Rosai could take it from him: Rosai's walls and card games are the same neutral subjects, carried forward, see #433 M6 and M7. The watchable behaviour is a radical method applied to the most ordinary thing in the room, and the ordinary thing is what proves the method, because the method changes the seeing, not the subject.

**Takeaway:** put the radical method on the most ordinary thing in the room, and the piece should facet a lamp, not chase a car.

## M5. Typography as image: BIFZF+18

The book that eats colour. BIFZF+18. Simultanieta e chimismi lirici, 1915, Edizioni della Voce, Florence, edition of 300, cover a hand-coloured collage of posters by Soffici himself, Papini: "una compenetrazione di manifesti colorati a mano, coi piu vivi blu, verdi, gialli, rossi", a compenetration of hand-coloured posters, with the liveliest blues, greens, yellows, reds. Two halves: Simultanita, 12 simultaneous poems in standard typography; Chimismi lirici, 10 poems using multiple fonts, signs, adverts, brand names, repetition and onomatopoeia, contemporary with Marinetti's Zang Tumb Tumb, prefiguring his Les Mots en Liberte Futuristes of 1919. A sample from Aeroplano: the pilot eats blues, "I eat violet blue, slices of azure; I swallow tankards of cobalt blue, heavens of lapis lazuli", colour eaten as sensation. The book stands in the area of European visual poetry of which Apollinaire had been the forefather, and it is described as "absolutely the most important book that came out of Florentine Futurism", per Scudiero. It was reprinted in 1919, which is why en.wikipedia's lead dates it 1919, see C1, and again by Vallecchi in 2002, anastatic, and never published in full in translation, and original copies became scarcer when the Arno flooded in 1966, and a copy of the Tipografia page is at the Getty Center. The mechanism is that the typography IS the image: the fonts change size and weight the way a painter changes brushstrokes, the brand names and adverts are readymade colour, the repetition is rhythm, the onomatopoeia is sound made visible, and the poem is also a picture, because the page is composed like a collage. The watchable behaviour is words that behave like objects on a page, growing, shrinking, colliding, repeating, and the reading is a looking, because the letters are the picture.

**Takeaway:** let the letters be the picture, and the piece should set words like objects, growing and colliding on the page.

## M6. Simultaneity: woman, cart, street, all at once

Spatial and temporal simultaneity as a poetics, from Apollinaire to the collage. Simultaneita di donna carretto strada, January 1914, Soffici's first collage, published in Lacerba in January 1914, per Del Puppo, "Realta bruta", Prospettiva 97, 2000: a simultaneity of woman, cart, street, collage as the meeting of real materials and Futurist simultaneity. Papini attacked collage in "Il cerchio si chiude" a month later, 14 February 1914, a Saturday, and the attack is part of the mechanism, because the Florentines were already arguing about what Futurism was, and the argument is the movement. The simultaneity is the same idea as BIFZF+18, see M5, and the same idea as the lamp painting, see M2: several things, several viewpoints, several moments, held in one frame at once, and the frame does not choose between them, because the choosing would be the old painting, and the simultaneity is the new one. Apollinaire is the forefather of the visual poetry, and Soffici painted his portrait in 1914, Ritratto di Guillaume Apollinaire, shown in the 2013 Lacerba 1913 exhibition, and wrote the monograph Apollinaire in 1937 and the poem Threne pour Guillaume Apollinaire in 1927. The watchable behaviour is three things that do not belong together, a woman, a cart, a street, arriving in the same picture at the same time, and the picture does not sort them into foreground and background, because the sorting would break the simultaneity, and the simultaneity is the subject.

**Takeaway:** put the woman, the cart and the street in the picture at the same time, and the piece should hold them together without sorting them.

## M7. The review as weapon: a hostile article, a slap, a reconciliation

A hostile article provokes a physical brawl, then a reconciliation, then a magazine. In 1911 Soffici visited the Futurist show in Milan at the Padiglione Ricordi and criticised it harshly, and the punitive expedition followed: Boccioni, Carra, Russolo and Marinetti travelled to Florence and the confrontation at the Caffe Giubbe Rosse ended, per Treccani, in an understanding, "un'intesa", and a common resolve to modernise provincial academic Italy. it.wikipedia gives the rougher version: Boccioni slapped Soffici, a furious brawl followed, settled by a police commissioner, and renewed the next night at Santa Maria Novella station, see C4, and en.wikipedia says the three, Marinetti, Boccioni, Carra, "assaulted Soffici and his La Voce colleagues". Who was with Soffici is itself disputed: Prezzolini and Rosso per it.wikipedia, unnamed La Voce colleagues per en.wikipedia. The slap and the brawl are the fuller account, and Treccani's "intesa" is the diplomatic aftermath, not a denial. The mechanism is that the review is a weapon before it is a platform: Soffici's La Voce articles carried a ferocious satirical charge against the Futurists, and the Futurists answered in person, and the answer produced the alliance, because the fight proved both sides wanted the same thing, the modernisation of provincial academic Italy, and the magazine Lacerba, founded January 1913, was the fight turned into a platform. His August 1911 La Voce article on Picasso and Braque probably influenced the Futurists towards Cubism, per en.wikipedia citing Marianne Martin, so the critic who was slapped also taught the slappers their next move. The watchable behaviour is a printed word that lands like a blow, and a blow that lands like an invitation, and the piece should show the argument becoming the alliance, because the argument was the alliance all along.

**Takeaway:** let the printed word land like a blow and the blow land like an invitation, and the piece should turn the fight into a magazine.

## M8. The magazine as movement: masthead, title, whole issue

Soffici builds the platform, designs the masthead, writes the whole issue himself when needed. La Voce, 1908, with Papini and Prezzolini, Soffici designed its masthead. Lacerba, 1 January 1913, a Wednesday, with Papini, Soffici suggested the title and drew the masthead, and it became the gathering point of the best young Italian cultural and creative energies, and Soffici published articles on Cubism there, later collected in Cubismo e oltre, Edizioni della Libreria della Voce, and invited his Paris friends to collaborate, and began publishing his Giornale di bordo, collected in volume 1915, a model of his style, art criticism, literature and philosophy braided with daily life. Lacerba was founded by Soffici and Papini only, see C3: Palazzeschi and Tavolato withdrew with them from La Voce but did not found it, and Palazzeschi was a collaborator, not a founder. By its fifth issue Lacerba wholly supported the Futurists, and the Futurists, Marinetti, Boccioni, Palazzeschi, began collaborating with it, and the Lacerba Futurist painting exhibition opened in Florence in November 1913 with Balla, Boccioni, Carra, Russolo, Severini and Soffici. Rete mediterranea, 1920, Soffici founded and directed it and wrote all four numbers himself. La Vraie Italie, 1919, with Papini, 12 numbers, aiming at an intellectual understanding between Italy and other European countries. Italia e Civilta, 1944, with Barna Occhini, 23 numbers, preaching patriotism, the social character of Fascism and loyalty to the Germans. La Fiamma, May 1899, three numbers, founded with friends, was the first, at twenty. The mechanism is that the magazine is the work: Soffici does not only write for a review, he makes the review, and the masthead is a drawing, and the title is a choice, and the whole issue written by one man is a solo show in print. The watchable behaviour is a page that builds itself, title, masthead, articles, all by one hand, and the hand is the movement, because the movement is the man who made the paper it is printed on.

**Takeaway:** let one man make the whole magazine, masthead and all, and the piece should build its own page before your eyes.

## M9. Collage as simultaneity: real paper in the picture

Real paper and print pasted into the picture, first tried January 1914. Simultaneita di donna carretto strada, see M6, is the first collage, and the BIFZF+18 cover, 1915, is a hand-coloured collage of posters, see M5, and Treccani's Rosai entry says Soffici was pointing out to Rosai "le possibilita della scomposizione cubo-futurista e del collage", the possibilities of Cubo-Futurist decomposition and of collage, which means the collage lesson was part of what Rosai took from him, and Rosai's Scomposizione di una strada, 1914, oil on canvas with collage, and Natura morta, il banco del falegname, 1914, built with fragments and scraps of printed paper, per #433, are the pupil's answer to the teacher's lesson. ## M10. The exhibition as import: the 1910 Florence Impressionist show

## M14. Mentorship by recognition: spotting the genuine trait

## M15. The lost manuscript: losing Campana, finding him again

**Takeaway:** lose the manuscript and let the memory rewrite it, and the piece should be the remembering, not the original.

## Aesthetic logic

The Soffici logic is that the bridge is a person and the magazine is a movement and the lamp is the world. He went to Paris and lived there seven years, and he came home with the avant-garde in his hands, not in his luggage, and the hands are the bridge, because the ideas arrived as friendships, Apollinaire, Picasso, Braque, Gris, Rousseau, Cezanne, and as rooms full of pictures, the 1910 Impressionist show, and as articles, the first Italian article on Cezanne, and the bridge is why his Futurism is a Futurism of still things, a lamp, a bridge, a woman combing her hair, because the man who carried Cubism home carried it as a way of seeing, not as a way of shouting, and the seeing works on a lamp as well as on a car, and better, because the lamp stays still and the facets multiply. The second logic is that the form is always neutral and the theory is always radical: the subject is a hill, a bottle, an apple, a peasant digging, and the method is decomposition, simultaneity, collage, typography as image, and the method changes the seeing, not the subject, and the subject stays ordinary so the method can be seen. The third logic is that the return to order is not a retreat but a synthesis: the "realismo sintetico" keeps the Impressionist root that de Chirico cut, and keeps the Cezanne volumes and the Cubist facets inside the naturalistic landscape, and the hill is painted as if it were a lamp, and the lamp was painted as if it were a hill, because the hand is the same hand. The fourth logic is that the critic and the painter are one man: the catalogue introduction that makes Rosai, the monograph that makes Rosso, the masthead that makes the magazine, the review that starts a brawl and ends in an alliance, the library opened to Martini, the lost manuscript that forces Campana to rewrite, and the writing is the painting's other hand, and the painting is the writing's other hand. The last logic is the house: one house at Poggio a Caiano for 57 years, one hill painted for 45 years, and the house becomes the museum, and the museum is the proof that the bridge, the magazine, the lamp and the hill were all one life, lived in one place, and the life ends with the signatures, the manifesto, the prize, the race manifesto, the prison camp, the acquittal, and the signatures are part of the logic too, because the man who signed everything signed the Fascism as well, and the study does not separate them.
