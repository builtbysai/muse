## M1 -- The web as a computable graph: nodes, edges, and nothing else

The founding move of the Buehler-Saraceno collaboration is a change of representation: a spider web stops being a picture and becomes a graph. Once Saraceno's laser scans were turned into data, Buehler's lab could treat every junction as a node and every thread as an edge, with a length, a thickness and a tension attached to each edge -- and then the web could be loaded, stressed, broken and played in simulation, the way an engineer loads a bridge. The 2014 model built from the "14 Billions" scans gave, for the first time, precise thickness, tension and length for every silk thread and how the threads interact; the 2018 Journal of the Royal Society Interface paper made the capture automatic and quantitative, reconstructing the digital 3D fibrous network -- every fibre and node, topology and porous regions preserved at high fidelity -- so that, combined with a mechanical model of silk, the digital network could be loaded in simulation to predict the real web's mechanical response. The same graph then feeds everything downstream: the VR simulation, the sonification, the 3D-printed silk, the instrument. The mechanism is representation as power: the web is not described by the graph, it is replaced by the graph, and every later operation -- breaking it, hearing it, rebuilding it -- is an operation on the graph. The watchable behaviour is the web as a dataset that can be loaded: pull one edge and watch the tension redistribute along the graph, threads lighting up in the order the load reaches them. Seen in W09, W13, W14 and W17. [C29182]

**Takeaway:** turn the web into a graph, and every question about it becomes a calculation. [C29182]

## M2 -- Scan to model: a laser sheet, slice by slice, until the invisible is a dataset

Before this collaboration, nobody had a precise three-dimensional model of a complex spider web, because the fibres are too thin for CT, ultrasound or confocal microscopy -- Saraceno's study records the same wall, and Buehler's lab is where the wall was climbed from the other side. The pipeline is mechanical and repeatable: a web is built by spiders in a carbon frame; a sliding sheet laser illuminates vertical slices of the web, 0.5 mm thick in the newer setup; one or two high-resolution cameras capture stereoscopic images of each illuminated slice; image-processing algorithms segment the silk in each slice and reconstruct the digital 3D network. The first successful deployment was in 2009, on the black widow web behind 14 Billions; the 2018 paper automated the platform so the same web could be imaged at successive construction stages without destroying it. The scale of the reconstruction is part of the point and part of the wobble: the hand-knotted rebuild of the scanned black widow web is given as sixteen, seventeen and even thirty times life size in different MIT sources, and this study quotes no single figure -- the disagreement is carried, not resolved. The mechanism is measurement as making: the scan does not describe the web and then stop; the scan becomes the web, rebuilt at human scale, played as an instrument, modelled as a structure, sonified as music. The watchable behaviour is the slice: a sheet of light moving through darkness, each pass revealing one cross-section of something too fine to see whole, the slices stacking in memory until the tangle stands up. Seen in W09, W13 and W17. [C29183]

**Takeaway:** slice the invisible with light, and the slices become the web. [C29183]

## M3 -- Protein sonification: the molecule has a voice, and it is in tune with itself

Buehler's most startling claim is also his most literal: a protein can be heard, because it already vibrates. In the materiomusic work reported in 2019, each of the 20 amino acids has a natural vibrational spectrum computed from quantum chemistry -- its normal-mode vibrations -- and those frequencies are transposed into the audible range preserving their relative relationships, a property the sources call transpositional equivalence, so each amino acid becomes a chord-like cluster of frequencies: a 20-tone amino-acid scale. A protein sequence becomes a sequence of notes on a piano roll; secondary structure sets each note's volume and duration, alpha helix sounding different from beta sheet, giving the music rhythm; and a recurrent neural network trained on the resulting scores generates new melodies that are translated back into protein sequences -- de novo proteins never seen in nature, including variations on spider silk proteins. Buehler reports he can hear a beta sheet or an alpha helix as it plays. The sources state the limit plainly: the AI cannot direct properties -- any change in strength or elasticity is essentially random, and new designs must be tested experimentally. Viral Counterpoint (2020) is the method at pandemic scale: the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, three chains of 1,288 residues each, folded into an intricate pattern, sonified in two versions -- amino-acid scale and equal temperament -- the amino-acid sequence giving the melodic line, secondary structure setting rhythm and volume, and the three entangled chains becoming three interwoven melodies, counterpoint in the strict sense, notes played against notes, on bells, strings, flutes and Japanese koto, about 1 hour 50 minutes long. The stated scientific use is comparative: matching vibrational patterns across coronaviruses, and searching for an antibody whose melody and rhythm match the spike's -- a compositional route to drug design. The music is described as pleasant, even relaxing -- "an invader disguised as a friendly visitor". The mechanism is transduction without translation loss: the frequencies are the molecule's own, only shifted into hearing range, so the ear becomes an instrument for reading structure. The watchable behaviour is a sequence scrolling as notes, a beta sheet sounding different from an alpha helix, a whole protein heard in seconds. Seen in W15, W16 and W07. [C29184]

**Takeaway:** shift the molecule's own vibrations into hearing range, and the ear can read the protein. [C29184]

## M4 -- Hierarchy: a few universal blocks, endless diversity

The frame that holds the whole programme together is materiomics, named in a 2010 review and a 2012 book: the systematic study of biological materials across scales, from molecule to tissue, linking structure to function. Its organising idea is the universality-diversity paradigm -- a small set of universal building blocks (amino acids, hydrogen bonds, beta sheets) arranged in hierarchical structures produces a huge diversity of functions -- and its three themes, stated in the 2010 review, are weakness turned to strength via hierarchy, breakdown in extreme and disease conditions, and applying the hierarchical paradigm to design new materials. The hierarchy is literal and countable: amino-acid sequence, triple helix, fibril, tissue in collagen (W02); amino acid, beta strand, filament, fibre, web in silk (W05, W08). The same hierarchy is what makes the olog work possible: category theory gives the levels a formal grammar, boxes for types of things and arrows for functional relationships, so that a protein filament and a social network of people texting nearest neighbours can be shown to have identical category-theoretic representations -- a precise isomorphism, not a metaphor (W07). That isomorphism is the engine behind the later claim that silk, music and language share a hierarchical grammar, and behind the knowledge-graph AI of MechGPT and SciAgents. The mechanism is scale as the design variable: the same chemistry at a different level of organisation is a different material, and the same branching logic at a different scale is a different system. The watchable behaviour is the zoom: amino acid, beta sheet, fibril, fibre, web, each level drawn with the same branching tree, silk and a musical phrase sharing one shape. Seen in W05, W06, W07 and W15. [C29185]

**Takeaway:** build from a few blocks at many levels, and the same grammar gives you silk, music and a network. [C29185]

## M5 -- Weak bonds, working together: three or four hydrogen bonds beat a hundred

Silk's strength is a paradox -- it is held together by hydrogen bonds, each 100 to 1,000 times weaker than a metallic or covalent bond -- and Buehler's early work resolves it twice over, with the same answer: arrangement beats strength. First, the 2008 cluster result: in beta-sheet protein stacks, short beta strands are held by clusters of three or four hydrogen bonds, and under mechanical stress the bonds in a cluster break together, not one by one, so the cluster resists more force than a longer strand with many bonds, where most bonds sit idle -- "lazy bonds". Strength peaks at three or four bonds; more bonds reduce resistance, because the entropic energy of the chain under force caps the useful cluster size. Second, the 2010 nanoconfinement result: beta-sheet nanocrystals of poly-(Gly-Ala) and poly-Ala, confined to about 3 nm, are stiffer, stronger and tougher than larger crystals of the same chemistry, because at that size deformation is uniform shear that uses the hydrogen bonds efficiently, broken bonds re-form quickly, and failure is gradual -- "graceful" -- rather than sudden, while larger crystals fail by crack-like, non-uniform deformation, and dissipative stick-slip sliding between sheets absorbs energy. The size itself is the design variable: same chemistry, different crystal size, very different toughness. The mechanism is cooperation at the smallest scale: weak things, correctly grouped and correctly sized, outperform strong things badly arranged. The watchable behaviour is the cluster lighting up as a unit and failing all at once, and the crystal growing past 3 nm and getting weaker as it grows. Seen in W03 and W05. [C29186]

**Takeaway:** group the weak bonds in threes and fours, keep the crystal small, and weakness becomes strength. [C29186]

## M6 -- Soften, then stiffen: the web sacrifices a few threads so the rest survive

The 2012 Nature paper is the programme's most elegant result, because it turns a material property into a survival strategy. A silk thread under tension first softens -- entropic unfolding of semi-amorphous protein domains -- spreading load across the whole web; as strain grows the molecules align, load transfers to the beta-sheet crystals, and the thread stiffens sharply until failure at about 1400 MPa and 67 percent strain (per the PhysicsWorld summary). Because only the threads near the applied load enter the stiff regime, damage stays local: a struggling fly breaks a few threads and the rest of the web keeps working "virtually the same way". Control simulations with linear-elastic or elastic-plastic silk in the same web geometry performed far worse -- the damage zone grew about sixfold. The team verified it by poking and pulling real webs in the field; they failed only where disturbed. The same grammar recurs elsewhere in the oeuvre: the 2006 crack-tip work, where local stiffening near the tip stabilises a moving crack and local softening destabilises it (W01), and the mussel byssus, where stiff and compliant regions distributed along each thread let the network survive wave impacts by redistributing force (W10, resting on the Wikipedia paragraph alone). The mechanism is localised failure as design: the structure does not resist damage everywhere, it confines damage somewhere, and the somewhere is cheap. The watchable behaviour is the fly struggling in one patch while the rest of the web holds, and a force curve that dips, then rises steeply, then snaps -- one thread at a time, never the whole web at once. Seen in W08, W01 and W10. [C29187]

**Takeaway:** let the thread soften first, stiffen second, and sacrifice a few threads to save the web. [C29187]

## M7 -- Granular synthesis: sound assembled the way silk is assembled

The sonification method has a second, quieter half that the MIT News 2021 account names explicitly: granular synthesis that mimics silk production. Sound grains -- tiny fragments of sound -- are assembled like molecular components into a new form, paralleling how silk is assembled from amino acids into beta sheets into fibres into a web. It is the materiomics idea turned into an audio technique: the grain is the amino acid, the texture is the fibre, the piece is the web, and the same hierarchical assembly that makes silk strong makes the sound cohere. The mechanism is process as composition: the sound is not written and then performed, it is grown grain by grain, the way the spider lays down silk thread by thread, and the listener hears the assembly happening. The watchable behaviour is grains accumulating into a texture -- first scattered points of sound, then a surface, then a structure with tension in it -- the way a web thickens as the spider works. Seen in W14 (via the MIT News 2021 account) and W15. [C29188]

**Takeaway:** assemble the sound grain by grain, the way the spider assembles the silk. [C29188]

## M8 -- The web as instrument: hearing distance, and the spider as first mover

Spider's Canvas / Arachnodrone (premiered 23 November 2018 at ON AIR, Palais de Tokyo; US premiere MIT, February 2019; installed at MIT.nano, spring 2019) is the collaboration made playable, and its rules are exact. The scanned web topology of a Cyrtophora citricola tent-web spider is turned into a mathematical model; each strand is assigned a frequency of sound based on its length and its tension and elasticity, like the strings of a harp. The ensemble navigates a virtual web in Unity and Max/MSP: Isabelle Su "drives" the audience through the web as the spider would see it; proximity to particular strings sends sonic information to Ian Hattwick, who sculpts it in real time; Evan Ziporyn (EWI) and Christine Southworth (electric guitar, eBow, projections layering web photographs over the scanned model) improvise on top. Only fibres within a "hearing distance" of the viewer sound, shown blue and white; fibres outside it stay silent, green and brown. Each performance is newly generated -- never the same twice. The piece is explicitly interspecies: "the real 'first mover' was the spider herself", in Ziporyn's words, carried from the #736 study. The 2021 PNAS work extends the instrument into a conversation: the team recorded web vibrations for different spider activities -- building, communicating, courtship -- and a machine-learning classifier distinguished the activities from the vibration patterns even though they sound similar to humans; the stated next step is generating synthetic vibration signals to "speak the language of the spider" and see whether spiders respond, alongside spider-mimicking 3D printers that build without support material, as a spider does. The mechanism is vibration as a common language with a gate: the web, the spider, the performer and the visitor all report in frequency, and proximity decides who gets heard. The watchable behaviour is a performer moving through a virtual web while nearby strings light and sound and far strings stay dark and silent -- and a played-back vibration, answered or ignored by the spider. Seen in W14, W17 and W18. [C29189]

**Takeaway:** tune every strand, let only the near ones sound, and let the spider move first. [C29189]

## M9 -- Behaviour as the design input: ask for a motion, get a protein

The recent turn inverts the whole programme. For twenty years Buehler analysed structures to find their behaviour; now he specifies the behaviour and lets the machine find the structure. ForceGen (2024) combines a protein language model with a diffusion model: give it a desired mechanical unfolding response -- unfolding energy, strength, a force-extension curve -- and it proposes amino-acid sequences, checked by molecular simulation. VibeGen (arXiv February 2025; Matter, 24 March 2026) inverts one step further: give it a desired pattern of motion -- a vibrational fingerprint, the lowest non-trivial normal mode -- and a dual-model architecture, a protein Designer that proposes sequences and a protein Predictor that evaluates their dynamic accuracy, refines random sequences by diffusion until the protein vibrates and flexes as specified. Dynamics becomes the blueprint and structure follows. The same inversion runs through the AI work around it: MechGPT (2024), a large language model fine-tuned on question-answer pairs distilled from the multiscale materials-failure literature, tested on knowledge retrieval and hypothesis generation; SciAgents (2024), a multi-agent system in which about 1,000 papers on biological materials are fed to a generative model to build an ontological knowledge graph -- the olog idea of 2011, now automated -- and specialised agents (Ontologist, Scientists, Critic) interact over subgraphs to generate evidence-driven hypotheses, the multi-agent structure deliberately mimicking biology's divide-and-conquer organisation. The reported VibeGen designs were evaluated computationally, with experimental lab validation described as future work. The mechanism is the question turned around: the behaviour is the specification, the molecule is the answer, and the machine does the searching. The watchable behaviour is a target curve or motion pattern typed in, candidate proteins appearing one by one, a predictor scoring each, and the winner flexing exactly as asked. Seen in W19, W20 and W15. [C29190]

**Takeaway:** specify the motion you want, and let the machine find the molecule that moves that way. [C29190]

## M10 -- The living, tunable web: it grows denser, stronger, and it repairs itself

The 2021 PNAS paper adds the one thing a static scan cannot show: time. The scanning platform was automated so the same web could be imaged at successive construction stages without destroying it, and the web under construction behaves like a work in progress with a direction: as it grows denser it becomes stronger and tougher; the spider keeps adding, reinforcing and repairing after the foundation is laid. Each construction stage is turned into different music, so the build sequence is audible as a changing harp-like piece -- the web's growth has a soundtrack that changes as the web changes. In VR, pulling the web apart raises string tension and pitch until strands snap with an audible snap, so the visitor can feel the construction run backwards into failure. The Arachnomancy Cards and App (2018-2019) push the same living web outward to citizens: a deck of 33 cards and an app that invites users to photograph and map spider webs around them -- "mapping against extinction" -- the pavilion in Venice a dark room with a large web lit by sun via mirrors and a floor shaking at very low frequencies, at the rhythm of vibrating spider-webs. Buehler's role in the App is indirect: MIT News lists it among the collaboration's undertakings broadly, and the cards and App are Saraceno-studio outputs built on the shared web archive. The mechanism is growth as tuning: the web is never finished, it is always being retuned by its builder, and the retuning is measurable, audible and repairable. The watchable behaviour is a web thickening over time with its music changing stage by stage, a broken patch being repaired, a pull that raises the pitch until the snap. Seen in W17, W13 and W18. **Takeaway:** let the web keep building, and hear it get stronger as it grows. [C29191]

## Aesthetic logic

Buehler's logic is the score with the instrument left on and the author stepping back into the data. Against Saraceno, whose sculpture knows it is a system and shows you the system -- the plastic bags, the fan, the carbon frame, the laser sheet -- Buehler's science knows it is a language and shows you the language: the graph, the slice, the frequency, the cluster of three or four bonds, the force curve that dips and then rises. The work is always the interval -- between the amino acid and the note, between the thread and the tension, between the scan and the simulation, between the vibration and the protein it designs -- and the interval is where a machine or a spider or a visitor has to stand, with their own body, their own web, their own calculation as the last component. What changes across twenty years is not the question but the direction of travel: first the molecule is analysed to find its music, then the music is played back into a new molecule, then the desired motion is specified and the molecule is generated to match it -- analysis, sonification, generation, each step handing more of the authorship to the system itself. He refuses the decorative fix, and the refusal is the point: the music is not laid over the science as an illustration, it is the science transposed into hearing range, the frequencies preserved in their relative relationships, so that a wrong note would be a wrong measurement. The meaning is the making, and the making is shared: the counted knot, the measured thread, the named spider, the specified vibration -- because the work is only honest about what it cost when the cost is on view, and only true when the ear can check the eye.
