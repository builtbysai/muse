## Core engine

**THE SITE IS THE INSTRUMENT: find the inaudible energy already moving through
a place (wind on glass, tide on a dock, light on a photocell, vibration in a
leaf stem), transduce it into sound, and let the place perform itself, treating
every input as a partner to respect rather than material to dominate.**

The named mechanisms:
1. **The transducer rig.** Home-built contact microphones and photocells (built
   with technician Bob Bielecki): WTC 1999, mics pressed to 91st-floor windows
   after Hurricane Floyd so the wind stressing steel and glass became the
   source; the photocell controller translated light vibration (color, speed)
   into tones. *Bright and Dusty Things* (New Albion, 2001, with Pauline
   Oliveros and David Tronzo) is the album form: "Light becomes sight becomes
   sound becomes music." Founding lesson, after Beverly Semmes told him at the
   residency: "don't add pictures and lights; the microphones, windows, sound
   and experience are enough."
2. **Sound path drawings.** The spatialization plan of a multi-channel
   installation IS a drawing. Early: *Wire Sound Drawings* (2006, f05: blue
   rubbed wire arcs and speckle spray). 2017: hand and robot plotting dense
   orthogonal networks for the ICA VCU show (f01: a plotter redrawing the plan
   on video; f03: circuit-map-like line tangle). 2018: *Diverging, converging...
   (sound path drawings #1-20)*, robot-drawn with Zach Duer, visually mapping
   how a chance algorithm re-moves voices through 18 speakers in *whether there
   was a bell or whether I knocked* (Borges, ICA VCU 2018). He calls the
   Speaker Drawings "my attempt at a form of automatic drawing," the visual as
   artifact of the sound process.
3. **Environment-performed public sculpture.** *Land Buoy Bells* (Seattle Pier 62,
   commissioned 2013, installed 2021): five stainless-steel tank ends struck by
   stored tidal and wave energy; random rhythms from weather, tides, and dock
   movement. A *Bell For Every Minute* (High Line 2010, 59 bells recorded around
   NYC, all chiming at the top of each hour). *All Those Vanished Engines* (MASS
   MoCA 2011, 19 channels) starts from the Boiler House's own pipes and metal
   drums, layered with a Paul Park text; still running through winters a decade
   later (f13).
4. **Feedback as instrument.** *Frogs in Feedback* (2001, MoMA collection): a
   microphone hung from a disco-ball motor, a self-playing pendulum whose
   rotating distance to the speaker generates the feedback (visual homage to
   Reich's *Pendulum Music*, but closed and singular). SculptureCenter 2004:
   speakers hung on long wires moving inaudibly, "looking at sound rather than
   listening to it."
5. **Wind-played strings.** The aeolian guitar (2010, f10): each string tuned to
   a resonant frequency of the alcove entrance at a Kentucky home, then left to
   the wind. *Flutter* (2007, with Molly Berg, World Financial Center): 100
   resonant sound-objects on parachutes dropped 150 feet, "a free-falling
   Aeolian harp."
6. **Insect-scale listening.** First field recordings in the Brazilian Amazon,
   2003 (Cartier Foundation, with anthropologist Bruce Albert, f09). Recent
   phase with biologist Kasey Fowler-Finn at Mountain Lake Biological Station
   (2014-2020): accelerometers on milkweed stems (f00), record needles reading
   insect vibration through leaves (f06), *Singing amongst the weeds* (2019)
   and *Too Hot To Sing* (2020) organized in temperature-keyed "chapters."
7. **Reversible siting in historic fabric.** Poitiers St-Jean Baptistery (2019,
   f08): working out speaker placement "without touching anything" in a space
   with a 4th-century pit and 7th-century walls; show titled *Waves, A Witching
   Stick, A Space for Melting Bells*.
8. **Collaboration as composition.** The duo-driven record practice (Taylor
   Deupree, Machinefabriek, Steve Roden, Robin Rimbaud, Brendan Canty + Hahn
   Rowe's *Second*, Balmat 2025) and the method-ethic from the Tonearm
   interview: "the sound itself was giving me more than anything else... I have
   to question what the content is and how best to respect it." Not session
   musicians: "musicians because of their sound and their ability to listen."
   Paik's license: "It'll make you a better musician."

## Evolution

- **1989-1998: soundtrack collaborator.** Soundtracks for Oursler, Birnbaum and
  others; EAI/Kitchen archival work; Paik apprenticeship from 1991. Never
  presenting his own work.
- **1999-2004: becoming a sound artist.** The WTC residency is the rupture
  (1999): transducers, field recording, *Bright and Dusty Things*. First
  installations and albums; Amazon fieldwork 2003; wire/speaker works at
  SculptureCenter 2004; *Frogs in Feedback* enters MoMA.
- **2005-2012: the public and architectural turn.** Bells and buildings: Julie
  Mehretu wall-drawing collaboration at the 2006 Sydney Biennale (10 days, the
  drawing as graphic score), *Flutter* 2007, *A Bell For Every Minute* 2010,
  *All Those Vanished Engines* 2011 (19 channels, his largest). Guggenheim 2011.
- **2013-present: permanent commissions, institutions, duo albums.**
  *Land Buoy Bells* develops over 2013-2021; MoMA's *Soundings* 2013; Poitiers
  baptistery 2019-20; the Fowler-Finn insect collaboration 2014-20; the
  18-channel chance-algorithm Borges installation 2018; and an increasingly
  duo-driven record practice (Deupree, English, Machinefabriek, Roden,
  Canty/Rowe). He describes "three co-existing overlapping careers:
  sound/music/art + academia."

His own verdict on what lasts: he is proudest of the works that are
site-specific, hardest to document, hardest to move. "The building is the
sound, the building holds the sound." He favors "unusual or problematic
spaces" over white cubes. "When you listen to really hear sound in space, it's
not just about hearing the thing, but about where it's coming from... I hear a
plane processed by the city."

## What to take for prompts

Vitiello's grammar is the transduction vocabulary of the whole 12k-adjacent
strand: transducers on glass and leaf stems, photocells reading light, bells
played by tides, strings tuned to a room's resonance, voices re-moved through
speakers by chance, and the plan of the system drawn as a score. For generative
pieces the translatable moves are: let the ENVIRONMENT play (weather, tides,
light), draw the spatialization as the score itself, tune to the resonant
frequencies of a place, listen at insect scale, and stage the mechanism (the
wire, the speaker, the pendulum) as the visual subject rather than decorating
over it. His punk logic applies: DIY intuition over trained skill, site
response over white cubes, respect the input.
