Penck is the painter who turned a border into a grammar. Born Ralf Winkler in Dresden in 1939, he began painting at ten, took lessons from Jurgen Bottcher (Strawalde) from 1953, co-founded the renegade Erste Phalanx Nedserd the same year, and then spent the next two decades being refused by every academy that mattered -- Dresden and East Berlin both turned him away because his work did not fit Socialist Realism. He worked as a stoker, a newspaper deliverer, a margarine packer and a night watchman, and taught himself from reproductions of Rembrandt and Picasso. The refusal is the work's first fact: a man denied the official language invented his own, and the invention was a system, not a style. [C26312]

The system arrived in the early 1960s and he called it Standart -- standard plus art, with an echo of Standarte, banner or flag. It was a vocabulary of simple archaic signs, stick figures and graphic icons recalling cave paintings, Asian calligraphy and graffiti, built from readings in language, mathematics, cybernetics and information theory. The pseudonym came with it: A. R. Penck, after the geologist Albrecht Penck, whose Ice Age scholarship gave him both a mask and a metaphor -- the Cold War as an ice age, anonymity as a strategy for exhibiting in the West. The year of the name is genuinely unsettled in the sources (1966, 1968 and 1969 are all printed by serious sources), and that instability is itself Penck-like: the name was a tool, not a birth certificate. [C26313]

From 1969 the Stasi made the cost of the system plain. Paintings were confiscated, membership in the artists' association was refused, the pseudonym was discovered in 1973, more paintings were confiscated in 1977, and in May 1979 his studio was broken into and works and records destroyed. He was meant to exhibit at Documenta 6 in 1977 and was prevented by Stasi influence, though several sources still list him as a participant -- the listing and the prevention sit side by side, unresolved. Michael Werner had been smuggling his work west since the 1960s, and the first solo shows came in Cologne in 1968, the first solo museum show at Haus Lange, Krefeld, in 1971, Documenta 5 in 1972, a Bern retrospective in 1975-76, the Will Grohmann Prize in 1975. Recognition abroad and pressure at home ran on parallel tracks for a decade. [C26314]

Then, in the fall of 1976, the West German painter Jorg Immendorff walked into his life in East Berlin, and the system acquired a second author. They campaigned in their work for the abolition of the inner German border and for Bahro and Havemann, met again on May 1, 1977, and showed together in Cologne that year under the teasing title "Penck mal Immendorff, Immendorff mal Penck" -- Penck paints Immendorff, Immendorff paints Penck. Immendorff's Cafe Deutschland series, begun in 1977, carries the friendship's emblem: in Cafe Deutschland I (1978) Immendorff reaches through the Wall to shake Penck's hand. On August 3, 1980, Penck moved west to Kerpen near Cologne. The border he had painted against for twenty years was now behind him, and the painting did not relax -- it got bigger, louder, and more colourful. [C26315]

The eighties were the breakthrough and the professorship: the Rembrandt Prize in 1981, the first New York show at Ileana Sonnabend the same year, Documenta 7 in 1982, a move to London in 1983, the Venice Biennale in 1984, the Aachen Art Prize for 1985, professor of painting at the Dusseldorf Academy from 1988 (Britannica says 1989; the end year is given as 2003 or 2005), Documenta IX in 1992, a Schirn retrospective in 2007. He died in Zurich on May 2, 2017, aged 77, after a prolonged illness, survived by his wife and children. A 2015 stroke is reported by a single gallery source and is not verified elsewhere. [C26316]

## M1 -- The stick-figure Standart sign system

The mechanism is a reduced lexicon: outlined stick figures, crosses, X's, triangles, circles, squares, eyes and dashes, set down like traffic signs or trademarks. Sotheby's description of Welt des Adlers I (1981) is the cleanest single statement of it in use: a monumental central figure, part-eagle, part-man, "surrounded by rudimentary shapes and symbols including a smaller stick figure, X's, triangles, circles and squares. Both figures' eyes are aglow in a pool of electric blue." The signs are not decoration on a figure; the figure is one sign among signs, and the whole picture reads like a sentence written in pictographs. The sources tie the idiom to hieroglyphs and cave paintings, and Christie's ties it the same way for 2 Bestimmte Formen (1994): a painterly idiom "defined by the rudimentary stickmen that Penck first developed in the 1960s." [C26317]

**Takeaway:** a picture that can be read at a glance and re-read slowly is a picture with two speeds, and Penck gets both from the same marks: the sign is instant, the arrangement is the argument.

## M2 -- World and System paintings

Penck's big pictures are not scenes but worlds: large "worlds" and "experience spaces" filled with symbolic abbreviations, motivated by the division of Germany. Grosses Weltbild (Large World Picture, 1965, oil on hardboard, 180 x 260 cm) fixes the idea before the Standart system fully hardens. Eau de Cologne (1975, synthetic polymer paint on canvas, 285 x 285 cm) is a near-square canvas almost three metres on a side, made while he was still in the East -- monumental scale as a claim that a private sign system could carry a public world. TRR (1982, acrylic on canvas, 290 x 501 cm, Kunsthaus Zurich) restates the same World-picture topic after emigration, over five metres wide, bought at Documenta 7. The Albertinum frames the Dresden period as running from early Rembrandt and Picasso interest through the development of the "World" and "System" paintings, motivated by the division of Germany. [C26318]

**Takeaway:** scale is not swagger here; it is the size a world-picture needs before the signs inside it can breathe.

## M3 -- The East/West pictograph contrast

In 1981 the Tate acquired a pair of 1980 paintings, Osten (East) and Westen (West), seen at Galerie Michael Werner in Cologne. The Tate curator's account is the mechanism stated plainly: in them Penck "deploys his inimitable language of graphic signs and symbols to contrast his first impressions of life in the capitalist West with the less open, more rigid structures he had endured in the socialist East." The same sign language does both jobs -- the contrast lives in arrangement, density and rigidity, not in a change of alphabet. The pair is the sequel to Ende im Osten (1979/80, oil on canvas, 130 x 175 cm), a farewell painting whose title translates as "End in the East". The duality returns inside a single body in Standart TTABTTT 5 (1984): a figure split by subtle colour variations, red against blue, read by Bonhams as "highly politically charged" and echoing strained relations between the Federal and Democratic Republics. [C26319]

**Takeaway:** one alphabet, two arrangements -- the political content is in the syntax, and a pair of pictures can argue with each other in the same tongue.

## M4 -- Colour-field grounds

The signs sit on flat grounds: grey in Welt des Adlers I ("dispersed black pigment on a grey ground"), stark white in Entscheidung (1983), bright yellow in 2 Bestimmte Formen (1994), where Christie's describes "white oblong geometries swell against a bright yellow ground as two figures sway and swoon" with thick brushwork and simplified geometries. The ground is never a background; it is the paper the signs are written on, and its colour sets the picture's temperature. Bonhams notes that Penck began to use more vibrant colours after emigrating to the West, and the 1984 TTABTTT canvas is the evidence: "the vibrant colours Penck began to use more after emigrating to the West" around a monumental, almost life-sized central stick figure, "part man, part abstract primitive deity". [C26320]

**Takeaway:** the ground is the first decision and the last thing you notice -- pick it like a key signature, then let the signs play in it.

## M5 -- Totemic stacking and found-material sculpture

The sign system was never only paint. Penck's sculptures use common materials -- wood, bottles, cardboard boxes, cans, packing tape, tin and aluminium foil, wire and paste -- "done with simplicity and spontaneity". Standart-Modell (1968) is a figure made from aluminium foil, 15 x 35 x 10 cm: the sign as a portable object. Memorial to an Unknown East German Soldier (1984), made for his first museum show in Britain at the Tate, is the mechanism at monumental scale: two identical bronze figures, one standing, the other lying across an altar or table, a sacrificial tableau against a large wooden figure and carved head -- his first work in bronze, which he called a "positive" version of the 5,000-year-old burial tomb at Newgrange. Small Idol (1985) is the same totem shrunk to pocket scale: bronze with brown patina, about 17.5 x 3.4 x 3.1 cm, monogrammed "RW", edition of 9. Marble enters from 1986. [C26321]

**Takeaway:** a sign that works in foil, wood and bronze is a sign that works at any scale -- test a motif by changing its material, not by redrawing it.

## M6 -- Jazz improvisation as composition

Penck was not a painter who liked jazz; he was a drummer who painted. He co-founded the free jazz group Triple Trip Touch (T.T.T.) with Frank Wollny, played with Butch Morris, Frank Wright, Billy Bang, Louis Moholo and Frank Lowe, and released his first album, "Gostritzer 92", in 1979. MoMA holds the "Piano Solo" LP jacket (1985). The mechanism shows up on canvas in Entscheidung (1983): "a large, red 'Standart' figure ... stands before us with spread arms and hands forming three Ts, which could be read as TTT (Triple Trip Touch), the free jazz band Penck joined in the early 1980s as drummer. In Entscheidung, the artist's characteristic figures and forms appear in red, blue, green, and black ... The shapes include floating crosses, dashes, eyes, faces, more secretive forms, and two clefs. Moreover, on the left side, with a little imagination, we can perceive a black grand piano and musical notes." Bonhams reads the TTABTTT canvas the same way: "the fluid, vibrant brush strokes and rhythmic composition pay homage to the artist's love of Jazz music." [C26322]

**Takeaway:** improvisation is a placement rule, not a mood -- put the signs down the way a drummer places hits, in time, and let the picture swing or stumble on its own rhythm.

## M7 -- Print and book as democratic reproduction

The Standart was designed to travel. Standarts (1970) is an artist's book (Galerie Michael Werner, Cologne; edition 1,000), and Ur End Standart (1972) is a screenprint portfolio (edition 75). Penck's own 1970 claim, quoted by Artsy, is the mechanism in one sentence: "Every Standart can be imitated and reproduced and can thus become the property of any individual." MoMA notes about 400 prints in lithography, etching, screenprint and woodcut. Three of Many (1990, acrylic and screen print on canvas, 160 x 130 cm) borrows the title of Jurgen Bottcher's film "Three of Many", banned in 1961, for a series of screen prints -- teacher, banned film and print medium joined in one work. [C26323]

**Takeaway:** a sign that anyone can copy is a sign that belongs to everyone -- build the motif so it survives being remade by other hands.

## M8 -- The soldier and memorial motif

Penck was drafted for half a year in 1974, into a nuclear-weapons unit of the Volksarmee, and the soldier never left his pictures. The Memorial (1984) reflects "sardonically on his national service in a nuclear-weapons unit of the Volksarmee", in the curator's words. The Future of the Soldier (1985, oil on canvas, 80 x 140 cm) is a "militant soldier figure", first shown in his Cologne gallery. A naming clash is carried unresolved: Wikipedia captions a Bonn image "Future of Soldiers (1995)", while the Van Ham lot is the distinct 1985 oil -- whether two versions, a retitling, or one work with conflicting dates was not established. A further soldier work, Soldato Conventionale (1990), shows a Standart soldier holding a rifle, with a crowd-control barrier and a snake, but its medium and dimensions were not stated in the source read and it is not counted among the twenty. [C26324]

**Takeaway:** the soldier is Penck's self-portrait in uniform -- a stick figure with a rifle and a barrier, standing where the artist once stood, and the memorial turns the joke into a tomb.

## M9 -- The animal and mythic hybrid

Welt des Adlers I (1981, emulsion paint on canvas, 280 x 250 cm) is the signature post-emigration painting and the hybrid stated whole: a monumental central figure, part-eagle, part-man, eyes aglow in electric blue, surrounded by the full sign vocabulary. Sotheby's reads the sources as cave art, ancient hieroglyphics and African tribal art, "at once mythic and contemporary, otherworldly and naive". The eagle is also the German imperial emblem reworked, per the auction record's framing. The painting was exhibited in its year of creation at Kunsthalle Bern, illustrated on the front cover of that exhibition catalogue, and set Penck's auction record at Sotheby's London on February 11, 2020, selling for 531,000 GBP (Wikipedia gives $687,201 for the same sale). [C26325]

**Takeaway:** a hybrid is an argument in one body -- half beast, half man, the eagle of empire and the stick man of the street sharing one pair of glowing eyes.

## M10 -- Late reduction to few forms

The vocabulary never changes; it thins out. 2 Bestimmte Formen in Beziehung (2 specific forms interrelated, 1994, acrylic on canvas, 71.4 x 71.4 cm) reduces a painting to two forms on a flat yellow ground: "white oblong geometries swell against a bright yellow ground as two figures sway and swoon." Beyond the twenty, the same reduction continues: Dritte Kraft (2005) is a monochrome black-and-white Standart painting with thick brushwork and reduced forms, System-Vermischung (2005, acrylic on canvas, 120 by 100.2 cm) and Stoerung (2005, oil on canvas, 80 x 60 cm) a densely structured sign-like composition on a light background. Spielen und Bauen (Playing and Building, 2002, acrylic on canvas, 140 x 179 cm) is the latest dated work in the set, and its title restates the whole practice as two verbs: play plus construction. [C26326]

**Takeaway:** when a system is true, you need fewer and fewer signs to say it -- the late test of any vocabulary is how little of it still carries the picture.

## Aesthetic logic

Penck's logic is a code before it is a picture. The Standart signs are deliberately primitive -- cave, hieroglyph, traffic sign, trademark -- because a primitive sign cannot be owned by the state that rejected him, and because a sign that simple can be read by anyone, copied by anyone, and smuggled anywhere, including out of Dresden in a dealer's car. The large grounds give the signs a world to live in; the colour, flat and declarative, gives each world a temperature; the jazz gives the placement its rhythm; the soldier and the eagle give the code its two poles, the man in uniform and the beast of empire. The early work codifies the system under pressure, the middle work (1979-1985) lets it explode into monumental colour at the moment of emigration, and the late work proves the system by subtraction, until two forms on yellow are enough. The friendship with Immendorff is the human hinge of the whole story: two painters on opposite sides of a wall, writing each other into pictures, shaking hands through a painted wall before the real one fell. [C26327]
