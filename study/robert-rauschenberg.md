## M1. The receptor surface (the flatbed picture plane)

The picture plane is treated as a horizontal surface on which things are laid, not a vertical window into which one looks, and the critic Leo Steinberg's sentence is the mechanism stated once and for all: each Combine is a receptor surface on which objects are scattered, on which data is entered, and the evidence is everywhere in the work, Bed hung vertically but made horizontally, a bed stood up, Monogram solved only when the canvas is laid on the floor, the goat centred on it as if in a pasture, Rebus assembled from the week's street material, and the watchable behaviour is that the eye scans across, not into, and objects sit side by side without hierarchy, and the picture is read the way a desk is read, left to right, thing to thing, with no vanishing point and no centre to arrive at. **Takeaway:** lay the picture flat and scatter things on it, so the eye reads across the surface instead of into a window, and let the neighbours do the composing. Against the chain: Twombly (#394) also built a surface, but his is a field for a single looping hand, and Rauschenberg's is a desk for everything the day brought in, and the difference is that Twombly's surface receives one mark and Rauschenberg's receives the world, and Mitchell (#393) also built up a surface, but hers is layered paint and his is laid objects, and the difference is between a surface that holds paint and a surface that holds things.

## M2. Inclusion: the found object as equal to the brushstroke

Rauschenberg's 1956 statement is the mechanism in his own words: I consider the text of a newspaper, the detail of a photograph, the stitch in a baseball, and the filament in a light bulb as fundamental to the painting as brush stroke or enamel drip of paint, and the evidence is the Combines of 1954 to 1964, the goat, the eagle, the quilt, the tire, and the object is not illustrated and not symbolised, it is present, and its context changes it, the object itself was changed by its context and therefore it became a new thing, and a tire on a wall is not a tire, it is a ring around a goat, and a bed on a wall is not a bed, it is a painting that remembers sleep. **Takeaway:** put a real thing in the picture at the same rank as a brushstroke, so the picture stops depicting the world and starts containing it, and let the new context change what the thing is. Against the chain: de Kooning (#387) also scraped and collaged, but his collage stays inside the painting, and Guston (#392) also painted objects, a shoe, a hood, a light bulb, but his are painted, and Rauschenberg's are present, and the difference is between painting a thing and admitting a thing.

## M3. Erasure as making

Remove marks instead of adding them, and treat the removal as the work, and the evidence is Erased de Kooning Drawing, 1953, and the preconditions matter as much as the rubbing: after the White Paintings, Rauschenberg asked whether an artwork could be produced entirely through erasure, and he erased his own drawings first and found the result lacking, so he asked de Kooning for a drawing to erase, and de Kooning, somewhat reluctantly, agreed and selected a densely worked drawing that would be difficult to remove, and Rauschenberg worked over a month with a variety of erasers, the about 40 erasers figure is his own claim, see C11, and he kept it in his studio for two years, and Jasper Johns persuaded him to exhibit it, placed it in a gilded frame, and inscribed the label with a metal template in blue ink, ERASED de KOONING DRAWING, ROBERT RAUSCHENBERG, 1953, and the frame and label are integral, not presentation, because without them the piece is indecipherable, and in 2010 SFMOMA digitally enhanced the remaining traces, several figures facing different directions, at least one female, probably pencil and charcoal, likely related to the Woman series of 1950 to 1955, and some traces may have been erased by de Kooning himself before the drawing changed hands, and no photograph of the original exists. **Takeaway:** erase something that is undeniably art, slowly, in public, with the label as part of the work, so the removal is the making and the absence is the picture. Against the chain: Twombly (#394) also erased, his inscriptions are written and crossed out on the same surface, but his erasure is a gesture inside his own painting, and Rauschenberg's erasure is the whole painting, made of another man's drawing, and the difference is between erasing your own word and erasing someone else's picture, and no one else in the chain asks permission to destroy.

## M4. The machine or the friend as drawing instrument

Delegate the mark to a device or a person following instructions, and the artist sets the conditions and the instrument makes the line, and the evidence is Automobile Tire Print, 1953, twenty sheets of drawing paper glued together on the pavement, John Cage driving his Model A Ford slowly over them while Rauschenberg re-inks one tire with black paint, two tire tracks resulting, one in ink and one the impression of the tread, the work 22 feet long, unrolled like a Japanese scroll, and the drawing instrument is a car, the hand is replaced by a driver following instructions, and the line is 22 feet of continuous tread, and the same displacement runs through the cyanotypes, sunlight and a body making the print, and the transfer drawings, solvent and rubbing making the image, and chance enters through the instrument's behaviour, not through the artist's wrist. **Takeaway:** hand the pen to a machine or a friend with instructions, so the line is made by something that is not your hand, and let the instrument's behaviour be the drawing. Against the chain: Twombly (#394) also displaced the hand, drawing blind in the dark in the army in 1953 to 1954, but his displacement is the eye switched off and the hand still drawing, and Rauschenberg's is the hand switched off and the car driving, and the difference is between a hand that cannot see and a hand that is not there at all, and Cage is the instrument in both, the driver here, the silence there.

## M5. Solvent transfer: the ghost image

Apply solvent, turpentine, later lighter fluid, to a magazine reproduction, rub with a pen tip, and the image transfers reversed onto paper, with hatching-like pen marks, and the image arrives faded, reversed and streaked, and it looks like a rubbing of a flat image, coalescing into ghostly echoes in the streaks and smears, and the evidence is the Dante's Inferno transfer drawings, 1958 to 1960, 34 drawings, one per canto, begun in late spring 1958, Rauschenberg having never previously read Dante, two and a half years to finish, late 1960, using John Ciardi's translation, one canto at a time, with Dante scholar Michael Sonnabend, contemporary figures inserted, John F. Kennedy, Adlai Stevenson, each canto on a separate book-size sheet, preserving Dante's structure, and the mechanism links erasure, M3, and silkscreen, M6: all three move an existing image by rubbing. **Takeaway:** rub a printed image through solvent onto paper, so it arrives reversed, faded and streaked, and let the ghost be the illustration, because a ghost remembers the picture without copying it. Against the chain: Twombly (#394) also transferred, his inscriptions are rubbings of writing, but his transfer is a word written and unwritten, and Rauschenberg's is a photograph rubbed into a ghost, and the difference is between a mark that was never a picture and a picture that becomes a mark.

## M6. Silkscreen with deliberate misregistration

Use a commercial reproduction process, then refuse its precision, and the evidence is the silkscreen paintings of 1962 to 1964, about 80 works, begun after a visit to Warhol's studio in 1962, and colour entered the silkscreens in June 1963, and Rauschenberg quickly abandoned attempts to replicate realistic colour and to maintain exact colour registration, choosing instead to highlight a handmade quality, and slippage and irregularities were allowed to remain uncorrected, and the result is gestural and handmade where Pop is coolly executed, and the screen is a Combine that has gone flat, and Barge, 1962 to 1963, the largest of the series, 32 feet long, was made predominantly over a 24-hour period, and Retroactive I, 1963, takes a news photograph of Kennedy, the screens made before the assassination on 22 November 1963, and Rauschenberg considered not using the Kennedy images, then realised not using them would be as self-conscious a decision as using them, and selected an active, authoritative image and integrated it into a variety of contexts, and Retroactive I is the colour silkscreen as a public monument made while the event is still raw, see C12 on Retroactive II. **Takeaway:** print a photograph by silkscreen and let the colours slip out of register, so the machine image keeps a handmade wobble, and the misregistration is the signature, not the mistake. Against the chain: no one else in the chain uses a commercial process at all, Mitchell (#393) and Twombly (#394) both trust the hand, and Guston (#392) trusts the drawn line, and Rauschenberg is the one who lets the machine in and then disobeys it, and the difference is between a hand that makes and a hand that corrects a machine.

## M7. Duplication that fails

Make the same painting twice, at the same time, and let the copy betray itself, and the evidence is Factum I and Factum II, 1957, a pair made simultaneously, the artist moving back and forth between them, attempting to replicate his own gestural brushstrokes down to the drips, and each contains the same collaged elements in corresponding locations, two printed photographs of a burning building, a snapshot of two trees, two faded images of Eisenhower, a print calendar for every month, and the duplication fails in small ways, one brushstroke more curved than its twin, one drip placed slightly off, and the pair asks whether a spontaneous gesture can be faked with enough care, and answers that the fake leaves traces, and Factum II is a calculated and painstaking recreation of Factum I, read as Rauschenberg questioning Abstract Expressionist authenticity, and the title Factum treats paintings as facts or deeds, not expressions of an interior world. **Takeaway:** paint the same picture twice at once, copying your own drips, so the small failures are the content, and the copy proves the original was never unrepeatable, only unrepeated. Against the chain: de Kooning (#387) also reworked and scraped, but his reworking is one painting changing, and Rauschenberg's is two paintings failing to be one, and the difference is between a painting that changes its mind and a painting that tries to be its own twin and cannot.

## M8. Orientation flip: wall to floor to casters

When an object will not sit in a painting, change the painting's orientation instead of the object, and the evidence is Monogram, 1955 to 1959, in three states: first state 1955 to 1956, the goat on a shelf attached to a Combine painting, viewable from one side only, that painting later reworked as Rhyme, 1956; second state 1956 to 1958, the goat encircled by a tire with tread painted white, in front of a vertical panel made from a dismantled five-panel White Painting, and Rauschenberg felt the goat looked as if it were pulling the painting, and that panel was reworked into Summerstorm, 1959; final state, winter 1959, following Johns's suggestion, the square canvas laid horizontally on the floor, the goat centred on it as if in a pasture, the platform raised on casters, and the title comes from the union of goat and tire, reminding Rauschenberg of interweaving letters in a monogram, and the platform carries footprints, a street barricade, a rubber shoe heel, and a paint-smeared tennis ball behind the goat's hind end, and the same flip runs the other way in Bed, a horizontal object hung vertically, and the mechanism is editorial: re-hang, re-lay, re-stage until the thing belongs. **Takeaway:** if the object will not sit in the picture, turn the picture over, lay it on the floor, put it on wheels, and let the orientation do the work the object cannot. Against the chain: Twombly (#394) also reoriented, his blackboard loops are writing turned sideways, but his flip is in the mark, and Rauschenberg's is in the furniture, and the difference is between turning a line and turning a floor, and no one else in the chain puts a painting on casters.

## M9. The working object inside the work

Put a functioning device in the Combine and let it run, and the evidence is everywhere in the early Combines, working radios, clocks, electric fans, and Broadcast, 1959, three radios concealed behind the canvas, viewers manipulating dials that adjust volume and change stations, and Third Time Painting, 1961, includes a working clock, and Music Box, 1953, a box that makes a hidden sound when handled, and sound, motion, light and the passage of time are incorporated quite literally, and the work is not a picture of a radio, it is a radio that is also a picture, and the sound in the room is whatever the local stations are broadcasting at that moment, so the Combine stops being a fixed image and becomes a receiver. **Takeaway:** hide a working radio or clock inside the picture, so the work tells the time and plays the room, and the picture is never the same twice because the broadcast is never the same twice. Against the chain: no one else in the chain lets a machine run inside the painting, Mitchell (#393) and Twombly (#394) both make pictures that sit still, and Guston (#392) paints a light bulb but does not switch it on, and Rauschenberg is the one who wires the picture to the wall socket.

## M10. Sound and light response

Build an environment that reacts to the audience, and the evidence is Oracle, 1962 to 1965, a sound-producing sculptural environment made with Bell Laboratories engineer Billy Kluver, five parts, each with its own sound behaviour, including radios and recorded sound, triggered and changed by the visitor's presence and movement, and Soundings, 1968, a monumental light installation responsive to ambient sound, and Dry Cell, 1963, voice-activated, speak into a microphone and a motor spins a piece of metal, and Mud Muse, 1968 to 1971, a tank of bentonite mud churned by hidden machinery, and the audience is a switch, and the work has no fixed state, it has a behaviour. **Takeaway:** make a room that listens, so the visitor's movement and voice change the sound and the light, and the work is a behaviour, not an object. Against the chain: Twombly (#394) also made a work that needs a body, his pole-brush loops need a body swinging, but his body is the painter's, and Rauschenberg's is the visitor's, and the difference is between a picture made by a body and a picture finished by a body that walks in.

## M11. Live decor: the set as a Combine

Make stage scenery from found objects and sounds, assembled in the moment, and the evidence is Minutiae, 1954, a Combine made as a set for Cunningham, a self-contained set through which dancers can move, and Story, on the 1963 to 1964 tour, each performance a new set from local objects, and neither dancers nor choreographers ever knew what to expect until curtain, and First Time Painting, 1961, made on stage in Paris during Homage to David Tudor, and the theatre is another receptor surface, M1, and the set is a Combine that dancers walk through, and the Combine learns to move because the dancers move through it. **Takeaway:** build the set from whatever the town offers on the day, so the dancers never know the stage until the curtain, and the scenery is a Combine that has learned to be walked through. Against the chain: no one else in the chain works in theatre at all, Mitchell (#393) and Twombly (#394) and Guston (#392) all stay on the wall, and Rauschenberg is the one who lets the picture leave the wall and become a room people dance in.

## M12. The grid as a filing system

Organise mixed material by putting one rectangle next to another, and the evidence is Bed's quilt grid, the way he organised his diverse images and materials, he puts one rectangle, or thing, next to another, and Rebus as a week's concentration, the White Paintings as multi-panel modules, and Hiccups, 1978, 97 transfer drawings joined by zippers into a 63-foot chain that can be recombined in an infinite number of combinations, and the grid does not unify the material, it files it, and the viewer reads across, comparing neighbours, and the grid is the only order the Combine admits, a filing cabinet with the drawers open. **Takeaway:** file the material in a grid of rectangles, so the viewer reads across and compares neighbours, and the grid is the filing, not the picture. Against the chain: Kline (#388) also used a grid, but his is a shaken grid of black beams, and Rauschenberg's is a filing grid of found things, and the difference is between a grid that holds a gesture and a grid that holds a week, and Twombly (#394) also used an armature under chaos, but his is hidden, and Rauschenberg's is the whole picture.

## M13. Recycling his own work

Dismantle a finished work and reuse its parts in the next one, and the evidence is the studio as a parts bin: Monogram's first-state panel becomes Rhyme, 1956, its second-state panel becomes Summerstorm, 1959, a five-panel White Painting is dismantled for Monogram's second state, Untitled, 1954, is cut into sections to make smaller red Combines, the Booster X-ray returns in Mirthday Man, 1997, made for his 72nd birthday, and the studio is a parts bin and old work is raw material, and nothing is final while it can be cut up. **Takeaway:** cut up a finished painting and build the next one from its pieces, so the career is one long recycling, and the old work is the material for the new work. Against the chain: Guston (#392) also reworked, his break was a return to his pre-1948 figuration, but his reworking is a change of mind, and Rauschenberg's is a change of address, the same panel in a new picture, and the difference is between repainting a picture and reusing it.

## M14. Material as place

Let the location choose the material, and the evidence is the whole career as an itinerary: New York street, scrap metal, wood, twine; Black Mountain garbage detail, classmates' trash; Morocco and Italy, fetish boxes from found materials; Captiva, cardboard, sand, fabric; Ahmedabad, colourful textiles for Jammers, a city famous for textiles; Chile, copper for ROCI; Xuan paper mill, China, paper for 7 Characters, and the mechanism is M2 with a travel itinerary, the found object is always local, and after the move to Captiva in the fall of 1970 he cleared his palette, retreating from urban imagery toward natural fibres, textiles and paper, and the image load drops away and the material itself carries the work. **Takeaway:** take only what the place gives you, so the material is the address, and a change of place is a change of palette. Against the chain: Twombly (#394) also let place choose, Rome gave him pale grounds and myth names, but his place is one city kept for fifty years, and Rauschenberg's is ten countries in seven years, and the difference is between a painter who settles in a place and a painter who tours the world to be given materials.

## M15. The travelling exhibition as the artwork

Make the tour itself the work, accumulating pieces as it goes, and the evidence is ROCI, announced 1984 at the United Nations, a seven-year tour to ten countries, Mexico, Chile, Venezuela, China, Tibet, Japan, Cuba, the Soviet Union, East Germany, Malaysia, 1984 to 1990, concluding at the National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C., in 1991, see C14, and in each country Rauschenberg photographed, gathered local materials, worked with local artists and artisans, made new work, exhibited it, and donated a work to a local institution, and more than 125 ROCI artworks, over 2 million visitors, a budget of 11 million dollars, mostly self-funded, the cost personal, a mortgaged house, Johns and Warhol works sold from his own collection, and each stop adds locally made work, so later audiences see earlier countries' work, and audiences are put in indirect communication, and the exhibition is an ever evolving enterprise, and the 1964 Cunningham world tour was a rehearsal, Rauschenberg called it a good out-of-town rehearsal for ROCI, and the mechanism scales M14 to the size of a foreign policy, the world as one large community, funded mostly by the artist himself. **Takeaway:** tour the exhibition so it grows a new room in every country, and let each audience see the last country's work, so the show is a conversation between places, not a delivery. Against the chain: no one else in the chain tours at all, Twombly (#394) left New York for Rome and stayed, Mitchell (#393) left for France and stayed, and Rauschenberg is the one who keeps moving, and the difference is between a painter who finds a place and a painter who makes the moving itself the work.

## Evolution

Seven phases. 1) Black Mountain and New York, 1948 to 1951: the school years, Albers discipline reacted against, Cage and Cunningham adopted instead, media tried in combination from the start, printmaking, painting, photography, drawing, sculpture, theatre, recurring themes already fixed, sequences and progressions through time, grid formats, doubling and mirroring, human scale, and the cyanotypes and the White and Black Paintings are the two poles, the body-print and the empty screen, and nothing is yet bought or scavenged at scale, the materials are paper, paint, gravel, light. 2) Travel and erasure, 1952 to 1953: the trip with Twombly, the fetish boxes, the return to New York and the Elemental Sculptures, and the work gets smaller, boxes, and then more conceptual, erasure, tire print, and the found object is now the subject, not the ground, and the Arno anecdote and the de Kooning erasure both date to this phase, and both are about destroying or discarding work as a way of making it, and the hand is deliberately displaced, by the car, by the eraser, by the found box. 3) Combines and silkscreens, 1954 to 1964: the long central phase, Red Paintings into Combines, first fully realised Combine summer 1954, Bed and Monogram as the famous peaks, Factum as the self-critique, the Dante drawings as the transfer laboratory, Canyon as the late Combine, then the silkscreen paintings 1962 to 1964, about 80 works, ending with Barge and the Retroactive pair, and the image load rises steadily, from objects, to photographs, to mass-media photographs at mural scale, and the work gets bigger, faster, more public, and the Venice Grand Prize, June 1964, is the institutional confirmation, and Rauschenberg abandons silkscreen entirely by 1964, never one to repeat himself. 4) Technology and performance, 1960 to 1969, a parallel track, not a successor: Broadcast, 1959, and Oracle, 1962 to 1965, E.A.T. founded 1966, 9 Evenings, 1966, Open Score, 1966, Soundings, 1968, Mud Muse, 1968 to 1971, Stoned Moon, 1969 to 1970, after the Apollo 11 launch, and the Combine leaves the wall and becomes a room, a performance, a system that responds to sound, and the collaborator changes from a fellow painter to an engineer, Billy Kluver, Bell Laboratories. 5) Captiva clearing, 1970 to 1983: the move to Captiva, fall 1970, urban imagery retreats, natural fibres advance, Cardboards, Venetians, Hoarfrost, Jammers, Spreads, Scales, paper pulp elevated to an art form through collaborations with paper mills in France and India, and the work gets quieter, more material, less image-crowded, and the 1976 mid-career retrospective prompts a return to earlier concerns in the Spreads and Scales, and the 1/4 Mile begins 1981. 6) ROCI and metal, 1984 to 1991: the work becomes a diplomatic mission, ten countries, local materials in each, exhibitions that accumulate work as they travel, copper in Chile leads to metal paintings, scrap metal leads to Gluts, and the scale is institutional and global, but the mechanism is unchanged, take what the place gives you, and the cost is personal, a mortgaged house, Johns and Warhol works sold from his own collection. 7) Late work, 1992 to 2008: new image technologies in sequence, Iris printer, 1992, fresco transfer, 1996, polylaminate and vegetable dye transfer, 1998 to 2008, and the imagery turns autobiographical and retrospective, the Booster X-ray returns in Mirthday Man, 1997, the Ruminations edition, 2000, refers to figures and moments from his life, and the stroke in 2002, right hand partly paralysed, work continues with the left hand, and the late work looks back without repeating, same inclusion logic, new support, new transfer chemistry. Early against recent, in one line: early Rauschenberg removes the image to see what is left, White Paintings, erasure, and late Rauschenberg multiplies the image across new supports to see what survives the transfer, silkscreen, then metal, then Iris print, then inkjet, and the constant is the transfer itself, something from the world is moved onto the work, and the move leaves a trace.

## Aesthetic logic

The logic is the gap between art and life, and the painting is not a picture OF the world but a piece OF the world laid on a surface, and everything is relational, the object against the brushstroke, the photograph against the paint, the radio against the canvas, the goat against the tire, the copper against the silkscreen, the country against the country, and the relations are what carry the meaning, and he is the Abstract Expressionist who walked out of Abstract Expressionism by the side door, who took the gesture and taught it to admit a goat, and the gesture is the point, because without the object the gesture would be decoration and without the gesture the object would be junk, and with both the two are a single argument, and the argument is that a painting can be a desk, and the desk can be a room, and the room can be a stage, and the stage can be a country, and the country can be a tour, and the tour can be the work, and the work is the world, laid out flat, for fifty-four years.
