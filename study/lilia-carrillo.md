# Lilia Carrillo: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #327.

**Site:** Lilia Carrillo Garcia (Mexico City, 2 November 1930 - Mexico City, 6 June 1974, age 43), Mexican painter of the Generacion de la Ruptura, consistently described as its only woman and most prominent female contributor. Multidisciplinary: oil and acrylic painting, drawing, collage, lithography, illustration, textile design, and costume and set design for the vanguard theater of Alejandro Jodorowsky. Daughter of Socorro Garcia and the aviator general Francisco Carrillo; only child, raised by her mother. Husbands: the philosopher Ricardo Guerra Tejada (married 1952, two sons Ricardo and Juan Pablo) and the artist Manuel Felguerez (married 1960 in Washington). A school self-portrait at eighteen with brush in hand; an aneurysm in 1970 that partially paralyzed her; nine paintings made in rehabilitation 1972-74; a last canvas titled by others "Sin titulo (inconcluso)". Identity anchors: the human kept alive inside the field; the fragment disintegrated until it is only a detail; the swift stroke after long meditation; the oscillation of white and black with a red caught inside; the late dialectic of vibration and white dissolution.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Vicente Rojo study: from the Ruptura's designer to its only woman painter. The correction travels with the hop: her husband was NOT Fernando Garcia Ponce. Fernando was a Ruptura peer and the younger brother of the critic Juan Garcia Ponce; Carrillo's husbands were Ricardo Guerra Tejada and Manuel Felguerez. She was 43 when she died, not 44; the 1933 birth year that persists on some sites is wrong; the claim of a 1974 divorce from Felguerez is single-source and contradicted by the inheritance record; the Osaka mural measures 5 by 6.40 meters. #326 ended with the alphabet getting shorter and the paintings deeper; #327 is the painter who kept a body inside the field, and whose forgetting was posthumous, not contemporary. The study lands mid-comeback: kurimanzutto 2024, the MPBA retrospective 2025-26, Americas Society 2026, kurimanzutto New York 2026-27, the monograph.

**Depth:** 15 sources read end to end (kurimanzutto press releases for the 2024 and 2026-27 shows, the full MPBA curatorial text for "Todo es sugerente", the INBA press release, Ochoa Sandy's Letras Libres essay fifty years after her death, the Cultura Colectiva profile, Arte al Dia on the Americas Society show, the AS/COA exhibition page, two artdaily pieces, es.wikipedia, the Sotheby's lot essay for Unidad que se dispersa, the Coppel Collection page, MAM's featured-work page for Introspeccion, El Universal on the 2024 personal-collection show); 36 titles cataloged; 8 load-bearing corrections (itemized below). VIEWED FIRST-HAND as images in the live browser: "Autorretrato escolar" (1948, via the kurimanzutto 2024 show page), "Tormenta de fuego" (1958, BBVA viewer, thick crusty impasto), "Esencia" (1961, AS/COA carousel, the small square oil consistent with the 1961 Esencia canvases), "Seradis" (1963, BBVA modal, wall label reads "Seradis", Lowe Art Museum collection), "Manana de octubre" (1964, kurimanzutto New York lead image, paint and fabric with the weave reading as a faint grid), "Unidad que se dispersa" (1968, Sotheby's Lot 327, 65 by 50 cm), the 1969 CENCROPAM painting "La ciudad desbordada, contaminacion del aire" (BBVA, sharing title and concept with the Osaka mural), "Premonicion" (1970, AS/COA carousel), "Otra dimension" (1970, BBVA, acrylic and sand), "Sin titulo [inconcluso]" (1974, BBVA, nearly bare white ground), "Sin nubes y sin estrellas" (1974, MPBA retrospective wall-label photo), and one early spectral portrait from the Felisa Gross period (signature not legible at available resolution; carried as a documented period work, not a confirmed signed one). FAILED VIEWS: "Mudo espia" (1956), "Jubilo neutro" (1959), and "Esencia 2" (1961) returned no labeled reproduction online (Esencia 2 exists as a photographed wall label only); the monumental 5 by 6.40 m Osaka 70 mural has no separate viewable image, only the related 1969 painting. NOT accessed: either monograph end to end (Moreno Villarreal 1993, the 2026 MPBA one); the Roberto Paramo 1969 interview; the kurimanzutto 2024 illustrated timeline (attachment 404); any Felisa Gross inventory; about twenty works known by title and date only. No mechanism invented; every engine below is footed to a viewed work or an end-to-end read.

## M1. Build-up-then-carve

She lays down dense, thick paint surfaces and then incises into them: "building dense surfaces that she would then carve or scrape" (AS/COA). The viewed "Tormenta de fuego" (1958) proves the mechanism at the start of her abstraction: a glowing yellow-orange nucleus at center-left, agitated strokes of burnt orange, ochre, and umber radiating outward like a firestorm, the paint thick and crusty, forcefully worked, deep brown-black passages anchoring the lower right. The gesture's path reads as a cut, not a stroke; the surface is a furrowed, engraved topography. Against the chain: Rojo (#326 M4) tore and folded the canvas as the violence; Carrillo carves the paint, not the support, the wound stays inside the color. **Takeaway:** Build the surface first, then wound it; the cut is the composition. Watch the surface: when the stroke reads as incision, the mechanism is on.

## M2. The disintegration doctrine

Collage as a constant practice from the late 1950s: fragments of prints, publications, and textiles embedded in the canvas (MPBA: "practica constante a lo largo de su carrera"). Her own rule, on comic cutouts: "Yo los desintegro, hasta que se funden, esfuman, desaparecen, existen sutilmente como pequenos detalles." Superhero comic clippings built into "una especie de vineta difusa y ambigua"; the San Antonio postcard collage with the conquistador Juan de Garay stamp "evocando historias, tiempos y memorias". The exact opposite of US pop's fixing of referents: disintegration, not fixation. **Takeaway:** The fragment must nearly disappear; survival as a detail is the point. Against the chain: Soriano (#324) extruded a line into bronze to make it last; Carrillo smudges the referent to make it breathe. Watch the fragment: when you almost cannot find the paper, the mechanism is on.

## M3. Dripping as plastic automatism

The Rahon/Paalen line: "varias tecnicas de automatismo plastico (como el chorreado o dripping)" plus "una reconfiguracion del espacio pictorico alejada de la perspectiva tradicional" (MPBA). Gravity-drawn rivulets, no vanishing point. The viewed "Unidad que se dispersa" (1968) carries it a decade later: dark umber and black gestural loops and scribbles pulling outward around a pale core, the drips and loops describing force, never depth. **Takeaway:** Let gravity be a brush and kill the vanishing point. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M7) put position to work as meaning; Carrillo's drips work as pure direction, meaningless and moving. Watch the fall: when the paint drips and nothing recedes, the mechanism is on.

## M4. Blurring, the breathable field

"Blurring her compositions" (AS/COA); "las veladuras y zonas de color que se funden... respiracion visual entre luz y sombra... se pierde en reproduccion digital" (Cultura Colectiva). The viewed "Seradis" (1963) is the mechanism made luminous: a large oil in warm ochres, golden browns, burnt sienna, and umber, with smoky sfumato passages and pale cream highlights, a tall central form suggestive of a standing figure emerging from turbulence via sweeping gestural marks and blended glazes. The surface glows with layered, fluid brushwork. The warning travels with it: this mechanism only pays off in front of the original; the camera cannot have it. **Takeaway:** Soften the edge until the field breathes; accept the loss on the screen. Against the chain: the chain's engines survive reproduction (spite's shaders, Rojo's Ts); this one punishes it, and punishing the reproduction is itself a discipline. Watch the edge: when the zone has no boundary, the mechanism is on.

## M5. Tool diversity

"Using brushes of varying sizes alongside other tools to apply and disperse pigment" (AS/COA). Fine lines and broad washes coexisting in one field, marks of visibly different widths and characters arguing with each other. The viewed "Otra dimension" (1970) shows the full kit at once: acrylic and sand on canvas, the sand giving a gritty matte grain, scratched sgraffito lines cutting through layered washes above deep blues and purples. **Takeaway:** Change the hand mid-painting; let the widths argue. Against the chain: Rojo (#326 M5) used the geometry set as his one language; Carrillo runs five tools in one canvas and lets the dialects clash. Watch the mark: when three different hands seem to work one canvas, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The infinite abstract

Raquel Tibol's term for the early-1960s solution: "combinacion enganosamente ambigua de dimensiones profundas y superficiales... enigmatica sugerencia de horizontes infinitos"; "texturas conseguidas por la misma aplicacion de pintura o por la tela cruda"; elements that "flotar y gravitar en el espacio; articulando una constelacion de elementos" (MPBA on Encuentro/Esencia). The palette signature: "oscilaciones entre el blanco y el negro, el siena con tonos pendulantes entre si que envuelven algun rojo, azul o amarillo" (Driben). The viewed "Esencia" (1961) is the mechanism small: a small square work in a plain wood frame, bold calligraphic black brushmarks and smudged grays over a beige ground, touches of rust-orange and ochre wash, dry sketchy brushwork. Small shapes floating in a deep-but-flat field; a sienna oscillating against white-black; a red caught inside. **Takeaway:** Float small things in a deep-but-flat field; let sienna oscillate and catch a color. Watch the horizon: when it never arrives, the mechanism is on.

## M7. Humanizing the abstraction

Her credo: "es a traves de las sugerencias de una linea o de otro elemento de origen que voy trabajando el cuadro... En mis cuadros siempre hay algo que recuerda a algo, puede ser una letra, puede ser una manita, como que necesito humanizar la abstraccion" (MPBA). From around 1964 the suggestions become legible female figures with ancient-culture symbolism (Mujer en blanco 1964, Emblema cabalistico 1965). The viewings confirm it across decades: "Manana de octubre" (1964) carries delicate graphite sketch-lines, figure fragments, script-like marks, the numeral 13, drifting across a nearly bare field; "Otra dimension" holds a white bird- or wing-like shape and tiny marks like distant figures; "Seradis" builds a tall central form suggestive of a standing figure or totem; "Sin nubes y sin estrellas" (1974) plants a tiny red cruciform figure with outstretched arms at center. The figure is a constant, reformulated: never figure, never pure form. **Takeaway:** Keep one small human thing alive inside every field. Against the chain: Rojo's secret alphabet refuses to resolve into letters (#326 M6); Carrillo's stains keep resolving into bodies. Watch the stain: when a hand or a letter surfaces, the mechanism is on.

## M8. Meditation-then-swiftness

"Pieces like Abstracto were the product of long processes of meditation: before executing any kind of brushstroke, she would spend lengthy periods of time thinking about what to do... the result of a prolonged period of reflection, which contrasts with the swiftness of their execution" (Coppel). The opposite of action painting's myth of pure spontaneity: think long, paint fast. The mechanism is in the process, invisible on the surface; the painting looks fast but sits in resolved balance. **Takeaway:** Earn the speed with stillness; the gesture is a discharge, not an improvisation. Against the chain: spite's loops are computed live under a four-second contract; Carrillo's loop is rehearsed in the head and fired once. Watch the tempo: when the painting looks fast but nothing is out of place, the mechanism is on.

## M9. Titles as reading instructions

Titles from Pellicer/Arreola poetry (Mudo espia: "Mudo espia mientras alguien a mi voraz observa"); elemental and cosmic titles (Tormenta de fuego, La voz del sueno, Premonicion); ecological titles (La ciudad desbordada, contaminacion del aire). "No son decorativos. Senalan una intencion politica y existencial" (Cultura Colectiva). The viewed "Premonicion" (1970) proves the aiming: the upper register pale off-white with a dark blue-black band at the top, the lower half sinking into deep umbers pierced by small golden glows; the title hangs over it like weather before the event. **Takeaway:** Title the mechanism, not the picture; let the words aim the eye. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M11) made naming a speech-act that canonized; Carrillo's titles are stage directions, quieter and aimed inward. Watch the label: when the title reads as an instruction for where to look, the mechanism is on.

## M10. Scale as material contrast

Intimate: "Esencia" (1961), oil on canvas on wood, 18 by 14.5 cm, "un trazo mas nervioso y directo" (Cultura Colectiva). Monumental: "La ciudad desbordada" for Osaka 70, 5 by 6.40 m acrylic, "el caracter atmosferico... adquiere una condicion ambiental... debido a su escala"; "Carrillo siempre celebro la libertad que le concedio este formato monumental" (MPBA). The same atmospheric language stretched across two orders of magnitude; the viewed small "Esencia" and the 1969 "La ciudad desbordada" painting (pale, hazy whites and grays, a smog-choked atmosphere in thin acrylic washes) are the same breath at different lung sizes. **Takeaway:** Run the system at both extremes; scale is a material, not a setting. Against the chain: Rojo scaled his vocabulary to the city but kept it the same drawing (#326 M11); Carrillo lets the same gesture change its body with the room. Watch the room: when the same gesture needs both a hand and a wall, the mechanism is on.

## M11. Material promiscuity

Supports and matter as structural decisions: oil on canvas on wood (Esencia); acrylic AND SAND on canvas (Otra dimension, viewed: the sand a gritty matte grain under the washes); the oil-to-acrylic shift by 1970; lithography; textile-pattern design; pre-Inca textile curation; fabric fragments literally in the painting (Manana de octubre, viewed: the weave reading as a faint grid under the paint, a tactile ground). Grain that is literally sand, fabric that is literally fabric. **Takeaway:** Let foreign matter do structural work; the grain is the composition. Against the chain: Rojo kept the scissors-and-glue syntax as pictorial motif (#326 M10); Carrillo buries the actual fabric and lets the weave hold the painting. Watch the ground: when the weave holds the painting up, the mechanism is on.

## M12. The white-dissolution dialectic

1972-74: "prominentes areas de color blanco donde la presencia de las formas y de los colores se disuelve, difumina, inclusive desaparece; una dialectica entre un dominio de representacion dinamico y energetico y otro que parece aspirar a un grado cero de representacion" (MPBA). The viewed "Sin titulo [inconcluso]" (1974) is the destination: a vertical canvas mostly bare white and off-white ground with only faint incidents, soft gray washes, a breath of golden-yellow at the left edge, faint mauve patches, thin brown scratches; the forms barely cohere; the palette "atenuandose, cediendo ante la disolucion del blanco". The viewed "Premonicion" (1970) anticipates it: broad fields of white over dark browns, the erasure already louder than the incident. **Takeaway:** Let erasure be a form equal to vibration; end by not finishing. Against the chain: the whole chain builds to completion; Carrillo's last mechanism is the unfinished as destination. Watch the white: when the absence is louder than the incident, the mechanism is on.

## M13. Rehabilitation vigor

"Se podria decir que la artista desplego su actividad y fuerza sobre las superficies pictoricas de una manera que ya no le era posible fisicamente" (MPBA, on the 1972-74 works). Nine paintings including "La gata" (1973): collage, layered paint, drawing, "rasgos y gestos que invitan a imaginar a un felino". Maximum gestural energy painted from a chair with an adapted easel: force delegated to the surface. The viewed late works carry the double register: "Sin nubes y sin estrellas" (1974) holds golden-yellow washes, pale window-like rectangles, and that tiny red figure at once simplified and urgent; "Sin titulo [inconcluso]" barely coheres at all, and the incoherence is the vigor. **Takeaway:** When the body cannot go, send the surface; energy is not strength, it is placement. Against the chain: the chain's engines are machines the artist operates (#325 M3, spite's 4s contract); this one is the machine operating on the artist's behalf. Watch the gesture: when the weakest hand makes the loudest mark, the mechanism is on.

## M14. The critical turn, 1968-70

Against the "lirica" reading: Contaminacion primaveral, Sueno alucinante, Detras del muro (division and segregation, Cold War resonances); the Osaka mural with air pollution as its theme; the improvised collective mural on the sheets covering the ruined Monumento a Miguel Aleman in Ciudad Universitaria; founding the Salon Independiente; the illustrations of Elena Garro's "El dia que fuimos perros" (1962) and Rita Murua's "El ultimo rey" (1969, on the October 2, 1968 massacre). The viewed 1969 "La ciudad desbordada" painting is the mechanism's document: pale, hazy whites and grays and faint ochre, soft dissolved edges, loose gray sweeps, small dense clusters of dark marks, touches of rust-orange suggesting a city dissolving into pollution, "expansive like an aerial view of contamination". **Takeaway:** Put the smog in the lyric field; the personal stain can carry the public air. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M12) resigned from the foreign service over Tlatelolco; Carrillo stayed and painted the air of the city that produced it. Watch the palette: when beige reads as contamination, the mechanism is on.

## M15. The Felisa Gross alter ego

A fake Czechoslovak artist identity used to sell commercial work for economic survival while raising two children: "no fue un juego conceptual: fue una estrategia de supervivencia" (INBA 2025). One early spectral portrait was viewed in the Felisa Gross period: a pale woman with very long flowing white hair billowing around her against a dark umber ground, in the loose expressive manner of the Carrington/Varo orbit (signature not legible at available resolution; carried as a documented period work, not a confirmed signed one). Two simultaneous oeuvres under one hand: one signed, one masked. No inventory of the Gross works has been found; the alter ego is documented as fact and strategy only. **Takeaway:** The pseudonym is a tool, not a joke; survival formats are formats. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M11) made naming a speech-act that canonized; Carrillo's alter ego un-names so the hand can eat. Watch the signature: when the name lies so the hand survives, the mechanism is on.

## M16. Textile thinking across disciplines

Fabric fragments in painting from the early 1960s, giving "una cualidad tactil" (MPBA on Manana de octubre); costume and pattern design; scenography as collage at stage scale (La opera del orden, 1962, with Jodorowsky, Rojo, Felguerez, Gironella); and, after the 1961-62 Peru trips at Fernando de Szyszlo's invitation, the 1963 pre-Inca Peruvian textile exhibition at Galeria Juan Martin, with Juan Garcia Ponce's catalogue text on the "maravilloso sistema de signos", the sense of color, of rhythm, the wonderful system of signs. One system of signs moving loom to canvas to stage. **Takeaway:** Follow the woven rhythm wherever it goes; the sign system is the same, the scale is the variable. Against the chain: the chain has treated tools as subjects (spite's instrumentarium, Rojo's toolbox); Carrillo treats the loom as a syntax that survives the brush. Watch the weave: when the loom's rhythm survives the painting, the mechanism is on.

## Aesthetic logic

The human inside the field. Carrillo builds a world and then wounds it: she lays down a dense, breathed-on field of color and stains, incises it, buries foreign matter in it (paper, fabric, sand, comics), blurs it, and finally lets white dissolve it. At every stage she keeps one small human thing alive inside: a hand, a letter, a female torso, a horizon that reads as a body. Her abstraction is not the American kind (a field you enter) and not the European kind (a matter you touch); it is a Mexican informalism where the body refuses to disappear. The palette oscillates, white and black, sienna pendulating, a red or a blue or a yellow caught inside, and the late work turns the oscillation into a dialectic: maximum vibration against zero-degree whiteness. Everything is suggestive because nothing is permitted to finish: the last painting is titled by others Inconclusa, and the career reads as one long preparation for a completion that illness took. The prompts for fly must keep a body, or the ghost of one, inside every field.

## Evolution

1947-51: traditional formation. Private classes with Manuel Rodriguez Lozano at sixteen; La Esmeralda 1947-51 (teachers Rodriguez Lozano, Agustin Lazo, Pablo O'Higgins, later Antonio Ruiz "El Corcito", Carlos Orozco Romero); graduates 1951 "maestra en artes plasticas con mencion como alumna distinguida". Figurative portraits and self-portraits: "Autorretrato escolar" (1948, viewed). 1951: a scaffold fall damages her spine (repercussions twenty years later). 1952-56: Paris. Marries Ricardo Guerra Tejada 1952, both scholarship holders; Casa de Mexico residency; Academie de la Grande Chaumiere; first solo at Casa de Mexico; the collective "Artistas Extranjeros" at the Petit Palais 1955; meets Felguerez there; post-Cubism, surrealist automatism, informalism. 1956-58: the turn. Returns to Mexico pregnant and separated; teaches at INBA from 1956; joint show with Felguerez at Galeria Carmel-Art 1956; "Mudo espia" (1956), the break with figuration, title from Pellicer and Arreola; first solo of abstract works at Galeria Antonio Souza 1957; the Felisa Gross alter ego for commercial survival; dripping and collage begin (the Rahon/Paalen line); "Tormenta de fuego" 1958. 1959-60: the American encounter. US trip with Felguerez 1959, sees Frankenthaler and Gottlieb, closer contact with Abstract Expressionism and European Informalism; "Jubilo neutro" 1959; marries Felguerez 1960 in Washington; the collage phase 1960. 1961-65: consolidation, the "infinito abstracto". The Esencia series, Encuentro, Playa escondida (1961); the Galeria Juan Martin group show 1961 (with Leonora Carrington, Remedios Varo, Gunther Gerzso, Enrique Echeverria); the VI Bienal de Sao Paulo 1961 (with Felguerez, Rojo, Nishizawa, Gironella, Sjolander, Vlady, Echeverria); the Antonio Souza solo 1961 (Westheim review); the textile turn after the 1961-62 Peru trips at Szyszlo's invitation; the 1963 pre-Inca textile exhibition at Galeria Juan Martin; fabric in painting ("Manana de octubre" 1964); "Seradis" 1963, second prize at the Salon ESSO 1965 (first prize to Fernando Garcia Ponce; the scandal "termino en golpes"); the inaugural MAM collective 1964; the Casa del Lago retrospective 1964; from 1964 legible female figures with ancient-culture symbolism (Mujer en blanco). 1966-69: politics enters. Confrontacion 66 at the Palacio de Bellas Artes, the Ruptura's naming moment; the 1968 student movement, the improvised CU collective mural, founding the Salon Independiente; the Nueve pintores mexicanos group photo at Galeria Juan Martin, she the only woman; the dark works (Unidad que se dispersa, Contaminacion primaveral, Sueno alucinante, Detras del muro); 1969 "La ciudad desbordada" mural for Osaka 70 (Fernando Gamboa's abstract-muralism project, ten fellow muralists). 1970-74: illness and the white dissolution. The 1970 spinal aneurysm and partial paralysis; the last pre-diagnosis solo at Galeria Juan Martin; a few paintings for MAM, the Museo Tamayo, Galeria Juan Martin, Galeria Ponce in Madrid; 1972 rehabilitation and nine paintings ("La gata" 1973); "Premonicion" and "Otra dimension" (1970, acrylic and sand); death 6 June 1974; the last painting, "Sin titulo (inconcluso)", its palette "atenuandose, cediendo ante la disolucion del blanco". Posthumous: the Homenaje at Bellas Artes 1974; the monograph La constelacion secreta (Jaime Moreno Villarreal, Conaculta/Era, 1993); the 2003 Museo Jose Luis Cuevas show "La abstraccion en la ruptura" (Cuevas presentation, Felguerez chronology); kurimanzutto 2024 "Lilia Carrillo's Lilia Carrillos" (59 unsold studio works, inherited via Felguerez); MPBA 2025-26 "Todo es sugerente" (100-130 works, Daniel Garza Usabiaga); the Zacatecas version at the Museo de Arte Abstracto Manuel Felguerez; Americas Society 2026 "Ruptures and Premonitions" (24 works, Tobias Ostrander); kurimanzutto New York 2026-27 "Manana de octubre"; the monograph with Garza Usabiaga, Megan Kincaid, Leah Dickerman. Through-line: the field gets thinner and the human survives.

## Study method note

The brief's load-bearing premises were re-footed twice: her husband was not Fernando Garcia Ponce (Ruptura peer; younger brother of the critic Juan Garcia Ponce) but Ricardo Guerra Tejada (1952) and Manuel Felguerez (1960, Washington); she died at 43, not 44. Downweighted or corrected: the 1933 birth year (debunked; 1930 confirmed by INBAL, kurimanzutto, the retrospective catalogue); the 1974 divorce from Felguerez (single-source, contradicted by the inheritance record, left UNCONFIRMED); the aneurysm-to-paralysis timeline (sources vary; carried as 1970 aneurysm, partial paralysis, 1972 resumption); the Osaka mural size (5 by 6.40 m, not "cinco por seis"); the "muse of the Galeria Juan Martin nucleus" (narrative framing, not sourced fact); "marginal in her time" (twenty years of recognition; the forgetting was posthumous). Twelve works viewed first-hand as images, all honestly itemized in Depth; no labeled image found for Mudo espia, Jubilo neutro, or the Esencia 2 painting itself; the Osaka mural itself not seen as a separate monumental image. No monograph read end to end; the Paramo interview not retrieved; the kurimanzutto 2024 illustrated timeline attachment 404'd; no Felisa Gross inventory; about twenty works known by title and date only. Future candidates: Fernando Garcia Ponce (the painter who took the ESSO from her) > Gunther Gerzso (the man who geometrized abstraction) > Manuel Felguerez (the widower who kept her work) > Alice Rahon (the surrealist who taught the drip) > Fernando de Szyszlo (the man who sent her to Peru) > Elena Garro (the writer of the story she illustrated).
