# Caius Williams: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-01. ig-artist-study #256.

**Site:** @caiuswilliams_ (3,849 followers, public, no verified badge); Cafe OTO artist page. London-based double bassist (also guitar, bass guitar), improviser, composer; works with "improvisation and explorations of resonance and perception" (Cafe OTO, web); co-programmer/producer of the GRAIN concert series and archiving project with Theodora Laird.

Caius Williams is the bassist in Tom Challenger's GRAIN axis and the quietest operator in that whole network: while the others tour and post, he builds the room. His practice has two faces. One is the body of the double bass played as a resonant site, bow hiss, finger slides, and palm knocks on wood counted as music on Gwannach, his solo OTOROKU tape. The other is the room itself: since 2022 he and Theodora Laird have run GRAIN as a concert series, label, and archive for experimental and improvised music across the liminal venues of South Bermondsey, Avalon Cafe to the Horse Hospital to Cafe OTO to Ormside. The numbered GRAIN Series#1 releases are rotating-patch-bay ensembles, the same small personnel re-dealt into trios; the publishing is hand-drawn, photocopied, and sold on Bandcamp; the largest statement is Mesh, a piece for five double basses, documented like a security-camera still. He is a bass player, a venue programmer, and an archivist, and in his work those are not three jobs. They are one instrument with three strings.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Tom Challenger study (#255): Challenger's GRAIN-residency axis partner, the bassist on the patch-bay ensembles (To Where's in Three, Stiga, Saurischia) and the co-producer of the series itself. Where the Challenger duo is about breath and rules played over years, Williams is about wood and rooms played this weekend, then archived on tape by Monday. Steve Noble, the cross-generational elder of the London scene, plays with him weekly. That tells you where he sits.

## M1. Body-as-instrument tactility

Gwannach (web, Cafe OTO shop page): solo double bass, 8 tracks, recorded March 2023, released 25/10/2023 on OTOROKU (Cafe OTO's in-house label), tape plus download. The copy names the mechanism precisely: "hiss and rasp of bow on string, each shift and knock of palm on (wooden) body" treated as part of the music. The corpus gives the working end: f12 (2025-11-24), a dark close shot of hands at the strings near the bridge with a contact mic/preamp visibly clamped to the bass. The instrument is not just bowed; it is miced at the point of contact and its material noises are amplified into the composition. His web bios frame the whole practice this way: "improvisation and explorations of resonance and perception." Resonance is not a metaphor here. It is the wood, the contact mic, the room.

**Takeaway:** play the material of the instrument, not just its notes; mic the touch itself.

## M2. GRAIN as numbered rotating-ensemble module

GRAIN Series#1 (corpus, all three confirmed in captions; web: Cafe OTO event pages): 'To Where's in Three' (2023-09-04, Challenger/Williams/Kosztolanszki, f02), 'Stiga' (2023-12-15, Taylor/Challenger/Williams, f03), 'Saurischia' (2024-10-12, Challenger/Stylianides/Williams, f08). Same network of players re-dealt into numbered trios; the series is a patch-bay, exactly the Build a Band logic Challenger articulated in #255, except here Williams is the one who built the residency the patch-bay lives in. The cover art is a visual grammar of its own: dark gray ground with black gestural calligraphic brush marks (f02), white worm-like organic shapes on warm brown (f03), heavy film grain with black fluid strokes on white (f08). Each release gets an ink gesture, not a band photo. GRAIN itself, per the OTO pages, is "concert series and archiving project for experimental and improvised music," running since 2022, "largely happening within South Bermondsey," programmed and produced by Williams and Laird.

**Takeaway:** number the meetings, keep the gesture; the archive is the artwork and the ensemble is a dealt hand.

## M3. Mass-bass formats

'Mesh' for five double basses (corpus): Tom Wheatley, Mark Wastell, Kobe Heath Ngugi, Sam Hollis, plus Williams, recorded at the GRAIN residency 2025-06-11, released on his Bandcamp. The frame (f11, 2025-07-04) is the mechanism made visible: a surveillance-camera wide shot of a crowded room with a CCTV timestamp overlay "06-11-2025 19:24:02" and "Camera 07," a double bassist playing among the audience, a paper moon hanging. Five low instruments in one room is a drone machine with five unstable sources; the documentation refuses the concert-photo register and borrows the CCTV one instead. The web leg adds that Squidco lists a completed/recorded large-ensemble project 'Thread,' which suggests the five-bass format was not a one-off.

**Takeaway:** stack the low end until it becomes architecture; document it like found footage, not a recital.

## M4. The partner-duo writing pipeline

Crosspiece (web: Wire Below The Radar 45; Struma+Iodine feeo interview; corpus): the duo of Williams and Theodora Laird, his life partner, who performs as fee0 (vocalist on Loraine James's For You And I). The Wire calls it "Caius and Theodora's first release as a duo, piecing together their long lasting collaboration." The pipeline matters: Cherche Encore, a small independent London label, approached them with Arts Council funding specifically for collaborative releases. Commissions find the duo, not the other way round. The corpus shows the texture: f05 (2024-05-14), the 'Language (nearly there)' single video still, B&W, person singing into a mic close-up, a hand holding an electric guitar neck in the foreground, pints on a table; f06 (2024-05-31), the loft room at taller.sociedad, patterned tile floor, double bass body in the foreground, red electric guitar, orange amp, lamp, speakers. Domestic venue, domestic scale. The interview adds the division of labor: he brings "an immense amount of energy" to her live band but is not involved in her recording/writing process. Two parallel careers, one shared duo, distinct boundaries.

**Takeaway:** let the label come to the relationship; keep the duo's room small and the day jobs separate.

## M5. Home-brew publishing aesthetic

The releases are published like zines. f04 (2024-04-15, 'Worn'): a near-white scan of faint hand-drawn marks, grids, loops, one long looping diagonal line, a sketch that doubles as score. f01 (2023-10-07, Gwannach): B&W photocopy-style art, black masses with oval negative spaces on cream. f10 (2025-05-02, 'Argia' trio with Tara Cunningham and Theo Guttenplan): charcoal line drawing of a double bass on cut paper collaged beside an abstract head/face profile. f16 (2026-09-17, the Zheng Hao tape on sndhls, 'Why I be forget yet same time remember'): white ground, vertical side text, a center field of irregular fingerprint-like dots of varying opacity. The captions describe voice-memo Bandcamp drops ('Worn,' 'Colonnade,' two short solo recordings) and a 4-track cassette recorder sitting on a table in a Tokyo green room (f17, 2026-10-02). No label polish anywhere in the corpus: photocopy, charcoal, fingerprints, voice memos.

**Takeaway:** publish like a zine, record like a witness; the cheapest medium that holds the sound is the right one.

## M6. Rooms as instruments

The venues are part of the compositional system. f13 (2025-12-07): GRAIN at a South Bermondsey festival, a player crouched with small drums and objects on stands in an urban underpass with corrugated walls, a site-specific outdoor setup. f15 (2026-04-03, 'Colonnade'): a red-lit bar, figure moving with the double bass, seated audience close around, drum kit visible. f06 (2024-05-31): the loft room. f17 (2026-10-02): the padded Tokyo rehearsal room, bass leaning against soundproof wall, covered piano, red rug. The feeo interview names the method: DIY, liminal spaces "going to be demolished... or redeveloped," chosen before the developers arrive. Cross-generational by design: Steve Noble plays with him weekly ("really good friends"), Maggie Nicols and Phil Minton in the circuit. The room does not decorate the music; it tunes it. Underpass, loft, bar, padded room: four different resonant chambers, four different pieces.

**Takeaway:** book the room like an instrument; play the space that will be demolished next.

## Evolution

- **2022: the room is built.** GRAIN starts at Avalon Cafe in Bermondsey, where Williams and Laird both work (web, feeo interview). The corpus opens a year later, so this founding year has no IG evidence.
- **2023: the solo statement.** Gwannach recorded in March (Cafe OTO shop page), released October on OTOROKU with Kit Derbyshire artwork; "Special thanks to Tom Challenger." The Tara Cunningham duo 'Demos' (f00). GRAIN Series#1 begins: 'To Where's in Three' (September), 'Stiga' (December). The corpus starts 2023-03-31 and thickens immediately.
- **2024: the pipeline fills.** 'Worn' voice-memo release (April, f04) with a release show at spanners.club; the Crosspiece single 'Language (nearly there)' (May, f05/f06); 'Saurischia' (October, f08); the 'Argia' trio (f10). Crosspiece becomes a working public project.
- **2025: mass formats and the north.** The Sachiko M and Tom Wheatley double-bass trio in March (f09); 'Mesh' for five basses in June (f11); the Milan solo with the contact mic visible (November, f12); GRAIN at the South Bermondsey festival in December (f13). The year moves from trio to quintet, from loft to underpass.
- **2026: the network carries him.** Berlin accompanying feeo in January (f14); 'Colonnade' solo recordings in April (f15); the Zheng Hao tape on sndhls in September (f16); the Tokyo duo with Sachiko M in October, 4-track in the green room (f17). London, Berlin, Tokyo: the rooms change, the bass and the DIY stays.

## Aesthetic logic

Everything in Williams's practice lowers the register: the bass, the room, the cost of the release. He plays the wood of the instrument as much as its strings, mics the contact point, and lets the hiss and the knock be the music. Then he puts that same attention into the venue: an underpass before demolition, a loft, a red-lit bar, a padded Tokyo room, each tuned like a chamber. The GRAIN series is the same impulse turned outward: instead of perfecting one ensemble, he runs a numbered dealing of ensembles and archives the results, cross-generational, photocopied, on tape. The partner duo is commissioned by labels who want the relationship on record; the solo records are voice memos and four-tracks. Nothing in the practice is about scale. It is about making the cheapest, lowest, closest version of each thing, and then keeping it.
