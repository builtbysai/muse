# Oscar Chavez: Study Notes

**Site:** Oscar Chavez Fernandez (born Wednesday 20 March 1935, colonia Portales, Mexico City; died Thursday 30 April 2020, Centro Medico Nacional 20 de Noviembre, Mexico City, of COVID-19 complications, aged 85), Mexican singer, composer, actor, radio actor and producer, researcher of popular music, theatre director and poet, called El Caifan Mayor, considered one of the greatest exponents of the canto nuevo in Mexico, and by the English Wikipedia "the major proponent of the Nueva Trova movement in Mexico in the 1960s and 1970s". He recorded about seventy named album titles by the Spanish Wikipedia table, more than a hundred discs by the Fonoteca Nacional count, and he sang in the Palacio de Bellas Artes in 1973 as one of the first popular singers ever admitted there, in the Auditorio Nacional every year from 1998 to 2015, in the Zocalo for his fiftieth anniversary on Sunday 20 March 2016, and at Vive Latino in 2019 before 30,000 people, and he won the Premio Ariel in 1966 for Los caifanes, the Premio Nacional de Ciencias y Artes in 2011 in Artes y Tradiciones Populares, the Ciudadano Distinguido de la Ciudad de Mexico in 2016, a doctorate honoris causa from the Universidad Autonoma del Estado de Mexico in 2016, and the title of Patrimonio Cultural Vivo de la Ciudad de Mexico in July 2019. He is not a singer in the sense of Pineda or Sosa, and that is the point of studying him here: he is the archaeologist and the parodist, the man who digs up the forgotten song and the man who rewrites the famous one to mock the government, and the chain needs him because Pineda brings the voice and Sosa brings the continent and Manzanero brings the song, and Chavez brings the memory, the country's own songbook carried in one baritone voice with a guitar and a trio that never leaves him. ## M1. The actor who became the singer

He acted first and sang second, and the order is the mechanism. Oscar Chavez Fernandez was born in the colonia Portales of Mexico City, his father from Zacatecas, his mother descended from Spaniards settled in the State of Mexico, and he spent his childhood and adolescence in the colonia Santa Maria la Ribera, then moved to the colonia Roma Sur, where he lived until his death. He began his working life in a bank, but he discovered a taste for the theatre, studying a year and a half in the academy of the actor Seki Sano, and his first play was in 1958, A ritmo de juventud, directed by Enrique Lizalde. In 1960 he entered the Escuela Teatral of the Instituto Nacional de Bellas Artes, where his teachers included Salvador Novo, Emilio Carballido, Pilar Souza and Sergio Magana, and in 1961 he went to work at Radio Universidad (XEUN) as an actor, announcer and producer of radio drama, of which he recorded more than 200 productions. He was twenty-seven when the singing began, and it began by invitation, not by ambition: toward 1962 he started his career as a musician, researcher and interpreter of Mexican popular music, at the initiative of the researcher Lilian Mendelssohn, who saw in Chavez a singular style of revaluing old Mexican pieces, and she put him in contact with the Polydor label, which recorded his first album, Herencia lirica mexicana, with the musician Jose Gonzalez Marquez. The mechanism is the actor's discipline carried into the song: the man who had spent four years speaking other people's words into a radio microphone, and directing Elena Garro's Un hogar solido and Ventura Allende in 1963, sings as an actor sings, with the text first and the voice second, and the Fonoteca survey says it plainly, that his speciality was the music, but the theatre was the training, and the TikTok-era summaries that call him a singer who also acted have it backwards. The mechanism has a second half that proves it: he was also a poet, and the Spanish Wikipedia preserves his own sonnet, written in November 1998 for Juan Jose Arreola's eightieth birthday, "Esgrimes el afan de la memoria, amarras las palabras con tu nudo, las pules con el aura de su escudo y te vas tan campante por la historia", you fence with the eagerness of memory, you tie the words with your knot, you polish them with the aura of their shield, and you go on so calmly through history, and the poem is about Arreola but it describes Chavez, the man who ties words with a knot. Watch the order: when the bank clerk studies theatre for a year and a half, and records two hundred radio dramas, and directs Garro, and only then, at twenty-seven, is invited by a researcher to sing the old songs, and the first album is called a lyrical inheritance, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** act first, then sing. The radio microphone is the school. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M1) also served ten years in the small rooms, but her small rooms were the school and the pen~a, and she sang under her own name from the start; Chavez's small rooms were a bank, a theatre academy and a radio studio, and he sang only when a researcher invited him, which is why his version is an invitation and hers is an apprenticeship. Sosa (#359 M1) also began behind a false name at fifteen; Chavez began behind other people's words at twenty-seven, which is why her version is a hiding place and his is a training.

## M2. The rescue

He dug up the forgotten song and sang it back into the country, and the digging is the mechanism. The ciencialatina article calls him "arqueologo de la cancion tradicional", archaeologist of the traditional song, and gives the rescue list: Roman Castillo, the first song he recorded, Son del ombligo, La tortuga, Ursula, La historia del hombre muerto, El gorrion hermoso, La Juanita, El pajaro y el chanate, La pulqueria, El guerrillero, A Colombina, El angelito, Rosa Carmela, and a long etcetera, most of them included in his best albums, Herencia Lirica Mexicana (1963-68), Latinoamerica canta (1970-71), Expresiones (1973), and the most celebrated, La llorona. The Spanish Wikipedia states the mechanism as a programme: his work was directed, besides interpretation, at the investigation of musical forms and of the lyric tradition associated with them, including forgotten texts to put them back in the public's taste, like romances of the viceregal period ("Roman Castillo"), coplas and songs of the nineteenth century ("La mariguana", "Mariana", "Corrido de Juan Cortina", "El Charro Ponciano"), and the Fonoteca survey extends the list to the regions, albums dedicated to the musical production of states with musical richness like Oaxaca, Chiapas, Yucatan, Nuevo Leon and Veracruz, and to traditions of song tied to historical events. The mechanism has a second half that makes it a map: the rescue is not one song but a geography, Oaxaca and Chiapas and Veracruz and Guanajuato and Guerrero and Nuevo Leon and the City of Mexico and Yucatan, and a history, the 1968 protest songs (Mexico 68 Vol. 1 y 2), the railwaymen's songs (Cantos ferrocarrileros), the liberal juarista tradition (Juarez no debio de morir), the Spanish Civil War and the Spanish exile (Canciones de la Guerra Civil y resistencia espan~ola), and the Zapatista guerrilla (Chiapas). The mechanism's proof is the Voz viva de Mexico series, in which he recited poems by Sor Juana Ines de la Cruz, Gilberto Owen and Amado Nervo, because the rescue includes the poem as well as the song, and the man who ties words with a knot ties the poem to the song. Carlos Monsivais gave the mechanism its sentence, quoted by the Fonoteca: "Desde hace an~os, Oscar Chavez - cantante, compositor, folclorista, antologo, luchador social -, trabaja en una triple vertiente: rescate de la herencia lirica, presentacion de nuevos materiales mexicanos y latinoamericanos, y manejo de las vetas satiricas de la cancion." For years now, Oscar Chavez - singer, composer, folklorist, anthologist, social fighter - works in a triple direction: rescue of the lyric inheritance, presentation of new Mexican and Latin American materials, and handling of the satirical veins of the song. Watch the digging: when the singer who began at twenty-seven by a researcher's invitation spends fifty years recording songs nobody had sung for decades, and the rescue covers eight states and five histories, and the same man recites Sor Juana on a record, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** dig up the forgotten song and sing it back. The archive is the repertoire. Against the chain: Manzanero (#358 M3) also wrote a song at fifteen and let others carry it, but his songs left him and never came back; Chavez's songs came to him from the dead and never left, which is why his version is a rescue and Manzanero's is a departure. Sosa (#359 M4) also took the song by singing it, but hers were living authors' songs, Violeta Parra and Victor Jara and Silvio Rodriguez; Chavez's were dead authors' songs, romances and coplas, which is why her version is a taking and his is a digging.

## M3. The film that named him

A film gave him his name, and the name became the identity, and the identity became a band's name, and that is the mechanism. In 1966, the theatre and film director Juan Ibanez invited him into the cinema with his film Los caifanes, alongside Julissa, Enrique Alvarez Felix, Sergio Jimenez, Ernesto Gomez Cruz and Eduardo Lopez Rojas, and Chavez played El Estilos, a member of a gang of young toughs, a caifan, who shows a pair of rich kids the secrets of the capital's nightlife, and in the film he sang "La nin~a de Guatemala" and "Fuera del mundo". The film was successful and increased the fame of its actors, and it gave Chavez two cinema prizes, a Diosa de Plata and a Premio Ariel, both in 1966 by the Spanish Wikipedia count, and it gave him the name he carried for the rest of his life: El Caifan, El Caifan Mayor. The mechanism is the nickname as a second birth: the man born Oscar Chavez Fernandez becomes the Caifan Mayor, the eldest tough, and the name is not a stage name chosen by a manager but a character he played, so the actor and the singer share one name, and the name does the work of both. The mechanism's proof is the afterlife: more than fifty years later, the rock band Caifanes, who took their name from the film, called him "Caifan mayor" on stage at Vive Latino in 2019 and played "Por ti" with him at the Foro Sol, and Panteon Rococo sang "Fuera del mundo" with him at the same festival, so the film's name came back to him from a generation that had not been born when the film was made. The year is carried two ways, 1966 by the Spanish Wikipedia filmography and the awards list, 1967 by the Gaceta UNAM, the OnCubaNews obituary, the Granma tribute, the Bicaalu essay and the ciencialatina article, and the study carries 1966/1967 and says so. Watch the naming: when the theatre actor plays a street tough in a film, and the film wins him two prizes, and the character's name becomes his name for fifty-four years, and a rock band named after the film calls him the eldest tough on stage before thirty thousand people, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the film name you. The character is the identity. Against the chain: Manzanero (#358 M7) also turned a veto into a trademark, his height of 1.54 metres, but his trademark was his body; Chavez's trademark was a character he played, which is why his version is a role and Manzanero's is a measurement. Sosa (#359 M1) also carried a name the people gave her, La Negra, but hers was a nickname given by the people; Chavez's was a character given by a director, which is why her version is a gift and his is a casting.

## M4. The year that turned him

1968 turned him, and the turning never turned back, and that is the mechanism. The Spanish Wikipedia states it in one paragraph: he formed part of the 1968 Movement in Mexico alongside other artists like Los Nakos, Los Folkloristas, Judith Reyes, Leon Chavez Teixeiro and Amparo Ochoa, interpreting songs in the protests and demonstrations in support of that cause, and his participation was recorded in the documentary El grito, Mexico 1968, by Leobardo Lopez Arretche, in which he appeared singing in the Ciudad Universitaria of the UNAM, and the Gaceta UNAM preserves the sequence, a memorable shot of him beside the Biblioteca Central, in front of a group of students scattered on the explanada of the Islas, singing a revolutionary corrido with his guitar. The student movement changed his artistic motivations, inclining him toward the composition and interpretation of protest songs, of social and political content. The mechanism is the year as a hinge: before 1968 he was the actor-researcher of the old songs, after 1968 he was the singer of the protest, and the hinge is visible in the discography, because the albums after 1968 are the parodies and the movement songs, Mexico 68 Vol. 1 y 2, Cantos ferrocarrileros, Nicaragua vencera, Guerrero, and the songs of the catalogue that the obituaries list first are the protest songs, "La casita", "Se vende mi pais", "Hasta siempre". The mechanism's proof is its duration: the commitment did not end with the movement, it ran for fifty-two years, from the explanada of the Islas on Wednesday 2 October 1968 to the Zapatista uprising of 1994, which he supported publicly, recording the album Chiapas in 2000, organising concerts to build clinics in the Zapatista caracoles, and keeping a public correspondence with the organisation, and then to 2018, when he gave concerts and collected signatures for the indigenous presidential candidacy of Maria de Jesus Patricio, Marichuy, and then to his last concert in the Bosque de Chapultepec on Saturday 16 November 2019, and och-08 shows it, Chavez on a dirt path with his arm around a person in a black balaclava with a red neckerchief. He described himself, in the Linea Directa interview quoted by ciencialatina, as "de izquierda y profundamente juarista", of the left and profoundly juarista, and the juarista album Juarez no debio de morir came out in 2009, forty-one years after the hinge. Watch the hinge: when the actor-researcher sings a revolutionary corrido on the explanada in 1968, and the movement changes what he sings, and fifty-two years later he is still collecting signatures for an indigenous candidate and standing on a dirt path with a masked Zapatista, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let one year turn you, and never turn back. The explanada is the career. Against the chain: Sosa (#359 M6) also was arrested for singing, but hers was an arrest on stage in La Plata in 1978, a punishment that sent her into exile; Chavez was never arrested for singing, he was turned by a movement and stayed turned, which is why her version is a cell and his is a hinge. Libertad (#356 M1) also started again from below after six years out of the media; Chavez never left, he turned and stayed, which is why her version is a restart and his is a hinge.

## M5. The parody as the weapon

He mocked the government in song, and the mockery was the weapon, and the weapon was a parody, and that is the mechanism. The turn of 1968 led Chavez to political parody songs, and he made the first album of five of Parodias politicas, recording the first of the series in the Teatro Blanquita in 1970, a theatre where he would do numerous seasons, and he also began cabaret shows in places in Mexico City like La Tasca and Cafe Colon, with contents of political criticism of the government of the time. The mechanism is the laugh as the delivery system: the denunciation arrives as a joke, and the joke arrives as a familiar tune with new words, so the audience laughs before it realises it has agreed, and the agreement is the point. The ciencialatina article gives the mechanism its epigraph, in Chavez's own words: "Hay gente que no se merece ni una parodia (...) el sentido comun es el menos comun de los sentidos (...) o reaccionamos o reaccionamos", there are people who do not even deserve a parody, common sense is the least common of the senses, either we react or we react. The mechanism's proof is the re-lettering: "La casita", the song the sources name first among his protest songs, was adapted by Chavez from the traditional lyric to reflect the enrichment of a political functionary, "De una casita que tengo, alla por el Pedregal. De una casita chiquita, con jardines, alberquita y calefaccion central", a little house I have, over in El Pedregal, a tiny little house, with gardens, a little pool and central heating, and the Bicaalu essay and the ciencialatina article agree that he renewed its lyric almost every six years, so the same song mocked a new president every six years, and the song became a calendar. The mechanism has a second half in the same register: "De ranchero a diputado", "Salario minimo", "La maquinita", "Se vende mi pais", "El pendejo", "Petroleo", "La devaluacion", "El Pan Y La Tortilla", and the Pie de Pagina obituary adds the house of Pena Nieto, the ostentatious residences that contrast violently with the conditions in which most Mexicans live. The mechanism's cost is stated by the Fonoteca: he wrote a piece for the magazine La Garrapata, "De chile, de dulce y de manteca", in number 3 of November 1968, the month after the massacre, so the parody began in the month of the repression. Watch the weapon: when the singer records five volumes of political parodies in a variety theatre, and does cabaret in La Tasca and Cafe Colon, and re-letters the same little-house song for every president for fifty years, and the joke is the denunciation, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** mock them in a familiar tune, and re-letter it every six years. The laugh is the delivery. Against the chain: Manzanero (#358 M10) also let his songs speak for the drama, but his were telenovela themes, the song as the voice of the evening; Chavez's were parodies, the song as the voice of the opposition, which is why his version is a weapon and Manzanero's is a theme. Sosa (#359 M6) also sang the forbidden songs, but hers were forbidden by a dictatorship and she was arrested for them; Chavez's were forbidden by nothing, they were laughed, which is why her version is a cell and his is a joke.

## M6. The other country's song

He sang the other countries' songs as if they were Mexican, and the adoption is the mechanism. The Spanish Wikipedia lists the reversions: he made his own versions of outstanding authors of the Latin American song that became popular in Mexico in his voice, like Carlos Puebla ("Hasta siempre, Comandante"), Daniel Camino Diez ("Macondo"), Violeta Parra ("Gracias a la vida"), Ramon Sixto Rios ("Merceditas") and Miguel Matamoros ("Lagrimas negras"), and he also set to music poetry and texts by Nezahualcoyotl, Jose Marti ("La nin~a de Guatemala"), Manuel Jose Othon ("La casita") and Andres Henestrosa ("La ixhuateca"). The mechanism is the adoption as a Mexicanisation: the Chilean song, the Cuban song, the Argentine song arrive in his baritone voice with a guitar and a trio, and they come out Mexican, not because he changes them but because he sings them as if they had always been Mexican, and the country believes him. The mechanism's proof is the Marti disc, och-09: Versos sencillos, Casa de las Americas, Havana, Cuba, 1972, four poems of Jose Marti set to music by Chavez, with arrangements by Benjamin "Chamin" Correa, and the Granma tribute says the Casa de las Americas published that disc with four Marti poems musicalised by him, and that whenever he gave a recital the public unfailingly asked for his version of "La nin~a de Guatemala". The mechanism has a second half in the same direction: he sang a song to Chabuca Granda, "A Chabuca Granda", listed among his themes by the OnCubaNews obituary, so the Peruvian composer behind Libertad's forbidden "Cardo o ceniza" and Manzanero's "Adoro" is also honoured by Chavez, and the same woman stands at the root of four studies now. The mechanism's proof in the other direction is "Macondo": Daniel Camino Diez wrote the song, Gabriel Garcia Marquez wrote the novel Cien an~os de soledad, and Chavez sang the song so that the novel's town became a song, and the Pie de Pagina obituary calls it a homage to the work, and the ciencialatina article calls it the habitual encore of his concerts, the piece that closes the night. Watch the adoption: when the Mexican singer records a Cuban poet's verses in Havana, and the Havana public asks for the Guatemalan girl every night, and the Colombian novel becomes a Mexican song, and the Peruvian composer gets a song of her own, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** sing the other country's song as if it were yours. The adoption is the Mexicanisation. Against the chain: Sosa (#359 M5) also sang the other country's song, Violeta Parra's "Gracias a la vida", and died on Violeta's birthday, but hers was an adoption of one woman, a tribute album and a single; Chavez adopted a whole shelf, Marti and Puebla and Parra and Garcia Marquez, which is why her version is a devotion and his is a shelf. Manzanero (#358 M5) also brought a Peruvian vals home and recorded it as a ballad; Chavez brought a Cuban poem home and recorded it as a Mexican song, which is why his version is an adoption and Manzanero's is a conversion.

## M7. La llorona

He made "La llorona" the reference version, and the reference is the mechanism. The album La Llorona came out in 1972, produced by the English label Polydor, and the ciencialatina article calls it one of the most relevant discs in the history of recorded Mexican music, a compilation of traditional songs of the Oaxacan region, many of which had new lyrics by the journalist and Zapotec poet Andres Henestrosa, and the title piece is without doubt the best known of all. ## Aesthetic logic

Chavez's aesthetic is the song as the memory of the country, sung by one baritone voice with a guitar and the same trio, in whatever register the moment needs: the rescued romance, the love song, the parody, the Marti poem, the Oaxacan lament, and the scarcity is the argument, because a singer with no costume, no mask and no character, only a black shirt and a black embroidered jacket and a guitar, has only the text to hold the room, and the text has to be exact enough to be believed in every register. The materials are a bank job left for the theatre, two hundred radio dramas, a researcher's invitation, a first album called a lyrical inheritance, a film that gave him his name, a corrido sung on a university explanada, five volumes of political parodies, a little-house song re-lettered every six years, a Cuban poet's verses recorded in Havana, a traditional Oaxacan song with no definitive version, a love song that began as hell, the same three men beside him for decades, a union that would not have him, eighteen years of the same hall, thirty thousand young people at a rock festival, a square filled on his eighty-first birthday, and a death in a pandemic five months after a last concert titled for when he dies. The palette is the one the photographs give: the black of the embroidered charro jacket and the black shirts of och-01 to och-04 and och-07, against the blue sky and the purple spotlight of the Zocalo; the black and white of och-05, the seated smile and the drum; the black puffer vest and the green trees of och-06; the dirt path and the green grass of och-08; the pink sleeve and the green rose label of the Versos sencillos vinyl in och-09. Where Pineda's logic was one country sung by one voice in every genre, and Libertad's was two countries sung by one voice in one night, and Manzanero's was every country sung by every voice in one song, and Sosa's was the continent sung by one voice because it must be sung, Chavez's is one country sung by one voice in every century, and the difference is the tense: Pineda sings the present, Libertad sings the crossing, Manzanero sings the song, Sosa sings the must, and Chavez sings the past, the nineteenth century and the viceroyalty and 1968, as if they were the present, which is why his version is a memory and theirs are a voice, a set list, a catalogue and a must.
