## M1. The one-volume synthesis from interviews plus printed sources

The Spanish Civil War was built in two years from 36 Spaniards and 49 other interviewees and "a great library of books", with no Spanish archive visited. The witness list is the mechanism, because a book whose authority comes from living witnesses before the archives open is a different book from one built on files, and the witnesses are the piece, and the piece must show eighty-five named voices arranged into one narrative, and the later editions keeping on correcting the witnesses, and the correction being the proof that the witnesses were real.

**Takeaway:** build from the witnesses first, and the piece should show the list, not the archive.

## M2. The telling anecdote as evidence

The Slave Trade introduction opens with the Williams dinner, then builds the book around men: Zulueta the vaccinated, baptised slaver; Marchionni the Florentine who financed da Gama and sold slaves at Elmina; Oswald, whose Bence Island golf course had slave caddies in kilts. The single named life is the mechanism, because one life carrying a whole structural claim is more memorable than the structure, and the life is the piece, and the piece must show one man, named, doing one thing, and the whole trade visible behind him, and the trade staying visible when the man walks off.

**Takeaway:** let one life carry the structure, and the piece should show the man, not the system.

## M3. The question-catalogue opening

The Slave Trade introduction lists a question for every kind of reader, morality, economics, church history, popular movements, commerce, monarchy, Jewish history, Islam, before the narrative starts. The catalogue is the mechanism, because a book that promises each reader their own question holds every reader through a book that answers none of them directly, and the promise is the piece, and the piece must show the questions laid out like a menu, each one lit, and the narrative walking past them, and the questions still lit at the end.

**Takeaway:** promise every reader their question, and the piece should show the menu, not the answers.

## M4. The "unfinished" frame

An Unfinished History of the World (1979) makes incompleteness the title; the Cold War history stops after volume one; the autobiography was unfinished at death. The frame is the mechanism, because a work that announces its own limits instead of hiding them cannot be caught out by them, and the announcement is the piece, and the piece must show the frame left open, the last chapter missing on purpose, and the missing chapter doing the work of the conclusion.

**Takeaway:** announce the limits in the title, and the piece should show the open frame.

## M5. Revision across editions as method

War death toll 0.61m (1961) to 0.37m (mid-1970s) to "about 500,000" (2012); executions ratio 40:60 to 58:42; Jarama 25,000 + 20,000 to 10,000 + 6,000; Guernica 1,654 to "nearer one hundred" to "perhaps as many as a thousand". The revision is the mechanism, because the same book, read in two editions, disagreeing with itself and saying so is more honest than a book that never changes, and the disagreement is the piece, and the piece must show the same page in two editions, the numbers changing, and the change being the argument, and the argument being that the numbers were always estimates.

**Takeaway:** let the book disagree with itself in public, and the piece should show the changing number.

## M6. The balance / equilibrium device

"Balanced account" is the reviewers' word, Carr, the Simon and Schuster publisher copy, and his own: weighing rival interpretations, "judicious weighing and balancing", Cox on Conquest. The balance is the mechanism, because every chapter giving both sides their best case before the verdict makes the verdict earned, and the weighing is the piece, and the piece must show two pans, each loaded with its best case, and the pans level, and the verdict arriving only after the level holds.

**Takeaway:** give both sides their best case first, and the piece should show the level pans.

## M7. The florid set-piece

Santa Maria de la Cabeza: "as savage as Red Indians... Flames engulfed the Sierra", the "Red Indians" line removed in the 3rd edition, the flames kept. The set-piece is the mechanism, because prose that suddenly goes cinematic inside a judicious history is the moment the reader remembers, and the cinema is the piece, and the piece must show the flat narrative suddenly flaring, the flames, the savagery, and then the quiet edit in the next edition, and the edit being the second half of the mechanism.

**Takeaway:** let the prose flare once, then edit it quietly, and the piece should show both the flare and the edit.

## M8. The dramatis personae

Beaumarchais in Seville opens with some fifty named persons before chapter one, "more than Beaumarchais listed in any of his own plays", H-France. The cast list is the mechanism, because a history that opens with its cast is a history that has already decided it is theatre, and the cast is the piece, and the piece must show fifty names, each one a doorway, and the doors opening one by one, and the play being the history.

**Takeaway:** open with the cast list, and the piece should show the doors opening.

## M9. The "professional historian in private practice" stance

Preston's title. No university post after Reading 1976; the work is done from Ladbroke Grove, the London Library, the House of Lords library and the archives. The stance is the mechanism, because a historian as freelance operator, not professor, writes without a department to please and without students to teach, and the freedom is the piece, and the piece must show the desk at home, the library card, the Lords library, and the books coming out of all three, and no university in the frame.

**Takeaway:** work from the house, not the department, and the piece should show the desk, not the chair.

## M10. The big-book cadence

720 pp. (The Spanish Civil War), about 1,700 pp. (Cuba), 760-900 pp. (The Slave Trade), 834 pp. (Conquest), 720 pp. (Rivers of Gold); about 30% of Conquest is appendices, glossaries, documents and notes. The cadence is the mechanism, because a book whose apparatus is a third of the book is a book that shows its working, and the apparatus is the piece, and the piece must show the narrative thinning as the notes thicken, and the notes doing the work the narrative cannot, and the book getting heavier as it gets more honest.

**Takeaway:** let the apparatus be a third of the book, and the piece should show the notes thickening.

## M11. The archive walk / place visit as research

Walking "round the still-elegant streets of Walsh's Nantes"; "in the Archivo de Indias in Seville... I have spent some of the most fruitful days of my life". The walk is the mechanism, because a footnote that begins "I once walked" is evidence of a different kind from a footnote that begins with a file number, and the walking is the piece, and the piece must show the historian on foot in the street the book is about, and the street answering back, and the answer going into the footnote.

**Takeaway:** walk the street the book is about, and the piece should show the walking.

## M12. The dinner-table origin

The Williams dinner launches The Slave Trade; the Notting Hill dinners, late 1982, do the politics. The table is the mechanism, because the book and the policy both start at a table, and the table is the piece, and the piece must show the dinner, the guests, the remark, and the book growing out of the remark, and the policy growing out of the second dinner, and both dinners being the real first chapters.

**Takeaway:** start the book at a dinner table, and the piece should show the remark, not the research.

## M13. The footnote apparatus as argument

Rivers of Gold: 84 pp. footnotes, 23 pp. bibliography; The Spanish Civil War last edition: 35 maps, no photos. The apparatus is the mechanism, because maps and notes do the work the pictures would do, and the maps are the piece, and the piece must show thirty-five maps where the photographs would be, and the maps carrying the argument, and the absence of photographs being the argument, that this history is argued, not illustrated.

**Takeaway:** let the maps do the picturing, and the piece should show the maps, not the photographs.

## M14. Source criticism by downgrading

Krivitsky downgraded between editions; the "secret documents" go from "not forgeries... dreams more than blueprints" (1961) to forgeries (1965), with the note "particularly grateful to Mr H. R. Southworth for helping me see the light". The downgrade is the mechanism, because a source demoted in public, with thanks to the man who demoted it, is criticism performed as courtesy, and the demotion is the piece, and the piece must show the source sliding down the scale, edition by edition, and the thank-you note doing the sliding.

**Takeaway:** demote the source in public and thank the man who showed you, and the piece should show the slide.

## M15. The correction-and-apology habit

The Gustavo Duran / Usera Tunnel scam is apologised for and dropped; the Durruti quote is re-sourced, Montreal Star to Toronto Star; Calvo Sotelo's killer goes from "Victoriano Cuenca" to "Luis Cuenca". The correction is the mechanism, because errors fixed in the next edition, not hidden, are the book's credibility, and the fixing is the piece, and the piece must show the error, the apology, and the corrected name, and the three together being the method.

**Takeaway:** fix the error in the next edition and say so, and the piece should show the corrected name.

## M16. The quantitative relief

"It is a relief to find some statistical evidence", on Sierra Leone origins: only 30% of the enslaved were war captures; Portuguese about 38% of the trade; about 1 in 10 slave ships had a rebellion; Dutch crew mortality about 18%, Africans dying at sea about 12%. The relief is the mechanism, because the numbers arrive exactly where the stories conflict, and the arrival is the piece, and the piece must show two stories fighting over one fact, and the number landing between them, and the fight stopping, and the number staying on screen while the stories leave.

**Takeaway:** let the number land where the stories fight, and the piece should show the landing.

## M17. The named-shareholder move

Defoe and Locke as shareholders in the South Sea and Royal African Companies; the RAC initials "branded on so many black breasts". The shareholder is the mechanism, because a familiar name attached to an unfamiliar guilt makes the guilt land on someone the reader already knows, and the attachment is the piece, and the piece must show the famous name, then the share certificate, then the brand, and the three in one frame, and the frame saying "almost no one is innocent".

**Takeaway:** attach the famous name to the guilt, and the piece should show the certificate.

## M18. The anti-villain rule

"Still, historians should not look for villains"; traders are not "worse" than the slaveowners "who after all constituted the market". The rule is the mechanism, because the moral charge spread until it has nowhere to land is a history that refuses the easy villain, and the spreading is the piece, and the piece must show the blame passed from hand to hand, trader to owner to market to system, and the blame never settling, and the unsettled blame being the point.

**Takeaway:** spread the blame until it has nowhere to land, and the piece should show the passing.

## M19. The "say what happened" plainness

"I have tried in this book to say what happened. In seeking the truth, I have not thought it necessary to speak of outrage on every page." The plainness is the mechanism, because the flat sentence after the horror carries more weight than the outraged sentence, and the flatness is the piece, and the piece must show the horror stated plainly, one sentence, no adjective, and the silence after it, and the silence doing the outraging.

**Takeaway:** say what happened, flatly, and the piece should show the silence after.

## M20. The counter-evidence admission

Conquest: he "merely weights things that support his idea; he includes a lot of detail and does not omit things which would counter his bias, so someone not like him... could make other inferences from the data", Noggle. The admission is the mechanism, because a book that hands the reader the material to disagree with it is a book that trusts its reader, and the handing-over is the piece, and the piece must show the evidence laid out on both sides of the table, and the reader invited to take either side, and the invitation being the honesty.

**Takeaway:** hand the reader the material to disagree, and the piece should show both sides of the table.

## M21. The personal-discovery hook

In the Archivo de Indias he found a ship bringing twenty slaves to Havana Bay in 1792 "captained by someone from Liverpool by the name of Hugo Tomas". The hook is the mechanism, because the historian finding his own name in the archive is the moment the book becomes personal, and the finding is the piece, and the piece must show the name surfacing in the file, the historian stopping, and the stop being the discovery, and the discovery being why the archive matters.

**Takeaway:** find your own name in the archive, and the piece should show the stop.

## M22. The lineage claim

His politics are read as a lineage, "Dr Johnson, Pitt the Younger, Burke, Benjamin Disraeli and Lord Salisbury", Nairn, and his world history as Spenglerian, "Mrs Thatcher's Spengler". The lineage is the mechanism, because an argument carried by a list of ancestors is an argument that borrows their authority, and the borrowing is the piece, and the piece must show the five names in a row, each one lighting the next, and the argument walking down the row, and the row being the proof.

**Takeaway:** carry the argument down a row of ancestors, and the piece should show the lighting.

## M23. The pamphlet as politics

History, Capitalism and Freedom (1979, foreword by Thatcher), Our Place in the World (1983), The Case for the Round Reading Room (1983), The Revolution on Balance (1983). The pamphlet is the mechanism, because the big historian writing small, and the small piece doing the political work, is the division of labour made visible, and the smallness is the piece, and the piece must show the fat book on one side and the thin pamphlet on the other, and the thin one moving the politics, and the fat one staying on the shelf.

**Takeaway:** write small when the work is political, and the piece should show the thin pamphlet moving.

## M24. The intermezzo as counterweight

Beaumarchais in Seville: 177 pp. against 1,100 pp. books; the subtitle says it, "An Intermezzo". ## M25. The translation / smuggling circuit as reception

## M28. The "vast scheme of things" normalisation

"All were caught up in a vast scheme of things which seemed normal at least till 1780." ## M29. The pen-portrait of the merchant

"It was the slave merchants themselves, sitting in their fine counting-houses in London or Lisbon, men who often never saw slaves but profited from their sale, who interested me." The portrait is the mechanism, because the man who never sees the thing he sells is the figure the whole book turns on, and the counting-house is the piece, and the piece must show the fine room, the ledger, the man who never leaves the room, and the trade happening somewhere else, and the somewhere else never entering the room.

**Takeaway:** portrait the man who never sees what he sells, and the piece should show the counting-house.

## M30. The refusal / institutional gesture

The refusal is the mechanism, because the prize turned down and the library defended are the same gesture, independence performed in public, and the gesture is the piece, and the piece must show the prize offered and refused, the reading room threatened and defended, and the two actions being one stance.

**Takeaway:** refuse the prize and defend the library, and the piece should show the one stance.

## Evolution: four phases, and the afterlife

PHASE 1 - THE CONTEMPORARY HISTORIAN, 1957-1971: two novels, The World's Game (1957) and The Oxygen Age (1958), then the commission that made him, MacGibbon's pitch for a broad survey of the Spanish Civil War, written in two years from interviews, 36 Spaniards and 49 others, and "a great library of books", with no Spanish archive visited; The Suez Affair (1967) turns his own Foreign Office experience into history; Cuba (1971) is the same method at maximum scale, seven years on "a short period in the history of a small society", about 1,700 pages. The driver: contemporary history from living witnesses before the archives open.

PHASE 2 - THE ESSAYIST-POLITICIAN, 1972-1986: John Strachey (1973), Europe: The Radical Challenge (1973), the Daily Mail defection articles, Tuesday 23 and Friday 26 November 1976, An Unfinished History of the World (1979), the CPS chairmanship 1979-1991, the Thatcher memoranda and the Falklands channel 1982, Armed Truce (1986), a Cold War history whose sequels never appeared. The driver shifts from witness history to argument: pamphlets, speeches, and a world history written from a stated political lineage, Johnson, Pitt, Burke, Disraeli, Salisbury, per Nairn.

PHASE 3 - THE ATLANTIC TURN, 1988-1997: Madrid: A Travellers' Companion (1988), then the two great archive books, The Conquest of Mexico (1993) and The Slave Trade (1997). Now the research is archival, Archivo de Indias, Seville, "some of the most fruitful days of my life", and the scale is centuries and oceans rather than one war. The Slave Trade introduction is the clearest statement of his mature method, M3, M11, M16-M19.

PHASE 4 - THE IMPERIAL TRILOGY, 2000-2014: Who Is Who of the Conquistadors (2000), Rivers of Gold (2003), The Golden Age (2010), World Without End (2014), the Spanish Empire in three reigns, Columbus to Magellan, Charles V, Philip II. Against the grain of the trilogy, the small intermezzo Beaumarchais in Seville (2006, 177 pp.) and the biography Eduardo Barreiros (2009). The driver: the same one-volume synthesis, now applied to a whole empire, with the small book as counterweight. Unfinished at death: an autobiography and an enormous personal archive, every paper, cuttings, journals, thousands of letters.

POSTHUMOUS AFTERLIFE, 2017-: died Sunday 7 May 2017, London, after a stroke, aged 85; Spanish government statement Monday 8 May 2017; Guardian obituary by Paul Preston Tuesday 9 May 2017; New York Times obituary by Alan Cowell Thursday 11 May 2017; Preston's Bulletin of Spanish Studies memorial, "A professional historian in private practice"; Inigo Thomas, "On Ladbroke Grove", London Review of Books, 15 June 2017, not read in this leg, paywalled. The desk keeps opening; the archive waits; the one-volume book remains unsurpassed.

## Aesthetic logic

Thomas's aesthetic is the one-volume synthesis that refuses to be a system. The book is big, the apparatus is a third of it, the maps do the picturing, and the verdict is earned by weighing, not by thesis. He trusts the witness before the archive, the anecdote before the structure, the number where the stories conflict, and the flat sentence after the horror. He distrusts the fat system and trusts the big book; he distrusts the villain and trusts the scheme; he distrusts the outrage on every page and trusts the plain statement of what happened. The voice is judicious, the models Johnson and Burke and the lineage he claimed, and the voice does the arguing: a sentence that weighs both sides lands its verdict in the final phrase, and the landing is the proof. The logic breaks where the man breaks: the same impartiality that made the Spanish Civil War unsurpassed sat beside the political lineage that made him Thatcher's adviser, the CPS chairman, the curriculum warrior against Thompson and Hobsbawm, and the history written from a stated lineage cannot be read apart from the lineage. The study does not resolve that tension; it records it, because the tension is the subject.

**Takeaway:** build the one-volume book, weigh both sides, say what happened flatly, and let the piece show the lineage standing beside the impartiality.

C0.
