# Dalmiro Sirabo: Study Notes

**Site:** Dalmiro Sirabo, born San Luis, Argentina, 1939 (no day or month found anywhere), death year unknown, Argentine painter, constructor, museologist and designer, the platense who crossed the sectarian line of his own generation -- from the informalist Grupo Si, which he co-founded in 1960, to a geometric constructivism he then refused to leave: first the flat achromatic figure on a plain ground, then the ambiguous depth that flips between tunnel and pyramid, then the structures that lean on one another and seem to defy gravity, then the painted-wood constructions of U- and double-T-profiles, the turning Giratorios, the fine painted iron that cuts space like a line, and the bilateral symmetries that turn a picture into a sign -- a "menos es mas" minimalism, a language of signs built to be read, not a picture to be looked at (C9771, C9776).

D4 "whose geometry never left the drawing board" -- HOLDS WITH GLOSS. No source uses the phrase "drawing board"; the clause is figurative, and the substance holds for the easel work: "concentrada en el plano", "el dialogo ortogonal", flat achromatic geometric forms, "menos es mas" minimalism. The gloss, and the tension the study must carry: a literal reading is contradicted by three documented object works -- the Giratorios mobiles of 1966, the iron Figura espacial, and Cubo 1968 with Grippo and Segura -- so the clause is read as "stayed planar in the easel work and never made the object the point", not "never left the plane". Unlike Puente (shaped canvases, three-dimensional structures) and Paternosto (painting pushed to the stretcher sides, then to objects), Sirabo's object work is a side excursion, not the turn: the plane is where he stayed.

## The life: San Luis oils, the Bar Capitol, the crossing

Dalmiro Sirabo was born in San Luis, Argentina, in 1939. The day and month are not found anywhere. The birthplace itself was the subject of a conflict carried from study #639 -- San Luis 1939 per a UNLP interview against La Plata 1934 per ArtsDot -- and this study resolves it: San Luis 1939 is attested by four independent voices (the 1975 Nessi/Gustavino thesis, built on a personal interview with the artist; the Galvez UNLP paper, footnote 1; the 2012 MACLA catalogue; ICAA record 766360), while La Plata 1934 rests on one voice, ArtsDot, whose biography WahooArt copies verbatim -- the same text, not a second source. The death side is worse for ArtsDot than "unresolved": its 2002 death date is refuted, not merely unconfirmed, because Sirabo led a two-hour public conversation at the Museo Pettoruti on 20 October 2011 and the 2012 MACLA catalogue says in the present tense "Actualmente vive y trabaja en City Bell". A man alive in 2011 cannot have died in 2002. The true death year remains open; no obituary, death notice or cemetery record was found. Never print 1934-2002 except in the conflict section (C9776).

As a youth in San Luis he painted oils without academy training, in his own words from the Nessi interview: "Yo era una especie de canal entre el color y la tela. La tela me pedia. Lo hacia sin preconceptos y con mucha libertad. Un color me pedia otro, una forma me pedia la otra; era un trasvasamiento. Muy lindo." He liked looking at wet landscapes and painting them. In 1958, Nessi dates the start of his visual investigations moving from the two-dimensional field into environmental space -- the spatial turn begins a full decade before it is shown. ArtsDot independently claims first exhibitions in 1958 of landscapes and portraits; that is ArtsDot-only and uncorroborated, and is not used as a dated exhibition fact.

He came to La Plata and studied Vision, Design and Architecture at the Universidad Nacional de La Plata, and colour theory and composition with Prof. Hector Cartier at the Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes, alongside Sitro, Ambrosini and Trotta; he studied painting in private workshops, not, so far as any source says, through a fine-arts degree. He took perfection courses in art museums at the Smithsonian Institution, Washington D.C. and New York -- the courses are confirmed by Galvez, the dates are not; ArtsDot's 1972-75 is unverified and is not printed as fact. The 2012 MACLA catalogue describes him as "Museologo y Disenador", museologist and designer, and as subdirector of the Museo Provincial de Bellas Artes de La Plata, dates of tenure not established.

The founding scene is 7 October 1960, Salon Estimulo, Museo Provincial de Bellas Artes, then in the basement of the Cine San Martin, La Plata. Young artists who thought they were alone as informalists discover each other; that night they move to the Bar Capitol round the corner, which becomes their headquarters -- "Fue nuestro cafetin discepoliano; alli aprendimos, filosofamos, amamos, debatimos, creamos, compartimos y crecimos" (Painceira, quoted in La Pulseada 2013). When the Salon came down they hung the pictures in the bar. Sirabo is named among the members, and Galvez is explicit that he was a co-founder, and that with Horacio Elena and Lalo Painceira he "exhibio los primeros cuadros informalistas en la ciudad de La Plata". The group's motto, per Nessi: "Si, a los demas, ante la indiferencia". The informalists and the concrete/geometric artists shared Cartier's classes at Bellas Artes and did not speak to each other: "Ambos colectivos manteniamos posiciones sectarias y enfrentadas. Sin posibilidad de dialogo" (Painceira). The irony for Sirabo's biography is that he began on the informalist side of that sectarian divide and ended as a geometric constructivist -- the crossing is the story of this study.

From 1960 the dated record is exhibitions and shows: Grupo Arte de Hoy in La Pampa and San Luis, 1960; Museo Nacional de Lima and MAMBA, 1961; the joint Grupo Si + Grupo de los Elefantes presentation, 7 October 1961; the Giratorios at the Premio Braque, MNBA, 1966; three primary-colour elements at the VIII Premio Ver y Estimar, MAMBA, 11 April 1967; La vision elemental, MNBA, 26 September-17 October 1967; an iron construction at Materiales, nuevas tecnicas, nuevas expresiones, MNBA, 20 September-13 October 1968; Cubo with Victor Grippo and Ruben Segura at the VI Bienal de Arte Joven, Paris, 1968; the conference-spectacle at the Camara Argentina de la Construccion, 1969, where his plastic tubes hung from the ceiling, floated over heads, and disappeared; Movimiento Si de La Plata, 1969; Semana de Buenos Aires and the III Festival de las Artes de Tandil, 1970; Muestra Grupo Si and Panorama de la escultura contemporanea, 1971. Then a long quiet stretch in the record: the 1987 donation of Advertencias Magicas II to the Museo Municipal de Bellas Artes; a first mention at the Bienal Chandon, 1989, the only dated prize found; a text in the Grupo Escombros materials, 2004; Isasmendi y Sirabo at MACLA, 2007, the year of the only dated painting in the catalogue, Construccion del simbolo; the 2011 Pettoruti conversation; the 2012 MACLA catalogue, "Actualmente vive y trabaja en City Bell". That is the last confirmed living trace found in any leg.

The doctrine, in his own words from the 2011 conversation (Galvez's notes are explicitly not a verbatim transcript): geometric abstraction "tiene aspectos similares a un codigo, patrones y regularidades que se pueden reiterar e identificar facilmente"; he wants to "construir un lenguaje propio"; "menos es mas". On the Western viewer's discomfort: "Cuando vemos un banco nos cuesta verlo como obra de arte, porque somos occidentales... El objeto solo existe como abstracto extrano. Uno siente un extranamiento... El artista vuelve a sus origenes, pinta como barbaro en una epoca de barbaros. Eso es el arte contemporaneo."

## M1 -- Flat achromatic figure-ground

Galvez's first formal group, Figs. 1-2: combinations of rectangles and squares in black, white and grey, high contrast, joined into one figure on a plain ground. The object "needs to be shown on a smooth ground to realise itself as figure". The figure-ground game is the whole event: the figure snaps in and out against the ground as the eye tries to decide which is the object, and the ground itself becomes active. The palette is the mechanism's first decision -- no colour at all, only value, so that nothing distracts from the snap. This is the "menos es mas" minimalism in its purest form, and it is also the most literal statement of the 1967 manifesto's flat homogeneous colour: the picture is a code of black and white, and the code is the picture.

**Takeaway:** the ground is not a background, it is half the figure. A piece that fills a coloured field with black shapes has missed the mechanism; the ground must be plain and smooth, the figure must be one high-contrast black-white-grey construction, and the viewer must feel the snap between figure and ground.

## M2 -- Ambiguous illusion: tunnel or pyramid

Galvez's second formal group, Figs. 3-4: colour applied in different values; lines of varying thickness closing spaces, or hollow frames superimposed on a base square; orthogonals fleeing to a centre. Fig. 3 reads as lines closing spaces, or as hollow squares over a base square. Fig. 4 reads as a tunnel, or as a pyramid seen from above. The poetic point is the ambiguity of reading: the same drawing flips between two incompatible depths, and the flip never settles. The mechanism is the manifesto's "physical-chromatic-spatial tension" made watchable -- the tension is not in the paint, it is in the eye, which cannot hold both readings at once and keeps switching. Colour enters here only as value: different values of colour, not different hues, drive the depth.

**Takeaway:** the depth must be undecidable, not illustrated. A piece that draws a tunnel has missed the mechanism; the same orthogonals must read as a tunnel and as a pyramid from above, and the flip must never settle.

## M3 -- Real superposition, vertical instability

Galvez's third formal group, Figs. 5-6: geometric structures physically leaning on one another, exhibited vertically so they seem to defy gravity -- "they should fall". The viewer asks how they hold up ("ah!, estan sujetados entre ellas"); ambiguity is lost and unease ("inquietud") is gained. Attested late: Fig. 6 is W02 Construccion del simbolo, 2007, the only dated painting in the catalogue -- but Figs. 1-4 are undated, so this cannot be claimed as a late development rather than a parallel procedure. The mechanism is the move from painted illusion to real instability: in M2 the depth is ambiguous and the picture is safe; here the structures are really leaning, really stacked, and the unease is real, because the eye knows gravity and the picture appears to break it. The title of the 2007 work is programmatic: the building of a symbol, not the depiction of a thing.

**Takeaway:** the lean must be real, not drawn. A piece that paints a leaning tower has missed the mechanism; the structures must be visibly stacked and leaning, exhibited vertically, so the viewer genuinely asks how they hold up.

## M4 -- U / double-T profile constructions

Sirabo's contribution to La vision elemental, MNBA, 1967: constructions in painted wood, "trozos de tramas, construidas con perfiles de U o doble T" -- fragments of a lattice, built from industrial U- or double-T-profile sections. Nessi's account is that Sirabo presented constructions "creando una estructura personal, superando los inconvenientes de realizacion de sus obras anteriores", and that they give satisfaction to the eye "tanto desde un punto de vista como al ser recorridas" -- from one viewpoint, and as they are walked around. The mechanism is the industrial profile as a pictorial unit: the U and the double-T are not shapes drawn on a surface, they are sections of a lattice, and the construction reads differently from every viewpoint, the profile edges catching flat colour differently as the viewer moves. This is the most object-like contribution of the seven signatories in its industrial-profile language, and it is the point where Sirabo's geometry most clearly leaves the panel without leaving the plane's discipline behind.

**Takeaway:** the profile is the unit, and the walk is the picture. A piece that draws U-shapes on a flat ground has missed the mechanism; the construction must be built from real profile sections, and it must read differently from every viewpoint as the viewer walks around it.

## M5 -- Giratorios: repetition mobiles

In 1966 Sirabo built small Giratorios -- small elements turning on a fixed axis, "moviles de repeticion" -- shown at the Premio Braque, MNBA: "the first time he goes out into space with colour and movement". His own words, via Nessi: "Estos trabajos no eran esculturas ni estructuras neutras." The mechanism is repetition made kinetic: one small element, one fixed axis, and the turn does the work -- the same element presents a different silhouette and a different colour face each quarter-turn, so the repetition is the system and the rotation is the variation. He and Puente explicitly distanced this work from US Minimal Art: "los nuestros tendian a neutralizar la expresion, en cambio ellos hacian una sintesis formal". The Giratorios are the hinge of the career: the 1958 spatial investigations become a shown object, and the move from the two-dimensional field into environmental space, dated 1958 in Nessi, finally has a dated work.

**Takeaway:** the turn is the variation. A piece that spins a shape for decoration has missed the mechanism; one element on one fixed axis must present a different silhouette and colour face each quarter-turn, so the repetition itself is the system.

## M6 -- Bilateral symmetry / specular archipelago

The late poetics, quoted in Galvez from Gonzalez de Nava 2007 (Isasmendi y Sirabo, MACLA): "La obra comienza a transmutarse, a transfigurarse en una inesperada metamorfosis simbolica... Aparecen asi las advertencias premonitorias que presagian turbulentas precipitaciones, mientras que su organizacion poetica, basada en austeras simetrias bilaterales, comienza a potenciar su operatividad simbolica... Inmerso en este verdadero 'archipielago' de alternativas especulares, siento haber accedido a una dimension espiritual dentro de la construccion simbolica que ilumina el camino de la 'inescrutable galaxia post moderna.'" The mechanism is austere bilateral symmetry as a symbolic operator: mirrored halves the eye bounces between, finding the symmetry and then finding where it breaks; the work reads as a sign, not a picture -- "advertencias premonitorias", premonitory warnings, a symbolic programme, not a depiction. The title Advertencias Magicas II belongs to this family: the magic warnings are warnings, a recording, a sign addressed to the future. The eye's work is the bounce: left half, right half, the break, the return.

**Takeaway:** the symmetry is a sign, not a decoration. A piece that mirrors a pattern for balance has missed the mechanism; the mirrored halves must be readable as a warning, and the eye must find where the symmetry breaks, because the break is the message.

## M7 -- Line as a cut in space

With Sitro he had "salido de la pintura especifica para entrar en el espacio" -- left specific painting to enter space -- not traditional sculpture but hanging pieces, minimal elements, "un hierro pintado, muy finito que recortaba el espacio, es como si se trabajara con lineas en vez de con formas": a very fine painted iron that cut space, as if working with lines instead of forms. Ribbons developing in space, undulating forms, great circles, "una recta inmensa de colores rojo y amarillo, un circulo rojo... otro amarillo que se entrecruzaban y salian". The mechanism is the line set free from the page: a coloured line draws itself through the air, and the viewer's eye follows it like a drawn stroke that has escaped. The 1969 Camara Argentina de la Construccion spectacle is the mechanism at public scale: his plastic tubes hung from the ceiling, then floated in the air over heads, and finally disappeared. The line is not a mark on a surface; it is a cut in the room, and the room is the surface.

**Takeaway:** the line must leave the page. A piece that draws a coloured line on a ground has missed the mechanism; the line must cut through real space, and the eye must follow it the way it follows a stroke, through the air.

## M8 -- The quiet object against the rushing public

W06 Expansion Urbana, acrylic on wood, about 240 x 240 cm, installed in the entrance hall of the Terminal de Omnibus de La Plata, photographed by Galvez in 2011: a large, simple object hung in a hall where travellers rush past under heavy stimulation; its "sencillez" asks the passer-by to stop and pay attention -- a deliberate contrast between "un tumulto veloz, alocado y contradictorio" and a single quiet object. The mechanism is refusal as attention: in a stream of movement, one still geometric object arrests the eye precisely because it does not compete with the speed around it. The scale is the point -- 240 cm in a bus terminal is not a painting hung for contemplation, it is an object placed in a current, and its stillness is what makes it visible. This is the only Sirabo work with a documented public, architectural setting and the only one Galvez saw in place.

**Takeaway:** the stillness is the action. A piece that adds motion to a busy scene has missed the mechanism; one large, simple, still geometric object must sit in a stream of movement and arrest the eye by refusing the speed around it.

## Evolution: informalism, the grid, the object, the terminal

1958-60: figurative beginnings reported only by the unreliable commercial sites, then the first informalist paintings in La Plata with Elena and Painceira -- matter-heavy surfaces, industrial and discarded materials mixed into paint, a collective, gestural, material phase, the opposite of what follows. From about 1964: the decisive, lifelong turn to geometric constructivism, codified in the 1967 manifesto -- non-illusionist image, flat homogeneous colour, simple concise forms, physical-chromatic-spatial tension, the object sufficient unto itself. Galvez is explicit that the three formal procedures of the mature work are a typology, not a chronology: flat achromatic figure-ground, colour and ambiguous illusion, real superposition and vertical instability -- only one of his six images could be dated (Fig. 6 = W02, 2007), so no dated stylistic sequence can be built for the easel paintings. The scale progression is the evolution that can be claimed: easel painting, to constructed object (Giratorios 1966, Cubo 1968, iron Figura espacial), to architectural and public scale (Expansion Urbana, about 240 cm, bus terminal). The constants throughout are bilateral symmetry, the mirror/specular, the sign rather than the image, and the deliberate provocation of the viewer's perception -- figure/ground, ambiguous depth, apparent instability. Galvez's summary: "Despojada de iconografia, la obra de Dalmiro Sirabo contiene la esencia del arte, manipula el lenguaje plastico, para estimular perturbadoramente nuestros sentidos."

## Aesthetic logic

Sirabo's aesthetic is the logic of the sign that admits the eye's confusion. Pick one simple geometric form, so simple it cannot be the subject, and let perception be the subject instead: the figure that snaps against its ground, the depth that flips between tunnel and pyramid and never settles, the structures that lean and should fall, the symmetry that holds until the eye finds where it breaks. Then let one rule -- "menos es mas" -- govern everything, and let the work be a code: geometric abstraction "tiene aspectos similares a un codigo, patrones y regularidades que se pueden reiterar e identificar facilmente", and the artist's job is to "construir un lenguaje propio". The geometry is minimal because it is made by a hand that wants few resources and a clear sign; the colour is flat and homogeneous because a flat colour activates a plane without illustrating anything; the system is symbolic because the bilateral symmetries and the specular archipelago turn a picture into a warning, a "dimension espiritual dentro de la construccion simbolica". That is why the work keeps returning to the same move in new scales -- acrylic on wood, acrylic on canvas, painted-wood profiles, turning mobiles, fine painted iron, a 240 cm panel in a bus terminal: the scale changes, the sign does not. A Sirabo that paints a clever geometric pattern has not simplified him; it has kept the shapes and sacked the perception. The ground must be plain, the depth must be undecidable, the lean must be real, the line must leave the page, and the symmetry must break somewhere, because the break is the message.

The contrast with Puente is the study's spine, and it is a contrast inside one friendship, between two men of the same city who shared a group, a manifesto and a critic. Puente's generator is a module repeated until it becomes an object, then a colour rule that generates unlimited objects, then a textile system. Sirabo's generator is a sign repeated until it becomes a language: the figure-ground snap, the ambiguous depth, the leaning stack, the mirrored warning. Puente's depth comes from generation, the object built by a rule the viewer can follow; Sirabo's depth comes from perception, the eye caught in a reading it cannot settle. Puente went back to the Andes and found his system already there, tied in string and woven in wool; Sirabo stayed on the plane and found his system in the eye itself, in the flip, the lean and the break. Both asked how a flat painting becomes an object, and Puente answered by making a system that makes the object, Sirabo by making a sign that makes the viewer stop. That is why the two studies belong together, and why Sirabo is the complement, not the follower: same generation, same city, same rooms, the same 1967 manifesto -- and from that shared room, two opposite mechanisms.
