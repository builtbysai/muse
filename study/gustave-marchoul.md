# Gustave Marchoul: Study Notes

**Site:** Gustave Marchoul, born 17 May 1924 at Liege, Belgium, died January 2015 at Bray (Binche) -- Belgian master engraver (etching, burin, maniere noire, aquatint, heliogravure, woodcut, lithography), painter, tapestry-cartoon author, editor and teacher. He studied drawing at the Academie royale des Beaux-Arts de Mons, winning a prize of excellence, and learned engraving self-taught, without a teacher. He met the young Gabriel Belgeonne in 1964 and taught him the rudiments of the craft; Belgeonne replaced him at the Mons academy when Marchoul moved to Brussels, where he held the engraving and book-illustration workshop at La Cambre from 1966 to 1989, then ran book-illustration workshops at the Musee royal de Mariemont from 1992. He co-founded Cap d'Encre (1963 or 1964 -- the sources split) and the Artistes de Thudinie (1956), created the Editions de la Grippelotte, and was elected to the Royal Academy of Belgium (corresponding 1983, member 1992, emeritus 2004). The Centre de la Gravure et de l'Image imprimee at La Louviere holds more than a thousand of his works -- 1,035 on its own artist page -- and in 2025 showed him as a teacher, in "Celles & Ceux de Marchoul", surrounded by his pupils and his family (C4301, C4303, C4401, C4412). The death day is disputed -- 2 January per the French Wikipedia body text against 5 January per its infobox and Connaitre la Wallonie -- and no single day is stated in this study (C4301, C4302).

## The life: the engraver who learned alone, then taught a generation

Marchoul is the mirror image of his pupil Belgeonne, and the pair is the point of the chain. Belgeonne abandoned painting for engraving and spent sixty years subtracting toward emptiness. Marchoul never had a teacher to abandon: he studied drawing at Mons, won a prize of excellence, and discovered engraving self-taught (C4301, C4303, C4307). Then he spent sixty years accumulating -- every technique in turn, colour suppressed and then unleashed, and a teaching line that runs Marchoul to Belgeonne (1964, the rudiments) to Penelle (La Cambre 1994-1999, under Belgeonne's direction) to Penelle's own students at La Cambre 2012-2019 (C4406, C4411). He settled at Thuin in 1954 with his wife, the painter-engraver Blanche Gillot, co-founded the Artistes de Thudinie in 1956, joined Hainaut Cinq and founded Cap d'Encre in 1964 -- the year he met Belgeonne -- taught briefly at Mons in 1965 (single-sourced to the Hainaut agenda quoting the Centre's director, C4304), then held La Cambre's engraving and book-illustration workshop from 1966 to 1989, a span now verified beyond the Hainaut culture page by French Wikipedia, the Centre's own artist page and the Hainaut agenda (C4301, C4303, C4304), satisfying the #610 flag. From 1992 he ran book-illustration workshops at the Musee royal de Mariemont, in the Atelier du Livre (C4301, C4303, C4307). Whether he founded the Atelier du Livre or joined it is not stated in any source read; he is not its founder in this study. Likewise, no source read says he co-founded the Centre de la Gravure: that claim belongs to Belgeonne, and Marchoul's documented relationship to the Centre is as its most-collected artist and the subject of its 2025 teaching exhibition (C4303, C4403).

The career arc, in the Centre's own periodisation: a painter first, drawing on Fauvism and Expressionism, then abstraction (C4301); "magisterial" abstract etchings from the 1960s (C4301); from the 1970s to the end of the 1980s mainly wood engraving, treating his subjects with a form of "visionary realism", then more poetically, through interior landscapes (C4301, C4303); the luminous, colourful woodcut period running from the end of the 1970s to 1989, divided into four series -- Ciel et Terre, Ciel et Pluie, Monts et Brumes, Terre et Nue (C4303); from the 1990s a turn nettement plus mystique, Cross, Calvary and Resurrection inside the landscape, with lithography returning strongly (C4301, C4303). As editor he created the Editions de la Grippelotte (C4301, C4302); no source read dates its founding or lists its publications. He took part in print biennales and triennales and won international prizes, notably at Ljubljana, Lugano, Venice, Florence and Fredrikstad -- single-sourced to French Wikipedia, no prize years or names given in any source read (C4301). The Royal Academy dates -- corresponding member 6 January 1983, member 30 April 1992, emeritus member 7 October 2004 -- are likewise single-sourced to French Wikipedia (C4301).

## M1 -- A technique per decade: mastery as the method

The first mechanism is the one the sources name outright: "Tous les specialistes s'accordent a reconnaitre a Gustave Marchoul la maitrise de toutes les techniques de la gravure" -- etching, soft varnish, wood engraving, lithography, burin, drypoint and aquatint (C4302, C4301). The catalogue shows what mastery means in practice: each technique dictates its decade. Etching carries the 1950s -- the numbered run Eau-forte n01 through n08, 1954-1956, interlocking dark blocks and white voids (W02, C4353, C4352). Burin, maniere noire and aquatint carry the black 1960s -- Nuit de feu (W04, 1962), Vague (W05, 1965), Vespree de la mort (W06, 1967), Vont-ils venir au jour ? (W07, 1968). Heliogravure arrives in 1975 and lets a photographic landscape into the plate for the first time (W09, C4354). Woodcut carries the 1980s -- Puy d'aurore (W11, 1982), Or crepusculaire (W12, 1983), Greve verdoyante (W13, 1984), Pluie d'or (W15, 1986), Vent pourpre (W17, 1988). Lithography returns in the 1990s for the mystical turn (W18, W19, C4356). The mechanism is not versatility as a boast; it is a procedure: learn a technique completely, let it set the decade's look, then move on and let the next one set the next. The sheet stays near 60 x 45 cm for forty years and editions stay tiny (mostly 7-15); what changes is what the plate holds, and the plate holds whatever the current technique can do that the last one could not.

**Takeaway:** master one technique per decade and let it set the look. Same sheet size, same small edition -- the change is in the tool, and the tool is the period.

## M2 -- Colour suppressed, then unleashed: the colour arc

The second mechanism is the variable that moves most across the catalogue: colour. It is present from the start -- Eau-forte en 2 couleurs (W03, 1958) is abstract in red and blue, overlapping translucent planes with a solid red disc, a red border framing the plate (C4353) -- because this is a painter turned engraver, Fauvist and Expressionist before abstract (C4357). Then it is suppressed through the black 1960s: the "magistral" etchings are black and sepia, night titles, faces emerging from darkness (W04-W07). Then it is unleashed in the 1980s woodcuts: green hatched mountain under an orange-pink dawn sky (W11), horizontal bands of orange and red with white scratch marks (W12), green, blue and white hatched strokes like water over a shore (W13), gold rain lines across a green rock (W15), red and orange turbulent sky over green (W17). Then it is transfigured into gold in the 1990s: a white cross on a gold and ochre ground of architectural blocks (W19). The arc is the opposite of Belgeonne's, whose colour stays restrained -- black, sepia, ochre -- for sixty years while scale and polarity move. Marchoul's colour is a held breath and a release: present, suppressed, unleashed, transfigured. The editions of the woodcut decade are the smallest in the catalogue (7-12), so the hottest colour arrives in the fewest copies.

**Takeaway:** let colour be a held breath. Suppress it for a decade so its return reads as weather, not decoration -- and when it comes back, let it be gold.

## M3 -- The interior landscape in four series: nature as haiku titles

The third mechanism is the woodcut decade's subject: the "paysage interieur", the interior landscape, organised into four named series -- Ciel et Terre, Ciel et Pluie, Monts et Brumes, Terre et Nue (C4303). The Centre's summary dates the luminous, colourful period to the end of the 1970s to 1989 and attributes it to intensive woodcut use, driven by a passion for nature and an interest in the arts of the Far East (C4303). The titles do the work of haikus: Vent et nuee, Aube d'argent, Puy d'aurore, Orage et sable (C4307, C4359); Pluie et gel, Pluie du pelerin, Pluies aux Riezes, Pluie et bruine d'or, Pluie de l'Assomption, Pluie de l'espoir (C4352). The woodcut hatching does the work the etching cross-hatching did in 1954 -- the same hand, a different tool, and now in green (W13, C4355). The mountain is built from woodcut hatching, the sky from flat inks (W11, C4354); dusk is stacked bands of colour, no mountain, no sky line, only the light itself (W12, C4355). The series titles name elements -- sky, earth, rain, mountains, mists, clouds -- and the individual titles name moments -- dawn, dusk, rain, wind. A series in Marchoul's hands is not a set of variations; it is a weather system being walked through, one sheet per condition. Note the catalogue trap, flagged by leg 2: "Ciel et terre" (W14, 1985) in the Centre listing is an offset exhibition poster for the series show at Louvain-la-Neuve, not a woodcut from the series -- the series' prints carry individual titles, and the series title survives in the listing as a poster (C4355, C4352).

**Takeaway:** name the series after elements and the sheets after moments. Sky and earth are the system; dawn, rain and wind are the weather passing through it.

## M4 -- Faces half-born from black: maniere noire as emergence

The fourth mechanism is the black decade's image logic: forms that are not drawn but emerge. Vont-ils venir au jour ? (W07, 1968), burin and maniere noire, trial proof: ghostly pale faces and bodies barely emerging from a black ground, dreamlike and crowded -- the question in the title, will they come into the light, is the image itself (C4353). Vespree de la mort (W06, 1967), aquatint, etching and soft varnish: nude female figures emerging from a black aquatint ground, a bright white patch glowing at the centre (C4353). Nuit de feu (W04, 1962): a near-black vertical mass, dense black hatching with pale scratched vertical marks, like an eye or a slit cut in darkness (C4353). The night titles cluster here -- Nuit me couvre, Nuit m'etreint (1960), Oh nuit obscure !, Lueurs de la nuit, Fugacite incandescente de la nuit (1961), Nee de la nuit (1962, three states) (C4352). The mechanism is emergence, not depiction: the black is laid down first and the figure is what the black fails to keep. It is the "realisme visionnaire" the sources name for the period (C4357) -- visionary because the figure is a vision out of darkness, realist because the darkness is the ground everything stands on. The same emergence returns at the other end of the career, inverted: in the 1990s the white cross stands on gold, the light emerging from the colour instead of the dark (W19).

**Takeaway:** lay the black down first and let the figure be what escapes it. A face that is half-born from darkness is a question -- will it come into the light -- and the question is the image.

## M5 -- The diagonal shaft of light: one line that cuts the sheet

The fifth mechanism is a compositional device that recurs across forty years: a single diagonal that cuts the sheet and carries the meaning. Vague (W05, 1965), aquatint and etching, 100 cm wide: sweeping black gestural loops and whorls across a pale grey ground -- the wave as a calligraphic swirl, the black no longer a mass but a stroke (C4353). Solstice d'hiver (W09, 1975), burin, woodcut and heliogravure: an industrial crane or bridge cuts diagonally across a cloudy sky, pale sepia ground, and in the wide lower margin a faint white line drawing of faces (C4354). Voix de l'Espoir (W16, 1987), woodcut and lithogravure: a mountainous landscape cut by one dramatic diagonal beam of light from upper left to lower right, dark peaks below, hatched sky above -- the voice of hope is a shaft of light (C4355). The diagonal is the one element that survives every change of technique and palette: the swirl in 1965, the crane in 1975, the beam in 1987. In each case it does the same job -- it divides the sheet into two conditions (dark/light, mass/sky, peaks/voice) and it moves, so the eye follows it. The crane diagonal of 1975 is the light-beam diagonal of 1987 with the subject changed; the mechanism is the line, not the thing it depicts.

**Takeaway:** cut the sheet with one diagonal and let it carry the meaning. A swirl, a crane, a beam of light -- the line is the same; only the subject changes.

## M6 -- The poster as print: flat colour and lettering

The sixth mechanism is the print that leaves the gallery and announces something. Zabele (W08, 1971), linocut poster: a flat yellow face on a lavender ground, bold black text -- "JEUDI 15 MAI (ASCENSION) a 18.30 H. / ZABELE / LES ARTISTES DE THUDINIE" -- a poster for his own Thudinie artists' group, printed by l'Imprimerie G. Gracia, Thuin (C4354). Ciel et terre (W14, 1985), offset poster: a blue-purple hatched sky over a pale ground, the title in white, announcing the series exhibition at Louvain-la-Neuve, 3-24 February 1985 (C4355). The poster is Marchoul the organiser and editor, not Marchoul the solitary printmaker: flat colour, lettering, a date and a place, the work as a public announcement. It is also the mechanism that keeps the series honest -- the poster for "Ciel et Terre" is the series title made public, and the individual woodcuts are the series made private. The flat yellow face of 1971 and the hatched sky of 1985 are the same move in different decades: take the current technique, flatten it to announcement, add the words. Editions are not the point here; the poster is printed to be seen by strangers, not collected in 7-15 copies.

**Takeaway:** make a poster of the series and let it announce. Flat colour, bold lettering, a date and a place -- the print as a public sentence, not a private sheet.

## M7 -- The book with a writer: the print answers the text

The seventh mechanism is the artist book, and it is where the teacher and the editor meet. Warum krieg ? (W20, 1996), a book with the writer Willy Bal, woodcut on Gekko Hodomura paper, edition 30: the inspected photograph shows the cover, the calligraphic title "Warum Krieg" in black script across cream paper, a red line drawing of figures below -- the woodcut itself is inside the book, not visually inspected, but the book form, not the single sheet, is the work (C4356). The later Centre holdings run to 2008 and are mostly further books -- with Roger Foulon (Jardins / Astres, 1998; La maison de feuilles, 1999; ...je cherche tes faces seigneur..., 2000; Passion (3), 2002), with Andre Lanotte (Figure figures, Faces, 1998-1999), and the 2001 homage to Rembrandt's "Hundred Guilder Print" (C4352). The last large group is Nuages (2005, at least 23 numbered sheets), and the last dated works are Non titre and Capritraits (2008) (C4352). The book changes the print's job exactly as it did for Belgeonne, but from the other side: Belgeonne's print answers a poem across a spread; Marchoul's print shares a book with a writer across a career, and the collaboration is the late manner. He ran the Editions de la Grippelotte as editor (C4301, C4302) and the Mariemont Atelier du Livre workshops from 1992 (C4301, C4303) -- the book is not a format he visited, it is the room he built and taught in. The career ends where the book began: making prints with writers, and looking at clouds.

**Takeaway:** end the career in books with writers. A print alone is a sheet; a print in a book is a voice in a conversation, and the conversation outlasts the technique that made it.

## M8 -- The cross on gold: the mystical turn, and two Taus

The eighth mechanism is the late turn, and it is the polarity flip of M2 carried to its end. After 1990 his inspiration turns nettement plus mystique -- Cross, Calvary, Resurrection -- without abandoning the mountains and abysses, and lithography comes back in force (C4301, C4303). Vers la Resurrection glorieuse (W19, 1993), woodcut and lithography: a white cross stands on a gold and ochre ground of architectural blocks, a pale blue scalloped pattern at the bottom -- the cross as architecture, the resurrection as gold light (C4356). The mountains of the 1980s have become blocks, the sky has become gold, and the valley titles of 1993 -- Vallee du Jugement, Vallee de Josaphat, Vallee de l'espoir -- name the Last Judgment landscape outright (C4352). Tau (W18, 1991), woodcut: pale and near-abstract, cream and yellow circles, dark green wood-grain arcs, a rose disc, a grey band across the top -- the tau as interlocking curves on white paper (C4356). And here the chain closes on itself: Belgeonne made a "Tau" in the same year, 1991 -- a single 122.5 x 79.8 cm sheet of two huge black gestural masses on cream paper, a monumental lithogravure, the clearest statement of his calligraphic turn (C4158 in study #610). Same title, same year, opposite polarity: the master's pale colour woodcut against the pupil's monumental black lithogravure. No source read links the two works; the coincidence is recorded, not explained (C4356). The mechanism is the turn itself: the landscape vocabulary kept, the subject changed to the cross, the colour transfigured into gold -- and the pupil, in the same year, answering the same title in black.

**Takeaway:** let the late work keep the vocabulary and change the subject. Mountains become blocks, sky becomes gold, and the cross stands where the mountain stood -- and if your pupil answers the same title in the opposite polarity, that is the teaching, visible.

## The aesthetic logic: accumulate, then transfigure

Marchoul's logic is accumulation carried across six decades, and the career reads as a single sentence with the colour arc as its grammar. He learns engraving alone, without a teacher, and then spends his life being the teacher he never had -- first informally, in the 1964 meeting that set Belgeonne on the engraver's path, then formally, twenty-three years at La Cambre, then at Mariemont, then in the 2025 show that presents him surrounded by the people he formed. The work follows the same accumulation: faces in 1948, geometry in 1954-58, black masses and night in 1960-68, posters and mixed media in 1971-75, colour woodcut landscapes in 1981-88, crosses and gold in 1990-93, books and clouds in 1997-2008 (C4352, C4357). The constant is not a motif but a procedure -- master every technique in turn and let each dictate its decade -- and the variable that moves most is colour: present in 1958, suppressed through the black 1960s, unleashed in the 1980s woodcuts, transfigured into gold in the 1990s. Against Belgeonne the contrast is sharp and it is the point of the pair: Belgeonne subtracts toward emptiness, the white of the paper doing more work every decade; Marchoul accumulates techniques and colour, and his late work is fuller, not emptier -- the cross on gold, the book with a writer, the clouds in twenty-three numbered sheets. The honest summary is the Centre's: a luminous, colourful period driven by a passion for nature and an interest in the arts of the Far East, divided into four series (C4303) -- and behind it, the teacher's line, Marchoul to Belgeonne to Penelle, which is the chain this study series is walking.
