## The life: seminarian, then the body as material

Michel Journiac was born on 7 October 1935 in Paris (14th arrondissement) and died on 15 October 1995 in Paris (13th arrondissement), aged 60, of a cerebral haemorrhage at home -- not of AIDS, as rumour claimed. At the age of ten he lost his younger brother, an event the French Wikipedia presents as a central motif of his later production (single-source, via the 2004 Strasbourg catalogue chronology). He studied theology (scholastic philosophy) at the Institut catholique de Paris and aesthetics at the Sorbonne; he entered the seminary in October 1956 and abandoned the priestly vocation in 1962. From July 1960 to July 1962 he was a cooperant in Syria, teaching French literature at the college in the Bab Touma quarter of Damascus. (C18808, leg 1 C18600-C18606; synthesis C18756)

His first paintings were Alphabet du corps (1965) and Signes du sang (1966), abstract canvases in which red forms evoke flayed flesh -- organs, viscera, veins and blood, with red dominating. The blood language is set before any action begins, and the suffering body and religious iconography are already the subject. His first exhibition was Parcours-piege du sang in 1968 (Cloitre des Billettes, Paris), though the estate CV lists a 1963 show, "Jean-Michel Journiac Peintures, Galerie des Jeunes" -- a different first name and an earlier date, unexplained in either source; do not state a first-exhibition year without noting this. His first performance, Messe pour un corps, took place at Galerie Daniel Templon in 1969: officiating as a priest, he offered the audience communion in the form of blood sausage made from his own blood. (C18809, leg 1 C18607, C18608, C18619; synthesis C18756)

Through Pierre Restany he met the critic Francois Pluchart, with whom -- Universalis says -- he founded the review ArTitudes (1971-1979); Pluchart became the theorist of art corporel in its pages. Guard: the French Wikipedia (both the Journiac and Pluchart pages) and the preface to Journiac's Ecrits credit the founding to Pluchart alone ("revue qu'il cree en 1971", "creee et animee de 1971 a 1977 par Francois Pluchart"). The safe form is: Pluchart founded artTitudes in 1971, Journiac was its central artist and a frequent contributor (his blood-sausage recipe appeared in its first issue, October 1971). The end date is also disputed: 1977 (French Wikipedia, Ecrits preface) vs 1979 (Universalis). In 1971 he built a full-scale guillotine, Piege pour une execution capitale, as a protest against the death penalty. From 1972 he worked in staged photography and travestissement: Hommage a Freud (1972, casting himself as his own father and mother), Piege pour un travesti (1972), Contrat pour un corps (1972) and 24 heures de la vie d'une femme ordinaire (1974, Galerie Stadler). (C18810, leg 1 C18609-C18611; leg 3 C18708, C18724; synthesis C18756, C18770)

Teaching posts are disputed and must not be stated flatly. The French Wikipedia says: between 1972 and 1975 he was charge de cours at Universite Paris-I Pantheon-Sorbonne, then maitre de conferences in art history at the Ecole des beaux-arts de Versailles, and finally professeur plasticien at the Ecole nationale des beaux-arts de Nancy. The Ecrits volume instead describes 24 years, 1972-1995, at Paris I Saint-Charles. These cannot both be literally true as a sequence; the Versailles/Nancy posts may have been concurrent or later, or the Wikipedia sequence may be compressed. Dates for Versailles and Nancy were not found. (C18811, leg 1 C18612, C18619; synthesis C18756)

From 1976 to 1983 he made a series of ritual-actions (Rituel du sang, Rituel pour un mort, Rituel d'identite aleatoire, 1976; Le Vierge-Mere, 1982-1983), followed by Icones du temps present (1988), with his own blood becoming his main material. In 1993 he learned he had generalised cancer; in January 1993 he began his final cycle, Rituel de transmutation, du corps souffrant au corps transfigure (1993-1995), opening with the postal mailing La Monnaie du sang (100-franc banknotes soaked in his own blood, denouncing the contaminated-blood scandal) and dedicated to friends and lovers lost to HIV/AIDS. He published two poetry books, Le Sang nu (1968) and Delit du corps (1978), wrote many articles, especially in ArTitudes, and his collected Ecrits were published by Beaux-Arts de Paris Editions in 2013. (C18812, leg 1 C18614-C18616; synthesis C18756)

His work is held in public collections including the Centre Pompidou-MNAM, the Centre national des arts plastiques, the Maison Europeenne de la Photographie, the Musee d'Art Moderne de Paris, the Musee d'art moderne et contemporain de Strasbourg, MAC VAL, the IVAM (Valencia), the Sammlung Verbund (Vienna) and the Pinault Collection. Major posthumous retrospectives: Michel Journiac, Musee d'Art Moderne et Contemporain de Strasbourg, 19 February-9 May 2004; Michel Journiac, l'action photographique, Maison Europeenne de la Photographie, Paris, 20 April-18 June 2017; Michel Journiac, Galerie Christophe Gaillard with Galerie Loevenbruck, Paris, 19 October-24 November 2018. (C18813, leg 1 C18617, C18618)


## The works: three phases, one trap

The catalogue runs W01-W20, 1965-1995. Four grades A exist (W04, W11, W13, W19); the grades below are B where a good secondary description was read end to end, C where only a bibliographic record exists. The evolution falls into three phases, and the phases are the argument: the trap moves from the institution, to identity, to the body itself. (C18814, leg 2 C18650-C18679; synthesis C18757, C18758)

**W01, "Alphabet du corps" (Alphabet of the Body), 1965** -- B. Journiac's first paintings. Abstract canvases in which red forms evoke flayed flesh: organs, viscera, veins and blood, with red dominating. Described as, with Signes du sang, the signal of "a new pictorial language associating the body with the sacred". The mechanism is pictorial, not performative -- the body is painted from the inside out. (C18815, leg 2 C18651)

**W02, "Signes du sang" (Blood Signs), 1966 (series); "Icone - Signe du sang", 1968** -- B. The 1966 series extends W01: red signs of blood as a sacred alphabet. A 1968 object-version, Icone - Signe du sang, is mixed media and oil on wood panel, 19 11/16 x 15 3/4 x 6 11/16 in. -- by 1968 the "sign" has become a small icon-object, prefiguring the later Icones du temps present (1988). Title spelling varies by source (Signes plural vs Signe singular). (C18816, leg 2 C18652)

**W03, "Parcours - Piege du sang" (Course - Blood Trap), 1968** -- C. Journiac's first exhibition and first installation/action, Cloitre des Billettes, Paris. Listed in the Merode/Gaillard chronology and in the French Wikipedia and illustrated in the Ecrits volume, but no source read gives a description of what the visitor walked through or what the trap consisted of. Title and venue verified; mechanism NOT verified -- do not reconstruct it. (C18817, leg 2 C18653)

**W04, "Les Substituts" (The Substitutes), 1969** -- A. Black-and-white photograph mounted on wood. Two full, naked figures, one anatomically male and one female, their faces circularly excised so individual expression/recognition is removed. Read in detail in Turbulences: the two figures "accept the other's nakedness" and "seemingly exchange their very nudity" -- an early, pre-drag statement of the other-in-the-self that the later travesti works literalise. The cut-out face is the mechanism: identity removed, bodies made interchangeable. Grade A because the image was reproduced and read in detail. (C18818, leg 2 C18654)

**W05, "Piege pour un voyeur" (Trap for a Voyeur), 1969** -- B. Installation, Galerie Marc Martin Malburet, Paris. A human cage of white neon lights built in the gallery, holding a naked actor ("hostage") inside strict Minimalist geometry. Frieze: "it wasn't clear who was more trapped, the naked actor or uncomfortable viewers." Universalis adds the reversal: the gallery becomes a kind of peep-show and the visitor ends up in the position of being seen. Mechanism: the viewer-voyeur is caught by the same apparatus that exhibits the body. The first of the Pieges (traps) -- a title-form Journiac reuses for a decade. (C18819, leg 2 C18655)

**W06, "Messe pour un corps" (Mass for a Body), 1969** -- B. Performance/action, Galerie Daniel Templon, Paris; re-enacted and filmed 1975, Galerie Stadler. His first public action and most famous work. Dressed as a priest (Pierre Cardin jacket), Journiac celebrates a real Mass in Latin -- chalice, hassocks, Bible-reading, full liturgical slowness -- then serves communion as slices of blood sausage (boudin) made from his own blood: three syringes drawn, spiced, lightly grilled. The audience is invited to eat it. Critic Francois Pluchart called it "the first faux-pas in the history of art". Companion object: Recette du boudin au sang humain (1969), a carefully typed recipe (90 cm3 liquid human blood, salt, spices, sausage casing) beside a black-and-white photograph of the bloodletting -- the Mass reduced to a cookery instruction. Mechanism: transubstantiation literalised -- the host is really his flesh/blood, and the spectator becomes a communicant/cannibal. (C18820, leg 2 C18656)

**W07, "La Lessive" (The Laundry), 1969 (Frieze dates it 1970)** -- B. Action/installation, Galerie Daniel Templon. Pieces of clothing labelled with the names of famous artists are sorted into two piles: those "worthy of attention" are whitewashed and hung out to dry; the others are thrown in the laundry bin. Frieze: a clean-up operation as art criticism -- the artist literally launders reputations. Mechanism: sorting + washing + line-drying as a verdict on the art world; the whitewashing prefigures the white-painted objects (guillotine, 1971) and the "fossilised" clothing of the contracts. (C18821, leg 2 C18657)

**W08, "Referendum Journiac" (Journiac Referendum), 1970** -- B. Action / poster campaign + polling station, Galerie Daniel Templon. Journiac holds a referendum with no message to communicate: poster campaign, voter cards, ballot papers, a polling station installed in the gallery, and a formal count (proces-verbal). The only question on the ballot is his own name. Universalis: the exhibition space "transforms into a voting office". The French Wikipedia notes Pluchart originated the idea, with a referendum dated 27 April 1969 -- the action itself is dated 1970 in the gallery chronology. Mechanism: the full bureaucratic apparatus of democracy deployed around an empty question -- the artist as candidate, brand and plebiscite at once. (C18822, leg 2 C18658)

**W09, "Distributeur automatique d'oeuvres d'art" (Automatic Artwork Distributor), 1970** -- B. Vending machine / installation, Salon de Mai, Centre culturel de Saint-Germain-en-Laye. A coin-operated distributor: for five 1-franc coins the visitor buys a mini-work of their choice. His surname in illuminated commercial-sign letters stands on top of the machine's facade. Mechanism: art sold like cigarettes or sweets, the artist's name as the brand on the machine -- the commercial stand (Stand Journiac, 1969, a trade-fair stand advertising the artist as a household brand) pushed to literal automation. (C18823, leg 2 C18659)

**W10, "Piege pour une execution capitale" (Trap for a Capital Execution / La Guillotine), 1971** -- B. Action + installation, American Center / Musee Galliera, Paris; filmed 1979. A three-metre-high guillotine built to Journiac's own measurements, painted immaculate white (his colour for "washing clean" and neutralising ideology). The action follows the stations of the condemned man's journey; Journiac lays pieces of raw meat under the blade so blood drips onto shiny Formica. MAGMA: the work denounces state violence reducing the individual to a "bloodied pile of meat"; it was meant to begin in the street and end in a television broadcast, but was banned from public space and censored by the police. Ten years before France abolished the death penalty (1981). Mechanism: a real death machine, rendered useless-white and fed meat instead of a neck -- the trap is for the state, not the body. (C18824, leg 2 C18660)

**W11, "Hommage a Freud - Constat critique d'une mythologie travestie", 1972** -- A. Photographic transfer on panel, 80 x 60 cm (large version: gelatin-silver prints on canvas, 250 x 200 cm, 4 panels of 110 x 90 cm); first issued as a mail-art postal sending, March-April 1972. Four family-portrait panels in bourgeois-photo / mug-shot style: top left the father Robert Journiac as himself; top right Journiac travesti en Robert, in his father's exact clothes, glasses, grey hair and scarf; bottom left the mother Renee (wig worn after illness, pearl necklace, medal, wool sweater); bottom right Journiac travesti en Renee, in her hairstyle and dress but posed with bourgeois rigidity. Read in detail in Turbulences: the repetition of the parent's exact gestures exposes the citational, imitable nature of gender and of the Oedipal family itself -- a parody of Freud, the family and the portrait, not an homage. Mechanism: drag as exact copy -- the son becomes both parents, and the family photograph becomes evidence that identity is a costume. Grade A because the image was reproduced and read in detail. (C18825, leg 2 C18661)

**W12, "Contrat pour un corps" (Contract for a Body / Body Contract), 1972** -- B. Contract + object; Galerie Stadler, Paris. A legal contract offered to the collector: after your death, Journiac transforms your body into a work of art, in one of three formulas -- (1) the painting wager: skeleton lacquered white; (2) the object wager: skeleton dressed in the collector's own fossilised clothing; (3) the sociological wager: skeleton gold-plated. The exhibited object-form is a gold-lacquered human skeleton (75.5 x 68.5 x 68.5 cm); the contracts also exist as sets of three silkscreens (one on gilded paper), signed and dated '72. Cairn: the contract's sacrificial conditions are "cede your body to the artist. Die." Mechanism: a signature converts a corpse into a collectible -- the ultimate readymade, with death as the delivery date. (C18826, leg 2 C18662)

**W13, "Piege pour un travesti" (Trap for a Transvestite), 1972 (panels dated 1972-1976)** -- A. Photographic series, three black-and-white photographs on Formica mounted on wood + mirror with raised text, 120 x 75 cm each panel. The model Jean-Paul Casanova mimetically reproduces feminine gestures to impersonate female divas -- Greta Garbo (1972), Rita Hayworth (1976) and Arletty among them. Read in detail in Turbulences: each series includes a mirror panel that reflects the viewer, aligning or misaligning their image with the impersonated divas and exposing the cosmetic, prosthetic conditions of the bodily self. Mechanism: gesture copied until it is both perfect and parodic; the mirror traps the spectator into the same impersonation. Grade A because the image was reproduced and read in detail. (C18827, leg 2 C18663)

**W14, "Contrat de prostitution" (Prostitution Contract), 1973** -- C. Installation/contract, Galerie Stadler, Paris. The companion contract to W12, realised as Hommage au Putain inconnu (Homage to the Unknown Whore). Universalis and the Gaillard chronology agree on title, date and venue; no source read describes what the visitor saw or what the contract's clauses were. Do not invent the mechanism: title, date, venue and the "unknown whore" dedication are all that is verified. (C18828, leg 2 C18664)

**W15, "24 heures de la vie d'une femme ordinaire" (24 Hours in the Life of an Ordinary Woman), 1974 (series 1972-1974; reiterated 1994-95)** -- B. Photographic series, gelatin-silver prints; two chapters, Le Quotidien and Fantasmes. Journiac in drag enacts a day of stereotyped feminine gestures compiled from women's magazines: housewife at work, the cover-girl fantasy, the abduction, the whore. Turbulences: the serial photo-story staging anticipates Cindy Sherman's Untitled Film Stills (1977-80) by several years. The Musee d'Art moderne de Paris holds La Putain 1 (1974, black-and-white silver print) from the Fantasmes chapter. Mechanism: a full day's timetable of femininity performed by a man -- gender as a schedule of poses, repeated until the stereotype is visible as a stereotype. (C18829, leg 2 C18665)

**W16, "L'Inceste" (The Incest), 1975 (Frieze dates it 1974)** -- B. Photographic series, 9-photomontage polyptych. Journiac stages himself with his own parents in incestuous role-play, "accomplished under the gaze of the voyeur son" (Les Inrockuptibles): he is suggestively enlaced in his parents' arms. The 9-panel polyptych is titled in the Art Press scan Fils fille amante / Fils garcon amant / Fils voyeur / L'inceste ("Son daughter lover / Son boy lover / Voyeur son / Incest"). Mechanism: the family triangle of W11 collapses into erotic role-play, with the son simultaneously participant and watching voyeur -- the Oedipal trap sprung from inside the family album. (C18830, leg 2 C18666)

**W17, "Rituel du sang" (Blood Ritual), 1976** -- B. Action + photographic transfer on canvas, 73 x 112 cm (image). First of the ritual-actions series (1976-1983). Shown at Galerie Diagrama, Milan, and at Galerie Stadler / FIAC, Grand Palais, Paris (1993 restaging noted in the Gaillard CV). The surviving object is a photographic transfer on canvas of the ritual -- the action itself (blood marking, in close-up photographs "executing the different times" of the rite, per the Art Press retrospective) is known only through its photographic trace, which is Journiac's consistent rule: photography as the completion of the action. Mechanism as far as verified: staged, close-up-photographed blood rites; the exact gestures are not described in any source read -- do not invent them. (C18831, leg 2 C18667)

**W18, "Rituel pour un mort" (Ritual for the Deceased), 1976** -- C. Action, Galerie Armand Zerbib, Paris, with Editions de la Difference. The companion ritual to W17, same year, same ritual series. The brief's suggested date of 1973 is NOT supported: the Merode chronology, the Gaillard CV and Artsy all place it in 1976. No source read describes the action's content beyond the Art Press note that the ritual's successive moments were photographed in close-up. Title, date (1976), venue and series context verified; mechanism NOT verified. (C18832, leg 2 C18668)

**W19, "Action de corps exclu" (Action of the Excluded Body) / "Marquage d'un corps", 1983** -- A. Action, Centre Georges Pompidou, Musee national d'Art moderne, Paris; photograph Marquage d'un corps - Action de corps exclu, 1983-1993, vintage colour silver print, 107 x 74 cm (MUCEM collection). Journiac publicly brands his own arm with a red-hot iron in the shape of a triangle -- the Nazi camp mark for homosexuals. Read in detail in Turbulences: the close-up of the branded arm, face omitted so the subject generalises to anyone, turns a historical mark of exclusion into a literal somatic wound; the scar's blistered skin is the photograph's punctum. The action was repeated in 1993 in the AIDS context, and the image became Marquage au present / Icone de marquage (1993, vintage silver print, 106.3 x 76.7 cm). Mechanism: self-inflicted branding as both wound and signature -- stigma burned in by the stigmatised himself, then exhibited as an icon. Grade A because the image was reproduced and read in detail. (C18833, leg 2 C18669)

**W20, "Rituel de transmutation, du corps souffrant au corps transfigure" (Ritual of Transmutation, from the Suffering Body to the Transfigured Body), 1993-1995** -- B. Cycle of 12 planned stages (actions, mailings, photographs, objects); shown College Marcel Duchamp, Chateauroux; Galerie J. & J. Donguy, Paris; Museo de Arte Contemporaneo, Bilbao. Dedicated to Journiac's friends and lovers lost to HIV/AIDS. It opens with La Monnaie du sang (Blood Money, January 1993): laminated reproductions of 100-franc banknotes soaked in his own blood, mailed out as a denunciation of the contaminated-blood scandal -- "constat du corps annule par l'argent" (the body cancelled by money). Later stages include La banque du corps - Cheque de sang (mailing), Icones des mots et du sang, Image marquee (mailing), lead ingots cast with the ashes of dead friends, and, in 1987's precursor La Mort de l'ami, rituel, the scattering of his friend Pierre N.'s ashes into the Seine from the Pont Marie (after Yves Klein's gold-throwing). Artsy/Palais de Tokyo: blood reverses polarity -- from sacred, vital liquid to emblem of disease and a deadly economy -- and art's function becomes ensuring "the permanence of revolt" and restoring social bonds of friendship, illness and mourning. The cycle was unfinished at his death; he died just before the exhibition Les createurs face au Sida at the Chapelle de la Salpetriere. Mechanism: the same blood, gold and ritual grammar as 1969, now turned into postal circulation, money and ash -- mourning as a transmissible object. (C18834, leg 2 C18670)

## The mechanisms

**M1 -- The piege (the trap).** Build the institution's own machine and let it catch its user. W05 (a white-neon human cage holding a naked actor; Frieze: "it wasn't clear who was more trapped, the naked actor or uncomfortable viewers"), W10 (a three-metre white guillotine fed raw meat instead of a neck), W08 (a referendum with a polling station and a formal count whose only question is his own name), W09 (a vending machine selling mini-works for five 1-franc coins, his name in illuminated letters on top). Watchable behaviour: the machine is built in full working order, a user steps in to use it, and the machine uses them instead -- the cage holds the watcher, the ballot asks only for the artist's name, the guillotine drops on meat. **Takeaway:** Journiac never attacks an institution from outside; he builds its machine faithfully and lets it incriminate itself. (C18836, synthesis C18760)

**M2 -- Transubstantiation literalised.** The host is really his flesh and blood, and the spectator becomes a communicant -- a cannibal. W06 (Messe pour un corps, 1969): dressed as a priest, he celebrates a real Mass in Latin, then serves communion as slices of blood sausage made from his own blood -- three syringes drawn, spiced, lightly grilled -- and the audience is invited to eat it. Companion object: Recette du boudin au sang humain, a typed recipe (90 cm3 liquid human blood, salt, spices, casing) beside a photograph of the bloodletting. Watchable behaviour: a slow liturgy, chalice and hassocks, then the host is served as food and the watcher must decide whether to eat -- the ritual completes only if someone swallows. **Takeaway:** the sacred is not mocked; it is performed until it is literally true, and the truth is what makes it unbearable. (C18837, synthesis C18761)

**M3 -- Drag as exact copy: identity is a costume.** The son becomes both parents, and the family photograph becomes evidence that identity can be imitated. W11 (Hommage a Freud, 1972): four panels -- father as himself, Journiac travesti as his father in his exact clothes, mother as herself, Journiac travesti as his mother -- the repetition of the parent's exact gestures exposes gender and the Oedipal family as citational. W13 (Piege pour un travesti, 1972-76): the model Jean-Paul Casanova mimetically reproduces feminine gestures to impersonate Garbo, Hayworth, Arletty, with a mirror panel reflecting the viewer into the same impersonation. W15 (24 heures de la vie d'une femme ordinaire, 1974): a full day's timetable of stereotyped femininity performed by a man, compiled from women's magazines. Watchable behaviour: a figure copies a pose exactly -- same clothes, same gesture -- and the copy is both perfect and parodic; a mirror panel catches the watcher trying on the same pose. **Takeaway:** copy a gesture precisely enough and the original is exposed as a copy too. (C18838, synthesis C18762)

**M4 -- The contract as work: a signature disposes of a body.** W12 (Contrat pour un corps, 1972): a legal contract offered to the collector -- after your death, Journiac transforms your body into a work of art, in one of three formulas: skeleton lacquered white, skeleton dressed in your own fossilised clothing, skeleton gold-plated. The exhibited object-form is a gold-lacquered human skeleton; the contract's sacrificial conditions are "cede your body to the artist. Die." W14 (Contrat de prostitution, 1973) is the companion contract -- title, date and venue verified; clauses not described anywhere read, so the mechanism rests on W12 alone. Watchable behaviour: a contract is signed on screen, a body is transformed by the chosen formula -- white lacquer, fossilised clothes, gold plate -- and the signature is what does the work; death is the delivery date. **Takeaway:** the signature is the readymade; the body is only what it is signed over. (C18839, synthesis C18763)

**M5 -- Democracy deployed around an empty question.** W08 (Referendum Journiac, 1970): poster campaign, voter cards, ballot papers, a polling station installed in the gallery, a formal count -- and the only question on the ballot is his own name. W07 (La Lessive, 1969/70): clothing labelled with famous artists' names is sorted into two piles -- the worthy are whitewashed and hung out to dry, the rest thrown in the laundry bin; a clean-up operation as art criticism. W09 (Distributeur automatique, 1970) is the commercial twin: art sold like cigarettes. Watchable behaviour: the full bureaucratic apparatus -- posters, ballots, a count, a washing line -- deployed around a question with no content; the machine runs perfectly and produces nothing but a name. **Takeaway:** run the democratic procedure faithfully on an empty question and the procedure itself is indicted. (C18840, synthesis C18764)

**M6 -- Blood as material: marking and branding the body.** W17 (Rituel du sang, 1976): staged, close-up-photographed blood rites; the exact gestures are not described anywhere read, so the mechanism is the rite's photographic trace, not a reconstructed gesture. W19 (Action de corps exclu, 1983, Centre Pompidou): Journiac publicly brands his own arm with a red-hot iron in the shape of a triangle -- the Nazi camp mark for homosexuals -- face omitted so the subject generalises to anyone; the scar's blistered skin is the photograph's punctum. Repeated in 1993 in the AIDS context. Watchable behaviour: a hot iron descends, a triangle brands the arm, the camera holds on the blistering skin -- stigma burned in by the stigmatised himself, then exhibited as an icon. **Takeaway:** the wound is not shown to shock; it is signed -- the brand is both injury and signature. (C18841, synthesis C18765)

**M7 -- Mourning as a transmissible object.** W20 (Rituel de transmutation, 1993-95): a cycle of 12 planned stages dedicated to friends and lovers lost to HIV/AIDS. It opens with La Monnaie du sang (January 1993): laminated reproductions of 100-franc banknotes soaked in his own blood, mailed out as a denunciation of the contaminated-blood scandal -- "constat du corps annule par l'argent" (the body cancelled by money). Later stages: La banque du corps - Cheque de sang (mailing), lead ingots cast with the ashes of dead friends, and the 1987 precursor La Mort de l'ami, scattering a friend's ashes into the Seine from the Pont Marie. Watchable behaviour: a banknote soaked in blood arrives by post; an ingot of lead and ash is cast; ashes scatter from a bridge -- the same blood, gold and ritual grammar as 1969, now turned into postal circulation, money and ash. **Takeaway:** blood reverses polarity -- from sacred liquid to emblem of disease and a deadly economy -- and the work's job becomes keeping revolt and friendship in circulation. (C18842, synthesis C18766)

**M8 -- The cut-out face: identity removed, bodies interchangeable.** W04 (Les Substituts, 1969): two full, naked figures, one anatomically male and one female, their faces circularly excised -- the two figures "accept the other's nakedness" and "seemingly exchange their very nudity". The cut-out face is the mechanism: identity removed, bodies made interchangeable -- an early, pre-drag statement of the other-in-the-self that the later travesti works literalise. Watchable behaviour: two naked figures stand side by side, faces blanked to perfect circles; the bodies swap places, and without faces there is nothing left to tell them apart -- the viewer supplies the identity the work removed. **Takeaway:** remove the face and the body stops being a person and becomes a proposition: anyone could be either. (C18843, synthesis C18767)

Two further mechanisms were evidenced in leg 2 but held out of the eight: M9, the voyeur-son (W16, participant and watcher at once -- the family triangle collapses into erotic role-play with the son simultaneously participant and watching voyeur); M10, white as neutraliser (the white guillotine, the whitewashed laundry, the skeleton lacquered white -- white is Journiac's solvent, it annuls rather than decorates). Both are strong seeds and may be used if the prompt block expands. (C18844, synthesis C18768, C18769)


## The network: Pluchart, Forest, Pane, Fischer, Thenot

The network is dominated by the 1973-74 convergence that produced the Sociological Art Collective -- a convergence Journiac convened and even originated, without ever joining. Francois Pluchart is the strongest tie: complicity from 1968, theorist of art corporel in artTitudes (founded 1971), originator of the cheque and referendum actions, Journiac his "confident le plus proche" (closest confidant); Pluchart initiated the 1973 debate, attended the 1974 meetings, and tried -- and failed -- to federate the same seven artists under a "groupe socio-corporel" label. Fred Forest is the newest direct tie this run: in 1973 Journiac visited Forest's Galerie Germain show, spotted "ART SOCIOLOGIQUE" on a flyer, and proposed forming a group, offering to be the linchpin of meetings at his rue Le Regrattier apartment. Herve Fischer (#683) is a debate partner, meeting co-participant and critical symbiote ("en etroite symbiose avec ses pratiques et avec celles d'autres artistes comme Herve Fischer ou Gina Pane"); no co-authored work with Fischer was found. Gina Pane is a debate partner, meetings participant, and documented naming rival: inside the meetings the two split over the movement's name -- Pane led the "corporel" clan defending the label "avec une rare aprete", while Journiac "se sente a l'etroit dans l'appellation Art Corporel". Jean-Paul Thenot (#684) is a debate partner and meeting co-participant only; no co-authored work was found. Journiac's closest brush with a Collective project was as an exhibitor in the December 1974 "L'art contre l'ideologie" show at Galerie Rencontres, organised by Bernard Teyssedre, which the Collective surveyed with a questionnaire. (C18845, leg 3 C18708-C18712, C18717, C18721, C18722; synthesis C18770-C18772)

Bertrand Lavier and Joan Rabascall now meet the #684 "find a real source" condition for meeting-only names: Lavier via Venice 1976 + Pluchart's "groupe socio-corporel" list; Rabascall via Tate/MACBA ("close to the Sociological Art Group in 1974") + the Dec 1974 show. Dorothee Selz, Thierry Agullo, Jocelyne Herve and Sacha Sosno remain meeting-only. Teyssedre is a critic, not a practicing artist, and a weak generative subject. (C18846, leg 3 C18715-C18718; synthesis C18772)


## Aesthetic logic: the trap, the body, the signature

Journiac's aesthetic is an aesthetic of the trap, and the trap always needs a real machine. He never denounces an institution in words; he builds its apparatus -- the Mass, the cage, the ballot box, the vending machine, the guillotine -- and lets the apparatus do the denouncing by working perfectly on the wrong thing: meat instead of a neck, a name instead of a question, a spectator instead of an exhibit. The body is the bait and the exhibit at once, and the signature is the other machine: a contract that disposes of a corpse, a brand burned into an arm, a recipe that turns blood into sausage. Everything he makes is a procedure with a signature at the end of it, and the procedure is always performed in full, slowly, in the correct order -- the liturgy in Latin, the stations of the condemned man, the count of the votes, the twelve stages of the ritual.

That is why the work survives the move from satire to mourning without changing its grammar. In 1969 blood is a prop -- sausage, served to eat. By 1976 it is a rite, photographed in close-up. By 1983 it is a wound, branded into the arm. By 1993 it is money, mailed through the post, and ash, cast into lead. The material never changes; the polarity does. And the photograph is always the completion of the action, never its record: the action happens so that the photograph can exist, and the photograph is where the trap stays set -- the mirror panel in Piege pour un travesti keeps catching the viewer long after the model has gone home.

A generative piece in his manner should therefore never render a beautiful body and stop; it should build a machine -- a cage, a ballot box, a contract, a brand -- and let the machine run on the watcher. The body is the material, the procedure is the form, and the signature is the point: who signs, who is signed, and what the signature costs. **Takeaway:** build the machine faithfully, feed it the wrong thing, and let the watcher discover they are the thing being processed. (C18847, synthesis C18760-C18767)
