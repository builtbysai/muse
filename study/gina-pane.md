## The life: earth, wound, absence

Gina Pane was born on 24 May 1939 in Biarritz, to an Italian father who made and restored pianos and an Austrian mother; she spent her childhood in Turin with a sister whose name was not found in any source read. She left Italy for Paris in 1961 to study at the Ecole des Beaux-Arts (also Atelier d'Art Sacre); the training end year is unsettled (1963, 1965 or 1966 across sources). Her first works were minimalist metal prisms, often blue -- Structures affrimees (1965-69) -- and geometric abstraction. In 1968 the body entered the work, and she herself distinguished three periods of her evolution: 1968-1971 placing the body in nature; 1970 to the late 1970s the active body in public; late 1970s onward the relationship of the body to the world. (C19108, leg 1 C18900-C18906; synthesis C19051, C19053)

From 1971 the actions are the work: about thirty public actions, all performed silently before gathered audiences, each meticulously prepared, photographed by Francoise Masson from Pane's own diagrams and sketches, and issued as a constat d'action -- a composed panel of photographs, drawings and texts that is itself the artwork, not a mere record. The wound is the instrument: razor, thorn, candle, spiked ladder, broken glass, maggots. The audience is the second instrument: in Le Lait Chaud they break silence and intervene; in Death Control children sing while maggots crawl. She taught painting at Le Mans 1975-1990 (variant 1976-1989, summary-only) and ran a performance atelier at the Centre Pompidou 1978-1979 at Pontus Hulten's request. (C19109, leg 1 C18907-C18910; leg 2 C18973; synthesis C19054, C19057)

From 1979 she stopped performing and sent instructions for others to interpret (Action de Chasse, 1979-81); from 1981 she built the Partitions, in which the body was replaced by its metaphor -- felt, copper, glass, iron -- celebrating its absence, the subjects often martyrs. The Galleria Nazionale puts it plainly: she abandoned her own body for expression due to physical limitations and returned to sculpture with the performative experience behind her. Her last work, La Priere des pauvres et le corps des saints (1989), is nine glass cases sorting relic, instrument and robe per saint. She died in Paris in March 1990, aged 50, of cancer after a long illness; her own summary of the whole trajectory was "All my work belongs to the trajectory: Earth-Sky". (C19110, leg 1 C18911-C18914; leg 2 C18970-C18972; synthesis C19052, C19057)

Her work is held in public collections including the Centre Pompidou, MoMA (Azione Sentimentale), the Musee d'Art Moderne de Paris (Serie b, acquired 1974), the Musee des Beaux-Arts Nantes and MAC VAL (Le Martyre de saint Sebastien, 1984). A Guggenheim holding is asserted but no specific work was identified. Major posthumous retrospectives: "Terre - Artiste - Ciel", Centre Pompidou, 16 February-16 May 2005, curated by Sophie Duplaix; the 2001-02 Hansard/Arnolfini tour (Tate Modern leg); MART Rovereto 2012 (160+ works); MUSAC Leon 2016; MFA Houston 2013. She showed at Documenta VI in 1977. (C19111, leg 1 C18915-C18918; synthesis C19055)


## The works: four phases, one trajectory

The catalogue runs W01-W20, 1968-1989. Thirteen grades A exist; the grades below are B where a good secondary description was read end to end, C where only a bibliographic record exists (none in this set). The evolution falls into four phases, and the phases are the argument: the body moves from nature, to the audience, to the Other, to absence -- and the trajectory is always Earth-Sky. (C19112, leg 2 C18950-C18979; synthesis C19056, C19057)

**W01, "Pierres deplacees" (Displaced Stones), 1968** -- A. Orco valley near Turin; colour photographic sequence. Walking in July 1968, Pane saw a pile of small stones, 0.15-0.2 m, north-facing, moss-covered, set in damp earth, that never received sun or warmth. She moved them one by one to an open, south-facing place. Mennour calls this her "first in vivo act", a spontaneous drive to "right a wrong"; Osart stresses the stones were chosen for precise traits -- northern exposure, moss, moist soil -- then moved south. Mechanism: slow relocation as repair -- the pile thinning in one place and growing in another, with light itself as the payoff. No wound, no audience, only attention and carrying. (C19113, leg 2 C18952)

**W02, "Terre protegee" (Protected Land) I, II, III, 1968-1970** -- A. Installation / in situ action, earth, wood, hemp, stone; black-and-white prints. On a bed of arable earth in the Italian countryside Pane set up 120 wooden structures tied with hemp belts and oriented to the compass points; under each structure -- like a lifebelt -- she placed a small packet of seeds, to protect the richness of the earth. For part II (1970) she lay on the ground, back to the earth, arms crossed, to block aggressions, take possession of the space, and let herself be absorbed by it. For part III (1970) she made a circle of stones to protect the inscription of the title sculpted in the ground. Guard: one Artforum account counts ten cloth straps in part I, aligned like tilled rows and weighted with wooden blocks -- a count that differs from Mennour's 120 structures, so the exact number is not settled. Mechanism: protection by covering and encircling -- seeds hidden under small wooden shelters; a body lying flat as a lid on the ground; a stone ring drawn around words in soil. (C19114, leg 2 C18953)

**W03, "Situation ideale: terre - artiste - ciel" (Ideal Situation: Earth - Artist - Sky), 1969** -- A. Photograph. Pane stands on freshly tilled brown ground against a pale blue cloudless sky: feet planted, hands in pockets, weight resting gently on her left side, right foot slightly forward, a strong vertical against the strong horizontal division of ground and sky. Her own words: "Between two horizontals: earth/sky, I placed my body vertically in order to provoke an ideal situation". Mennour reads it as the programme of her whole work: the artist as intermediary, passeur, catalyst between earth and cosmos. Mechanism: the body as a vertical bar between two horizontals -- a figure who does not move at all, while the image is organised entirely by her stillness. (C19115, leg 2 C18954)

**W04, "Stripe-Rake", 1969** -- A. Participatory installation, 4 sq m of sand and 1 sq m of humus (earth), with a rake made by the artist; homage to Malevich's black square. Visitors were invited to "activate this space" by drawing a network of lines (stripe) with the rake, linking desert sand -- dead matter where nothing grows -- to earth -- living matter, symbol of future crops. In the 2018-19 Mennour rehang it could no longer be handled and became a static artifact, which the Artforum review notes as a loss of its participatory core. Mechanism: the visitor as the actor -- a surface that only becomes a drawing when someone drags a tool across it, and the drawing is a bridge between two states of matter. (C19116, leg 2 C18955)

**W05, "Nourriture - actualites televisees - feu" (Food - TV News - Fire), 1971** -- B. Paris apartment; action filmed on video, documented in photographs. Pane force-fed herself and spat back up 600 grams of raw ground meat, watched the nightly news on television while staring past a nearly blinding light bulb, and extinguished flames with her bare hands and feet. A timeline source dates the video documentation to 24 November, in a Paris apartment. Guard: no step-by-step account of the action's order was found, so the sequence of the three elements (meat, TV, fire) is not established. Mechanism: ingestion and rejection on loop, against a screen -- eat / spit / stare / smother, with the TV as a second, indifferent performer. (C19117, leg 2 C18956)

**W06, "Escalade non-anesthesiee" (Unanaesthetised Climb), 1971** -- A. Pane's Paris studio; no audience; metal ladder with sharp / razor-blade rungs; documented in 69 black-and-white photographs by Francoise Masson plus the ladder itself. Barefoot, in jeans and a plaid shirt, a red rose clenched between her teeth, Pane climbed up and down the spiked metal frame for about 30 minutes, until her hands and feet were so wounded she could not continue. Frieze calls it her first "pain performance". Her own note frames it as "American escalation in Vietnam": physical pain in one or several parts of the body standing for internal, deep, moral pain -- the opposite of an anaesthetised climb. The climb had no audience and the documentation was planned shot by shot in advance with Masson. Guard: the date is April 1971 (TheArtStory/Frieze) vs 26 June 1971 (FR Wikipedia) -- do not print a day. Mechanism: climbing as endurance meter -- each rung costs skin; the climb stops only when the body cannot continue, with the rose in the teeth as the one unbroken, decorative fact. (C19118, leg 2 C18957)

**W07, "Le Lait Chaud" (The Hot Milk), 1972** -- A. Paris apartment; ten-part photographic series; Pane also filmed the audience reaction herself. Dressed in a white shirt, Pane sat with her back to the audience and sliced her back with a razor blade; blood sank into the fabric, red stains on white like a drawing. At another point she faced the audience, laid the razor against her cheek, and blood came to the surface; the silent participants suddenly screamed "No, not the face!" A second account adds that she interrupted the cutting to play with a ball, then started cutting again, until the public prevented her from continuing. Guard: the year 1972 is verified; the 31 March day is a timeline entry on a 1970 page, unverified. The Italian form "Il latte caldo" is a translation, not a separate work. Mechanism: the audience breaking silence -- the cut is slow and the back is turned; the shock is not the wound but the moment the watchers can no longer stay silent and intervene. (C19119, leg 2 C18958)

**W08, "Je" (I), August 1972** -- B. One evening in August 1972, Place aux Oeufs, Bruges; window ledge of a second-floor apartment above a cafe overlooking the square. Pane placed herself on the window ledge between private (inside the flat) and public (the square below). From the ledge she took Polaroid photographs of the domestic scene inside the flat and dropped them to assistants below, who distributed them among the gathering crowd; she also read short texts about self and other, coloniser and colonised. On the ground below the window were fixed the words "Les Autres" (The Others), surrounded by buttons grouped like coins thrown to a street performer. Her own text: by placing her body on the parapet between two zones she had "the power of transposition that shattered the limits of individuality so that 'I' could share with the 'Other'". Guard: Bamford's paper on Je was read via search extracts only, hence B. No standalone Pane work titled "Io sono" was found in any source searched; the nearest "Io" title is W17, Io mescolo tutto (1976). Mechanism: the ledge as a threshold, and photographs as falling objects -- drop, catch, pass on. (C19120, leg 2 C18959)

**W09, "Action autoportrait(s): mise en condition / contraction / rejet" (Self-Portrait(s): Conditioning / Contraction / Rejection), 1973** -- B (thin). Action; venue not stated in the sources consulted. The thinnest entry in the set: the work is listed in the standard action sequence for 1973, and the one concrete description found is that milk in the artist's mouth is mixed with the blood of cut lips or tongue. The three-part subtitle -- conditioning, contraction, rejection -- is the only structural information found; no source read narrates the three phases in order. Mechanism, cautiously: the mouth as mixing vessel -- white milk and red blood meeting in one mouth, then rejected. Do not build more than that from this entry. (C19121, leg 2 C18960)

**W10, "Action Transfert" (Transfer), 19 April 1973** -- B. Portfolio of 16 black-and-white vintage silver prints, each laminated on board, in a blue cloth box. Tied up, Pane desperately tried to drink from a glass of milk, broke the glass, and lapped at the shards with her mouth. All her actions were performed silently before gathered audiences. Guard: no fuller narration of the tying, the breaking, or the lapping order was found. Mechanism: a simple need made impossible, then pursued past breakage -- reach, fail, break, lap; the transfer of milk from glass to mouth by the only route left. (C19122, leg 2 C18961)

**W11, "The Conditioning", 1973** -- A. Performance and photographic documentation; metal bedframe over two rows of burning candles; venue not stated in the sources consulted. Pane entered a room with the bedframe over two rows of burning candles, lay fully clothed on the frame, and did not move or speak for half an hour. She said later: "needless to say, the pain started right away and was very difficult to dominate." The visible evidence was the wringing of her hands. When it ended she stood and softly caressed her skin: "When, half an hour later, I was able to get up, I caressed my body very gently. There was no violence; my body hurt but I could feel my touch". Marina Abramovic re-performed it in Seven Easy Pieces at the Guggenheim in 2005. Mechanism: stillness over heat -- nothing moves except hands wringing and then, at the end, hands caressing; pain readable only in the hands, the smallest moving part carrying the whole signal. (C19123, leg 2 C18962)

**W12, "Azione Sentimentale" (Sentimental Action), 9 November 1973** -- A. Galleria Diagramma, Milan; women-only audience seated in chalk rings with the word "Donna" (Woman) at the centre; seven chromogenic colour prints (MoMA set) / 16-print constat in other accounts. In three rooms -- a black velvet square with a white satin rose, a projected slide of Pane holding red roses, then the performance room -- Pane, dressed in white, entered with a bouquet of red roses, offered and took it back while sitting, standing, and lying on the floor, ending curled like an embryo hugging the bouquet. She then pierced her forearm with eight rose thorns and sliced her palm with a razor blade; the outstretched bleeding hand symbolised love passing from hand to hand. Two voices read letters between mothers and daughters, friends, and lovers; the performance closed with Frank Sinatra singing "Strangers in the Night". She repeated the whole sequence with a bouquet of white roses: the red roses turned white, the white dress was stained red. Pane called it a "projection of an intra space" dealing with the mother-child relationship. Guard: TheArtStory heads this work 1974, but the performance date 9 November 1973 is given by Interalia, Osart and the Galleria Nazionale; 1973 is used here. Mechanism: offer and withdraw, twice, with a colour swap -- red roses become white, white dress becomes red; the bouquet is pushed away and held close; the wound is arranged as a rose, thorns in a row on the arm, palm cut as corolla. (C19124, leg 2 C18963)

**W13, "Action Psyche" (Psyche), 1974** -- A. 27 minutes 32 seconds at the Galerie Rodolphe Stadler, Paris; constat of 25 unique colour photographs, preparatory drawings, and ephemera in a metal case. Pane applied red lipstick directly on a mirror so her face was obscured, then cut the skin below her eyebrows so blood dripped down her eyelids, like tears of blood, evoking Saint Lucy. She blinded herself with bandages, gesticulated with her arms, bound her wrists, clasped symbolic objects -- feathers, coloured balls -- suckled her own breast, and engraved her belly with a cross-shaped incision near the navel, "the location of original connection of fetus and mother". Osart's three-photo Essai version condenses the most agonising phases: eyes closed as tears of blood fall; four lines starting from the navel, the "I" as centre extending outward in four directions. The spelling "Psychique" is a variant; the standard title is Psyche. Mechanism: the face erased, then rewritten in blood -- lipstick on a mirror hides the face; cuts below the brows make blood do the work of tears; a cross is cut at the navel as a centre with four directions. (C19125, leg 2 C18964)

**W14, "Death Control", 1974** -- B. Venue not stated in the sources consulted; only witnesses' accounts and a few close-up photographs survive. Pane lay on the floor and covered her whole body with maggots while a group of children surrounding her sang "Happy Birthday" as the worms crawled through her face, eyes, and ears. The idea: with each birthday you get closer to death; she had told a class of six-year-olds "When you die, your body rots, and maggots eat you. That's it". Pascal Lievre later adapted it, covering herself with cake while children sang. Mechanism: a birthday song over a body being eaten -- stillness plus crawling plus children's singing; celebration and decay in the same frame. (C19126, leg 2 C18965)

**W15, "Action melancolique 2x2x2" (Melancholic Action 2x2x2), 1974** -- A. First performed at Studio Morra, Naples. Melancholy is explored by evoking the relationships of couples in all possible combinations. The wound was made close to the ear, alluding to Van Gogh's gesture. Here the Other appears: beside the "I" represented by Pane there is the "you" represented by the naked back of a girl, with a heart drawn on it, setting the love relationship in a space no longer codified by gender but by the absolute force of sentiment. Pane: "The Action allows others to become aware of the conflict and to pass from an isolated situation to a unifying one". Azione Teorica (1977) was the second performance at Studio Morra; this 1974 action was the first there. Guard: the task's "Melanotte" does not exist as a Pane title in any source searched -- the nearest real title is this one, and "Melanotte" is treated as a garbled form of Melancolique. Mechanism: 2x2x2 as combinatorial couples -- two figures, two roles, two combinations; a wound by the ear; a heart drawn on another's back; pairing and re-pairing, with the second body present as a back, not a face. (C19127, leg 2 C18966)

**W16, "Action Il Caso no. 2 sul ring" (The Case No. 2 on the Ring), 1976** -- B. Performance; constat of five framed panels comprising 20 colour photographs, two sheets of handwritten text, and 20 preparatory drawings. Pane simulated a boxing match of four rounds in which she was the only fighter, alternating self-wounding, gesturing, interacting with her reflection in a mirror, and playing with a toy horse. Like the other actions it was performed silently before a gathered audience. Guard: no round-by-round narration was found, hence B. Mechanism: a solo boxing match against a mirror -- four rounds, one fighter, a reflection as opponent, a toy horse as the incongruous object; shadow-boxing that wounds the boxer, with the mirror returning every blow. (C19128, leg 2 C18967)

**W17, "Io mescolo tutto: Cocaina, Fra Angelico" (I Mix Everything: Cocaine, Fra Angelico), 30 October 1976** -- A. Galleria Comunale d'Arte Moderna, Bologna; first Pane action in a museum. While two youngsters threw a ping-pong ball to each other along a table set against a wall, Pane took a splinter from a shattered pane of glass and used it to draw on her forearm the outline of pieces of a game found on the ground. Shattered glass is an important material for Pane: breaking out of the frame means "going out into the street, into the world, not as mannequins but as flesh and blood". Mechanism: a game that goes on regardless, beside a body drawing with broken glass -- two rhythms side by side: ball back and forth, glass line growing on skin. (C19129, leg 2 C18968)

**W18, "A Hot Afternoon", 1977** -- B. Conceived 1977 for Documenta 6, Kassel; constat of 29 elements (19 photographs and 10 preparatory drawings with texts), now in the Beaux-Arts de Paris collection. No step-by-step narration of the live action was found in the sources read. What is documented is the constat's structure: a highly elaborate structure revisiting her main themes -- wounds, the body, childhood, and the social and cultural obstacles that constrain women or silence their voices. The Beaux-Arts calls it one of her most accomplished performances and one of her final works in body art. Mechanism, cautiously: a summation action -- all her themes staged and examined at once, arranged by a formal rather than narrative logic. Do not invent the live gestures from this entry; the constat's arrangement is the evidence. (C19130, leg 2 C18969)

**W19, "Action de Chasse. C'est la nuit cherie..." (Hunting Action. It Is the Beloved Night...), 1979-1981** -- A. Invited to show in the US in 1979; performed by two interpreters from her instructions (texts and drawings) two years later at Franklin Furnace, New York, then in Lyons; third realisation, the "Partition of the Action", exhibited 1981 at Galleria Multimedia d'Arte Contemporanea, Brescia -- a large mural of 24 colour panels (black, white, blue, red, orange) in charcoal, pastel, and gouache, plus a tiny green wooden house. This is the turning point: after some thirty public actions Pane had decided not to perform any more, so she sent instructions for others to interpret. The mural's night is full of perils: stylised masculine figures wrestle animals, enemy brothers clash in jousting matches crossing knives and arrows, three angels watch, and the little house offers a zone of comfort amid the unleashing of passions. The dialogue she added: "Deep is the night. Terrifying things happen. A woman asks a man: what is all that? He answers: It's the night sweetheart..." Mechanism: the artist absent, the instructions performing -- two interpreters carry out written directions; the work survives as a 24-panel night and one small green house as refuge; hunting and combat in panels, with a single small shelter that never moves. (C19131, leg 2 C18970)

**W20, "La Priere des pauvres et le corps des saints" (The Prayer of the Poor and the Body of the Saints), 1989** -- A. Pane's last work; exhibited posthumously 1990 at CAC Passages, Troyes; solemn sculptural Partition, nine horizontal glass cases in three groups of three, brass, glass, felt, forged iron, wood. Three groups of three cases honour three saints Pane had paid homage to before -- Saint Sebastian, Saint Francis, Saint Laurence. The central cases show the saints' bodies brushing the surface of their brass sarcophagi. The left cases show the instruments of martyrdom: a forged iron bow bristling with arrowheads (Sebastian), a rusted iron Tau cross (Francis), charcoal-blackened wood for the gridiron (Laurence). The right cases show the saints' robes in symbolic felt colours: red for Sebastian's pierced body, brown for Francis's poverty, sky blue for Laurence's ascesis and air. Words from the "prayer of the poor" -- an invocation for simple needs -- are engraved in brass and glass, recalling that the saints gave their lives to bring salt, honey, and fire to others. Mechanism: the body replaced by its traces -- no performer, no wound made live; instead nine cases sort relic, instrument, and robe; a viewer reading across three cases per saint -- body, weapon, clothing -- and assembling the martyr from parts. (C19132, leg 2 C18971)

Beyond the 20: Structures affrimees (1965-69), minimalist metal prisms, often blue, Pane's first works; Acqua alta / Pali / Venezia (1968-70), her first large environment -- Duralinox posts rising from a pool of dirty water, with the words Acqua alta, pali, Venezia spread across floor, walls, and ceiling; Continuation d'un chemin de bois (1970), extending a rural byway with wooden planks to make communication possible, and J'ai jete 4 dessins dans le torrent Chisone (1969), throwing four drawings into a river to reach the sea; Azione Teorica (1977), the second Studio Morra performance, on the frailty and vital energy of the body; Action Little Journey I (1977), named in the same breath as Sentimentale for its razor work, but not described in any source read; L'Homme a la branche verte... L'homme indien en priere, version 3 (1986-88), copper and iron panels worked as if they were her own body -- "copper is a live material, as soon as you touch it you can see the traces". ## The mechanisms

**M1 -- Slow relocation as repair.** A small pile is carried stone by stone from shade into sun; seeds are hidden under small wooden shelters; a body lies flat as a lid on the ground. W01 (stones moved one by one from a damp north-facing pile to an open south-facing place, "to right a wrong"), W02 (120 wooden structures over seed packets; a body lying flat as a lid; a stone ring around words in soil). Watchable behaviour: a quantity thinning in one place and growing in another, light itself as the payoff. **Takeaway:** repair does not need a wound; moving small things slowly, in the right direction, is already the work. (C19134, synthesis C19059)

**M2 -- The body as a vertical between two horizontals.** The figure does not move at all while the image is organised by her stillness -- ground below, sky above, body as the join; or a body motionless over heat, pain readable only in the hands. W03 (standing vertical between earth and sky, "to provoke an ideal situation"), W11 (half an hour motionless on a metal bedframe over burning candles; hands wringing, then caressing). Watchable behaviour: stillness as structure, the smallest moving part carrying the whole signal. **Takeaway:** when nothing moves, the smallest movement becomes the whole event -- put the signal in the hands, not the body. (C19135, synthesis C19060)

**M3 -- The visitor as the actor.** Raking draws lines that join dead sand to living soil; the surface only becomes a drawing when someone drags a tool across it. W04 (visitors rake lines linking dead sand to living earth; in the 2018-19 rehang it could no longer be handled, and the Artforum review notes the loss of its participatory core). Watchable behaviour: a bridge drawn between two states of matter, by the viewer's hand. **Takeaway:** the work is unfinished until a hand enters it -- the tool, not the artist, finishes the drawing. (C19136, synthesis C19061)

**M4 -- Endurance as a meter.** Climbing a ladder that wounds until the body cannot continue; lying over candles for half an hour; thorns pressed in a row on the arm. W06 (barefoot climb of a spiked ladder, rose between teeth, about 30 minutes, until hands and feet were too wounded to continue), W11 (half an hour over candles), W12 (eight rose thorns pierced into the forearm, palm sliced with a razor). Watchable behaviour: repetition that costs skin, stopping only at the body's limit, with one unbroken decorative fact (the rose in the teeth, the bouquet) held against it. **Takeaway:** endurance is legible only against a limit -- the meter is the body, and it stops when the body stops. (C19137, synthesis C19062)

**M5 -- The audience breaks silence.** The cut is slow and the back is turned; the shock is the moment watchers can no longer stay silent and intervene. A birthday song over a body being eaten. W07 (razor on her own back, white shirt reddening line by line; the room screams "No, not the face!" and prevents her from continuing), W14 (body covered in maggots while children sing "Happy Birthday"). Watchable behaviour: white cloth reddening line by line, then a room that shouts; celebration and decay in the same frame. **Takeaway:** silence is the audience's part of the contract -- the work is finished when they break it. (C19138, synthesis C19063)

**M6 -- The mouth as mixing vessel; need made impossible.** White milk and red blood meeting in one mouth, then rejected; thirst plus binding plus glass equals drinking from shards. W09 (milk mixed with the blood of cut lips/tongue; phases unverified beyond their names -- build only the mixing vessel, not the phases), W10 (tied up, drinking milk from broken glass shards; reach, fail, break, lap). Watchable behaviour: reach, fail, break, lap -- transfer by the only route left. **Takeaway:** make the need simple and the route impossible, and the body will invent a third route. (C19139, synthesis C19064)

**M7 -- The Other enters.** A second body present as a back, not a face; a solo boxing match against a mirror reflection; a ping-pong rally that goes on, regular and indifferent, beside a body drawing with broken glass. W15 (couples in all combinations; a girl's back with a heart drawn on it), W16 (four-round solo boxing match against her mirror reflection, with a toy horse), W17 (ping-pong rally beside her drawing on her forearm with shattered glass). Watchable behaviour: pairing and re-pairing, two rhythms side by side, the wound becoming a relation. **Takeaway:** the wound stops being a solo act the moment a second rhythm enters the frame. (C19140, synthesis C19065)

**M8 -- The absent body.** Pane stops performing and sends instructions for others to interpret; then felt, copper, glass and iron carry what the body used to carry. Nine cases sort relic, instrument and robe per saint. W19 (instructions performed by two interpreters; 24-panel night mural, one small green house), W20 (nine glass cases in three groups of three: relic, instrument, robe per saint), and the Partitions series 1980-89 (felt, copper, glass, iron; martyrs). Watchable behaviour: a viewer reading across cases and assembling the martyr from parts; hunting and combat in panels, with one small shelter that never moves. **Takeaway:** when the body leaves, the objects must carry the wound -- sort them, and the viewer will reassemble the person. (C19141, synthesis C19066)

Two further mechanisms were evidenced in leg 2 but held out of the eight: M9, falling images from a threshold (W08 Je -- a body on a window ledge between inside and outside; Polaroids of the private interior rain down and are handed out in the square; drop, catch, pass on); M10, the face erased, then rewritten (W13 Action Psyche -- lipstick on a mirror hides the face; cuts below the brows make blood do the work of tears; a cross cut at the navel as a centre with four directions). Both are strong seeds and may be used if the prompt block expands. (C19142, synthesis C19067, C19068)


## The network: Journiac, Pluchart, Fischer, Forest, Thenot

The network is dominated by the 1973-74 convergence that produced the Sociological Art Collective -- a convergence Pane argued inside and never joined. Journiac is the strongest tie: co-participants in the fall-1973 debate and the summer-1974 meetings, and inside the meetings they split over the movement's name -- Pane led the corporel clan "avec une rare aprete" while Journiac felt cramped by the corporel label, which Pane occupied entirely. Francois Pluchart is the second-strongest tie: debate initiator, artTitudes founder 1971, who wrote "L'Etre selon Gina Pane" (1974) and a "Dossier Gina Pane" (1974); his refusal to rename art corporel broke the meetings. Herve Fischer (#683) is a debate partner and meeting co-participant; Marges frames the split as Pane (art corporel, "fonction jouissante") vs Fischer (objective sociological practice). Fred Forest (#682) is narrator and witness: he names her in the first meeting's attendee list and records the three founders drafting the manifesto alone at a bistro while the room argued the name; his "three personnalites: Fischer, Forest, Thenot" excludes her from membership. Jean-Paul Thenot (#684) is a debate partner and meeting co-participant only. No co-authored Pane work with any of them was found; Pane is not in the December 1974 show exhibitor list. (C19143, leg 3 C19008-C19012; synthesis C19070)

## Aesthetic logic: the wound as a unit of attention

Pane's aesthetic is an aesthetic of attention paid in the body, and the wound is its unit. She never shows pain to shock; she shows it to measure. The early work measures the land -- stones moved to sun, seeds sheltered, a body standing vertical between earth and sky -- and the measure is care: small things carried slowly, in the right direction. Then the same measuring hand turns on her own body, and the unit becomes the cut: one rung of the ladder, one thorn in the arm, one line of blood sinking into a white shirt, one half-hour over candles. The wound is always counted -- eight thorns, 69 photographs, 30 minutes, 600 grams of meat spat back -- because a counted wound is a fact, and a fact can be watched without flinching.

That is why the audience is part of the work and not its spectator. The actions are performed in silence, and the silence is a contract: the watchers agree to watch, and the work is finished when they break the agreement -- screaming "No, not the face!", preventing her from continuing, singing "Happy Birthday" over maggots. The constat is the other half of the contract: every action is prepared from diagrams, photographed by Francoise Masson shot by shot, and issued as a composed panel of photographs, drawings and texts. The action happens so that the constat can exist, and the constat is where the wound stays set -- the photograph is the completion of the action, never its record.

And that is why the work survives the body leaving it. When Pane stops performing, nothing in the grammar changes: the instructions are performed by two interpreters instead of by her, the 24-panel night mural is a constat drawn large, and the nine glass cases of the last work sort relic, instrument and robe the way the early actions sorted stone, seed and body. The wound becomes a photo at the centre of an installation, the martyr becomes nine cases, and the trajectory is still Earth-Sky: from the ground she stood on, to the sky she stood under, to the saints whose bodies brushed brass. A generative piece in her manner should therefore never render a wound and stop; it should count something -- stones, thorns, minutes, lines -- and let the counting be the work, with the watcher as the one who finishes it by breaking silence or by dragging the rake. **Takeaway:** count the wound, prepare the document, and let the watcher complete it. (C19145, synthesis C19057-C19066)
