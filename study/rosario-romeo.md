Born at Giarre, his passion for history triggered at 14 by reading Gioacchino Volpe's Il Medioevo, and he enrolled at Rome in 1942 to study with Volpe, and the war of 1943 cut the contact, and he returned to Sicily, and graduated in political science at Catania in 1947, see C6, with a dissertation on the Risorgimento in Sicily, and won the 1947 scholarship of the newly founded Naples Istituto italiano per gli studi storici, and Chabod, the institute's president, invited him to collaborate on the Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani and made him institute secretary in 1953, and he stayed in Naples till 1956, and his first book, Il Risorgimento in Sicilia, Catania 1948, Laterza 1950, argued Sicily joined the Risorgimento through the progressive exhaustion and death of the Sicilian nation, and ended with a judgement, Unity highly positive, undoing it harmful and unrealistic, and Panfilo Gentile implausibly called it a Marxist work, and Walter Maturi called Romeo "the liveliest and most brilliant historian of the latest historiographical levy", and the modernist detour, Le scoperte americane nella coscienza italiana del Cinquecento, 1954, closest to Chabod, and the medievalist detour, the Origgio article, Rivista storica italiana, 1957, siding with Salvemini and Davidsohn against Ottokar and Chabod. ## Aesthetic logic

Romeo's aesthetic is the liberal judge with a ledger in one hand and a verdict in the other, and the ledger is always smaller than the judgement it serves. Nothing in his work arrives as a mood: the dying nation is counted and then judged, the proposed revolution is built as a model and then demolished with its own tools, the gambler is given five opponents and the last card, the four protagonists are wired with hatred and left to fight to the end, the counting machine is switched off by hand when it threatens the political question, and the piece that follows him must therefore be built from judgements, not from atmospheres. The palette follows the same law: the Sicilian sun in ochre and whitewash for the dying nation, the Piedmontese ink in black and paper cream for the gambler and the archive, the parliamentary green for the state as moderniser, the newspaper grey for the column, the gold medal and the Lincean red for the honours, and the empty space between two scholars at one table in cold shadow, because the refusal is the portrait. Colour must arrive as a verdict, the ledger turning into a landscape, the small forces migrating, the one hand staying untaken, never as decoration laid on top. His time is the long duration that ends in judgement, so the loop must never merely repeat: the book must keep growing volume by volume, the model must keep being taken apart, the timeline must keep stretching from a medieval village to a European parliament, and the end must come from the last-word article, because after a life of judgement there is exactly one convergence, and it is cut off mid-sentence, and the cutting off is the point.
