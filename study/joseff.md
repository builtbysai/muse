## Background

Joseff N, a graphics motion designer who makes looping GIFs with code. He started coding in 2018, taught himself Processing, and within two years was profiled by Beebom (Saturday 23 May 2020) as making "some of the trippiest looping GIFs" on the internet. At that time he was a maths student at the University of Warwick, UK-based, sharing work on Twitter, Tumblr and Dribbble, with a GitHub page that was "pretty much empty". The mathematics degree matters: his whole practice is mathematical objects made to move. He does not illustrate maths; he animates the objects maths cannot build: impossible triangles, impossible rings, impossible portals, cubes inside cubes. The loop is the point. Every post is a loop or an animation; there is no still portfolio, no statement, no shop. The X bio is the whole manifesto: "I make looping animations using code."

The practice has one tool and one constraint. The tool is Processing, the open-source sketchbook for visual code, the same tool bleuje uses. The constraint is the seamless loop: all motion as functions of t in [0,1), mapped to 2*pi*t, so the last frame equals the first. Joseff uses the bleuje recipe, and the two artists collaborate, which is why this study hops from #446. But Joseff's subject is different from bleuje's. Bleuje's subject is the field: waves, flow lines, noise, lattices. Joseff's subject is the object: one impossible thing, centred, rotating, breaking, reforming, glowing on black. Where bleuje fills the frame with a system, Joseff puts one paradox in the middle of it and lets it turn.

## Works inspected

### W1. The impossible portal (December 2023)

The viral work. A tweet of Wednesday 6 December 2023 (status 1731993030691807347) carries the caption, quoted in reposts: "Inspired by a long tradition of impossible objects, this 'impossible portal' created by coded loop artist @jn3008 challenges your mind leaning toward you or away from you according to the point of the portal you focus on." The portal is a rectangular frame-like opening whose depth flips: look at one corner and it leans toward you, look at another and it leans away, and the flip never settles. It travelled far beyond the loop scene: Boing Boing headlined it "an impossible portal into another dimension where perception is in the eye of the beholder", The Automatic Earth reposted it on 6 December 2023, and it was still being reposted in 2026. The mechanism is ambiguous depth, not motion: the loop moves, but the work happens in the viewer's visual cortex. This is the most widely seen Joseff piece, and the one that proves the impossible object is a mass medium, not a niche.

### W2. Infinite counter-rotating cubes within cubes

Reposted with the line "These infinite counter rotating cubes within other cubes created by coded loop artist @jn3008 bring recursion to a new level of complexity." Cubes nested inside cubes, each layer turning against the one outside it. The recursion is the mechanism: the same motion at every scale, so the eye cannot find the bottom. No date was found for the original; the repost is undated in the sources read. The work is the cleanest statement of Joseff's second grammar after the impossible object: nesting.

### W3. Penrose triangle, wireframe, rotating (reposted March 2026)

A rotating 3D wireframe Penrose triangle on black, reposted 11 March 2026 with the @jn3008 handle visible and the on-screen text "This object can only exist in the human mind, not in the physical world". The wireframe matters: a solid Penrose triangle hides its cheat in shading, but a wireframe shows every beam and still cannot resolve, because the over-under ordering is drawn, not rendered. The rotation is slow and the loop is exact, so the triangle turns forever without ever becoming possible.

### W4. Wireframe torus / Mobius strip (2021)

A wireframe torus rotating slowly against black, its surface a fine mesh of tiny white dots, "giving it a sparkling, star-like surface", per a Threads repost of 22 March 2026 that traces the piece to jn3008.com in 2021 and calls it "MC Escher style magic". Commenters identified the shape as a Mobius-strip variant and debated the orthographic view the illusion needs: the camera must be exactly placed, because the paradox lives in the projection, not in the object. This is the earliest dated Joseff work found, and it already has the whole vocabulary: wireframe, dots, black ground, slow rotation, orthographic camera.

### W6. Cubes camouflage (collaboration with bleuje, before January 2024)

### W7. Gosper hexagon cubes (collaboration with bleuje, 2025)

### W8. Flow-field mandala (collaboration with bleuje, 2026)

The first loop on bleuje.com's 2026_1 archive page, captioned "Collaboration with jn3008". This is the most recent collaboration and the most bleuje-like of the three: the field dominates and Joseff's contribution is inside it, in the singularities and the dotted cluster, rather than as a separate object. ## Mechanisms

## M1. The impossible object, drawn clean

The Penrose triangle, the impossible ring, the impossible portal: geometric paradoxes rendered as clean beveled beams or wireframes, with precise over-under ordering so depth can never resolve. The drawing is the mechanism, not the rendering: a solid Penrose triangle hides its cheat in shading, but Joseff's wireframes (W3, W4) show every beam and still cannot resolve, because the ordering is drawn into the lines. The eye keeps trying to resolve the object and keeps failing, and the failing is the work.

**Takeaway:** draw the paradox, do not render it, and the piece should let the eye keep failing to resolve it.

## M2. The travelling pulse on an impossible perimeter

A bright element travels the perimeter of the impossible object forever: the amber pulse of "Strides" (W13), the light circling the ring in the 2026 carousels (W9, W10). The pulse is the clock of the piece: it gives the eye a thing to follow around a shape that cannot exist, and because the shape cannot exist, the following never arrives. The pulse must be exactly periodic, one circuit per loop, or the seam shows.

**Takeaway:** put one light on the impossible perimeter, and the piece should let it circle forever without arriving.

## M3. Break apart and reform

The 2026 ring variations (W9, W10) all run the same motion: a ring breaks into segments, the segments drift apart, then they return and the ring reforms, exactly, on the loop. The disassembly is the drama and the reassembly is the proof: the pieces remember where they belong. The mechanism is replacement in disguise: each segment has a periodic path out and back, so the breaking is as seamless as the forming.

**Takeaway:** break the object and bring it back whole, and the piece should let the pieces remember where they belong.

## M4. Particle convergence: chaos into form, hold, dissolve

Hundreds of luminous particles on harmonic paths gather into a perfect form, a ring, a shape, hold it for a beat, then dissolve back into chaos (W14, and the particle spheres of W9 slide 2). The attractor phases in and out on the loop cycle: the form exists only in the middle of the loop, and the ends are noise. The hold is the reward: the viewer waits through the chaos for the moment the form snaps into being.

**Takeaway:** let chaos gather into form, hold it one beat, and the piece should let the form dissolve again.

## M5. Counter-rotation and nesting: cubes within cubes

Cubes nested inside cubes, each layer turning against the one outside it (W2). The recursion is the mechanism: the same motion at every scale, so the eye cannot find the bottom of the object. Counter-rotation matters: if the layers turned together, the nest would read as one solid; turning against each other makes every layer visible and makes the depth unreadable, which is the point.

**Takeaway:** nest the same motion at every scale, turn the layers against each other, and the piece should let the eye never find the bottom.

## M6. The portal that leans both ways

The impossible portal (W1) is a frame whose depth flips with the viewer's focus: look at one corner and it leans toward you, look at another and it leans away. The mechanism is ambiguous depth, not motion: the loop moves, but the work happens in the viewer's visual cortex, and two viewers can see opposite leanings at the same moment. This is the only Joseff mechanism that is social: the argument about which way it leans is the piece's afterlife, and it is why the portal travelled through Boing Boing and The Automatic Earth while the other loops stayed in the scene.

**Takeaway:** build one frame with two depths, and the piece should let the lean flip with the focus.

## M7. The seamless loop as the only constraint

All motion as functions of t in [0,1), mapped to 2*pi*t, the bleuje recipe, in Processing. The constraint is the discipline: every quantity must be periodic, every path must close, and the seam must be invisible. Joseff's version of the discipline is stricter than bleuje's, because his objects are rigid: a field can hide a seam in noise, but a ring cannot hide a seam in its own geometry. The loop must close exactly, or the ring visibly jumps.

**Takeaway:** make every motion periodic in t, and the piece should let the last frame equal the first exactly.

## M8. Dark ground, luminous form

Near-black ground, one or two glow colours, additive blending: cyan particles (W14), teal-to-indigo streamlines (W15), red-orange and dark-blue torus (W10), white wireframe on black (W3, W4, W9). The darkness is functional: it removes all context, so the impossible object has nothing to be impossible against except itself. The 2026 turn toward rainbow gloss and iridescence (W10) is the first break in the discipline: colour and material are now allowed, but the ground stays black.

**Takeaway:** keep the ground black and the form luminous, and the piece should let the object be impossible against nothing but itself.

## M9. Variations as a series

The October 2026 carousels (W9, W10) are not single works but variations: one motif, three or four slides, the same motion with different materials, colours, and finishes. The series is the mechanism: the viewer compares the slides, and the comparison teaches the motif. Joseff's feed is built this way throughout: the same ring, the same cube, the same triangle, returned to with a new finish. The variation is cheaper than a new idea and deeper than a repost.

**Takeaway:** return to one motif with a new finish, and the piece should let the comparison teach the motif.

## M10. Collaboration as mechanism: field plus object

The three bleuje collaborations are one mechanism in three stages. "Cubes camouflage" (W6): Joseff's cubes dissolve into bleuje's dot field and re-emerge. The Gosper cubes (W7): Joseff's cubes sit on bleuje's hexagonal mathematics, in a starfield. The flow-field mandala (W8): the field dominates and Joseff's contribution is inside it, in the singularities and the dotted cluster. The division of labour is stable: bleuje brings the field, Joseff brings the object. The collaboration is a mechanism because it is repeatable: field plus object, three times, three different balances.

**Takeaway:** put one object into another artist's field, and the piece should let the balance between them change each time.

## M11. The viral repost: the work as a meme object

The impossible portal (W1) travelled through Boing Boing, The Automatic Earth, Threads and X reposts for three years, because the caption did the work: "challenges your mind leaning toward you or away from you according to the point of the portal you focus on". The caption is part of the mechanism: it tells the viewer what to test, and the test is the experience. Joseff's other loops have no such caption and stayed in the scene. The lesson is not to chase virality; it is that an impossible object needs an instruction, one sentence, telling the eye what to try.

**Takeaway:** give the paradox one sentence of instruction, and the piece should let the viewer test it.

## M12. The catalogued impossible object

Vlad Alexeev's im-possible.info has run since 2001 as the web's archive of impossible figures, and it keeps two Joseff works (W11, W12). The catalogue entry is a mechanism of its own: the work leaves the feed, where it scrolls past, and enters the canon, where it stays. Joseff's loops are built to be kept: one object, one motion, one paradox, exactly the size of an archive entry.

**Takeaway:** build the work to the size of an archive entry, and the piece should let it stay.

## Evolution

Phase 1, Learning in public, 2018-2020. Starts coding in 2018, teaches himself Processing, a maths student at the University of Warwick. First GIFs on Twitter, Tumblr and Dribbble. Beebom profiles him on Saturday 23 May 2020: "some of the trippiest looping GIFs" on the internet, made in two years from a standing start. The vocabulary is already set: black and white loops, impossible geometry, Processing. No collaborations yet, no compo entries found, no colour.

Phase 2, The wireframe years, 2021. The wireframe torus / Mobius strip (W4), the orthographic camera, the dotted mesh. "Cube Fountain" (W5) places 4th in the Animated GIF competition at Revision Online 2021, April 2021, in the same competition where bleuje's "Valleys" places 9th: Joseff is now showing loop GIFs to the demoscene and placing. The site jn3008.com is live. The work is monochrome, wireframe, and geometric; the impossible object is the whole subject.

Phase 3, The portal and the first collaboration, 2022-2023. The counter-rotating cubes (W2), the recursion grammar. The impossible portal (W1) goes viral in December 2023, carried by a caption that tells the eye what to test, and is still being reposted in 2026. "Cubes camouflage" (W6), the first bleuje collaboration, appears before January 2024: the object dissolving into the field. The practice now has two audiences, the loop scene and the wider internet, and two modes, the object alone and the object in a field.

Phase 4, The hexagonal collaboration, 2024-2025. The Gosper hexagon cubes (W7), the second bleuje collaboration, posted Saturday 15 November 2025: black cubes with white designs, floating in a starfield, on hexagonal mathematics. Steady Instagram output through the period. The work stays monochrome and object-centred; the collaborations carry the development.

Phase 5, The ring variations, 2026. Two carousels in October 2026 (W9, W10): the same toroidal ring in three, then four, variations, from white wireframe to rainbow gloss to iridescent cube segments, breaking apart and reforming. The flow-field mandala (W8), the third bleuje collaboration, in which the field finally dominates. Colour and material arrive, late and all at once, after eight years of black and white. The motif stays the same: one ring, one object, turning on black.
