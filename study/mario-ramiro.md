## The life: Taubate, ECA-USP, and Flusser

Mario Ramiro was born in Taubate, Sao Paulo state, in 1957. No day or month of birth was found anywhere, and no source read gives any family background, childhood or personal-life detail; the study invents none. He graduated from the Escola de Comunicacoes e Artes of the University of Sao Paulo (ECA-USP) in 1978-1982. He studied at USP together with Rafael Franca; the two met Hudinilson Jr. at a joint underground exhibition, and from there the three began meeting and exchanging ideas in greater depth. That meeting is the only meeting story in the Ramiro sources: Vallauri is not mentioned in any Ramiro source read. (C17308, leg 1 C17100, C17101, C17105, C17107; synthesis C17250)

In Sao Paulo in the late 1970s he met the philosopher Vilem Flusser, introduced via Gabriel Borba, and was immediately taken by his ideas and style. Flusser influenced and supported his career throughout the 1980s: in Ramiro's words, Flusser was a "fundamental influence during my university years". With Walter Zanini, Flusser was one of the two recommenders for Ramiro's DAAD grant to Germany, and after a 1988 studio visit he placed Ramiro with Gottfried Jager. The Flusser relationship is evidenced here through the Flusser Studies abstract and Ramiro's own account; the full Flusser Studies article body did not render, so the relationship is not described beyond the abstract. Flusser is the top pick for study #681, where his own biography leg must verify his dates independently. (C17309, leg 1 C17108; leg 3 C17205, C17206; synthesis C17273)

The arc after ECA-USP runs through Germany and back: a DAAD scholarship to the Kunstakademie Dusseldorf, 1992-1993; a master's degree in Photography and New Media from the Kunsthochschule fur Medien Koln (Academy of Media Arts Cologne), 1994-1997; a doctorate in Visual Arts from ECA-USP in 2008. He is a professor in the Department of Fine Arts and the Graduate Program in Visual Arts at ECA-USP. He is also a musician -- a member of the band Do/Z, alongside Adriano Leal, Diogo de Nazare and Guilherme Xavier -- and his research unfolds through sound performances with groups such as Kurokos, Os Macaco and TheBanda, plus curatorial work and long-term projects. In 2025 he curated the exhibition Arte rock, samba, tropicalia, pop, punk, noise at the Ecole Superieure des Beaux-Arts de Bordeaux, France, and his works were included in Hudinilson Jr.: Echoes in Xerox at the Kunsthaus Pasquart in Biel, Switzerland. (C17310, leg 1 C17100, C17104, C17107; synthesis C17250)


## 3Nos3: the city as a map, 1979-1982

In 1979 Ramiro co-founded the collective 3Nos3 with Hudinilson Jr. and Rafael Franca. In his own first-person account, the group "was responsible for the greatest number of large-scale Urban Interventions, primarily in the city of Sao Paulo", working mostly in the early morning hours, clandestinely, with large quantities of industrial plastic sponsored by a Sao Paulo company; works were often removed by police or firefighters and publicised through press coverage. Ramiro's own framing of the actions is the one this study follows: they were "drawings on the city map" and "drawings on the city blueprint", "visual marks imposed on and in dialogue with the architecture", made in "spaces of public circulation" rather than "spaces of public concentration". (C17311, leg 1 C17102; leg 2 C17150, C17151; synthesis C17262)

The named actions, in order: Ensacamento (Bagging), 1979, the group's inaugural action, in which between midnight and 05:00 the three hooded the heads of public monuments with black or blue trash bags tied closed with rope, "like a noose, simulating strangulation", starting at the Monument to the Independence of Brazil (W03); X-Galeria, night of 2 June 1979, in which the entrances of twenty-seven commercial art galleries plus MASP on Avenida Paulista were sealed with a large X of masking tape, with mimeographed leaflets reading "O que esta dentro fica / O que esta fora se expande" (What is inside stays / What is outside expands); Triptico (Triptych), 24-26 August 1979, three panels of 2.20 x 1.40 m each arranged as a triangular ensemble on the sidewalk in front of the Theatro Municipal, each artist claiming one panel -- Ramiro and Franca worked pictorially, Hudinilson used assemblage -- the collective's longest intervention, three days until the Sanitation Department removed the screens (W04); Interdicao (Interdiction), 21 September 1979, bands of blue, yellow, brown and red plastic stretched across the roadway on Avenida Paulista near MASP from 16:00, the group standing motionless like street vendors when the light turned green, leaving motorists to decide whether to cross (W05); Interversao VI, night of 15 June 1980, a 100-metre-long, 4-metre-wide roll of red plastic laid across the two large oval cavities connecting Avenida Paulista with Avenida Consolacao, descending from Paulista into Consolacao and returning to the surface, "similar to sewing" (W06); Coneccao, 13 May 1981, a 25-metre roll of glossy red polyethylene from a ventilation shaft at Avenida Reboucas / Avenida Dr. Arnaldo; Arco 10 (Arc 10), night of 7 December 1981, a 100-metre roll of yellow cellophane suspended from the viaduct along Avenida Doutor Arnaldo, hovering above Avenida Sumare below, "bridging separation without touching the ground" (W07). The group also took part in the Evento de Fim de Decada, a large-scale occupation of Praca da Se, with the groups Viajou Sem Passaporte, GEXTU, d'Magrelos, TIT and others. (C17312, leg 1 C17102; leg 2 C17150, C17151; synthesis C17258)

Two counts of the collective's output do not agree, and neither is printed as definitive: the Catalogo das Artes history says the group carried out eighteen interventions around the city up to 1982, when it dissolved; the Engramma study counts at least seventeen further interventions between 1979 and 1982 in addition to the museum episodes. The decisive dissolution trigger is likewise unsettled: the Pinacoteca experience plus the farewell Acabou (Engramma) against Franca's departure for SAIC Chicago (ICAA). Both are carried; neither is presented as the sole cause. What is settled is the institutional turn and the end: in May 1982 3Nos3 was invited to exhibit at the Pinacoteca do Estado de Sao Paulo in the show 3NOS3/3ANOS (19 May-6 June 1982), in which each member showed an individual work -- Hudinilson's Posicao amorosa, Ramiro's Estruturas de Especulacao, Franca's video Carta 23 -- plus a shared red plastic band across the museum facade (27.4/3 - II), installed at a height that let visitors enter while compelling them to lower their bodies. The breakup of 3Nos3 in 1982 was, in Ramiro's own account, "a turning point" for him. The collective's book project was conceived in 1982; the book 3NOS3: Intervencoes Urbanas - 1979-1982 (240 pages, Ubu Editora, Rumos Itau Cultural 2013-2014) was consolidated and produced by Ramiro himself and launched on 12 December (year not stated on the page) at Itau Cultural, Sao Paulo. (C17313, leg 1 C17101, C17102; leg 2 C17150; synthesis C17258, C17273)


## The machines keep changing: xerox, telecom, force, heat

Ramiro starts where Hudinilson starts -- the copier glass, the pressed body, the cheap flat mark -- but where Hudinilson stays with the copier for thirty years, Ramiro keeps swapping the machine. Since the late 1970s he has worked with technical media to reflect on image production and consumption, with xerographic prints as a central medium. From the early 1980s he experimented with photocopiers alongside Hudinilson Jr., while Franca worked with closed-circuit video; the aim was works without an "original", each copy becoming a master for other copies. His Sufocacao (Suffocation), 1979, is a body compressed against the photocopier surface, the image multiplied in series; the show text for "Brazil in Layers" at Zipper Galeria reads the technical precariousness of the medium as a measure of political censorship under the dictatorship: "the image, multiplied in series, inscribes in the reproduction itself the sensation of asphyxiation that gives the work its title". Guard, carried from the synthesis and from #679: this is NOT Hudinilson's Sufoco (#679 W05) -- two different 1979 works by two different artists with near-identical titles. Do not merge them. His Passes de Magica (Magic Stunts), early 1980s, are xerographic sequences "similar to storyboards" dealing with "the appearance and disappearance of objects and body parts, as in old magic shows". He made an artist's book in 1979 using a photographic image of a hand approaching a white band, and his self-published photocopy book CAPACITOR appeared in 1984. He also wrote about Hudinilson's work: "Zona de Tensao", Arte em Sao Paulo No. 18, 1983. (C17314, leg 1 C17100, C17102, C17103; leg 2 C17150, C17153, C17155; synthesis C17260)

The second machine is telecommunications. His first videotex works, a small series showing the dematerialisation of objects, were presented at the Museum of Image and Sound (MIS) in Sao Paulo in 1982 (W11). He continued working with telecommunications systems until 1988. The key work is Clones -- uma rede simultanea de radio, televisao e videotexto (1983, with Jose W. Garcia), in which one object -- a horizontal red bar -- was transmitted simultaneously through three systems: on nine videotex terminals it appeared as a flat graphic displaced on a 2D grid; on two TV monitors the same bar, received from a live broadcast in the city, moved in perspective and slowly went out of focus, losing its rigid shape; on radio it was given sound by dropping a 1-metre, 1-cm-diameter iron bar from a height of 1 metre, recording the impact and manipulating it with an analog synthesizer (after Duchamp's "3 Stoppages Etalon"). The 4-minute work was installed in a circular room at MIS with videotex terminals, TV monitors, radio and speakers (W12). In 1984 he took part in Level 5, a project bringing together an architect, six visual artists and a writer to manipulate virtual 3D space on a CAD system; his contribution represented mechanical waves as color-plotter prints and video animations. Altamira (1986) was a Slow Scan TV (SSTV) bidirectional network created by Sao Paulo artists and MIT's Center for Advanced Visual Studies for the Sky Art Conference: Ramiro's installation, a "media cave", was set in the unfinished concrete skeleton of the Museum of Contemporary Art of USP, a large projection screen behind which the ballerina Laly Krotoszynski performed under flashing lights, her movements "a primitive ritual dance around a bonfire", accompanied by rhythmic percussion on a sheet of metal and electronic music from a large steel object similar in shape to Sputnik (W16). In 1987 he first taught a course titled "The Use of Telecommunications Systems in Art", exploring telephone answering machines as instruments, with students setting machines to induce callers to leave improvised lyrical, noisy or theatrical replies. In 1988 he and Eduardo Kac created Retrato Suposto -- Rosto Roto (Presumed Portrait -- Foul Face): Ramiro in the studios of a TV Cultura youth variety program in Sao Paulo, connected by fax to Kac in his Rio de Janeiro studio; Ramiro faxed a repertoire of images -- eyes, mouths, noses, ears -- for a potential portrait, and minutes later Kac faxed back a montage. The whole exchange happened on the air, though the TV program only reached Sao Paulo state, so Kac could not monitor the broadcast. Kac, quoted by Ramiro: the link "was not a 'work' in the strict sense... It was an esthetic investigation that subverted the automatic and passive use of telecommunications media" (W17). He notes that in the 1980s Brazilian commercial galleries promoted neo-expressionism and technology-based work was mostly shown away from mainstream venues, including the Sao Paulo Bienal. (C17315, leg 1 C17102; leg 2 C17150, C17154; synthesis C17266)

The third machine is force itself. Since the early 1980s his sculptural work has aimed to undermine mass in sculptural form and explore physical forces in 3D space. Geoescul (1982) is his first named sculpture series: wooden geometric wall sculptures "that revealed the presence of a physical force upon the material by making use of mechanical tensions to shape a rectangle" (W09). Tempo em 8 (Time in 8, 1984) turns the observer's presence into the switch: a photoelectric cell activated by the observer controlled a regulator connected to a water tank on the ceiling; drops fell into a parabolic dish on the floor, and the mechanized waves on the dish's surface were reflected by a flat mirror at eye level -- a "chain reaction of waves", in his own account (W14). Gravidade Zero (Zero Gravity, 1986), glass, brass and wood, uses an electromagnet regulated by a photoelectric sensor to levitate small metallic objects: a light beam crosses the upper part of the object and hits a light-dependent resistor, which drives an amplifier connected to the electromagnet; if the object falls, more light reaches the sensor and magnetic force increases; if it rises too high, the force decreases -- a feedback loop that holds a body in the air, sculpture freed from its base and from "above" and "below", the support replaced by a circuit (W15). Between 1986 and 1991 he made the Campo de Forca (Force Field) series, steel, copper and ceramic sculptures with a radiating heat source inside, heat waves "modelling" the space around the object, an invisible volume felt through the skin, and the Escudos (Shields) series, round wall objects of wood, aluminum, nylon, steel cable and 110-V resistors. (C17316, leg 1 C17102; leg 2 C17150; synthesis C17270)


## Germany and the invisible field, 1991-1998

He moved to Germany at the end of 1991. In summer 1992 he made Entre o Norte e o Sul (Between North and South) with Japanese artist Morio Labonete Nishimura: a "radical teleart experience" of "scenic teletransport" in which the two artists, both living in Dusseldorf, travelled to opposite ends of Europe -- Nishimura to Lieksa, Finland, Ramiro to Amorgos, Greece -- each building an installation with a glass "antenna", a vial of Rhine river water containing a filament of their own hairs connected by gold leaf. At a predetermined day and hour each tried to transmit an image telepathically and drew whatever came to mind. Compared afterwards in Dusseldorf: of Ramiro's eight drawings, two were chalices similar to the lotus Nishimura had transmitted; of Nishimura's five, two showed a horizontal cross and a circle of fire similar to the burning sword Ramiro had transmitted. The work was inspired by the roughly 500 Asian towers holding relics of the Buddha, believed to form a network beyond time and space (W18). (C17317, leg 1 C17102; leg 2 C17150; synthesis C17273)

In the laser and photography laboratory of the Academy of Media Arts, Cologne, he made Schlieren photographs and "Lasergrams": photograms made by laser light on color photographic paper, in which the photographed object sits INSIDE the camera, between film and lens; transparent objects project shadows as dark as opaque ones; heat appears as sinuous forms; negative processing yields colors complementary to the laser -- a green laser gives reddish-magenta, a blue laser gives yellow-orange-red. The first series, Light Turbulences, c. 1993-1996, "revealed images of bodies in movement, rising and falling amidst flames" -- heat as fire, purification or destruction; the dating is inferred (move to Germany end of 1991; second Schlieren series began 1996) and no exact year is stated (W19). The artist's book Light Turbulences was produced in 1995 for his solo exhibition at Hame-Diehl Gallery, Dusseldorf, in June 1995. A second Schlieren series begun in 1996 used conventional white light, reintroducing the human figure, "seen simultaneously as material and evanescent". His artist's book Luftkorper (Aerial Bodies) was published in Cologne in 1997. His most recent work described in the 1998 Leonardo article is Atlas, o suporte do mundo (Atlas, Support of the World), c. 1997-1998, based on an age-old Chinese myth of the Earth as a flat disk held up by a tortoise shell of cosmic proportions: the installation combined thermal sculptures (Escudos / Shields) activated by motion sensors, Schlieren photographs, and an area defined by granite blocks inside which three live tortoises moved about on a heat field produced by light reflectors (reptiles need light to store heat, being cold-blooded) (W20). (C17318, leg 1 C17102, C17103; leg 2 C17150; synthesis C17272)

## The mechanisms

### M1 -- The pressed body: the copier glass as a suffocation chamber

Mechanically: press a body against the photocopier surface and multiply the image in series. The copy is not a portrait but a record of compression -- the machine flattens the body, the series repeats the flattening, and the technical precariousness of the medium (fading toner, cheap paper) becomes the measure of political censorship. In Sufocacao (W01) "the image, multiplied in series, inscribes in the reproduction itself the sensation of asphyxiation that gives the work its title". In Passes de Magica (W08) the same glass becomes a stage: objects and body parts appear and vanish across a storyboard of stills. The evidencing works are W01, W02 and W08. (C17320, leg 2 C17150, C17153, C17155; synthesis C17260)

Watchable behaviour: a body part pressed flat against a glowing field, its edge smearing where contact fails, then the same flattened image repeated until the repetition itself feels like suffocation. A piece built on this mechanism should press a form onto a flat field and let the copy, not a drawn line, decide the edge -- and let the series, not a single image, carry the pressure.

**Takeaway:** press the body on the glass and copy it until the copy suffocates. (C17321)

### M2 -- Cheap flat line across the city: plastic as drawing on the city map

Mechanically: take a roll of cheap industrial plastic -- a garbage bag, a band of cellophane, a 100-metre roll of red polyethylene -- and lay it across a real piece of the city at night, clandestinely, in the early morning hours. Ramiro's own framing: the actions were "drawings on the city map" and "drawings on the city blueprint", "visual marks imposed on and in dialogue with the architecture", made in "spaces of public circulation" rather than "spaces of public concentration". The gesture is fast, nocturnal and gone by morning, surviving as press coverage and photographs; the removal by police or firefighters is part of the work. The evidencing works are W03 (Ensacamento), W04 (Triptico), W05 (Interdicao), W06 (Interversao VI), W07 (Arco 10), W10 (Estruturas de Especulacao, the same flat geometry brought indoors), plus the unnumbered Coneccao and 27.4/3 - II. (C17322, leg 2 C17150, C17151; leg 3 C17202; synthesis C17262)

Watchable behaviour: a familiar public object -- a statue, a crossing, a viaduct, a museum facade -- is briefly, visibly altered by one cheap coloured line, and the alteration is the artwork. A piece built on this mechanism should change one ordinary thing in the scene overnight with a single flat coloured mark, and let the mark be the whole event.

**Takeaway:** the city is the map. Draw one cheap line across it and the city has to look. (C17323)

### M3 -- The barrier that reroutes: the viewer's decision is the work

Mechanically: lay a barrier that does not block so much as reroute -- and hand the decision to whoever meets it. In Interdicao (W05) the plastic bands close the intersections "until a motorist had the initiative to drive through the plastic, freeing up the flow of traffic"; the group stood motionless and let the motorist decide. In Arco 10 (W07) the police closed the road after drivers feared the arc would obstruct traffic, "causing far greater obstruction than the installation had posed". In X-Galeria the gallery doors are sealed with a masking-tape X so that "what is inside stays, what is outside expands". In Interversao VI (W06) the red plastic sews two avenues together and the authorities restore order within hours -- the removal is the spectacle. The evidencing works are W05, W06, W07 and the unnumbered X-Galeria and 27.4/3 - II. (C17324, leg 2 C17150, C17151; leg 3 C17201, C17202; synthesis C17264)

Watchable behaviour: a bright, cheap, temporary line across the scene, and everything in the scene -- traffic, visitors, attention -- bending around it or choosing whether to cross it. A piece built on this mechanism should lay one coloured line across the scene and let the viewer, not the artist, decide whether to break it.

**Takeaway:** do not block the road. Cross it with a line and watch the city decide. (C17325)

### M4 -- One form, three media at once: simultaneity as the artwork

Mechanically: take one object and transmit it simultaneously through three different systems, so the same form is flat, perspectival and audible at the same time. In Clones (W12) a horizontal red bar appears on nine videotex terminals as a flat graphic displaced on a 2D grid, on two TV monitors as the same bar moving in perspective and slowly going out of focus, and on radio as the sound of a 1-metre iron bar dropped and manipulated with an analog synthesizer. In Retrato Suposto -- Rosto Roto (W17) a portrait is assembled by two hands in two cities, in real time, over fax inside a live TV broadcast. In the 1982 videotex works (W11) an object is reduced to a low-resolution teletext graphic until it dematerializes. The evidencing works are W11, W12 and W17. (C17326, leg 2 C17150, C17154; synthesis C17266)

Watchable behaviour: one shape arriving in three channels at once, each channel warping it differently -- flat here, blurred there, audible somewhere else -- and the viewer watching the same object disagree with itself. A piece built on this mechanism should send one form through two or three different renderings simultaneously, so the form is never the same twice.

**Takeaway:** send one thing three ways at once and the disagreement is the picture. (C17327)

### M5 -- The copy as cave painting: the newest machine re-enacts the oldest image

Mechanically: use the newest reproduction machine to re-enact the oldest image-making. In Lascaux Copy (W13) photocopy toner stands in for natural pigment and a hand reproduced with a standard photocopier presents "the same characteristics of a cut-out image on a dark, pigmented background seen in the original Lascaux palm print" -- a white, negative hand against a dark ground. In Os ultimos registros de Lascaux (1991) images are superimposed without spatial order, a "temporal compression" like cave paintings where one generation draws over another. In Passes de Magica (W08) the copier is a stage magician making body parts appear and vanish. In Altamira (W16) the "media cave" sets a ritual dance behind a projection screen in an unfinished concrete skeleton, prehistoric ritual and satellite technology in one room. The evidencing works are W08, W13, W16 and the unnumbered Os ultimos registros de Lascaux. (C17328, leg 2 C17150; synthesis C17268)

Watchable behaviour: a handprint, white on dark, made by a machine -- the first image and the latest copy in the same gesture. A piece built on this mechanism should make its newest-looking image out of the oldest gesture (a hand, a print, a mark on a wall) and let the machine's cheapness be the point, not the flaw.

**Takeaway:** the handprint is the first image and the last copy. Make it with the newest machine. (C17329)

### M6 -- Force made visible: tension, feedback and heat as the sculptor

Mechanically: let a physical force do the sculpting, and make the force itself visible. Geoescul (W09) shapes a rectangle with mechanical tension. Gravidade Zero (W15) holds a metallic object in the air with a feedback loop: a light beam, a light-dependent resistor, an amplifier and an electromagnet, the force rising as the object falls and falling as it rises. Tempo em 8 (W14) turns the observer's presence into the switch: the visitor breaks an invisible infrared beam, gravity drops the water, a mechanical wave forms, and its image returns as visible light. Campo de Forca and Escudos radiate heat so the invisible volume around the object is felt through the skin rather than seen. The evidencing works are W09, W14, W15, W20 (Atlas, whose thermal sculptures are motion-sensor activated) and the unnumbered Campo de Forca and Escudos. (C17330, leg 2 C17150; synthesis C17270)

Watchable behaviour: an object that floats, ripples or warms without being touched -- a body held in the air by a circuit, a wave that starts when you walk in, a volume you feel on your skin but cannot see. A piece built on this mechanism should replace the pedestal with a loop (light, heat, tension) and let the loop hold the form.

**Takeaway:** do not carve the form. Build the loop and let the force hold it up. (C17331)

### M7 -- The invisible made visible: heat, turbulence and Schlieren light

Mechanically: photograph what the eye cannot see -- heat radiation, air turbulence, the volume of a body in space -- with Schlieren optics and laser light. In the Lasergrams (W19) the photographed object sits INSIDE the camera, between film and lens; transparent objects project shadows as dark as opaque ones; heat appears as sinuous forms; negative processing yields colors complementary to the laser (green laser gives reddish-magenta, blue gives yellow-orange-red). The series "revealed images of bodies in movement, rising and falling amidst flames" -- heat as fire, purification or destruction. The white-light Schlieren series from 1996 puts the human figure at the center, "seen simultaneously as material and evanescent". In Atlas (W20) Schlieren photographs sit beside live tortoises moving on a heat field. The evidencing works are W19, W20 and the unnumbered white-light Schlieren series. (C17332, leg 2 C17150; synthesis C17272)

Watchable behaviour: a body that appears as a shimmer -- a heat plume, a sinuous flame-form, a shadow as dark as the solid that cast it -- in colours that belong to the laser, not to the object. A piece built on this mechanism should render a warm body as its own turbulence, and let the invisible field be the portrait.

**Takeaway:** the heat around the body is the portrait. Photograph that. (C17333)

### M8 -- The network with the electronics removed: telepathy as a protocol

Mechanically: run a telecommunications artwork with the telecommunications taken out. In Entre o Norte e o Sul (W18) two artists at opposite ends of Europe -- Lieksa, Finland and Amorgos, Greece -- each build an installation with a glass "antenna", a vial of Rhine river water containing a filament of their own hairs connected by gold leaf, and at a predetermined day and hour each tries to transmit an image telepathically and draws whatever comes to mind; two of Ramiro's eight drawings match the lotus Nishimura transmitted, two of Nishimura's five match Ramiro's burning sword. In Altamira (W16) the SSTV network carries a ritual dance as transmitted shadows. In Retrato Suposto -- Rosto Roto (W17) the fax is a dialogic medium inside a unidirectional broadcast. The evidencing works are W16, W17 and W18. (C17334, leg 2 C17150; leg 3 C17206; synthesis C17273)

Watchable behaviour: two distant points linked by nothing but a shared hour and a fragile material -- water, hair, gold leaf -- and an image that may or may not arrive. A piece built on this mechanism should set two points a long way apart, give them one shared moment, and let the transmission be uncertain.

**Takeaway:** take the wires out of the network and see what still arrives. (C17335)


## The aesthetic logic: a network, not a canvas

Ramiro's logic is a network, not a canvas. He starts where Hudinilson starts -- the copier glass, the pressed body, the cheap flat mark (M1, M2) -- but where Hudinilson stays with the copier for thirty years, Ramiro keeps swapping the machine: the street gives way to the videotex terminal, the fax, the Slow Scan TV link (M4, M8); the sculpture gives way to tension, feedback and heat (M6, M7); and the image gives way to the invisible field around it. The through-line, in his own words, is "forms and forces that are visually imperceptible and yet perform an important role in the structure of the visible world", and bodies "transformed into pure energy". The barrier that reroutes instead of blocking (M3) is the one mechanism he shares whole with the collective; the copy as cave painting (M5) is the one place he looks backward, to the oldest image, while everything else looks forward to the next transmission.

The watchability follows from that changing machine. A body pressed on the glass (M1) gives an edge decided by contact, not by a drawn line; a statue hooded overnight (M2) makes the city look at what it walks past; a bright line across a crossing (M3) hands the decision to whoever meets it; one bar sent through three channels (M4) disagrees with itself in front of the viewer; a handprint made by toner (M5) is the first image and the last copy in the same gesture; an object held in the air by a circuit (M6) replaces the pedestal with a loop; a body rendered as its own heat (M7) makes the invisible field the portrait; and two distant points linked by nothing but a shared hour (M8) make the transmission itself uncertain.

What he is not: not a painter (the early pictorial panel in Triptico is a panel on a sidewalk, not a painting practice); not a gallery artist in the 1980s (the galleries promoted neo-expressionism; his technology work showed away from the mainstream, including the Sao Paulo Bienal); not a Vallauri contact (no tie evidenced); not a late-style artist (there is no late thinning -- the post-1998 record is simply thinly documented, and the study says so rather than inventing a late phase); not a collector's artist with verified museum holdings (none found). The closest kin in the studied corpus is Hudinilson, who also lets a machine do the picturing -- but Hudinilson's machine is the copier, one body pressed on the glass until the machine turns it into pattern, while Ramiro's machine keeps changing: copier, videotex, fax, electromagnet, laser, Schlieren. His operation is his own: press the body, draw one cheap line across the city, let the viewer decide whether to cross it, send one form through three channels at once, re-enact the cave painting with toner, build the loop and let the force hold the form up, photograph the heat, and take the wires out of the network to see what still arrives. (C17336)


## Works inspected (W01-W20)

Grades: A = strong mechanism evidence from a real source read end to end; B = decent (reliable secondary source); C = weak/second-hand. 20 works verified, 0 invented, 0 C-grade: 19 A, 1 B. (The single B is W02, Prisioneiro 2, known only from a book illustration list seen via search summary.)

- W01 Sufocacao (Suffocation), 1979, xerographic print -- A. A body compressed against the photocopier surface, the image multiplied in series. Shown in "Brazil in Layers" at Zipper Galeria, Sao Paulo (18 June-18 December 2026); the show text calls Ramiro "a pioneer in the use of xerographic prints" and reads the technical precariousness of the medium as a measure of political censorship under the dictatorship: "the image, multiplied in series, inscribes in the reproduction itself the sensation of asphyxiation that gives the work its title". GUARD: this is NOT Hudinilson's Sufoco (#679 W05) -- two different 1979 works by two different artists with near-identical titles. Do not merge them. (M1, M5)
- W02 Prisioneiro 2 (Prisoner 2), 1979, xerox -- B. Listed and illustrated (fig. 4.5) in Mari Rodriguez Binnie, "The Sao Paulo Neo-Avant-Garde" (University of Texas Press, 2024), chapter 4 on 3NOS3 and xerox art. Title only from the book's illustration list, seen via search summary; the image was not inspected and no description beyond the listing was read. Kept at B and not built on alone. It corroborates W01: Ramiro's 1979 xerox work already used the pressed, confined body. (M1)
- W03 Ensacamento (Bagging), 27 April 1979, 3NOS3 urban intervention, plastic garbage bags over statues [COLLECTIVE] -- A. The group's inaugural action: between midnight and 05:00 the three hooded the heads of public monuments with black or blue trash bags tied closed with rope, "like a noose, simulating strangulation", starting at the Monument to the Independence of Brazil. Ramiro's angle: his own 1998 text calls these "stains" on the urban architectural fiber and "drawings on the city map", done clandestinely in the early morning, removed by police or firefighters, existing afterwards mainly as press and television stories. DATE CONFLICT, carried: Engramma dates it 27 April 1979; the USP thesis (via #679) 14-15 March 1979; Momus (via #679) overnight 26 April 1979; the Gygax press release 29 April. COUNT CONFLICT, carried: "some sixty" (Engramma) vs 68 (KBCB via #679; Gygax). Do not merge into one date or one count. (M2, M3)
- W04 Triptico (Triptych), 24-26 August 1979, 3NOS3 urban intervention, three painted panels and assemblage [COLLECTIVE] -- A. Three panels, 2.20 x 1.40 m each, arranged as a triangular ensemble on the sidewalk in front of the Theatro Municipal. Each artist claimed one panel: Ramiro and Franca worked pictorially, Hudinilson used assemblage. The collective's longest intervention, three days until the Sanitation Department removed the screens. Ramiro's angle: his panel is the pictorial one; the piece was largely ignored as art, read as theatre advertising, used as a bulletin board, and "ripped apart and nearly destroyed" -- the work's reception as urban debris is part of its mechanism. (M2)
- W05 Interdicao (Interdiction), 21 September 1979, 3NOS3 action, bands of colored cellophane across roadways [COLLECTIVE] -- A. From 16:00 the group closed a section of Avenida Paulista near MASP by stretching bands of blue, yellow, brown and red plastic across the roadway, then stood motionless like street vendors when the light turned green, leaving motorists to decide whether to cross. Ramiro's own account: "diverse bands of colored plastic material obstructed the intersections near the Sao Paulo Museum of Art. The intersections remained closed off until a motorist had the initiative to drive through the plastic, freeing up the flow of traffic". When motorists asked what to do, Ramiro replied: "You, sir, are the one who knows the answer". (M2, M3)
- W06 Interversao VI, night of 15 June 1980, 3NOS3 urban intervention, 100 m red plastic roll [COLLECTIVE] -- A. The first action to bear the group's neologism "interversao" (intervention + inversion, defined by the group in 1981 as "the act of inverting; changing the natural or habitual order"). A 100-metre-long, 4-metre-wide roll of red plastic laid across the two large oval cavities ("buracos") connecting Avenida Paulista with Avenida Consolacao, descending from Paulista into Consolacao and returning to the surface -- "similar to sewing", a slow stitch binding two sides of a membrane. Chosen for its relation to the Nova Paulista redevelopment plans. Lasted only a few hours before military police, fire brigade and transport department restored order. The plastic was produced exclusively for 3Nos3 by Plastic Five and its entrepreneur Guacira Quinto Malatesta. (M2, M3)
- W07 Arco 10 (Arc 10), night of 7 December 1981, 3NOS3 urban intervention, yellow cellophane arc over a viaduct [COLLECTIVE] -- A. A 100-metre roll of yellow cellophane suspended from the viaduct along Avenida Doutor Arnaldo, hovering above Avenida Sumare below -- a line linking vertical and horizontal space, "bridging separation without touching the ground". Ramiro's own account: "a large, yellow plastic structure installed on one of the highest viaducts in the city, whose removal by the authorities was a great attraction covered by the press". Police closed the road after drivers feared the arc would obstruct traffic, "causing far greater obstruction than the installation had posed". (M2, M3)
- W08 Passes de Magica (Magic Stunts), early 1980s, xerox sequences -- A. Xerographic sequences "similar to storyboards" dealing with "the appearance and disappearance of objects and body parts, as in old magic shows". Other sequences explored ambiguities between the flat paper plane and three-dimensional space, "practically impossible to register on equipment designed to copy flat surfaces". Ramiro's own description. Date approximate ("the beginning of the 1980s"); no single year stated. (M1, M5)
- W09 Geoescul, series, 1982, wooden geometric wall sculptures -- A. Ramiro's first named sculpture series: wooden geometric wall sculptures "that revealed the presence of a physical force upon the material by making use of mechanical tensions to shape a rectangle". The hinge between the flat xerox work and the later levitation and thermal sculptures: from image of a force to a force acting on matter. (M6)
- W10 Estruturas de Especulacao (Structures of Speculation), 1982, geometric flat sculptures, shown at 3NOS3/3ANOS, Pinacoteca do Estado, 19 May-6 June 1982 -- A. Ramiro's individual contribution to the collective's museum show: "a series of geometric flat sculptures engaging an intramuseal synchronization of neoplasticism and land art" (Engramma), installed in a museum display case alongside Hudinilson's Posicao amorosa and Franca's video Carta 23. The street's flat plastic geometry brought indoors and set against the museum. (M2)
- W11 Videotex works, 1982, videotex images, shown at the Museum of Image and Sound (MIS), Sao Paulo -- A. Ramiro's first videotex works: "small series of images showing the dematerialization of objects", presented at MIS Sao Paulo in 1982. Among the earliest Brazilian artworks made on the videotex system. (M4)
- W12 Clones -- uma rede simultanea de radio, televisao e videotexto (Clones -- A Simultaneous Radio, Television and Videotex Network), 1983, with Jose W. Garcia, intermedia network event, MIS Sao Paulo -- A. One object -- a horizontal red bar -- transmitted simultaneously through three systems: on nine videotex terminals it appeared as a flat graphic displaced on a 2D grid; on two TV monitors the same bar, received from a live broadcast in the city, moved in perspective and slowly went out of focus, losing its rigid shape; on radio it was given sound by dropping a 1-metre, 1-cm-diameter iron bar from a height of 1 metre, recording the impact and manipulating it with an analog synthesizer (after Duchamp's "3 Stoppages Etalon"). The 4-minute work was installed in a circular room at MIS with videotex terminals, TV monitors, radio and speakers. (M4)
- W13 Lascaux Copy, 1984, photocopy toner applied manually to a wall, fixed thermally with an iron ("xerographic mural") -- A. Toner used instead of natural pigment: a hand reproduced with a standard photocopier presenting "the same characteristics of a cut-out image on a dark, pigmented background seen in the original Lascaux palm print" -- a white, negative hand against a dark ground. First presented in 1984; performed again live at the University of San Diego in September 2017 (event listing, summary only). (M5)
- W14 Tempo em 8 (Time in 8), 1984, installation with photoelectric cell, water tank, parabolic dish and mirror -- A. A photoelectric cell activated by the observer's presence controlled a regulator connected to a water tank on the ceiling; drops fell into a parabolic dish on the floor, and the mechanized waves on the dish's surface were reflected by a flat mirror at eye level. Ramiro's own account: a "chain reaction of waves" -- the observer breaks invisible infrared waves, gravity drops the water, a mechanical wave forms, and its image returns to the observer as visible light. (M6)
- W15 Gravidade Zero (Zero Gravity), 1986, glass, brass and wood, electromagnetic levitation sculpture -- A. An electromagnet regulated by a photoelectric sensor levitates small metallic objects: a light beam crosses the upper part of the object and hits a light-dependent resistor, which drives an amplifier connected to the electromagnet; if the object falls, more light reaches the sensor and magnetic force increases; if it rises too high, the force decreases. A feedback loop that holds a body in the air -- sculpture freed from its base and from "above" and "below", the support replaced by a circuit. (M6)
- W16 Altamira, 1986, Slow Scan TV (SSTV) performance-installation, Sky Art Conference -- A. A bidirectional SSTV network created by Sao Paulo artists and MIT's Center for Advanced Visual Studies, transmitting sounds and still video images over the telephone. Ramiro's installation, a "media cave", was set in the unfinished concrete skeleton of the Museum of Contemporary Art of USP: a large projection screen behind which the ballerina Laly Krotoszynski performed under flashing lights, her movements "a primitive ritual dance around a bonfire", accompanied by rhythmic percussion on a sheet of metal and electronic music from a large steel object similar in shape to Sputnik. Named after the prehistoric cave in Spain; "Altamira" also read as a high point to fix one's sights on, referring to a satellite in orbit. (M5, M8)
- W17 Retrato Suposto -- Rosto Roto (Presumed Portrait -- Foul Face), 1988, with Eduardo Kac, fax machines and live TV transmission -- A. A live event: Ramiro in the studios of a TV Cultura youth variety program in Sao Paulo, connected by fax to Eduardo Kac in his Rio de Janeiro studio. Ramiro faxed a repertoire of images -- eyes, mouths, noses, ears -- for a potential portrait ("Retrato Suposto"); minutes later Kac faxed back a montage ("Rosto Roto"). The whole exchange happened on the air, though the TV program only reached Sao Paulo state, so Kac could not monitor the broadcast. Kac, quoted by Ramiro: the link "was not a 'work' in the strict sense... It was an esthetic investigation that subverted the automatic and passive use of telecommunications media". (M4, M8)
- W18 Entre o Norte e o Sul (Between North and South), summer 1992, with Morio Labonete Nishimura, point-to-point link Finland-Greece with no electronic equipment -- A. A "radical teleart experience" of "scenic teletransport": the two artists, both living in Dusseldorf, travelled to opposite ends of Europe -- Nishimura to Lieksa, Finland, Ramiro to Amorgos, Greece -- each building an installation with a glass "antenna", a vial of Rhine river water containing a filament of their own hairs connected by gold leaf. At a predetermined day and hour each tried to transmit an image telepathically and drew whatever came to mind. Compared afterwards in Dusseldorf: of Ramiro's eight drawings, two were chalices similar to the lotus Nishimura had transmitted; of Nishimura's five, two showed a horizontal cross and a circle of fire similar to the burning sword Ramiro had transmitted. Inspired by the roughly 500 Asian towers holding relics of the Buddha, believed to form a network beyond time and space. (M8)
- W19 Light Turbulences, first series of lasergrams, c. 1993-1996, laser photography -- A. "Lasergrams": photograms made by laser light on color photographic paper in the laser and photography laboratory of the Academy of Media Arts, Cologne. The photographed object sits INSIDE the camera, between film and lens; transparent objects project shadows as dark as opaque ones; heat appears as sinuous forms. Negative processing yields colors complementary to the laser: a green laser gives reddish-magenta, a blue laser gives yellow-orange-red. Unlike the earlier thermal sculptures, which revealed invisible space, this series "revealed images of bodies in movement, rising and falling amidst flames" -- heat as fire, purification or destruction. Dating inferred (move to Germany end of 1991; second Schlieren series began 1996); no exact year stated. (M7)
- W20 Atlas, o suporte do mundo (Atlas, Support of the World), c. 1997-1998, installation with thermal sculptures, Schlieren photographs and live tortoises -- A. Based on an age-old Chinese myth of the Earth as a flat disk held up by a tortoise shell of cosmic proportions. The installation combined thermal sculptures (Escudos / Shields) activated by motion sensors, Schlieren photographs, and an area defined by granite blocks inside which three live tortoises moved about on a heat field produced by light reflectors (reptiles need light to store heat, being cold-blooded). Ramiro's most recent work at the time of writing (1998). (M6, M7)

Further documented works (not numbered, all verified): X-Galeria, night of 2 June 1979 [COLLECTIVE] (27 galleries plus MASP sealed with a masking-tape X; leaflets "O que esta dentro fica / O que esta fora se expande"); Coneccao, 13 May 1981 [COLLECTIVE] (25-metre roll of glossy red polyethylene from a ventilation shaft at Avenida Reboucas / Avenida Dr. Arnaldo); 27.4/3 - II, 1982 [COLLECTIVE] (horizontal red plastic band across the Pinacoteca facade, window to window, at a height that let visitors enter while compelling them to lower their bodies); artist's book, 1979 (photographic image of a hand approaching a white band; page sequence emphasizing temporal dislocation "similar to the succession of frames in a movie"); Os ultimos registros de Lascaux (Lascaux's Last Records), 1991 (images superimposed without spatial order, a "temporal compression"); Campo de Forca (Force Field), series, 1986-1991 (steel, copper, ceramic sculptures with a radiating heat source inside; heat waves "model" the space around the object, an invisible volume felt through the skin); Escudos (Shields), series, c. 1986-1991 (round wall objects of wood, aluminum, nylon, steel cable and 110-V resistors); Schlieren photographs, white-light series, from 1996 (human figure "seen simultaneously as material and evanescent"); Level 5 contribution, 1984 (CAD virtual-3D project; mechanical waves as color-plotter prints and video animations); telephone answering-machine course work, 1987 (students set answering machines to induce callers to leave improvised lyrical, noisy or theatrical replies; unwitting non-participant callers also contributed). Exhibition-level only (no individual work titles found): Cine-Roboticismo (2001), Ruido Branco (2013), Abrakadabra (2014), Improvavel (2017), Caixa Preta (2018), Arte Veiculo (2018); Kurokos / Mobile Radio BSP at the 30th Sao Paulo Biennial (2012). (C17337, leg 2 C17150-C17157; synthesis C17258)

Early vs late arc: Early (1979-1982, copier and street): the pressed body on the copier glass (W01, W02) and the cheap flat line across the city (W03-W07), the collective's night actions under the dictatorship, the work gone by morning and surviving as press and photographs. Middle (1982-1991, telecom and force): videotex and the three-channel Clones (W11, W12), the fax portrait with Kac (W17), the SSTV media cave (W16), and the sculpture that lets tension, feedback and heat do the carving (W09, W14, W15). Germany (1991-1998, the invisible field): telepathy as protocol (W18), lasergrams and Schlieren light (W19), tortoises on a heat field (W20). Late (1998 onward): no titled work with a medium was found -- only the exhibition record, the teaching post, the band and the sound performances, and the 2025 curating and Biel inclusion. What changes across the arc is the machine -- copier, then street plastic, then videotex, fax and SSTV, then electromagnet and heat, then laser and Schlieren -- and the scale, from a body on a glass plate to a network spanning two ends of Europe. What does not change is the operation: take a force the eye cannot see, give it a cheap material to work through, and let the transmission, the loop or the heat do the picturing. There is no late thinning or late style -- only a thinly documented later record. (C17338; synthesis C17258, C17273)
