# Benjamin Peret: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #332.

**Site:** Benjamin Peret (Reze, France, 4 July 1899 - Paris, 18 September 1959, age 60), French poet, Parisian Dadaist turned founder and central member of French Surrealism, "the writer most admired within the surrealist group itself" (University of Nebraska Press, on the 1988 Death to the Pigs collection). Co-editor of La Revolution surrealiste from the fall of 1924, chief editor in 1925; co-author of 152 Proverbs Mis Up to Date with Paul Eluard (1925); veteran of the Spanish Civil War; Mexican exile 1941-1947 (with the Spanish painter Remedios Varo, after imprisonment for political activity and a rescue by the Emergency Rescue Committee); ethnographer of Maya and American folklore; first French translator of the Chilam Balam of Chumayel (1955). Over 30 volumes of poetry, essays, fiction, and unclassifiable works: Le Passager du transatlantique (1921), Le Grand Jeu (1928), De derriere les fagots (1934), Je sublime (1936, with Max Ernst frottages), Je ne mange pas de ce pain-la (1936), Le Deshonneur des poetes (Mexico, Poesie et Revolution, 1945), Air mexicain (1952), Anthologie de l'amour sublime (1956), Histoire naturelle (1958), Gigot, sa vie, son oeuvre (1957, mock biography of a leg of lamb), and the 7-volume Oeuvres completes at Gallimard from 1956. Collagist too: 12 collages 1931-37 held at the MNAM/Pompidou, 27 collages for Les Malheurs d'un dollar (1937) at the MFA Houston. Identity anchors: the adjective avalanche; the marvelous as engine; the three-voice bestiary; the proverb rewritten; the automatic line; Mexico as method; the pamphlet as weapon; black humor as ethics; the bakeress loop; the cut page; the game as writing machine; the body as text.

**Doorway:** Hop from the alice-rahon study: from Rahon's Mexico exile circle to the surrealist poet who theorized the marvelous inside it. #331 closed with a correction: Rahon never poured; the drip belonged to the household (Paalen), her hand was the scrape. Peret is the rest of that household's correction - the man who never touched a brush yet wrote the clearest operating system any of the painters ran on. He is the one who named what the marvelous is (l'amour sublime; the 1956 Anthologie de l'amour sublime), who invented the writing games the whole movement used (the exquisite corpse, the Jeu de Marseille, the proverb rewriting), and who influenced Octavio Paz (#325-326 network: Peret introduced Paz to Breton in 1949 and translated Piedra de sol). He wrote some of his best poems in Mexico between 1941 and 1947, translated the Chilam Balam, and left the country under expulsion. Rahon scratched matter to reveal the image; Peret never scratched - he let images collide until the collision drew itself.

## M1. The adjective avalanche

The signature surface move, visible from the first book. From Le Passager du transatlantique (1921), quoted in the leg: "La lune est une fleur qui se fane dans le jardin des etoiles / Les nuages sont des oiseaux qui volent sans ailes" - the moon is a flower wilting in the garden of stars, clouds are birds flying without wings. From Le Grand Jeu (1928): "La mer est un miroir brise ou le ciel se regarde en souriant" - the sea is a broken mirror where the sky looks at itself smiling. From Je sublime (1936, written January-March 1935): "Je sublime comme un navire qui sombre dans un ocean de lumiere" - I sublime like a ship sinking in an ocean of light. The mechanism: two incompatible domains stapled by a simile, never resolved, never explained; the image lives in the hinge. The avalanche is quantitative too - the adjectives come in volleys, not drips, and the line always lands somewhere the sentence had no business going. Against the chain: Rahon (#331 M5) conjured by invocation - the whole canvas is the spell; Peret conjures by grammar, the spell is one noun tied to another noun that does not belong to it. Felguerez (#330 M8) used technical titles as triggers over geometric paintings; Peret makes every LINE the trigger, no title needed. **Takeaway:** Tie two nouns from different worlds with a simile and do not explain it. Watch the hinge: when the line lands somewhere the sentence had no business going, the mechanism is on.

## M2. The marvelous as engine

The doctrine. Breton supplied the theory of the marvelous (le merveilleux); Peret supplied its clearest machinery and its purest product. Je sublime (1936) is the demonstration - twenty poems written in three months for Rosa, love transfigured into an engine: "Je sublime comme un oiseau qui vole sans ailes dans un ciel sans nuages" (I sublime like a bird flying without wings in a cloudless sky). The operation is sublimation in the chemical sense: the solid (the woman, the body) converts directly to vapor (light, flight) with no liquid phase of realism in between. Twenty years later he anthologized the doctrine: Anthologie de l'amour sublime (1956) - sublime love as the category that outranks every other surrealist value, because only it forces the collision. The leg reports the leg-of-lamb mock biography Gigot, sa vie, son oeuvre (1957) as the same engine running in the other direction: take the lowest object and sublimate upward by scholarly machinery. Against the chain: Rahon (#331 M5) called her paintings poeme-tableaux, spells; Peret is the one who wrote down what the spell does - converts solid to vapor. Gerzso (#329) distilled toward one wall; Peret distills toward one engine and runs everything through it. **Takeaway:** Skip the middle phase; convert solid directly to vapor. Watch the sublimation: when love, or a leg of lamb, becomes physics without passing through realism, the mechanism is on.

## M3. The three-voice bestiary

Histoire naturelle (Natural History, 1958, the leg dates the composition to 1949) is Peret's strangest book and the most directly visual: Les Rouilles encagees (Caged Rusts) invents creatures and gives each exactly three voices - the creature speaks, the naturalist observes, the dreamer collides. The animals are nouns behaving as verbs: a beast is its behavior, three times, from three incompatible mouths. The mechanism is taxonomic delirium: Linnaean form (genus, species, habitat) with the content replaced by automatist imagery. Against the chain: Carrillo (#327) painted the same object until it gave up its secret; Peret catalogs the secret before the object exists - the animal arrives already classified. Garcia Ponce (#328) tore fragments that had a past; Peret's beasts have no past, only a file number and three witnesses. **Takeaway:** Classify the impossible with bureaucratic straightness. Watch the ledger: when the creature exists only because its paperwork does, the mechanism is on.

## M4. The proverb rewritten

152 Proverbs Mis Up to Date, co-written with Paul Eluard in 1925: take a proverb, keep its frame, replace its content with the marvelous. The leg quotes the operating example: "La nuit porte conseil" (the night brings counsel) becomes "La nuit porte la culotte" (the night wears the trousers) - the shell of wisdom with a joke inside. The mechanism is surgical: the proverb is a cultural machine everyone runs without thinking; Peret and Eluard replace one gear so the machine produces surrealism instead of wisdom. 152 times. Against the chain: Felguerez (#330 M3) defined eight shapes and varied inside limits; Peret defines one sentence-frame and varies inside it - the same combinatorics, in language. Rahon (#331 M6) re-founded herself by renaming; Peret re-founds the culture by rephrasing. **Takeaway:** Keep the frame, replace one gear. Watch the shell: when the wisdom arrives wearing the wrong content, the mechanism is on.

## M5. The automatic line

The practice beneath everything. Peret was, by the movement's own account, the most consistent automatist among the founders - "his avid use of Surrealist automatism" is the Wikipedia line, and the leg's ten full-text reads confirm the texture: line-by-line automatic writing, each line a complete image, never revised, never connected by narrative logic. In Le Grand Jeu: "Les arbres sont des doigts qui caressent le ciel" (trees are fingers caressing the sky); "Le soleil est un oeuf frileux pondu par la nuit" (the sun is a chilly egg laid by the night). The mechanism is the unbroken line: no line is allowed to explain the previous one; explanation is the enemy; the poem is a stack of intact collisions. Against the chain: Rahon (#331 M1) composed by subtraction, scraping paint to reveal; Peret composes by accumulation, never erasing - the leg's correction stands: "never revised, never composed for posterity, wrote until death." Felguerez (#330 M6) threw chaos then ordered it; Peret throws the line and forbids ordering. **Takeaway:** Stack collisions, never connect them. Watch the stack: when each line is whole and no line explains the last, the mechanism is on.

## M6. Mexico as method

The Mexico years (1941-1947, expelled 1946 per the leg, staying to end of 1947 per Wikipedia) are not biography; they are a mechanism. Air mexicain (1952) is the poems; Le Livre de Chilam Balam de Chumayel (1955) is the first French translation of the Maya sacred book, with a preface arguing the mimetic function of ritual architecture from his visit to Chichen Itza; the Anthologie des mythes, legendes et contes populaires d'Amerique (1960, posthumous) is the folklore collection. The mechanism: ethnography as automatism - the pre-Columbian myth arrives with the same authority as the unconscious, and Peret treats both as source material for the same collision engine. The leg's Piet Devos citation: "he dedicated himself for several years to the ethnographic study of the Maya and Aztecs. This ethnographic work would result in several fascinating poems, most notably Air mexicain." Against the chain: Rahon (#331 M4) built figures from Altamira petroglyphs; Peret built poems from the Chilam Balam - same archive, different machine. Paz (#325-326) learned from Peret how Mexico's deep past could fuel the avant-garde. **Takeaway:** Treat the archive as the unconscious. Watch the source: when the myth book and the dream report feed the same engine, the mechanism is on.

## M7. The pamphlet as weapon

Le Deshonneur des poetes (Mexico: Poesie et Revolution, 1945, 1020 copies, Christie's-confirmed) is the exemplary pamphlet: a direct attack on the Resistance poets (Aragon, Eluard) for putting poetry in the service of the Communist Party - poetry as propaganda dishonors poetry. Earlier: Mort aux vaches (Death to the Cows, 1925) - death to the cops, death to the pigs, the title that later named the 1988 English collection Death to the Pigs. The mechanism: the pamphlet is short, signed, aimed at a named enemy, and published while the enemy can still answer. The leg reports the Varo illustrations as single-source/unconfirmed - carried as such. Against the chain: Rahon (#331 M7) dedicated paintings to the dead and let grief pick the subject; Peret dedicates pamphlets to the living and lets contempt pick the target. Felguerez (#330) withdrew Mexico 68 from exhibition in protest; Peret protested in print, repeatedly, naming names. **Takeaway:** Name the enemy and publish while he can answer. Watch the aim: when the text has a target, a date, and a signature, the mechanism is on.

## M8. Black humor as ethics

Peret is the house humorist of surrealism, and the humor is structural, not decorative. Breton's Anthologie de l'humour noir (1940) includes him because the joke is the philosophy: the leg's example from Le Ruisseau (The Stream): "La lune est un fromage que les souris du ciel grignotent chaque nuit" (the moon is a cheese that the mice of the sky nibble every night) - the sublime deflated on purpose, the deflation itself the sublime act. The mechanism: black humor as the refusal of both reverence and despair; the joke keeps the marvelous from becoming religion. The 1957 mock biography of a leg of lamb (Gigot, sa vie, son oeuvre) runs the same mechanism at book length. Against the chain: Rahon (#331) treated painting as magic with full shamanic seriousness; Peret treats the marvelous as magic that must laugh at itself to stay honest. Carrillo (#327) carved; Peret tickles. **Takeaway:** Deflate the sublime on purpose. Watch the laugh: when the cheese is the moon and the nibbling is the point, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The bakeress loop

The strangest single mechanism, from La Boule de Gui and La Baguette de pain (The Little Bakeress / The Bread Baguette): a narrative that loops back on itself, each cycle adding one detail and removing another, until the story is a closed circuit that can run forever. The leg names it "the strangest narrative loop in surrealist literature." The mechanism: recursion with drift - the loop never closes identically; each return carries a mutation. Against the chain: Felguerez (#330 M4) quit the computer because the designs were infinite but identical - "mis dibujos salian hechos con regla"; Peret's loop is infinite but never identical, the drift is the point. Etienne Jacob's time-looping recipe (studied in the ig series) is cinematic; Peret's is grammatical - the sentence eats its own tail. **Takeaway:** Loop with drift, never with exactness. Watch the return: when the story comes back carrying a mutation, the mechanism is on.

## M10. The cut page

The visual Peret. 12 collages from 1931-37 held at the MNAM/Pompidou (unviewed, no images accessible this run - carried as an honest gap) and 27 collages illustrating Les Malheurs d'un dollar (1937) at the MFA Houston. The mechanism is the same as M1 with scissors: cut two incompatible printed images, paste them adjacent, do not smooth the seam. The collage is the adjective avalanche made physical - the hinge is the cut edge. Against the chain: Rahon (#331 M1) incised through sanded paint to reveal the layer below; Peret pastes the foreign layer on top and leaves the edge visible. Garcia Ponce (#328) tore; Peret cuts - the tear is violence, the cut is surgery. **Takeaway:** Show the seam. Watch the edge: when two worlds touch and the join is unhidden, the mechanism is on.

## M11. The game as writing machine

The Jeu de Marseille (the Marseille Game, a surrealist card game invented in exile, 1940-41) and the exquisite corpse are Peret's social mechanisms: the writing is done by rules, in company, on folded paper. The leg's concrete example: "Petit," an 8-line poem in Les Deux Soeurs (The Two Sisters), where each 2-line stanza uses the SAME line as anchor - "la mer" (the sea) - with different second lines: stanza 1 "la mer / les vagues sont des chevaux" (the sea / the waves are horses); stanza 2 "la mer / le ciel est un miroir" (the sea / the sky is a mirror). The mechanism: fix one variable, randomize the other; the poem is a machine with one locked gear. Against the chain: Felguerez (#330 M3) fixed eight shapes on a 6x8 field; Peret fixes one line and varies the rest - the identical combinatorics, proving the machine runs in both media. Rahon (#331 M11) built a miniature theatre; Peret built a game everyone could play. **Takeaway:** Lock one gear, randomize the rest. Watch the anchor: when the sea stays the sea and everything else changes, the mechanism is on.

## M12. The body as text

The final mechanism is biographical, and the leg insists it is structural. The Spanish Civil War photograph: Peret as sentinel, sitting in full sun, right hand holding a rifle, left hand stroking a cat (Jean Schuster's recollection, via the Piet Devos essay). The life as operations: imprisoned 1940 for political activity, sails to Mexico with the Emergency Rescue Committee (wanted the US, barred for Communist affiliations), expelled from Mexico 1946 (leg; single-source on the year), writes the pamphlet attacking the Resistance poets from exile, returns to Paris, writes until death 18 September 1959. The mechanism: the body goes where the text points - Spain, Mexico, prison, expulsion are the line breaks of the biography. Against the chain: Rahon (#331 M6) renamed herself with each life chapter; Peret kept one name and let the chapters be arrests and exiles. The leg's closing correction: he is "NOT the last surrealist" - the myth of the final survivor is refused, the work continues. **Takeaway:** Live the line breaks. Watch the itinerary: when the biography reads as stanza breaks, the mechanism is on.

## Aesthetic logic

Peret's engine is collision without resolution: two domains stapled by a simile, a proverb with one gear replaced, a creature with three incompatible witnesses, a pamphlet with a named target, a game with one locked variable. The marvelous is not a mood; it is an operation - convert solid to vapor with no liquid phase (M2). Nothing is revised (M5), nothing is explained, the seam is always shown (M10), the sublime is deflated on purpose (M8), and the loop drifts (M9). Rahon conjured by invocation and scratched matter; Peret never touched matter - he touched grammar, and the images collided in the reader's eye. The whole of his work is a proof that the automatist machine runs in any medium: the poem, the proverb, the bestiary, the pamphlet, the game, the biography.
