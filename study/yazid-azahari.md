## The life: from nowhere

Almost nothing about the person is public beyond what he chose to say in one interview, and the study does not fill the gaps. The evidenced record: Yazid Azahari, based in Brunei (every source agrees; no city given). X @Yazid, Instagram @mintedbyyazid (the Instagram handle from Art For Art only; the page was not opened, see C1647). His own site, yazidazahari.com, is a minimal landing page. On objkt he describes himself as "Experimental Artist / Generative Art". He majored in Interactive Media at university and studied Human-Computer Interaction. He began as a graphic designer, then became a software developer building functional applications and websites, freelance and side projects included. The Verse interviewer addresses him as: "In the day, you're a head of innovation and a father!" No source names the employer. No source gives a birth date or an age, and the study invents neither (C1636).

The origin story is his own, told in the Verse interview, and it is the spine of the study. As a teenager he made things with Flash: "It almost takes me back to a time in my teens when I used to love creating things with Flash. It was where I discovered ActionScript, which might've been one of my earliest introductions to generative art, though back then I didn't even know that's what it was." That teenage passion led him to major in Interactive Media, "but due to the local market and industry, I ended up pursuing a more pragmatic career in software development." More than a decade later, while researching blockchain technologies, he rediscovered generative art: "I was pleased to learn that the same skills that I had obtained over the years could be used for more than just purely utilitarian reasons like maintaining records and calculating taxes. I felt like I had found something that helped me marry both the creative and analytical parts of my brain." In February 2021 he minted his first work (title and platform unknown; fxhash did not launch until November 2021, so the first mint was elsewhere -- see C1657). He has said plainly that "accepting the identity of an artist was (and in many ways is still) something I struggled with."

Two of his sentences carry the whole practice. On distance: "I grew up with the internet but far away from the typical epicenters of technology, so all the developments I used to see almost seemed like fiction, like a movie for me. Having that distance has probably shielded me from some of the more techno-dystopian aspects so I'm left with more of a romanticist view of it." On the machine: "I used to be fascinated by the ability to make a computer do something I told it to. Now I'm finding ways to make it do things I didn't tell it to." And on what the technology is for: "I also see it as an equalizing force. It gives someone like me from nowhere a chance at participating." The phrase "someone like me from nowhere" is the study's spine because it is the only self-placement in the record: not a biography, a position -- far from the epicenters, romantic about the machine, and inside the room anyway.

## The practice: mornings, nights, weekends

The practice conditions are the practice. He makes art "in the early mornings, late nights, weekends, basically any opportunity I can find in between" -- a head of innovation by day, a father, and the art fitted into the gaps. The method matches the conditions: "Yazid mentions how he does not follow a specific guideline but rather constantly does random explorations, which end up less about the outcome but more about the process and the practice." "In most cases, he repurposes some code created throughout the process to achieve a particular goal -- a sort of prompt that drives him towards experimentation, interpretation, and rework before execution and the final outcome." And: "I enjoy the process of immersing myself in code. Sometimes being in this state reveals unexpected parallels with my own personal experiences."

The stack is documented and narrow: JavaScript and SVG, vanilla JS, custom scripts. Art For Art: "He employs code, algorithms, and systems -- primarily JavaScript, SVG, and custom scripts -- to generate abstract compositions." Sotheby's lists (kinder)Garden, Monuments as "Vanilla JS". Split is JavaScript + SVG, built on a polygon-clipping algorithm refined over weeks. p5.js is evidenced for one project only: Hashed Cities, whose fxhash iteration page says it was made in p5.js (see C1691). Do not claim p5.js as his general stack (C1632). No source gives code-level detail for any project -- no repo, no code walkthrough; Split's polygon-clipping and the coordinates-first method are described only at the prose level.

His named influences are consistent across sources: the early computer artists Manfred Mohr, Michael Noll, Georg Nees, Vera Molnar; "simplified flat design, 60s graphic design, geometry and mathematical visualizations". The HCI background is not a credential in the story; it is a subject. He studied how the earliest graphical interfaces were designed with familiar metaphors in mind (desktop, folders, trash can), and he keeps the keyword from that training: "feel". "Generating a feeling. That space between analog and digital, man and machine, is in many ways still very much blurry so it provides quite a latent space for exploring opportunities to evoke feelings." The blur between analog and digital is the room his whole oeuvre stands in.

## The evolution: arcs to split

February 2021 is the first mint, title and platform unknown. November 2021, fxhash launches, and Yazid is there in the first weeks. Hashed Arcs (project #44) is pure system: randomized arc segments, 360 editions at 1 tez -- the minimalist thesis in its barest form. Weeks later, Hashed Cities (#1726, 30 November 2021) already adds the two moves that define everything after: time as a material (each mint has a native Day or Night setting, combined with the current time of viewing it moves celestial bodies in real time, and the piece re-renders every minute) and analog imperfection as a texture (an algorithm that generatively simulates the texture of print imperfections, randomized at each render, explicitly inspired by Grace C. Hertlein's "City Painting"). The same year, Automatism on Art Blocks asks the conceptual twin of the question: simple human-programmed instructions producing outputs that look human, even intentional. The question shifts from "what shapes?" to "what would a machine draw?"

2022 is institutional arrival and collaboration. 16 April 2022: (kinder)Garden, Monuments with Michael Zancan opens fxhash 1.0 -- the first smart-contract-level collaboration on the platform. Yazid's flat, hard-edge building blocks go three-dimensional inside Zancan's organic garden; the work is explicitly about youth, play, and refuge, and it becomes the emblematic fxhash project (later a Sotheby's lot). May 2022: Art Basel Hong Kong live-mint -- the work is now performed live for strangers scanning QR codes; 5,000-8,000 editions expected across the eight artists. December 2022: Art Basel Miami Beach (listed; see C1692) and Verse's Magical Realism: Part I -- the Windows in my Soul series moves from pure abstraction toward depiction (windows) and literary reference (Murakami), from systems about shapes to systems about a feeling and a metaphor. The window is both a magic-realist motif and the literal computer window -- the HCI background returning as subject matter.

June 2023: Split -- the polygon-clipping system changes every day; the piece is "slowly evolving", interactive, real-time. The daily-change idea was already latent in Hashed Cities (re-render every minute, sun only by day) but Split makes temporality the concept itself: division as something that happens daily, to everyone. Code "equally succinct" -- minimalism now applies to the program, not just the image. After Split, the record goes quiet: no new Yazid release surfaced in any search pass for 2024-2025. The only 2024 record is secondary-market: (kinder)Garden, Monuments #96 at Sotheby's Digital Art Day. Whether he released new work on fxhash, objkt, or Verse in that window, or worked quietly, is unverified (C1664). His X and Instagram were not directly accessed in any leg (C1665).

**Takeaway:** the evolution is a deepening, not a widening. One vocabulary -- arcs, buildings, polygons, windows -- is put through three years of operations, and the operations are the oeuvre: first the system, then time inside the system, then the system performed live, then time as the subject itself.

## M1 -- The hashed atom

The signature mechanism, and the earliest: one primitive, randomized, and nothing else. Hashed Arcs is described by the artist in a single line -- "Randomized arc segments by @Yazid" -- and that line is the whole of it. No palette, no background, no arc-count range is stated anywhere in the sources read (C1659); the minimalism is so complete that the description is the work. Hashed Cities is the same atom with a second primitive added: "a deterministic set of minimal, abstract buildings of varying widths, heights, transparencies, and patterns, assembling a skyline view of your own unique imaginary city." The arc and the building are the same idea at two scales: a small vocabulary of flat geometric primitives, a rule for placing them, and the edition as one roll of the rule. Art For Art calls Hashed Arcs "an early project featuring randomized arc segments that established his signature minimalist language", and the language never widens -- Split is still polygons, Windows in my Soul is still windows. The atom is the discipline: the piece is what one primitive does when you let it vary.

**Takeaway:** the atom is not a starting point he outgrew; it is the whole method. Every later work is the same atom with one more condition attached -- time, imperfection, a collaborator, a day.

## M2 -- Time as a material

The mechanism that separates Yazid from the other minimalists in the room. Hashed Cities does not merely depict a sky; it consults the clock. Each mint has a native setting, Day or Night, "which combined with the current time of viewing influences real-time movements of celestial bodies in your mint, e.g. on a mint with the Day setting, the sun will only appear when viewed during the day. The piece will re-render every minute." The work is never finished because the viewing time is part of it -- the same token is a different picture at noon and at midnight, and the difference is not a mode the viewer selects but a fact about when they looked. Split takes the same idea and makes it the concept: "a slowly evolving piece that changes every day", the polygon-clipping system re-cutting itself daily, "representing the myriad ways that we are divided every single day". Between the two sits Gradually, the Basel 2022 live-mint work, whose title is itself a time word -- the method described as establishing "a base of randomly-assigned coordinates on the canvas, then gradually created different visual elements until I had a whole library of them to draw from". Time enters first as a viewing condition, then as a title, then as the subject.

**Takeaway:** time is not an animation loop laid over the image; it is an input to the system, like the hash. A piece that re-renders every minute cannot be screenshotted, only visited.

## M3 -- The analog imperfection

The deliberate defect, applied with restraint. Hashed Cities carries "some of my latest developments of an algorithm that generatively simulates the texture of print imperfections. They are randomized at each render to give it a unique character. Note that depending on the complexity of each mint, some renders may introduce a brief pause." The imperfection is computed, not suffered: the machine is told to misprint, differently every time, and the pause -- the render stuttering under its own complexity -- is disclosed in advance as part of the work. Hatched Forms, known only from one line in the Verse interview, "attempted to digitally generate familiarity from imperfections of analog plotters" -- the same idea from the other side, the plotter's wobble as the thing that makes a machine drawing feel familiar. Whether Hatched Forms is a separate series or an earlier name for the same line of work is not established (C1651); the link to Hashed Cities' print-imperfection algorithm is the documented instance of the idea. The through-line is the blur from the HCI training: the space between analog and digital is "still very much blurry", and the imperfection is how the digital reaches back toward the analog without pretending to be it.

**Takeaway:** the imperfection is not noise added for warmth; it is a second system, randomized per render, running alongside the first. The machine misprints on purpose, and the purpose is the feeling.

## M4 -- The machine that draws like a human

Automatism is the question stated as a work. In Yazid's own framing, via the Verse interview: "Automatism was exploring what machines might draw" -- "a tongue-in-cheek attempt to see if I could blur that space further in a fun way. Could a set of meaningless human-programmed instructions drive a machine to produce something autonomously that not only looks human but perhaps also gives the impression that it is projecting some human-like intentions or meaning?" The joke is serious: the instructions are meaningless, the output looks meaningful, and the gap between the two is where the viewer supplies the intention. The work's own description, from the Generative Art Index side, is automatic drawing -- "where a machine's mind and 'hand' would wander" given a writing implement and a blank surface. No text description of Automatism's visual appearance was found in any source read; the Verse interview reproduces one captioned image that cannot be described from text (C1650). Tokens numbered at least to #400 were seen across Art Blocks collector profiles, so at least 400 minted; the edition cap and release date are not established, and the collection tier is unresolved -- Factory in most Art Blocks listings, Playground for one token, Curated in the Generative Art Index and some collector pages (C1689). Treat the work as 2021-22 until a primary Art Blocks project page is read (C1672). The unresolved tier matters because it is the one place where the record disagrees with itself about Yazid's standing; the study does not resolve it by picking a side.

**Takeaway:** Automatism is the only mechanism that is a question rather than a system. The arcs, the cities, the windows all do something; Automatism asks whether doing is enough to look like meaning.

## M5 -- The window as metaphor

The turn from abstraction to depiction, and the HCI background returning as subject matter. The Windows in my Soul series (2022, shown in Verse's "Magical Realism: Part I", 14 December 2022) is "about my relationship with technology. There is a duality from the 'window' wordplay, both being a visual element often featured prominently in the genre of magic realism/surrealism, as well as a concept/metaphor that still remains in the technology that we use daily. Hence you will find various depictions of windows in this series." The title riffs on Haruki Murakami on first hearing the Beach Boys and the Beatles on the radio: "their music has thrown open a new window in my soul" -- which Yazid maps onto discovering "myself and this new world through art and NFTs, each window liberating me from the constraints of reality in its own ways." The window is doing three jobs at once: the magic-realist motif, the operating-system metaphor his HCI training taught him to notice, and the liberation narrative of the NFTs themselves. Two titled members are evidenced -- Picture For Proof and The Tech Shall Set You Free, both reproduced as images in the Verse interview with no text description of their appearance (C1653, C1654). The number of works in the series, their platforms, and their edition counts are not verified (C1658), and whether the series was minted anywhere is not established (C1638). The mechanism is the move itself: after two years of arcs and buildings, the system is asked to depict something, and what it depicts is the frame it is seen through.

**Takeaway:** the window is the first subject that is not a shape. It is a metaphor about the medium, and the medium is the thing being looked through.

## M6 -- Two systems, one contract

The collaboration as a mechanism, not just a credit. (kinder)Garden, Monuments (with Michael Zancan; fxhash, released the morning of the fxhash 1.0 launch, 16 April 2022, after a month-long pause on new mints during the beta-to-1.0 hiatus) is 309 unique iterations in Vanilla JS, outputs in SVG. The Notion essay: "As (kinder)Garden, Monuments first renders, we witness the growth of a peaceful and inviting garden. Foliage and flowers steadily grow before our eyes, just as cubes, triangles, and spheres stack within the space." Zancan's grass.js garden plus Yazid's "building blocks and playful colors": "strong and sharp cubes, spheres, and prisms stacked within every scene. These vertical stacks of shapes remind us of the buildings in Yazid's Hashed Cities, now masterfully reimagined into three dimensions and bursting through the serene natural garden." The concept turns on the contrast with Zancan's own Garden, Monoliths: where those structures "convey the process of aging to eternity, the small towers here suggest ever-repeating cycles of youth"; the shapes "immediately evoke children's building blocks". The work is also a protocol first: "the first formal collaboration released on the opening morning of fxhash 1.0" and "the first project on fxhash to allow collaboration on the smart contract level" -- multiple artists releasing together and sharing revenue and royalties in perpetuity. Because the outputs are SVG, collectors were offered code to plot or print themselves, plus a print shop for signed, numbered prints from Zancan -- the screen work extending into physical plots. One iteration, #96, was offered at Sotheby's Digital Art Day 2024. Mint price and sellout details are not verified (C1661); the 309 figure is consistent across Sotheby's and the essay (C1643).

**Takeaway:** the collaboration is a mechanism because the two systems stay visible inside one frame -- the garden grows, the blocks stack, and neither absorbs the other. The smart contract is the frame that lets them share the wall.

## M7 -- Coordinates first, library second

The method, stated by the artist for the Basel work and the clearest description of how he builds anything. For Gradually he "started by first establishing a base of randomly-assigned coordinates on the canvas, then gradually created different visual elements until I had a whole library of them to draw from" -- and of the work as a whole: "there is a harmony between chaos and order." The sentence is a complete poetics in two clauses: chaos first (random coordinates, no plan), then order (a library of elements, drawn from, not imposed), and the harmony is the relation between them rather than either pole alone. It is the same method the Fakewhale Log describes generally -- no fixed guideline, random exploration, repurposed code -- now stated as a sequence. Gradually itself is the least documented of the major works: its appearance is unverifiable from text (only an animated GIF filename in the xtz.news article), its edition count unknown, and whether it is a standalone fxhash project or a Basel-only series title is not established (C1652, C1668, C1634). The title survives as a description of the method even where the images do not: gradually is how the coordinates become a library, and gradually is how the library becomes a work.

**Takeaway:** the method is the mechanism. Random coordinates, then a library, then the draw -- chaos first, order second, harmony as the relation, never as a compromise.

## M8 -- The live mint as performance

The work performed, not just released. At Art Basel Hong Kong 2022, in the fxhash live-mint installation inside "NFTs + The Ever-Evolving World of Art" (TZ APAC, Hall 1A, 27-29 May 2022, 250 square metres), visitors scanned a QR code, triggering a live mint of a 1/1 from one of the eight participating artists' series; the minted work was projected in the installation and sent to the visitor's wallet as a gift. Expected total across all eight artists: 5,000-8,000 editions. The eight, named identically in three independent reports: Yazid Azahari, Sarah Ridgley, Marcelo Soria-Rodriguez (#215, studied), Ryan Bell, Jinyao Lin (#212, studied), Aluan Wang (#211, studied), Max Oshima (Lunarean), Aleksandra Jovanic. Yazid's contribution was Gradually. The mint is the performance: the stranger, the QR code, the projection, the wallet -- the system generating in public, once, for one person, and the audience watching the generation happen rather than arriving after it. The same installation grammar returned at Art Basel Miami Beach 2022 ("Performance in Code: Deciphering Value in Generative Art", 1-3 December 2022), where Yazid is listed among the 13 additional artists and the three featured live-mint artists were Tyler Boswell, DistCollective, and Ivona Tau; which Yazid series was shown at Miami is not specified, and his Miami participation is listed, not primary-confirmed (C1692). The live mint is where the "equalizing force" stops being a sentence in an interview and becomes a room: someone who walked in off the fair floor leaves with a 1/1 in their wallet.

**Takeaway:** the live mint turns the generative system into a performance with a stranger as the audience of one. The work is not the edition; the work is the moment the edition is made in front of someone.

The network is a room, and the room is Art Basel Hong Kong 2022. ## Aesthetic logic: the line that does more with less

Yazid's aesthetic is a wager: that a smaller vocabulary, run through a better system, is more beautiful than a larger one. The vocabulary never grows -- arcs, buildings, polygons, windows -- and the systems do the growing: time enters, imperfection enters, a collaborator enters, a day enters. The palette is restrained by the same rule ("a restrained palette that highlights structure over ornament"); the code is restrained by the same rule again (Split's "underlying code is equally succinct"). The restraint is what makes each addition legible: on a busy canvas a print imperfection would be noise, on this canvas it is the event; on a complicated program a daily change would be a feature, on a succinct one it is the concept. The audience's responses track exactly this -- collectors counted in the thousands (more than 1,000 by September 2023, more than 3,000 in the later aggregator pages; dated figures, see C1629), a Sotheby's lot, a Basel installation -- for work that refuses to show off. And the artist's own account of it, the sentence he gave, is the aesthetic stated plainly: technology as "an equalizing force. It gives someone like me from nowhere a chance at participating." The work is not about minimalism; the work IS minimalism, made watchable by the one thing that keeps changing -- the time, the imperfection, the collaborator, the day.
