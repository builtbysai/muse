# Arnulfo Aquino: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-05. ig-artist-study #342.

**Site:** Arnulfo Aquino Casas (Nochixtlan, Oaxaca, 18 July 1942; d. March 2025), Mexican printmaker, member of Generacion 65, founder of Grupo Mira (1977-1982), custodian of the 1968 graphics, teacher of foto-serigrafia, author of Comunicado Grafico N. 1 (1978), the 48-panel heliographic mural that travels in a tube. Identity anchors: the poster as the answer; heliography as the portable mural; the script before the image; montage as the method; the fingerprint in the crosshairs; the recycled emblem; the quote as weapon; the archive as the work; the evaluation loop; the sound version; the workshop as the school; the house as headquarters; the book as the final project; the street as the gallery.

**Doorway:** Hop from the maris-bustamante study: from Bustamante's No-grupo, the group that met at Hersua's house, to Aquino, the Generacion 65 printmaker who built Grupo Mira in 1977 and carried the same groups movement into the street poster and the workshop. #341 closed with the woman who refused the object altogether: no ring, no monument, no wall, only a cardboard face handed to a stranger, a taco declared her legal property, a belly made of styrofoam and worn on live television. #342 opens with the man who kept the object but changed what it was for: not a thing to hang, a thing to answer with. Where Bustamante makes the artwork disappear into the hands of the audience, Aquino makes the artwork multiply into the hands of the audience, forty-eight sheets at a time, and the multiplication is the point. Where she files a patent to claim a taco, he files a comunicado to indict a city, and the filing is the work in both cases, only his filing comes back as a mural you can carry under your arm.

## M1. The poster as the answer

The work is a reply to violence, not a depiction of it. Aquino, quoted in the Guevara Romero thesis: "te estan golpeando y tu contestas con papel!" They are beating you and you answer with paper. The sentence is the whole theory of the 1968 brigades in one line: the poster exists because the beating happened, and its urgency is its medium. No claudicaremos (1968), the one 1968 poster firmly Aquino's own hand among those viewed, is built as an answer in that exact sense: a red top band shouting NO CLAUDICAREMOS, red horizontal bars like rifle barrels, a black field, a big white disc with a black 2 and OCTUBRE, red drips running down the disc like blood, MEXICO 68 at the bottom. The poster does not illustrate the 2 October massacre; it answers it, in the street, the next morning, in ink that is still wet. The same answer-engine drives the whole corpus viewed in the Casa del Tiempo renders: Mexiac's padlocked mouth, Moreno Capdevila's beaten crouching man under NO MAS AGRESION!, the halftone photograph of soldiers beating a young man shielding his head under PUEBLO DE MEXICO, ESTO ES LO QUE TE OCULTAN. Each poster is a counter-statement pasted where the beating happened, and the pasting is the reply. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M6) built the poster as an invitation, a lucha-libre flyer promising a fight and delivering masks instead of punches, and the arrival was the work; Aquino builds the poster as an answer, a reply pasted where the blow landed, and the reply is the work, and the invitation is replaced by the retort. Hersua (#340) built a ring you complete by walking through it; Aquino builds a poster you complete by reading it on the run, and the walking is replaced by the glancing. **Takeaway:** Make the poster because the beating happened, and let the pasting be the reply; the paper is the answer and the wall is where the answer goes. Watch the wall: when a poster appears overnight where a beating happened, and the ink is the counter-statement, the mechanism is on.

## M2. The image as communication, not contemplation

Efficacy over aesthetics, stated by Aquino himself as theory, not as apology. Quoted in the Hernandez Morales thesis, from the 1981 CEESTEM/IIE-UNAM meeting "El cartel politico en America": "el cartel es expresion y comunicacion grafica... en el confluyen ideologia, arte y tecnologia en un sistema de senales y simbolos que obedecen a un proceso de diseno... Corresponde a una intencion y cumple su funcion en la medida que establece relaciones entre emisor y perceptor en circunstancias especificas." The poster is graphic expression and communication; ideology, art, and technology meet in it as a system of signs and symbols obeying a design process; it answers an intention and fulfills its function to the extent that it establishes relations between sender and perceiver in specific circumstances. Two resources, named in the same passage: lenguaje formal (drawing, painting, photography, typography) and tecnologia de produccion (artisanal or industrial reproduction). The whole 1968 corpus viewed this run enacts the definition: a padlocked mouth stamped MADE IN U.S.A. (Mexiac), a dollar-sign blindfold and a snake tongue over a city skyline (Prensa corrupta), a profile of Diaz Ordaz in blue and cyan with a magenta jacket (Deslinde de responsabilidades), a fist breaking chains on a magenta field (Muerte a los que masacran). None of these is made to be contemplated; each is made to establish a relation between a sender and a perceiver in a specific circumstance, which is a street, a wall, a morning. The beauty, where there is any, is a byproduct of the signal working. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M16) told the joke first and let the argument ride in behind it, the laugh opening the ear the lecture cannot; Aquino states the communication theory first and lets the poster prove it, the signal opening the relation the contemplation cannot, and the laugh is replaced by the link. Silva (#339) gave the work away as a gift and let the dialogue keep it alive; Aquino gives the work away as a signal and lets the relation keep it alive, and the gift is replaced by the transmission. **Takeaway:** Design the image as a system of signs between a sender and a perceiver in a specific circumstance, and let the relation be the work; contemplation is what happens when the signal fails. Watch the relation: when a poster on a wall changes what a passer-by does next, and the image is the message, the mechanism is on.

## M3. Heliography as the cheap, portable mural

Neografica, the heliographic print, turns a mural into 48 sheets of 60 by 60 cm that travel in a tube, mount differently in every venue, and multiply into 9 copies. Comunicado Grafico N. 1 (1978) is a mural that refuses to be a mural: no wall, no plaster, no permanence, only sheets that can be pinned up in a union hall, a school, a museum, a colony, and taken down again. The same work is a different wall each time it is shown. The itinerary in the thesis proves the portability: March 1978, seven venues in one month, the STERM Tendencia Democratica local, IPN Zacatenco, ENAP, the Museo Nacional de Historia, the Escuela Heroes de Churubusco, Colonia Tultepec, the EDA-INBA, then the MAM Bienal Nuevas Tendencias, the MUCA in February 1979, the Museo Regional Michoacano in 1985, the Museo Amparo in 2017, the MUAC in 2018-19, reconstructed again in 2026. The three-section install viewed first-hand, on the salmon-pink MUAC wall, shows what the portability buys: three 4x4 grids of sepia heliographs, each grid a wall of its own, the whole thing pinned, not built. The cheapness is the point: heliography is the blue-print process, the copy-machine process, the process that costs almost nothing and scales to anyone who can run a sheet through it. Against the chain: Sebastian (#338) built the letter large enough to walk into, a single monumental form at city scale, and the scale was the work; Aquino builds the mural small enough to carry under an arm, forty-eight sheets at a time, and the carrying is the work, and the monument is replaced by the tube. Hersua (#340 M12) decided the work as a maquette and let the site scale it; Aquino decides the work as a sheet and lets the venue arrange it, and the scaling is a rearrangement, not an enlargement. **Takeaway:** Print the mural on forty-eight cheap sheets and let every venue hang it differently; the wall is wherever the sheets are pinned, and the portability is the monument. Watch the tube: when the same work is a different wall in every venue, and the sheets multiply into nine copies, the mechanism is on.

## M4. The script before the image

Research first, then the picture. The Comunicado was built from a Guion Tematico, a thematic script, a grid of themes and "Consideraciones previas", before any drawing: study circles, reading lists, and a written argument that the panels then illustrate. The thesis, the single most important source this run, describes the method panel by panel: the group researched the city, argued about it, wrote the script, and only then drew, photographed, and montaged. The image is the last step, not the first. The script is what makes the 48 panels a single argument instead of 48 posters: section 1, Problemas de la Ciudad; section 2, Estructura del poder; section 3, Conflictos sociales y alternativas. The argument has a shape before it has a look, and the look serves the shape. This is the opposite of the expressive gesture: nothing in the Comunicado is improvised, and the discipline shows in the grid, where every panel knows which section it belongs to and which claim it carries. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M4) filed the legal paper and let the paperwork be the sculpture, the state office as foundry; Aquino writes the script and lets the argument be the mural, the study circle as foundry, and the filing is replaced by the drafting. Gurria (#336) kept time with a hammer and let the signal be the work; Aquino keeps time with a reading list and lets the script be the work, and the hammer is replaced by the syllabus. **Takeaway:** Write the argument before drawing the picture, and let the script be the work; the panels illustrate the research, and the research is the mural. Watch the script: when every panel knows which claim it carries, and the argument has a shape before it has a look, the mechanism is on.

## M5. Montage as the method

One panel holds photo plus drawing plus logo plus statistic plus newspaper clipping plus quote, and the collision IS the argument. The eye has to read across media inside a single 60 by 60 cm square, and the reading is the thinking. The section-3 grid viewed first-hand is the proof: the MOVIMIENTO FERROCARRILERO panel holds a train photograph, a detained man circled in ink, and three small circles labeled a, b, c, Patria, soldier, monkey; the 2 OCTUBRE panel holds a dove, a hammer and sickle, a swastika, a star, a revolver, and a falling figure with a target reticle on its chest, under the words "funcion continua"; the MOVIMIENTO TELEGRAFISTA panel holds a torn official telegram form, a map, and the line "7000 telegrafistas en huelga"; the HUELGA panel holds a march photograph at the Monumento a la Revolucion and a Proceso quotation; the SPICER panel holds a hunger-strike banner and a crowd of raised fists. Nothing is drawn from scratch where a photograph or a clipping will do the arguing faster. The montage is not decoration: the train next to the circled man next to the monkey is a sentence, and the sentence is the accusation. The technique comes from the 1968 brigades, where linocut, serigraphy, halftone photography, and hand lettering already shared one sheet, and Aquino's group systematized it into a grid. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M8) brewed one recipe and let one cook drift until a potion emerged, the drift the work; Aquino collides five media in one square and lets the collision argue, and the drift is replaced by the pile-up. Escobedo (#335) repeated one frame and let one parameter drift until a snake emerged; Aquino repeats one square and lets five media collide until an argument emerges, and the snake is replaced by the sentence. **Takeaway:** Put the photograph, the drawing, the clipping, and the quote in one square and let them collide; the montage is the argument, and the reading across media is the thinking. Watch the square: when one panel holds five media and the collision is the accusation, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The logo as fingerprint-in-crosshairs

Mira's emblem: a telescopic sight, a target, with a fingerprint inside it. Watching and being watched at once, the watched who watches back. The name Mira means to observe with responsibility and depth, in the reading Felix Beltran gave it in his prologue for the group's work, and the emblem draws the definition: a circle that is both a lens and a target, a print that is both an identity and a piece of evidence. The fingerprint detail viewed first-hand, a fingerprint inside a circle, is almost certainly a detail of Comunicado panel 1, and the same fingerprint halftone appears over the walking workers in the final panel, under the Praxedis Guerrero quote. The logo is not a brand; it is a statement of method, printed on the work itself. To look closely is to be marked, and to be marked is to be identified, and the group claims both directions: we watch the city, and the city has our print on it. The emblem recurs wherever the group signs its work, and the recurrence is the signature. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M10) swapped the head and let the wrong face say the slogan, the swap the argument; Aquino draws the fingerprint inside the crosshairs and lets the watched watch back, the circle the argument, and the swap is replaced by the sight. Sebastian (#338 M4) built the letter large enough to walk into; Aquino builds the target small enough to wear as a logo, and the walking-into is replaced by the looking-through. **Takeaway:** Draw the fingerprint inside the crosshairs and let the emblem state the method; the watched watches back, and the print is the signature. Watch the circle: when a target holds a fingerprint, and the looking goes both ways, the mechanism is on.

## M7. The city contour as fingerprint

The map as identity: Comunicado panel 1 draws the contour of Mexico City as a fingerprint. The city is not a place but a print, unique, bodily, incriminating. The detail viewed first-hand, a fingerprint filling a circle, reads at first as a thumbprint and only then as a map: the ridges are the streets, the whorl is the center, the edge is the city's outline. The metaphor is exact in the group's terms: a fingerprint identifies one person and no other, and the city's contour identifies one city and no other, and both are evidence left behind by a body. The panel opens the whole Comunicado, section 1, Problemas de la Ciudad, with the claim that the city itself is the accused and the witness at once. The map is not drawn to navigate; it is drawn to identify, and the identification is the indictment. Against the chain: Hersua (#340) built a ring of 64 prisms in a lava field and called it the pyramid of today, a work you complete by walking through it; Aquino draws a city as a fingerprint and calls it the problem of today, a work you complete by recognizing it, and the walking is replaced by the identifying. Cabrera (#337) cut one T into stone and let the incision carry the body; Aquino draws one contour as a print and lets the outline carry the city, and the stone is replaced by the paper. **Takeaway:** Draw the city's outline as a fingerprint and let the map identify the accused; the contour is the evidence, and the city is the body that left it. Watch the whorl: when a map reads as a thumbprint, and the city is the print, the mechanism is on.

## M8. The recycled emblem

Official symbols turned against themselves. The Mexico 68 Olympic rings with soldiers marching through them, bayonets fixed, black on white, viewed first-hand in the unattributed image: the regime's own mark of celebration carrying its own accusation. The peace dove deforming into a swastika across four squares in section 3, viewed first-hand in the full grid: the dove's wings thickening, the shape hardening, the fourth square a swastika, the deformation the argument. The padlocked mouth under the Mexico 68 Olympic logo in Mexiac's Libertad de expresion (1968), viewed first-hand in the Casa del Tiempo render: the logo at the bottom of the poster, the padlock stamped MADE IN U.S.A. above it, the celebration and the censorship on the same sheet. The UNAM shield reworked for STUNAM 1977 in section 3, viewed first-hand: the university's own emblem redrawn as a union's, POR MI RAZA HABLARA EL ESPIRITU beside STUNAM in letters that fill the panel. The mechanism is theft in reverse: the group does not invent symbols, it steals the regime's and turns them around, and the turning is the work. The emblem arrives already carrying authority, and the group spends that authority against its owner. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M11) paid tribute by staging an attack, a kidnapping the victim enjoyed, and the attack was the honour; Aquino pays tribute by stealing the emblem, a logo the regime cannot enjoy, and the theft is the accusation, and the victim is replaced by the mark. Goeritz (#321) built the Ruta de la Amistad as a corridor of monumental sculptures experienced from a car; Aquino builds the accusation as a stolen logo experienced from a wall, and the corridor is replaced by the recycling. **Takeaway:** Steal the regime's own emblem and turn it against its owner; the logo arrives carrying authority, and the turning spends it. Watch the rings: when soldiers march through the Olympic rings, and the dove hardens into a swastika, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The quote as weapon

A borrowed sentence does the arguing the image cannot. Marx on wages, beside a police helmet, in the section-2 panel viewed first-hand: the helmet is the image, the wage is the argument, and the quote is what connects them. Joly-Machiavelli on power, "Los poderosos imploran a su alrededor un brazo energico...", beside a tank on a road and marching soldiers' legs, viewed first-hand: the tank is the image, the imploring is the argument, and the quote is what names the imploring. Praxedis Guerrero on liberty, "Si os parece que andando no llegais a la libertad, corred entonces", under the factory and the hill and the walking workers in the final panel, viewed first-hand: the workers are the image, the running is the argument, and the quote is what tells them to run. Careaga and Quiroz Cuaron on the prison system, in the section-2 panel viewed first-hand, mugshots and a bound prisoner on a chair under the line about Mexico reflecting its reality in its penitentiary system, "enormes molinos de carne formadoras de delincuentes": the mugshots are the image, the meat-grinder is the argument, and the quote is what calls the prison a grinder. A Proceso quotation on the strike, in the HUELGA panel. Text is not caption here; it is ammunition, loaded into the panel and fired at the reader. The group quotes its enemies' theorists, its own martyrs, and the press, and the range of the sources is the range of the argument. Against the chain: Hersua (#340 M8) pinned words in a grid and let the reader recombine them, the poem as a sculpture you read; Aquino pins a borrowed sentence in a panel and lets the sentence argue, the quote as a sculpture you obey, and the recombination is replaced by the citation. Bustamante (#341 M5) wrote one sentence that told the viewer to eat, the slogan as instruction; Aquino borrows one sentence that tells the viewer to run, the quote as weapon, and the eating is replaced by the running. **Takeaway:** Load the panel with a borrowed sentence and let the quote do the arguing; text is not caption, it is ammunition. Watch the citation: when Marx sits beside a police helmet, and the sentence is the accusation, the mechanism is on.

## M10. The archive as the work

Custody, donation, cataloguing as a creative act. Aquino and Perez Vega guarded the 1968 graphics for decades and donated them to UNAM, 1993 in Aquino's own essay, 2002 in every other source, the date carried unresolved here; Aquino, Perez Vega, and Hidalgo custodied and catalogued Melecio Galvan's work from his murder in 1982 to 2002, with a 1997 FONCA grant for the joint cataloguing; the book La grafica del 68 (1982) is the group's final project, compiling the collection the group guarded. The work is keeping the work. The Casa del Tiempo captions, read end to end, record the donation on every poster: col. MUAC, donacion Aquino 2002, on No claudicaremos, on the Mexiac, on the Moreno Capdevila, on the Novelo, on the Alcazar, on the CNH volante, on the Prensa corrupta. The posters survive because someone kept them in a flat file for thirty-four years and then gave them to a university, and the keeping is the second half of the making. Galvan's work survives the same way: a murdered colleague's drawings, held by three friends, catalogued on a grant, published as a book in 2010. The archive is not what remains after the work; it is the work, continued by other means. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M14) staged the gesture for the camera and let the photograph be the performance, the shutter the venue; Aquino stages the custody for the archive and lets the donation be the performance, the flat file the venue, and the shutter is replaced by the keeping. Silva (#339 M15) gave the work away as a gift and let the dialogue keep it alive; Aquino gives the work away as a donation and lets the cataloguing keep it alive, and the gift is replaced by the custody. **Takeaway:** Guard the work for decades, catalogue it, donate it, and let the keeping be the work; the archive is the second half of the making. Watch the caption: when every poster in a museum carries one man's donation line, and the keeping is the creation, the mechanism is on.

## M11. The evaluation loop

Audience opinions were recorded at each Comunicado venue and used to redesign the images: the public talks back and the work changes. The thesis documents the loop: the seven venues of March 1978 were not just showings, they were tests, and the 1978 MAM Bienal Nuevas Tendencias showing included a graphic report plus audience opinions, the opinions themselves exhibited as part of the work. The group did not ask whether the public liked the panels; it asked whether the panels worked, whether the argument landed, and then it changed the images. The loop is the opposite of the finished masterpiece: the work is deliberately unfinished until the street has read it, and the street's reading is the next draft. The mechanism has a politics: the audience is not a consumer of the message, it is a co-author of the message, and the co-authorship is recorded, not assumed. The same loop ran informally for years in the workshop, where the Spicer poster was taught start to finish and the teaching was also a test of whether the process could be learned. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M2) handed the object to the audience and let the pocket keep it alive, the distribution the work; Aquino hands the image to the audience and lets the opinion change it, the feedback the work, and the pocket is replaced by the report. Hersua (#340) built a ring and let the visitor be the center; Aquino builds a loop and lets the visitor be the editor, and the center is replaced by the revision. **Takeaway:** Exhibit the work, record what the audience says, and redesign the images on the evidence; the public is the co-author, and the loop is the work. Watch the report: when audience opinions are exhibited beside the panels, and the next version changes because of them, the mechanism is on.

## M12. The sound version

The Comunicado translated into radio: only section 1 was broadcast, Radio Educacion, 2 February 1979, with Ricardo Medina, Programa 17, metro noises and popular songs standing in for pictures. The image set has an audio twin. The full libretto is in Arkheia, and the thesis records the translation problem honestly: how do you broadcast a 4x4 grid of heliographs? You do not describe them; you replace them, with the sounds of the city they depict, the metro, the songs, the voices, and the argument survives the change of medium because the argument was a script before it was a picture. The radio version is the proof of M4: a work built from a written argument can be re-expressed in another medium, because the argument is not the ink. Only section 1 was broadcast, and the incompleteness is part of the record: the sound version is a fragment, like the group itself, and the fragment is enough to hear the method. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M9) made the performance for the television and let the broadcast be the venue, the set the stage; Aquino makes the mural for the radio and lets the broadcast be the wall, the metro noise the picture, and the set is replaced by the signal. Gurria (#336) kept time with a hammer; Aquino keeps time with a metro, and the signal is replaced by the sound. **Takeaway:** Translate the image set into sound and let the argument survive the change of medium; the script is the work, and the radio is another wall. Watch the dial: when metro noises stand in for pictures, and the argument is heard instead of seen, the mechanism is on.

## M13. The workshop as the school

Everyone passes through the foto-serigrafia workshop. Aquino ran the EDA workshop so that the whole school, teachers and students, learned the process; the 1976 Mazatlan seminar saved the EDA with a licenciatura plan co-written with Hidalgo, Alamo, Alejandre, Maceda, and Fernandez; the Spicer strike poster (1975) was taught start to finish to Silvia Fernandez, from photographing the 600-plus striking workers to the finished print. The workshop is the school in the literal sense: the place where the skill is learned, the place where the politics is learned, and the two learnings are the same lesson. Fernandez's Piso 9 tribute, read end to end, is the evidence: she went with Aquino to photograph the Spicer workers, July to October 1975, a strike that lasted 120 days, and he taught her the whole poster process, and the teaching is what she remembers fifty years later, not the poster. The mechanism scales: the EDA workshop taught a school, the Mazatlan seminar saved a school, the UAP Escuela Popular de Arte taught a university, and each time the lesson was the same, that the poster is a process anyone can learn and therefore anyone can answer with. The workshop is also the group's recruitment: the people who learn the process become the people who make the next poster. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M12) built the group as four separate actions under one title and let each carry its author's name, the signature the work; Aquino builds the group as one workshop under one roof and lets each student carry the process away, the teaching the work, and the signature is replaced by the skill. Silva (#339) proposed the Espacio Escultorico and let the maquette win; Aquino proposes the workshop and lets the student win, and the maquette is replaced by the lesson. **Takeaway:** Teach the whole process, from photographing the workers to the finished print, and let the teaching be the work; the workshop is the school, and the student is the next poster. Watch the lesson: when a strike poster is taught start to finish, and the student remembers the teaching fifty years later, the mechanism is on.

## M14. The house as headquarters

Aquino's house hosted Mira's discussions, sketches, and photomontages. The domestic interior is the studio, the archive, and the meeting room at once: the group is literally housed. The Piso 9 essay records it plainly, and the thesis records the production context: the Comunicado was argued out and montaged in a living room, not in a studio, and the living room is therefore the first venue of the work. The house explains the group's intimacy and its secrecy: eight people, or six, arguing over a Guion Tematico at a kitchen table, cutting photographs with scissors, pasting them on albanene, running heliographic matrices in a process that smells of ammonia. The house also explains the group's survival: no rent, no institution, no permission, only a roof and a table. The mechanism is the domestication of the political: the headquarters is not a headquarters, it is a home, and the home is what lets the group meet without asking anyone. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M15) took the apron, the pot, and the bed out of the house and put them on the stage, the domestic object as set; Aquino takes the argument into the house and puts it on the kitchen table, the domestic interior as studio, and the stage is replaced by the table. Hersua (#340) hosted the meetings where the No-grupo formed, his house the group's first venue; Aquino hosts the meetings where Mira formed, his house the group's first venue, and the two houses are the two groups' shared address, the movement meeting at home because it could not meet in public. **Takeaway:** Meet in the house, argue at the kitchen table, and let the living room be the studio; the group is housed, and the housing is the headquarters. Watch the table: when a mural is montaged in a living room, and the house is the first venue, the mechanism is on.

## M15. The book as the final project

The group closes not with a show but with a book. La grafica del 68. Homenaje al movimiento estudiantil (1982), second edition 1988, third 1993, is treated as Mira's last project, compiling the collection the group guarded. The book is the group's self-archiving: three sections, Demandas del Movimiento, La Represion, Consignas de Caracter General, with poems and texts by Paz, Sabines, Castellanos, Revueltas, Huerta, and Banuelos, shown at the Academia de San Carlos on 2 October 1978 for the tenth anniversary and published four years later. The book does what the posters cannot: it stays still, it can be shelved, it can be cited, it can be reprinted, and it carries the collection into the next decade. The later books continue the same engine: Imagenes y simbolos del 68 (2004), a compilation of photography, graphics, poems, and songs; Melecio Galvan. La ternura, la violencia (2010), the catalogue of the murdered colleague's work; Imagenes de rebelion y resistencia. Oaxaca 2006 (2011), the documentation of the next generation's street graphics. The group ends by becoming its own publisher, and the publishing is the last act of custody. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341) closed her group's story with a thirty-year run of masks, aprons, and television, the performance continuing past the group; Aquino closes his group's story with a book, the publication ending the group, and the continuing is replaced by the compiling. Hersua (#340) built a ring that outlasts its builder; Aquino builds a book that outlasts its group, and the ring is replaced by the spine. **Takeaway:** Close the group with a book, compile the guarded collection, and let the publishing be the final project; the book is the self-archive, and the spine is the monument. Watch the shelf: when a group's last act is a book, and the collection it guarded is the book's content, the mechanism is on.

## M16. The street as the gallery

Carteles, pegas, volantes, mantas: the wall is the page. The 8.2 by 7.5 cm pega, Muerte a los que masacran (1968), viewed first-hand in the Casa del Tiempo render, magenta field, white circle, purple MMLM, a fist breaking chains, is the smallest unit of the same system as the 48-panel mural: the same image at a different distance from the passer-by. The volante, Pueblo de Mexico, esto es lo que te ocultan (1968), viewed first-hand, black ink on tan paper, a halftone photograph of soldiers beating a crouching young man, is the same image at hand distance, folded into a pocket. The cartel, No claudicaremos (1968), viewed first-hand, is the same image at wall distance, pasted where the crowd passes. The manta, the banner, is the same image at march distance, carried overhead. Scale is chosen by where the body meets the wall: the sticker for the lamppost, the flyer for the hand, the poster for the wall, the banner for the march, the mural for the hall. The street is the gallery because the gallery is too slow, too small, too polite, and the wall is already there, already public, already waiting. The later Oaxaca work confirms the engine outlived the group: the ASARO stencil on petate (2006), viewed first-hand, a seated skeleton figure with a rifle, pink and magenta face, NUESTROS MUERTOS NO SE OLVIDAN, MALDITOS ASESINOS, on a woven petate with a pink border, and the 31 July 2010 MACO intervention, viewed first-hand, a young man stenciling a wall on a street with onlookers behind him, are the same system twenty-eight years later, the same wall, the same page, the same passer-by. Against the chain: Bustamante (#341 M6) pasted one poster on a wall and let the crowd gather, the arrival the work; Aquino pastes the same image at four scales and lets the body choose its distance, the scale the work, and the arrival is replaced by the meeting. Goeritz (#321) built a corridor experienced from a car; Aquino builds a wall experienced from a sidewalk, and the car is replaced by the foot. **Takeaway:** Print the image at every scale from sticker to mural and let the body choose its distance; the wall is the page, and the street is the gallery. Watch the distance: when the same image is a sticker, a flyer, a poster, and a banner, and the scale is chosen by where the body meets the wall, the mechanism is on.

## Evolution

1942-1965: formation. Born 18 July 1942, Nochixtlan, Oaxaca. A student at ENAP, the Academia de San Carlos, in the mid-1960s, by inference from Grupo 65; his teachers there are not named in any source read, and no ENAP dates are established, so none are stated here. Grupo 65 forms with Rebeca Hidalgo, Melecio Galvan, and Jorge Novelo: alternative pedagogy, personal expression, emancipation from traditional teaching. No pre-1968 student work is named, described, or photographed in any source read; the early work is asserted, not shown.

1968: the interruption. The Brigadas Graficas interrupt Grupo 65. Linocut, serigraphy, the poster as answer: No claudicaremos, the Mexiac padlock, the Moreno Capdevila beating, the CNH volante, the Prensa corrupta blindfold. The movement ends the group's painterly phase before it has a chance to become one, and the poster replaces the painting as the group's medium for the next fourteen years.

1971-1975: the outlier and the turn. Los Inmigrantes (1971), acrylic on four stretchers, is a stylistic outlier, bright, optical, painterly, orange and yellow sunburst rays receding to a horizon, purple silhouetted migrant figures stooping along the rays, a red sun with 65 in its center, a bottom band of five red and orange circles with green profile faces: the only Grupo 65 painting image found, and nothing before or after it looks like it. San Francisco in 1972, Chicano posters with Chicano and Latino communities, solidarity posters for Nicaragua and Cuba. Puebla 1973-74, the Escuela Popular de Arte at the UAP, Perez Vega's poster for its workshops. Experiencia grafica (1974-75), the UAP poster with the handprint, the star, the quartered circle, and the snarling dog recycled from Perez Vega's 1968 Grafica 68. The Spicer strike poster (1975), foto-serigrafia taught start to finish. The Mazatlan seminar (1976) saves the EDA. The arc of these years is from the painting that looks like nothing else to the process that looks like everything after it: the poster, the workshop, the teaching.

1977-1982: Grupo Mira. Founded in the summer of 1977. The Comunicado Grafico N. 1 (1978) is the central work and the group's signature: 48 heliographs, three sections, seven venues in one month, the MAM Bienal, the MUCA, the Puebla first prize, the INTERGRAFIK Gran Premio in Berlin in 1980, the Salon Nacional rejection in 1979, the radio version in February 1979, the second comunicado begun and never completed. America en la mira (September 1978), three venues, the FMGTC. La grafica del 68 shown at San Carlos in October 1978, published in 1982. Galvan murdered in 1982, and the group dissolves the same year, its last project a book.

1982-2002: custody. Twenty years of keeping: Galvan's work catalogued with Perez Vega and Hidalgo, the 1997 FONCA grant, the book in 2010; the 1968 graphics guarded and then donated; the 1985 earthquake graphics with the Union de Vecinos y Damnificados; the MACO co-founded in 1992; the Estela de Tlatelolco in 1993, Aquino's exact role unresolved; the Sistema Nacional de Creadores 1993-2000; the Diplomado in 1994; the Pompidou in 1996; the Comite 68 in 1998. The work of these years is invisible in the gallery sense and central in the archive sense: the keeping is the making.

2002-2025: return and documentation. The donation formalized in 2002, by the majority account. Imagenes y simbolos del 68 (2004). Return to Oaxaca City in 2005. The Oaxaca uprising of 2006, APPO and Seccion 22, Aquino participating and documenting ASARO and Artejaguar, the books of 2010 and 2011 his account of the next generation's street graphics. The retrospectives: IAGO in 2012, the Museo Amparo and MUAC in 2017-19, the reconstruction in the 2026 MUAC Los grupos show. Death in March 2025, the obituary on 4 March, the exact day not established in sources read. The arc closes where it opened: the poster on the wall, the wall in the street, the street in the city, and the city as a fingerprint.

## Aesthetic logic

The aesthetic is efficacy. Aquino's own theory says it: the poster is graphic expression and communication, ideology, art, and technology meeting in a system of signs, and it fulfills its function to the extent that it establishes a relation between sender and perceiver in specific circumstances. The beauty of the work is the beauty of a signal that lands: the red band, the black field, the white disc, the blood drips, the padlocked mouth, the dollar blindfold, the fist breaking chains, all high-contrast, all readable at a glance, all designed for a passer-by who will not stop. The palette is the palette of cheap reproduction: black ink, red ink, magenta, sepia heliograph, cream paper, the colors of the copy machine and the mimeograph, not the colors of the studio. The scale is the scale of the body: sticker, flyer, poster, banner, mural, each chosen by where the body meets the wall. The authorship is collective: the group signs the work, the workshop teaches the work, the audience revises the work, the archive keeps the work, and the individual hand, where it can be found at all, is one hand among eight or six. The time is the time of the event: the poster appears overnight, the mural is pinned for a month and taken down, the radio version airs once, the book is the only thing built to last, and the book is built to last by compiling what was never built to last. The logic is a refusal: a refusal of the contemplative image, of the unique object, of the gallery wall, of the signature, of the masterpiece, and the refusal is carried out not by proclamation but by process, by heliography, by montage, by the script, by the workshop, by the house, by the donation. The work is what the process leaves behind, and the process is the work.
