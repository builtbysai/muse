Herold is the artist who escaped a state by trying to walk out of it and then spent fifty years making art out of cheap things taken literally. Born 26 July 1947 in Jena, he took his Abitur and technical-draughtsman training in 1966, studied mathematics at Friedrich-Schiller-Universitaet Jena in 1966-1967 and dropped out, then trained as an art and building locksmith in Eisenach -- the dates split 1967-1969 (Zander CV) against 1967-1973 (DE Wikipedia), and the wording splits too, "training as a metalworker and building fitter" (Zander) against "traineeship as an artist blacksmith" (Wikipedia, ARTZUID). The craft training matters more than the discrepancy: everything he makes afterwards is built like a locksmith builds, from straight members joined at crude angles, and the material is always the cheapest thing that will hold the shape. He studied at the University of Art and Design Halle (Burg Giebichenstein) from 1969 to 1973. [C26925]

In 1973 he attempted to flee the GDR, was arrested, and spent nine months in political imprisonment before being ransomed by the West German government. The escape is not a biographical footnote; it is the engine. The GDR bureaucracy's comic vocabulary became raw material -- the Abgangsverhandlung, the "departure negotiation", was turned into art a quarter-century later as easy going (1999, W15) -- and the whole practice keeps the same structure: an official language, taken at its word, built until it collapses into farce. He studied at the Akademie der Bildenden Kuenste Muenchen 1974-1976, then at the Hochschule fuer bildende Kuenste Hamburg under Franz Erhard Walther and Sigmar Polke, where the dates split four ways and the Polke mentorship ends 1981 (EN Wikipedia) or 1983 (gallery bios). In Hamburg he met Guenther Foerg, Martin Kippenberger, Albert Oehlen and Werner Buettner -- the "bad boys", the enfants terribles circle -- and appeared in the 1985 documentary Martin Kippenberger und Co -- Ein Dokument. He worked as a stage designer at the Schaubuehne am Lehniner Platz and the Schillertheater in West Berlin, 1979-1982, and has lived and worked in Cologne since 1983. [C26926]

The teaching career runs alongside the exhibiting one. He was a visiting artist at De Ateliers in Amsterdam 1992-2001, professor at the Staedelschule in Frankfurt 1993-1999, and then professor of sculpture at the Kunstakademie Duesseldorf, where the end date is the lane's open split: Zander's CV gives 1999-2014, while Ketterer, EN Wikipedia, Nicolas Krupp, ARTZUID and the Lapis CV all say "since 1999" with no end date, and no Kunstakademie source was located. He won the Guenter-Fruhtrunk-Preis of the Munich Academy in 1998. The exhibition chronology is long and steady: the first lath presented at the HfbK Hamburg in 1977 (W01); the first verified solo show Ein Kreuzweg at Galerie Klein, Bonn, in 1983; first shows at Galerie Max Hetzler, Cologne, in 1984; Koennen wir vielleicht mal unsere Mutter wiederhaben! at the ICA London and Kunstverein Hamburg in 1986, with Buettner and Albert Oehlen; Ziegelbilder at Kunsthalle Zuerich and Caviar and Underwear at the Daniel Weinberg Gallery, Los Angeles, in 1989; SFMOMA, Koelnischer Kunstverein and Luhring Augustine in 1990; the public-space brick work Mehr Licht at the Badische Landesbibliothek, Karlsruhe, in 1991; documenta IX in Kassel in 1992, with the two doors of There is nothing left -- There is no right (W13); Just do it! at the Stedelijk in 1993 and Projects 39 at MoMA the same year; Skulptur Projekte Muenster in 1997; the retrospectives What a Life at Staatliche Kunsthalle Baden-Baden in 2005 (travelling to Hannover and Klagenfurt), wo man kind at Museum Ludwig in 2007, and Da wo die ... at Kunstmuseum Bonn, 21 September 2017 to 7 January 2018; the most recent solo show in the Zander CV is Zander Galerie, Cologne, in 2023, and the caviar series continues into a 2026 Capitain Petzel show. Collections holding the work include the Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam, SFMOMA, MACBA Barcelona, the Staedel Museum Frankfurt, Kunstmuseum Bonn, Museum Ludwig Cologne, S.M.A.K. Ghent and MOCA Los Angeles. With dated activity into 2026 and no obituary found, he is living as of November 2026. [C26927]

The evolution is a straight line drawn with a crooked ruler. The first lath is a bare presentation in 1977 -- a single roof batten, shown as itself, in the German Autumn. By 1985 the lath has become a picture: inscribed, gilded by title, wrapped in fabric, built into a pyramid by sheer accumulation (W03, W04, W09). In 1989 the materials split in two directions at once, luxury and waste: Beluga caviar smeared across canvas, begun 1988/89 (W07), and bricks laid on linen as a module that is also a weight (W08). In 1992 language takes over as the building material: two grey doors the viewer must choose between (W13), a title that performs an alchemy the material cannot (W03). The mid-1990s bring the pseudo-scientific display case (W14); 2001 brings video housed in mini TVs inside glass boxes (W16); 2005-2007 bring the brick towers and the glossy slat figure, slender, angular, long-limbed, faceless, contorted (W17, W18); 2017-2020 bring the large lath grotesques and the caviar paintings still going (W19, W20). Through it all, Albrecht Duerer is the anchor: the Hare of 1502 is cited for the roof-lath works, and the sources record an intensive engagement with Duerer in recent years -- the highest art history, quoted through the cheapest possible material. The titles are puns and wordplay throughout; the work is Dada-inflected and ironic-critical towards art history, politics, ideology and religion, and the signature materials run from roof laths, bricks, buttons, nails, thread, socks, nylon tights, mattresses, tea strainers, handbags and carpets to, since 1988/89, expensive Beluga caviar. [C26928]

## M1 -- The lath as line and structure

A single cheap roof batten (Dachlatte) is a drawn line, a yardstick and a building unit at once. In W01 the lath alone is the whole presentation; in W03 laths are inscribed and gilded by title into a picture plane; in W09 a wood construction becomes a pyramid by sheer accumulation; in W19 laths, screws, canvas and twine form a large grotesque figure 172 x 370 x 115 cm. The watchable behaviour is the joint: thin straight members meet at crude nailed or screwed angles, so a figure or a volume reads as a jagged, pixel-like polyline before it reads as a body. The lath is the cheapest possible line, and it is also the most honest one -- it cannot pretend to be anything but straight wood.

**Takeaway:** build the figure out of one cheap straight member repeated; let the joints do the drawing. [C26929]

## M2 -- The lath-painting: support becomes picture

The traditional stretcher is relieved of supporting the canvas; the raw lath itself takes the role of the canvas and picture carrier. W04, Kunstraub III (1985), is a batten frame 200 x 169 x 7 cm with fabric wrapped only around the upper right corner -- the frame is the painting, and the one wrapped corner is the joke about what a painting is supposed to cover. W12, Pfannkuchentheorie (1992), the centrepiece of MoMA Projects 39, is roof laths and canvas in which the canvas is a slack skin over the lath core, described in the catalogue as "horizontal, slack-skinned, and all elbows and knees". The picture plane is incomplete by design: gaps, drapes and exposed frame let the viewer see through the work, and the "painting" is what the frame does, not what covers it.

**Takeaway:** let the stretcher be the picture; leave the canvas slack, gapped, or half-wrapped so the support shows. [C26930]

## M3 -- Caviar as cellular unit, numbered

Beluga caviar is used as a granular, countable painting medium -- each egg a cell, each cell individually numbered in many works. W07, ohne Titel (Beluga) (1989), is caviar, acrylic and lacquer on canvas at 210 x 360 cm, a large dark field. W11 (1991) draws four DNA-like spirals from Beluga eggs, each egg painstakingly numbered. The 2011-2020 caviar pictures (W20 and its siblings) clump and disperse the eggs into cosmic or topographical expanses and into portraits. The watchable behaviour is the approach: a dark, greasy, seductive sheen at distance resolves on coming closer into thousands of discrete beads, each counted -- luxury material turned into tedious manual labour, and a figurative image that dissolves into grain. It is the most expensive possible paint, used in the most labour-intensive possible way, to make a picture that falls apart into dots.

**Takeaway:** paint with something absurdly expensive, one countable unit at a time, until the image dissolves into grain. [C26931]

## M4 -- The brick as module and gravity load

A standard brick is placed on, in, or through a canvas as a pictorial module that also pulls the canvas down by its own weight. W08, The Bow (1989), is bricks and linen, 48 x 97 x 6 inches, in SFMOMA. W17, Delivering the WOW (2005), is a plain linen canvas with stacks of bricks in white cement, towers three bricks high protruding from the surface. The 2004-2005 brick canvases Rumsfeld, Red Square and Platz des himmlischen Friedens look as if they might collapse under their heavy burden of red bricks. The watchable behaviour is the sag: the canvas wrinkles, stretches and pulls under the load, and the bricks sit in compositions that seem to defy gravity while visibly threatening to fall. The brick is at once a unit of building, a unit of politics, and a unit of weight.

**Takeaway:** put a real weight on the canvas and let the sag be the picture; the module must threaten to fall. [C26932]

## M5 -- Language as material: pun, title, instruction

A word or bureaucratic term is taken literally and built, so the joke is the construction. W13 (1992) is two neutral-grey doors at documenta IX, one marked "There is nothing left", the other "There is no right" -- the viewer must choose, and either choice is a dead end. W15, easy going (1999), turns the GDR Abgangsverhandlung into art. W06, G.O.E.L.R.O. (1988), is two yellow extension cords on an unworking socket, titled with the initials of Lenin's 1920 electrification plan. W03 performs an alchemy in its title -- Verwandlung von Dachlatten in Gold -- that the material, roof laths and dispersion, cannot perform. The watchable behaviour is the order of operations: read the title first, then see the object obey it too literally. The pun is not a caption on the work; it is the work's building instruction.

**Takeaway:** take the word literally and build it; the title is the instruction, not the caption. [C26933]

## M6 -- Poor materials, displaced

Low-grade everyday materials are used with little or no alteration, moved out of their proper use. W05, Genetischer Eingriff in die Erbmasse bei Frau Herold (1985), is a wire DNA double helix descending to a wooden base, interrupted by chunks of board wood -- the science is built from scrap. W14 (1995) puts satirical pseudo-scientific labels on ordinary glass bottles and jars. W16 (2001) puts mini TVs, VCD players, video CDs, cables and plates into glass boxes. The watchable behaviour is recognition first, then refusal: the viewer knows what the thing is, and then the use refuses to make sense. The material is never disguised and never upgraded; its cheapness is the point, and the displacement is the joke.

**Takeaway:** use the cheapest recognisable thing, unaltered, in the wrong place; let recognition come first and sense refuse to follow. [C26934]

## M7 -- Skin versus armature: conceal or expose

A work is either laid open, with its frame and joints visible, or tightly skinned in lacquered canvas so the interior is concealed. W04 and W12 expose the batten frame -- the armature is the picture. W18, Figur I-V (2007), does the opposite: canvas stretched over timber tinted in solid primary colours with glossy car paint, so the bone structure is hidden under a taut, lacquered skin and the figure reads as a smooth, coloured body. The watchable behaviour is the eye's search: under a taut skin it looks for the hidden armature, and at an exposed frame it follows the joints. Herold works both sides of the same decision, and the decision is the mechanism -- what is shown and what is hidden are chosen, not given.

**Takeaway:** choose, for each work, whether the armature shows or the skin hides it; never do both by accident. [C26935]

## M8 -- The distorted stick figure

The body is built from filiform, stretched members, pushed to the point of breaking, in extreme postures. W18 gives five figures, slender, angular, long-limbed, faceless, contorted, in solid primary colours with glossy car paint. W19, Accident Groteske (2017), is the same body grown to 172 x 370 x 115 cm in roof laths, screws, canvas and twine. Bluehendes Leben (2009) is a pink slat dancer. The watchable behaviour is the posture: the pose is the meaning, and the thin members make it look unstable, as if the body might snap at the next joint. It is the lath mechanism (M1) grown into a body, and the Duerer reference sharpened into a figure -- the human form reduced to sticks, and the sticks arranged until the posture is grotesque.

**Takeaway:** build the body from sticks and pose it until it looks about to snap; the posture is the meaning. [C26936]

## M9 -- The display case and the pseudo-scientific multiple

Ordinary or absurd things are put in display cases, made as multiples, or given scientific-looking labels, borrowing museum and laboratory authority. W14 (1995), Kuenstlerische Medizin, Patho-Ontologie (Cabinet patho-psychologique), is glass bottles and jars with labels that look scientific and turn out, on inspection, to be pseudoscientific and satirical. Prototypen (2002) and Collected Water Levels (1996) work the same way. The watchable behaviour is the lean-in: the viewer steps close to read a label, and the label turns out to be a joke. The authority is borrowed wholesale -- glass, case, label, multiple -- and then spent on something that does not deserve it.

**Takeaway:** borrow the museum's authority -- case, label, multiple -- and spend it on a joke the viewer only gets by leaning in. [C26937]

## M10 -- The mini-TV / small video as an object

Video is not projected large but housed in small, cheap, domestic hardware inside glass boxes, so the moving image becomes one small object among others. W16 and its 2001 siblings -- it's more the strategy than the attraction, the mind of the kind, ideology of intelligence -- are glass boxes 162 x 29 x 34 cm holding mini TVs, a VCD player, video CDs, cable and plates. The Lapis Press edition LAX Bolero is a perpetually running iPod video continuing the mini-TV media series, and the 2026 Capitain Petzel show includes video works featuring dancers alongside caviar paintings and cast sculpture. The watchable behaviour is the scale mismatch: a tiny screen glows and loops inside a vitrine-like box while the surrounding hardware -- cables, plates, glass -- stays inert. Motion is miniaturised, domesticated, and made slightly ridiculous.

**Takeaway:** shrink the video to a domestic object in a box; let the tiny glowing screen be the only thing moving. [C26938]

## Aesthetic logic

Herold's logic is the cheap thing taken literally. The lath is a line, a yardstick and a building unit; the caviar is luxury degraded to counting; the brick is a module that is also a weight; the word is a construction; the body is sticks posed until it nearly snaps. Nothing is invented from nothing: a bureaucratic term, a roof batten, a fish egg, a brick, a mini TV -- each is taken as found and built until it obeys its own name too literally, and the joke is the obedience. Against Oehlen's dense pile, Herold's work is sparse and skeletal, held together by a pun rather than a beat: the picture is mostly frame, mostly gap, mostly skin over an armature the viewer can almost see. Duerer is the high anchor, quoted through the cheapest material available, so that the most serious art history and the least serious material meet in the same object -- a Hare made of roof laths, a DNA helix interrupted by board wood, an electrification plan as two dead extension cords. The irony is not a tone laid over the work; it is the construction method. [C26939]
