# Enrico Melozzi: Study Notes

**Site:** Enrico Melozzi (b. Melozzi was born in Teramo, Italy, in 1977 [C29556]. He took up piano at eight and began composing immediately, self-taught; Treccani notes that although he recognises the importance of his institutional studies, "the artist does not hesitate to call himself self-taught". He took a cello diploma, then a composition degree at the London College of Music, of which he is a Fellow [C29557]. He took an international Master in film music at the Centro Sperimentale di Cinematografia in Rome under Luis Bacalov, studied applied music with sound engineer Federico Savina in 2001, and from 1999 worked as assistant, arranger and artistic producer to the German composer Michael Riessler, including work on the score of Edgar Reitz's film Die Andere Heimat [C29558]. His conducting debut came in 2002 -- English Wikipedia places it at the Auditorium Parco della Musica in Rome, his official site specifies the opera debut with Oliver Twist the same year; the venue and work pairing is not pinned to a single event by one source [C29559]. No teaching post was found anywhere: a targeted search returned no conservatory, academy or university appointment, and the study claims none [C29569]. What exists instead is a career built on parallel tracks that never merge into one job -- opera, ballet and film composition; pop arranging and conducting; electronic music; record producing -- and on ensembles he founded himself, because no existing institution offered the room he wanted.

The early catalogue is chamber miniatures with playful titles: "Levigazioni" (1996, W01, flute, piano and cello, the earliest dated work), "Variazioni su temi di Ennio Morricone" (1997), "3 immagini per violoncello e pianoforte" (1997), "Luna Bassa (rossa)" (1998, piano), and the first orchestral-scale piece, "Il grande Abete Rosso" (1999, W02, a symphonic fairy tale for narrator and orchestra), written the same year he became Riessler's assistant. The middle period learns the trade sideways: the Lisma Project (W04), the electronic duo with DJ Stefano De Angelis founded in 2004, whose "Lisma Vive" (album 2007) is scored for electronics, visual art and five improvisers and whose "Io sono nero" (album 2009) sets radio voices, electronics, cello and percussion; film scores from 2000, including "La casa sulle nuvole" (2009, W05, Premio Roma Videoclip for best soundtrack) and "Il Gioco" (2009, a short western by Adriano Giannini, Nastro d'argento and Best Foreign Language Award at the New York Film Festival) [C29573]. His film-music awards also include the BAFF best soundtrack for "Le cose in te nascoste", a special mention at the Olympia Film Festival in Athens for "L'uomo fiammifero", and the Roma Creative Contest 2017 best soundtrack for "Penalty" [C29568]. The "Sinfonia Concertante" (2007, organ, string orchestra and percussion; album 2010, W06) is the first large concert work, and "Cello Songs" (2011, W07, with singer Sarah Jane Morris) re-works pop classics -- Tracy Chapman's "Fast Car", Damien Rice's "The Blower's Daughter" -- for voice and a cello-led ensemble, the first dated instance of the translation mechanism that later runs Sanremo [C29574].

The 2010s are the scaling-up decade. "Pinocchio -- The Ballet" (2011/2012, W08), an opera-ballet in two acts for the West Australian Ballet, premiered 15 September 2012 in Perth with a Dance Australia review praising a "commissioned, 'simpatico' orchestral score ... with an extraordinary range of genres and instruments", a gigantic Mangiafuoco puppet and an enormous antique barrel organ on stage. Then 100 Cellos (W09, founded 2012 with Sollima during the Teatro Valle occupation): three days of concerts became 72 hours of continuous music with flash mobs in town, cellists "of all ages and from all over the world and different disciplines -- from rock cellists to Baroque cellists", arrangements by Sollima and Melozzi, repertoire from Bach to Pink Floyd's The Wall, and a closing concert on 18 March 2012 with Marco Mengoni, Sarah Jane Morris and Elsa Lila. The "Berlin 1989" concert (W10, Teatro Regio, Turin, 9 November 2014) marked 25 years since the fall of the Berlin Wall with Bach and German Baroque plus an arrangement of music from The Wall, readings, and a film of Rostropovich's impromptu performance at the Wall. "The never-ending Childhood" (2015, W11), for cello and piano with an orchestral version in 2016, is dedicated to cellist Leila Shirvani and, in the album note, "explores in particular the entire range of timbres the cello has to offer, from the lowest octave to the most daringly high registers". The opera strand follows: "Il Piccolo Principe" (2017, W12, Teatro Regio di Parma, orchestra, white-voices choir, six singers, after Saint-Exupery) and "Don Giovanni all'Inferno" (2018-19, W15), a sequel sending Mozart's Don Giovanni to hell. The Orchestra Notturna Clandestina becomes his house band on record: "A Clandestine Night in Rome" (2017, W13, Sollima with the ONC, conducted by Melozzi, Decca/Universal) and "100Cellos, Live" (2018, W14, Decca/Universal) [C29575].

The Sanremo thread is the spine of the 2010s and 2020s. First conducted there in 2012 (Noemi, "Sono solo parole", 3rd place); ten editions in all (2012, 2014, 2019-2026), 14 songs conducted in competition in 2020-2025 -- an all-time record in the Amadeus era -- four podiums and one outright win, Maneskin's "Zitti e buoni" in 2021 (W17), which then won Eurovision; Melozzi "arranged and conducted the Sanremo Orchestra during their performance" [C29564]. The 2020 edition alone had him conducting four competing songs (Pinguini Tattici Nucleari's "Ringo Starr", W16, 3rd place; Fasma; Anastasio; Junior Cally). The folk strand arrives last and becomes his own creation: maestro concertatore of the Notte della Taranta 2021 (W18, Italy's biggest folk festival, with singer Madame, his reading described as "symphonic rock"), then La Notte dei Serpenti (W20, first edition 29 July 2023, Pescara seafront, a modern reinterpretation of Abruzzo folk songs, Rai 1 rerun 10.5% share), annual since, the fourth edition on 25 July 2026 drawing about 50,000 people to the Stadio del Mare and 1,617,000 viewers on Rai 1 (14.68% share) [C29565]. In between, the symphonic pop re-reading: Niccolo Fabi's "Meno per meno" (W19, album 2 December 2022, Fabi's songs re-scored for the ONC), and the composed-for-one-soloist strand on record in "Cello-Konzerte" (W21, Antes, 5 November 2021, Leila Shirvani with the ONC, including Melozzi's "Childhood" and a Persian melody from Shirvani's Iranian grandfather, orchestrated by Melozzi) [C29576]. The recent period consolidates rather than swerves: "Concerto in Mi" for guitar and orchestra (2021, W22, album "Guitar Concerto in E", 2024, guitar Lorenzo Micheli), "Sinfonia n.2 in Mi minore" (2023), "Velasca Vertigo" with the ONC (2025, W23, title-only in the sources), the theme and music of Rai 3's Report (2025), and the two La Scala podiums -- 4 June 2025 conducting the 100 Cellos in "Tucidide. Atene contro Melo" with Alessandro Baricco and Sollima, and 16 April 2026 conducting the Banda dell'Esercito Italiano for the 80th anniversary of the Italian Republic, premiering his own "Intermezzo sinfonico 1946" (W24) [C29567] [C29577]. The evolution is a steady scaling of forces -- chamber miniatures (1996-2003) through electronic hybrids (2004-2007) and cello-orchestra writing (2014-2016) to opera and symphony (2017 onward) -- while the pop-arranging strand runs in parallel from 2012 and the folk-revival strand arrives last, in 2021-2023 [C29578].

## M1 -- Massed-cello accumulation: one instrument, a hundred players

The 100 Cellos mechanism is accumulation of a single timbre until it behaves like three instruments at once. One cello is a voice; a hundred cellos are an organ, a choir and a percussion section, and the piece is the counting -- the field fills cello by cello, the unison passages thicken from a single line into a wall, and the audience can watch the texture being built player by player. The ensemble's own description stresses that the players "mirror each other" in multiplicity, and the membership is the mechanism's second half: professionals, famous soloists, students, amateurs and enthusiasts, ages 6 to 80, all playing the same instrument in the same room. The repertoire is chosen to show the accumulation working across material -- Bach's Suite No. 1 Prelude, Modugno and Pasolini's "Cosa sono le nuvole", Pink Floyd's "Another brick in the wall", Nirvana's "Smells Like Teen Spirit" -- because the point is not the song but the massing. Seen in W09, W10, W14, W24. [C29579]

**Takeaway:** a hundred cellos is not a bigger cello; it is a single timbre accumulated until it becomes a crowd. [C29579]

## M2 -- The conductor who swaps hats mid-performance

Sollima and Melozzi "skillfully alternate and have fun as conductors and soloists" -- leadership in 100 Cellos is a token passed between two people on stage, not a fixed podium role. Whoever is not conducting is playing, and the ensemble's centre of gravity visibly shifts each time the baton, or the bow, changes hands. The division is fluid by design: both men are composers, arrangers, conductors and, above all, cellists, and the project description says they divide themselves "between the roles of soloist and director". The watchable behaviour is the handover itself -- a conductor sitting down to play, a player standing up to conduct -- and the fact that the audience sees the leadership move rather than assuming it. The same fluidity appears in Melozzi's own practice: he conducts the Orchestra Notturna Clandestina, plays cello in it, arranges for it, and at the Romaeuropa finale on 15 November 2026 he sang the Commendatore from the podium, having "directed the voices of others all evening" and taking the microphone "for the only time". Seen in W09, W13, W24. [C29580]

**Takeaway:** the podium is not a chair; it is a token, and the music is watching it change hands. [C29580]

## M3 -- Classical-pop arrangement as translation: the Sanremo mechanism

A three-minute rock or pop song is re-scored for a full festival orchestra without losing its punch. The mechanics are translation, not decoration: the riff becomes a string section, the drum kit becomes orchestral percussion, the vocal line stays intact over a rewritten harmonic bed, and the arrangement is the drama -- a familiar pop hook arrives carried by violins and brass instead of guitars. Melozzi "arranged and conducted the Sanremo Orchestra" for Maneskin's "Zitti e buoni", the song that won Sanremo on 6 March 2021 and then Eurovision, and he conducted 14 songs in competition in 2020-2025, a record in the Amadeus era. The same mechanism runs in the studio: "Cello Songs" (2011) re-works "Fast Car" and "The Blower's Daughter" for voice and cello-led ensemble, and "Meno per meno" (2022) re-reads Niccolo Fabi's songs symphonically with the Orchestra Notturna Clandestina. The skill is not orchestration in the abstract but translation under a deadline, live, for a song the whole country already knows. Seen in W07, W16, W17, W19. [C29581]

**Takeaway:** the arrangement is not a dressing for the song; it is the song, re-spoken in another language. [C29581]

## M4 -- The open orchestra: the self-convened players

100 Cellos was called "un'orchestra social, gli autoconvocati delle note" -- a social orchestra, the self-summoned of the notes. Recruitment is open and self-selecting rather than auditioned: Melozzi's email of 15 March 2012 convened whoever answered, over a hundred adhesions, a contribution of 10 euros a day, and joining made one a founding member of the Fondazione Teatro Valle Bene Comune. The ensemble re-forms in each city from whoever shows up -- professionals, students, amateurs and children side by side -- and the rehearsal is part of the performance: the 2012 gathering ran three days of concerts and 72 hours of music, and the Ravenna weeks run three days and three nights of cello music. A core of about twenty regulars teaches its tricks to those in need, in Sollima's account, "with great humility". The watchable behaviour is strangers assembling into an orchestra in real time, and the mechanism is the registration form as a score: level, cello history, "her own magic move on the cello", a photo with the instrument, preferred section. Seen in W09, W10. [C29582]

**Takeaway:** the orchestra is not hired; it is convened, and the convening is the piece. [C29582]

## M5 -- The occupation-born ensemble: music from a seized space

100 Cellos was born as an experiment during the occupation of Rome's historic Teatro Valle, and the Orchestra Notturna Clandestina was, in the Festival Verdi press release's words, "born at the beginning of the decade at Teatro Valle Occupato". The founding condition is a political act -- artists taking over a closed 17th-century theatre and running it as a commons -- so the music carries the address of the place it was made in. The 2012 email is the document: "Cari Violoncellisti, vi scriviamo dal caldo Teatro Valle... questo folle progetto, fortemente voluto dal M. Sollima e da tutti noi occupanti" -- the warm theatre, the mad project, strongly wanted by Sollima and by all of us occupiers. The stay was the theatre's 13th "Permanenza", the programme included "blitz metropolitani" (surprise urban performances, including at night, with headlamps) and an improvised concert during the Rome Marathon on Sunday morning, broadcast internationally. The watchable behaviour is a concert that begins as a gathering in a reclaimed room: the venue is a character, not a container. Seen in W09, W13. [C29583]

**Takeaway:** the room is not where the music happens; the room is why it happens. [C29583]

## M6 -- The classical rave: the night orchestra, in long form

The Orchestra Notturna Clandestina -- the clandestine night orchestra -- stages multi-hour late-night events that treat classical repertoire as club material. "Un rave in maschera" (Teatro Regio di Parma, 13 October 2021) set Verdi's Un ballo in maschera as a masked rave; "Un rave classico" (Ravenna Festival, 15 July 2023) did the same for the festival night; "The Classical Rave Party" (Romaeuropa finale, 15 November 2026) ran about four hours with guests Niccolo Fabi, Sollima, Gloria Campaner, Giovanni Antonini, Alessandro Baricco reading, and the Banda dell'Esercito Italiano, the audience to sing Verdi's "Va, pensiero" and the Don Giovanni finale's chorus of devils, Melozzi himself singing the Commendatore from the podium. The mechanics are duration plus darkness plus guest crossovers replacing the two-hour concert format: the orchestra plays on while the room's energy, not a programme, decides the arc. The first rave was 30 April 2018, twelve hours of music in an occupied palazzo in via di Santa Croce in Gerusalemme, Rome. The orchestra is volunteer-based, rehearses four days before a concert, and after ten years still has no rehearsal hall of its own. Seen in W13, W23, W24. [C29584]

**Takeaway:** the concert is not shortened for the night; the night is lengthened for the concert. [C29584]

## M7 -- Electronic duo layering: the cello against the grid

The Lisma Project is Melozzi plus DJ Stefano De Angelis: a live cello and composition against DJ electronics, two layers with different time-feels -- a DJ's looped, beat-locked grid under a cellist's free, bowed phrasing -- negotiated live. "Lisma Vive" (album 2007) is scored for electronics, visual art and five improvisers; "Io sono nero" (album 2009) sets radio voices, electronics, cello and percussion; "FLATS" (2006) is for piano, electronics and rock band. The duo also scored the 1921 silent film Amleto in 2007 and "Le cose in te nascoste" (2008), which won Melozzi the BAFF best soundtrack. The evidence is thin and the study says so plainly: the duo's existence is attested, but no dated Lisma Project performance, review or streaming description surfaced this run, and the mechanism should not carry a prompt without a dated performance. What is visible is the laboratory strand running parallel to the orchestral work -- the cello as one voice among electronics, improvisers and visuals, not as the soloist in front of an orchestra. Seen in W04. [C29585]

**Takeaway:** the cello does not lead the electronics; it bends against them, and the bending is the music. [C29585]

## M8 -- Folk-song reinvention: the old tune, a new body

La Notte dei Serpenti takes Abruzzo folk songs and re-composes them for orchestra, pop guests and an all-female choir, staged as a mass outdoor ritual. The mechanics are conservation plus rebuilding: a dialect folk melody is kept singable while its harmony, rhythm and forces are rebuilt for a stadium -- old tune, new body. The choir is the hinge between folk and spectacle: ordinary women who auditioned by tape, like Marinella Olivieri and Valentina Di Iulio in the 2026 choir, rehearsing with Melozzi calling "SCIAMBINDETT!" -- Abruzzo dialect for "Bless you!" -- across the room. The scale is the evidence: first edition 29 July 2023 on the Pescara seafront, Rai 1 rerun 10.5% share; fourth edition 25 July 2026, Stadio del Mare, about 50,000 people, Rai 1 prime time 1,617,000 viewers and 14.68% share, the most-watched programme of the evening, hosted by Elettra Lamborghini with Patty Pravo, Raf, Baby K, Clementino and Mr. Rain on the bill. Melozzi's own account of the project: "The project is not just about music, but about how a community recognizes and tells its own story" -- and "The project was born in Teramo", his birthplace, where the diffused rehearsals were held in July 2026. The same mechanism, at festival scale, is his Notte della Taranta 2021, a "symphonic rock" reading of the pizzica repertoire. Seen in W18, W20. [C29586]

**Takeaway:** the folk song is not preserved; it is re-bodied, and the crowd singing it back is the proof. [C29586]

## M9 -- The composed-for-one-soloist portrait: dedication as mechanism

Melozzi writes concertos for a named player and their specific sound, and the biography becomes the orchestration. "The never-ending Childhood" (2016) is dedicated to cellist Leila Shirvani -- "a work dedicated to me", in her album note -- and explores "the entire range of timbres the cello has to offer, from the lowest octave to the most daringly high registers". "The Sound of Falling Walls" (2014) is for two solo cellos and cello orchestra, recorded by Sollima and Shirvani for Decca. On Shirvani's "Cello-Konzerte" album (Antes, 5 November 2021) with the Orchestra Notturna Clandestina, a Persian melody "passed down orally by my Iranian grandfather" is orchestrated by Melozzi -- a family tune, a player's full register, a dedication, turned into a concerto movement. The watchable behaviour is one solo voice against the orchestra, with the orchestra repeatedly handing the melody back to her in new clothes, and the dedication audible as the reason the piece exists in that shape and no other. Seen in W11, W21. [C29587]

**Takeaway:** the dedication is not a label on the piece; it is the piece's shape. [C29587]

## M10 -- Opera as a crime scene: the interactive opera

"Opere Crime. Delitto all'opera" (Festival Verdi) is an interactive experience by Melozzi and Stefano De Angelis, built on Verdi's Rigoletto, with music composed, conducted and orchestrated by Melozzi, the Orchestra Notturna Clandestina performing alongside cellist Leila Shirvani and De Angelis on percussion, three singers, and the voices of Adriano Giannini and Pietro Biondi. The mechanics are dismantling: a canonical opera is taken apart into a whodunit the audience moves through, and the score becomes evidence -- familiar arias arriving as clues, singers and audience sharing one investigative space. The exact date and year of the performance were not pinned down this run (the OperaWire piece is undated in the retrieved text and the Festival Verdi edition is not stated), so the study asserts the work and its mechanism but not a year. It is the same dismantling habit as "Don Giovanni all'Inferno" (2018-19, W15), which sends Mozart's Don Giovanni to hell, and the completion of the lost finale of Donizetti's "Le Nozze in Villa" (2020, with Elio and Rocco Tanica): the canon is material to be opened, not a monument to be guarded. Seen in W15. [C29588]

**Takeaway:** the opera is not performed; it is investigated, and the audience holds the evidence. [C29588]

## Aesthetic logic

Put the ten mechanisms together and one logic holds them. Melozzi does not write pieces; he convenes rooms. The cello is the human voice that starts the gathering, the orchestra is a flexible body that can be a hundred cellos or a night orchestra or a festival orchestra or a folk choir, and the concert is a collective ritual rather than a recital. The material is always borrowed and re-bodied: a pop song re-scored for strings, a folk tune re-built for a stadium, a Verdi opera re-cut as a whodunit, a Persian family melody orchestrated for a dedicatee, a Bach prelude played by a hundred cellos in an occupied theatre. The frame is always the same too: a gathering with a door left open -- anyone registers, anyone sings, the audience is invited to sing "Va, pensiero" -- and the open door is where the music happens. The place is never decoration: an occupied theatre in Rome, a palazzo in via di Santa Croce in Gerusalemme, a seafront in Pescara, a Sanremo stage, La Scala. Early he wrote chamber miniatures for small rooms; late he writes for rooms of fifty thousand, but the move is the same -- find the people, give them the material, and let the gathering be the performance. His own programme, in Treccani's summary, is that classical music is "la cosa piu folle, piu spudorata, piu proibita" -- the craziest, most shameless, most forbidden thing -- and in the Melos interview, "La musica regge tutto. Il rito no." Music can take anything. The ritual can't. So he keeps the music and rebuilds the ritual, every time, in a new room. [C29589]
