## M1. The sea as a character

The Suffolk coast is not a backdrop but an actor, and it judges, threatens and consoles, and the mechanism is a landscape that changes state between scenes while the people stay put. Peter Grimes is the proof, and the Four Sea Interludes, Dawn, Sunday Morning, Moonlight, Storm, are four different seas, and none of them is free of foreboding, and the Passacaglia interlude is the Borough's verdict, delivered by the orchestra while the singers are silent, and the Sinfonia da Requiem ends the same way, and its final Requiem aeternam ebbs away into the sea that symbolises both reconciliation and death. The sea is the one character who never leaves the stage, and it returns in a different mood each time, and the people are judged by which mood they meet. A Boy Was Born is the early form of the same instinct, the sea already there in the variation set, and Death in Venice is the late form, the lagoon as the silent water the boy crosses and never returns from.

**Takeaway:** do not paint the sea behind the action, cast it as the actor, and let the place change its mood between scenes while the people stay put, because a backdrop is scenery, and a sea that judges is drama.

## M2. The boy's voice as innocence

The treble voice, boys' voices, children's chorus, is used as the sound of uncorrupted feeling, set against adult violence or indifference, and the mechanism is a small, clear voice cutting through a large adult texture. A Boy Was Born puts boys' voices inside a formal variation set at nineteen, and Friday Afternoons writes for schoolchildren, and Noye's Fludde makes children the Ark's crew, and the Missa Brevis and the boys' chorus in the War Requiem put the treble inside the liturgy, and the treble roles of the 1950s were sung by David Hemmings and Michael Crawford, boys who became famous men. The innocence is not decorative, and the drama then corrupts or destroys it, and the operas return to the same wound, the child the adults fail, and the voice is the conscience the crowd cannot imitate.

**Takeaway:** do not use the child's voice as decoration, use it as the conscience, and let the adult texture swallow it, because a treble that only sings sweetly is a choir, and a treble the drama destroys is a tragedy.

## M3. The passacaglia and ground-bass architecture

A repeating bass or theme that the whole structure is built on and cannot escape, and the mechanism is slow, circling repetition underneath fast surface activity, the feeling of being walked round in a tightening circle. The Violin Concerto finale is a Passacaglia, Andante lento, a lament that will not resolve, and the Peter Grimes Passacaglia interlude is the Borough's judgement as a ground bass, and the Cello Symphony finale is a Passacaglia, Andante allegro, that turns from pessimism to radiant happiness, and the Piano Concerto's revised third movement, the Impromptu added in 1945, is the same ground-bass logic in a lighter dress, see C4, and The Turn of the Screw is the same logic in serial dress, a twelve-note theme stated at the opening and varied through fifteen scenes, one theme, fifteen turns, no escape. The ground never moves, and the surface runs faster against it, and the trap is the form.

**Takeaway:** do not let the form wander, put a ground under it that will not move, and let the surface run faster against it, because a piece with no ground is a walk, and a piece walked round a tightening circle is a trap.

## M4. The operatic interlude as a structural joint

Purely orchestral passages placed between scenes that do the dramatic work, time passing, weather changing, judgement forming, so the singing never has to explain it, and the mechanism is the stage emptying and the orchestra taking over, and the place itself speaking for three minutes. Peter Grimes is the proof, and the Four Sea Interludes plus the Passacaglia survive as a concert suite because they are complete dramas without a voice, and the interlude is a joint, not a pause, and the scene before it and the scene after it are held together by the weather between them. The singers never narrate the sea, and the sea narrates itself.

**Takeaway:** do not make the singers explain the weather, empty the stage and let the orchestra speak for three minutes, because narration is telling, and an interlude that changes the weather is showing.

## M5. Chamber opera for small forces

Full-scale drama with a handful of singers and a dozen players, built to tour parish halls and small theatres, and the mechanism is a whole opera in a room the size of a village hall, every player visible, nothing hidden by spectacle. The Rape of Lucretia is eight singers and a chamber orchestra, premiered Friday 12 July 1946 at the first post-war Glyndebourne Festival, and the tour lost money heavily, and Christie underwrote no more tours, which pushed Britten to found the English Opera Group, and Albert Herring is the comic counterpart to Lucretia's tragedy, same small forces, opposite temperature, premiered Friday 20 June 1947, and The Turn of the Screw is six singers and thirteen players, and The Little Sweep is the children's version of the same machine. The smallness is not a limitation, and it is the intensity, and the audience is close enough to see the players breathe, and the drama has nowhere to hide.

**Takeaway:** do not buy the big room, write the opera for the room you have, and let the smallness be the intensity, because spectacle hides the singing, and a village hall with thirteen players hides nothing.

## M6. Writing for a specific voice or performer

Music composed for one named person's sound, range and temperament, so the piece is inseparable from the player, and the mechanism is a part that fits one body like a glove, the instrument or voice as the subject, not the vehicle. The song cycles are for Peter Pears, the Seven Sonnets of Michelangelo of 1940 as a declaration of love, in David Matthews's phrase, and the Serenade and the Nocturne and most of the tenor writing, and the Cello Sonata, the Cello Symphony and the three Cello Suites are for Rostropovich, who wrote on 6 June 1962, "Write for the cello everything that your heart tells you, never mind how difficult it is; my love for you will help me to master every note, even the most impossible ones", and the Suite for Harp is for Osian Ellis, and Nocturnal after John Dowland is for Julian Bream, and Songs and Proverbs of William Blake is for Dietrich Fischer-Dieskau, and The Poet's Echo is for Galina Vishnevskaya, and Phaedra is for Janet Baker. The performer is not the interpreter of the piece, and the performer is the reason the piece exists.

**Takeaway:** do not write for the instrument, write for the named hand and the named throat, because a part written for nobody is an exercise, and a part that fits one body like a glove is a portrait.

## M7. The collision of two text-worlds

Two incompatible materials, liturgy and war poetry, sacred and secular, kept in separate ensembles and separate languages until they are forced together at the end, and the mechanism is two groups on stage that do not acknowledge each other for an hour, then finally sing the same line. The War Requiem is the proof, and the Latin Requiem Mass is for soprano, chorus and full orchestra, and the poems of Wilfred Owen are for tenor, baritone and chamber ensemble, and the two text-worlds stay apart until the end, when Owen's "Strange Meeting" mingles with the In paradisum, and the soloists are chosen as a statement, Galina Vishnevskaya Russian, Peter Pears English, Dietrich Fischer-Dieskau German, and Shostakovich told Rostropovich it was "the greatest work of the twentieth century", and Matthews says "With the War Requiem Britten reached the apex of his reputation". The collision is the piece, and the reconciliation is earned by an hour of refusal.

**Takeaway:** do not blend the two worlds, keep them apart in separate ensembles until the last line, because a blend is a compromise, and two musics that refuse to meet until the end make the meeting the whole piece.

## M8. The church parable and Noh structure

Opera stripped to ritual, a small cast, a church, processional entrances, a narrator, masks and stylisation borrowed from Japanese Noh, seen on the 1956 Far East tour, and the mechanism is a play that begins as a procession and ends as a blessing, and the audience watches a ceremony, not a story. Curlew River of 1964, The Burning Fiery Furnace of 1966 and The Prodigal Son of 1968 are the three Parables for Church Performance, all premiered at Orford Church, and the Prince of the Pagodas of 1955-56 is the other half of the same Far East turn, a full-length ballet in which a Western orchestra imitates gamelan with high percussion, piano duet and celesta, after Britten heard Balinese gamelan again and saw Noh for the first time, "some of the most wonderful drama I have ever seen". The church is not a venue, and it is the form, and the ritual is the plot.

**Takeaway:** do not stage the story, stage the ceremony, and let the procession in and the blessing out be the whole arc, because a story acted is theatre, and a ritual watched is a parable.

## M9. The borrowed theme, transformed

Taking another composer's theme and making a whole work out of its transformation, and the mechanism is a familiar tune that keeps returning in disguise, waltz, march, funeral march, fugue. The Variations on a Theme of Frank Bridge of 1937 take the second of Bridge's Three Idylls for string quartet, Op. 6 No. 2, of 1906, and run it through ten character variations, parodying Italian opera and Viennese waltz and march, and close with a Mahlerian funeral march and an exuberant fugue in D major, sketched in ten days and fully scored by 12 July 1937, at Boyd Neel's request for Salzburg, and Matthews calls it the first Britten work to become a popular classic, and The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra of 1945 is built on a theme by Purcell, and the Purcell realizations with Imogen Holst, Dido and Aeneas of 1951 and The Fairy-Queen of 1967, are the same instinct applied to whole operas, and The Beggar's Opera of 1948 is a wholesale recomposition keeping Gay's melodies. The mirror image is Walton borrowing Britten's own Piano Concerto Impromptu for the Improvisations, and the borrowed theme is the ancestry, and Britten answers his predecessors by wearing their tunes until they become his.

**Takeaway:** do not quote the theme, wear it in ten disguises, because a quotation is a nod, and a theme that returns as waltz, march, funeral march and fugue is a whole career in one piece.

## M10. Orchestration as casting

Each group of characters is given its own instrumental colour, so the orchestra tells you who is on stage, and the mechanism is the sound changing completely when a new group enters, before anyone sings a note. A Midsummer Night's Dream of 1960 is the proof, and the fairies get bright percussive harps, keyboards and percussion, and the lovers get warm strings and wind, and the mechanicals get lower woodwind and brass, and Death in Venice of 1973 is the late proof, and Tadzio and his family are silent dancers, accompanied by colourful, glittering tuned percussion "to emphasize their remoteness", and the boy who never sings is marked by the one sound that never touches the singers. The orchestra is not the accompaniment, and it is the casting director.

**Takeaway:** do not dress the characters in the same orchestra, give each group its own sound, because a shared orchestra is a room, and a sound that changes completely when a new group enters is a casting.

## M11. The outsider against a hostile society

The recurring dramatic engine, one isolated individual, a whole community against him, and the mechanism is a crowd that moves as one body, and a single figure who cannot join its rhythm. Peter Grimes is the Borough against one fisherman, and Billy Budd is the ship, a closed male society, against the innocent the system destroys, and The Turn of the Screw is the governess against the house and the children, and The Rape of Lucretia is Lucretia against Rome's men, and the extent to which this reflects Britten's own position, pacifist and homosexual in 1930s-50s England, is debated by critics, and the debate is part of the mechanism, because the engine works whether the identification is biographical or not. The crowd is not a villain, and it is a rhythm, and the outsider is the one figure who cannot keep it.

**Takeaway:** do not give the hero an enemy, give him a community that moves as one body, because an enemy can be fought, and a crowd that will not let one figure join its rhythm is a verdict.

## M12. The song cycle as a life-long form

The cycle, not the symphony, as Britten's thinking form, short poems set in sequence, each song its own world, the whole held together by a single voice, and the mechanism is one singer, one night, many small rooms, each song a different light. The Quatre Chansons Francaises of 1928, age fourteen, are the first room, and Our Hunting Fathers of 1936 is the cycle as political pamphlet, Auden as the widening influence, and Les Illuminations of 1940 and the Seven Sonnets of Michelangelo of 1940 are the cycle as declaration, and the Serenade for Tenor, Horn and Strings of 1943 and the Nocturne of 1958, a different obbligato instrument in every song, are the cycle as a night's walk, and The Holy Sonnets of John Donne of 1945 put atonality in the first song and resolve to a firm B major at "Death, be not proud", and Songs and Proverbs of William Blake of 1965 interleave proverbs with songs in one continuous stream. The symphony argues, and the cycle accumulates, and the accumulation is the argument.

**Takeaway:** do not build the big form first, build the small room and then another small room, because a symphony argues, and a cycle of small rooms, each with a different light, is a life.

## M13. Community performance as a design constraint

Works designed so that children, amateurs and professionals perform together, and the audience is part of the piece, and the mechanism is the line between stage and hall dissolving, the audience singing, children conducting the animals two by two. Noye's Fludde of 1958 is the proof, premiered Wednesday 18 June 1958 at Orford Church, Aldeburgh Festival, children, amateurs and professionals together, the audience joining the hymns, animals processing two by two, handbells, recorders, bugles, strings and percussion, and Friday Afternoons is written for schoolchildren, and The Little Sweep and Let's Make an Opera are entertainments for young people, and Saint Nicolas and A Ceremony of Carols let the audience join the hymns. The community is not the audience for the piece, and the community is the piece.

**Takeaway:** do not write for the professionals and invite the children to watch, write the piece so the children are the crew and the audience sings, because a concert with an audience is a performance, and an Ark with the audience inside it is a community.

## M14. The twelve-note thread inside tonal music

Twelve-note rows and cycles used as colour and structure inside music that stays essentially tonal, and the mechanism is a melody that uses every note of the scale before it repeats, giving a sense of completeness with no home key. Canticle III, Still Falls the Rain, of 1954, puts the twelve-note cycle in the first five bars of the piano part, and it became a regular part of Britten's technique thereafter, and The Turn of the Screw of 1954 is the twelve-note theme as the ground for fifteen variations, and the late works generally introduce elements of atonalism from the 1950s on, "though remaining essentially a tonal composer". The row is not a system, and it is a thread, and the thread colours the key because the key is never abandoned.

**Takeaway:** do not choose between the row and the key, thread the twelve notes through the tonal frame, because a row that abandons the key is a system, and a row that colours the key is a thread.

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. The Bear premiere year. Wikipedia, "William Walton": "premiered at Britten's Aldeburgh Festival, in June 1966". Against: Craggs, William Walton: A Catalogue (Oxford University Press, 2015): first performance Aldeburgh, Jubilee Hall, 3 June 1967, twentieth Aldeburgh Festival; Encyclopedia.com, "Aldeburgh Festival": "Walton's The Bear (1967)"; ArkivMusic and Chandos: "premiere took place in Aldeburgh in June, 1967 as part of the twentieth Aldeburgh Festival". The 1967 date fits the composition dates, 1966-67, and the autograph dated 30 April 1967. The Walton article's 1966 is an error. Doorway item 5 still HOLDS on the 1967 date.

C2. Improvisations date. Wikipedia, "Benjamin Britten": "In 1970 Walton composed Improvisations on an Impromptu of Benjamin Britten". Against: Wikipedia, "William Walton": lists it as 1969; Encyclopedia.com Walton entry: "1968-69; San Francisco, Jan. 14, 1970". Resolution: composed 1968-69, premiered 14 January 1970. The Britten article's "1970" is the premiere year, not the composition year.

C3. Violin Concerto premiere day. Wikipedia, "Violin Concerto (Britten)" and most sources: 29 March 1940, Friday, Carnegie Hall, Brosa, Barbirolli, New York Philharmonic. Against: Carnegie Hall's own article: "March 28, 1940"; Boston Symphony programme notes: "March 27, 1940". All agree on venue, soloist, conductor and orchestra; only the day differs, 27, 28 or 29 March 1940. Unresolved. The study says "late March 1940" where the day matters, and gives 29 March with a caveat elsewhere.

C4. Piano Concerto 1945 revision, passacaglia or Impromptu? Wikipedia, "Benjamin Britten": the 1945 revision replaced the third movement "with a more sombre passacaglia". Against: Wikipedia, "List of compositions by Benjamin Britten": "rev. 1945, the original third movement - Recitative and Aria - replaced by an Impromptu"; Wikipedia, "Improvisations on an Impromptu of Benjamin Britten": "Britten's theme, labelled an impromptu, was added to the score of the concerto in 1945 when he revised it"; the Scribd catalogue excerpt: revised version movements are Toccata, Waltz, Impromptu, March. The title "Impromptu" is confirmed by three sources; the "passacaglia" label in the Britten article is uncorroborated and may be a confusion with the Violin Concerto's Passacaglia finale or the Grimes Passacaglia. The study does not repeat the passacaglia label.

C5. Aldeburgh Festival founding year. Britannica, twice: the festival was "founded 1947". Against: Wikipedia, "Benjamin Britten": "The Aldeburgh Festival was launched in June 1948"; Encyclopedia.com: "Annual fest. at Aldeburgh, Suffolk, since 1948". Resolution: the English Opera Group was founded in 1947; the festival itself first took place in June 1948. Britannica's 1947 is wrong for the festival.

C6. Simple Symphony date. Britannica: "the Simple Symphony for strings (1925)". Against: Wikipedia, "Simple Symphony": written December 1933 to February 1934 using material written 1923-26; premiered 6 March 1934; Encyclopedia.com: "Simple Symphony (Norwich, March 6, 1934, composer conducting)". Britannica's 1925 is the year of some of the source material, not the work.

C7. A Boy Was Born revision year. Britannica: "A Boy Was Born (1933; revised, 1958)". Against: Wikipedia, "List of compositions": "A Boy Was Born, Op. 3 (1933; revised 1955)"; the A Boy Was Born article: "Britten revised the work in 1955. C8. Departure for America. Wikipedia, "Benjamin Britten": "In April 1939 Britten and Pears sailed to North America". Against: EBSCO Research Starters: "Britten and Pears followed in May, traveling to Canada and then to New York." Minor; April is the better-sourced date, Wikipedia cites Matthews and Carpenter biographies.

C9. Cello Symphony premiere venue. Wikipedia, "Benjamin Britten": "Britten composed his cello suites, Cello Symphony and Cello Sonata for Rostropovich, who premiered them at the Aldeburgh Festival." Against: Hyperion notes, Mervyn Cooke, and the Chandos booklet: the Cello Symphony premiered in the Great Hall of the Moscow Conservatory on 12 March 1964, Rostropovich and the Moscow Philharmonic, Britten conducting; the first UK performance followed on 18 June 1964 at Aldeburgh. The Cello Sonata, 7 July 1961, and the suites did premiere at Aldeburgh; the Cello Symphony did not. The Wikipedia sentence over-generalises, and the study does not repeat it.
