# Hugo Demarco: Study Notes

**Site:** Hugo Rodolfo Demarco, born 13 July 1932 at Buenos Aires, Argentina, died 28 November 1995 at Aubervilliers, France, aged 63 -- Argentine-born French kinetic and op artist, a founder of the Centre de Recherche d'Art Visuel who left before it became GRAV, and the man who made a flat surface buzz by superimposing complementary colours: the painter of the vibration that needs no motor, who ran that flat buzz beside a parallel career of motorised light objects, mirrors and black light, and who closed his work, in 1994, with rotating colour circles in a lightbox -- the first work refined to a single turning gesture. He trained at the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes, Buenos Aires, degree 1957, moved to Paris in 1959 with Julio Le Parc and Horacio Garcia Rossi, held his first solo show at the Galerie Denise Rene in 1961, and was shown by Denise Rene from 1961 to 1993 (C7001, C7002, C7005, C7007, C7008, C7019).

## The life: the founder who left before the rename

Hugo Rodolfo Demarco was born on 13 July 1932 in Buenos Aires and died on 28 November 1995 at Aubervilliers, in the Paris area, aged 63 (C7001, C7002). The French Wikipedia cites the INSEE death register for the date; the full name, date and city agree across the English and French Wikipedia entries, the Mayor Gallery and the NGV record. He was Argentine by birth and French by later nationality; no source gives a date for acquiring French nationality, and none is stated here (C7003). The Mayor Gallery alone says he was of Italian descent and spent time in Brescia, Italy; no other source read confirms either, and both are carried as soft, not as fact (C7004).

He studied at the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes in Buenos Aires, finished his degree in 1957, then taught painting and design; his classmates there included Julio Le Parc, Horacio Garcia Rossi and Francisco Sobrino (C7005). Capitolium Art alone records two Buenos Aires group shows in 1956 and 1959 (C7006). In 1959 he moved to Paris with Le Parc and Garcia Rossi, "to join the French art scene", and made his first kinetic paintings there; his first solo exhibition, paintings and reliefs, was at the Galerie Denise Rene in 1961 (C7007, C7008). In 1963 he and other Latin American artists received a French government grant to stay in Paris, and the move became permanent (C7009). The NGV's "lived/worked in France 1962-95" conflicts with the sourced 1959 arrival and 1963 permanent move and is not used (C7009).

The group history is the contested core of this study and must be told exactly. In July 1960 Demarco was among the founders of the Centre de Recherche d'Art Visuel: ZKM states verbatim that Hector Garcia Miranda, Horacio Garcia Rossi, Hugo Rodolfo Demarco, Julio Le Parc, Vera and Francois Molnar, Francois Morellet, Sergio Moyano, Servanes (Simone Revoil), Joel Stein, Francisco Sobrino and Yvaral founded the Centre, which rented a small garage in the Marais as a common meeting and exhibition space (C7010). The French Wikipedia's CRAV list has ten names, omitting Moyano and Servanes; the ZKM twelve-name list is the fuller one, and no single canonical headcount is quoted without naming its source list (C7010). In 1960 the Motus group -- Morellet, Stein, Garcia Miranda and Yvaral, joined for the Brussels show by Garcia Rossi, Demarco, Moyano, Le Parc and Sobrino -- was the immediate run-up to the founding, renouncing individual signatures and declaring a non-commercial plastic research (C7014).

Then the fork. The Centre was renamed Groupe de Recherche d'Art Visuel in July 1961, and from then on the members were the six: Garcia Rossi, Le Parc, Morellet, Sobrino, Stein and Yvaral. Demarco left with the Molnars and Garcia Miranda over anonymity and the shared rue Beautreillis space (C7011). He was therefore a CRAV founder, not a GRAV member. The loose language in the Mayor Gallery and English Wikipedia -- "among the early members of GRAV" -- is shorthand for his CRAV role and must not be repeated uncritically (C7013). The "GRAV manifesto signatory" label is contested and is not used: Le Parc's own site reproduces the January 1961 "Propositions sur le mouvement" signed by five names -- Morellet, Le Parc, Sobrino, Yvaral, Stein -- with no Demarco, and the "Propositions generales" signed by six, again with no Demarco; the seven-name listing including Demarco comes only from an Artsy staging-page gallery text and could not be checked against the document (C7012).

In 1971 he joined Position, a group of Argentine geometrical artists living in Paris, including Antonio Asis, Carlos Agueero and Armando Durante; nothing beyond the founding membership is attested in the sources read (C7015). His work was featured in two Nouvelle Tendance documentaries, Le mouvement (1966) and Lumiere et mouvement (1967) (C7016). In 1967 he held one-man exhibitions at the Op Gallery Esslingen and at Denise Rene's Hans Nager Gallery in Krefeld, Germany (C7017). On 7 June 1968, at 3 a.m., he and Le Parc were arrested by police at the Saint-Cloud bridge, en route to Flins while scuffles were starting at the Renault factory; after 24 hours in custody they were told to leave France within ten days, and were expelled on 18 June, returning after about five months (C7018). At his arrest a retrospective of his work was running at Denise Rene. Solo exhibitions worldwide followed from 1961 to 1989, shown by Denise Rene from 1961 to 1993; museum holdings include the Musee d'Art Moderne de Paris, the Reina Sofia, the Albright-Knox, the Walker, MoMA and the Hermitage, and the NGV holds Relations transformables, edition 34/175, bought 1972 (C7019). His wife was Amalia; the children's names and number are unattested and are not given (C7020). He served as a trainer for the PUC rugby club, no dates given (C7020). The reported auction record, a 2008 Sotheby's New York sale of Relief a Deplacement Continuel for US$67,000, is reported only, not independently verified (C7020).

His network is the Buenos Aires cohort carried to Paris. Le Parc was his classmate, shared-studio companion, fellow traveller to Paris in 1959, fellow Centre founder, and fellow arrestee and exile in June 1968 (C7110). Garcia Rossi was the same classmate and traveller, and the one of the three who stayed into the six-member GRAV -- the pair marks the exact fork this study turns on (C7111). Jesus Rafael Soto was befriended in Paris among the Latin American expats, making kinetic work together, and hung with Demarco in group shows including Em Movimento in Sao Paulo in 2013 (C7112). No dated one-to-one Demarco-Yvaral collaboration, joint show or letter was found; the evidenced link is the shared founding plus the shared Denise Rene orbit, and it must not be overstated as a collaboration (C7113).

## M1 -- The superimposed complementary pair: the flat surface buzzes

The core device is one sentence, and it is the most repeated Demarco formulation in the sources: "achieving a unique optical vibration through superimposing complimentary colors" (Artnet, C7107). Vibration (W01, 1962, gouache on cardboard mounted on panel, 54 x 54 cm) is the earliest dated work in the catalogue and the clearest statement of the device: a square checkerboard of small red and blue squares on a white ground, the red-blue alternation regular across the whole field, no centre motif, the vibration purely flat and chromatic -- no motor, no relief (C7051). The mechanism is the pair, not the picture: two complements set against each other in a regular module, so that each colour advances and recedes against the other and the flat ground seems to move. The complementary pair changes across the career -- red/blue in 1962, green/magenta in Couleur 1981, blue/pink in Couleur 1984 -- but the device is constant (C7064, C7066).

**Takeaway:** superimpose the complements and the flat surface moves. The motor is the eye, not the object.

## M2 -- Two tracks at once: the eye moves, or the work moves

The first dated works already contain the whole career in miniature, because they are opposites made in the same year. Vibration (W01, 1962) makes a flat gouache shimmer with no moving part; Deplacement continuelle (W02, 1962, electrical system, black neon light with reflector, aluminium and painted metal on a wooden box) makes a box actually move with light (C7052). Demarco never chooses between optical and real movement; he runs both, and the rest of his career is a dialogue between them (C7052). The titles name the two poles: Vibration is what the eye does; Deplacement continuelle is what the object does.

**Takeaway:** run both tracks and let them argue. The oeuvre is a dialogue, not a choice.

## M3 -- The mirror and the plexiglass: the reflection changes the picture

Reflexion changeante (W03, 1967, coloured plexiglass and mirrors within a wooden casing with an aluminium frame, 38.5 x 38.5 x 8.5 cm, edition of 50, Editions Denise Rene) is the bridge between the flat paintings and the motorised reliefs: colour is no longer only painted on, it is reflected and changed by the viewer's position and the light (C7053). The title says it: the reflection itself changes. Mechanically, the mirror returns a second image that disagrees with the first as the viewer moves, so the work has no single settled state -- the picture is a function of where you stand. This is the same operation as the complementary pair, moved from pigment into optics: the colour is now in the light path, not on the surface.

**Takeaway:** put a mirror in the work and the viewer becomes a variable. The picture is finished by the position, not by the artist.

## M4 -- The motorised relief: light as the event

Relief a deplacement continuel (W04, 1967, painted plastic, metal and cork, mounted on a wooden case, motor, light bulbs, 60 x 50 x 20 cm) is the 1967 system in its most collectable form: a tall black panel, almost entirely dark, with a small loose cluster of yellow-green dots of light near the centre -- at thumbnail resolution the relief structure itself is barely legible and the light dots are the visible event (C7054). Structure animee a deplacement continuel (W05, 1968, painted metal and cork, motor, black light, wooden cases) is the scale jump: a 203 cm floor-standing light-tree, a tall thin vertical stem from a small dark base topped by a branching cluster of coloured points of light, photographed in darkness (C7055). Black light (UV) is new in the medium list: colour now comes from light itself, not only from pigment or reflection (C7055). The relief is a shallow box the motor turns inside; the viewer watches dots of light travel across darkness.

**Takeaway:** hide the motor in a black box and let the light be the work. Darkness is the ground, not the absence.

## M5 -- The identical module, transformed: seriality, not composition

Relations transformables (W06, 1970, stainless steel, transparent synthetic polymer resin, motor, 40 x 40 x 17.5 cm, edition 34/175, Editions Denise Rene, NGV, bought 1972) reduces the moving parts to identical chrome crescents: a square brushed-steel box photographed from above, on its top surface seven polished chrome C-shaped tubular arcs, each on a thin vertical rod, arranged in a loose scattered cluster (C7056). The "transformable relation" is between identical elements in different rotations -- seriality, not pictorial composition (C7056). The motor is hidden inside a clean steel box; nothing is depicted; the work is the relation between seven copies of one module, each turned differently. The NGV record and the reverse label disagree on depth (17.5 cm against 10 cm); the two figures are flagged, not reconciled (C7056).

**Takeaway:** repeat one module and turn each copy differently. The relation is the picture.

## M6 -- The print multiple: vibration needs no unique object, and no colour

Relation Couleur (W10, 1973, silkscreen on velincarton, edition of 200, hand-signed and numbered, Denise Rene Editions blind stamp) editions the vibration as a print portfolio: the optical effect is reproducible and portable, no motor, no unique object, and the Relation in the title is the point -- colour exists only in relation, sheet to sheet (C7060). The untitled screenprint from a plotter drawing (W11, c. 1970-75, black-and-white screenprint on paper, 81.5 x 66 cm, V and A, accession E.78-2008) proves the vibration did not depend on colour at all: a black field inside a thin pale border, filled with a dense regular grid of small white dots that brighten and cluster toward the centre-left, swelling into a soft luminous cloud and fading again toward the edges (C7061). A dot grid, modulated in density, vibrates on its own. The attribution is provisional -- the V and A itself says "probably by Hugo Demarco", the CAYC Buenos Aires name and address on the reverse of the plotter drawing being the attribution evidence -- and it is not presented here as a certain Demarco (C7061).

**Takeaway:** edition the effect and it survives without the object. Density alone can vibrate; colour is optional.

## M7 -- The pure Couleur system: grid, pair, glow, diagonal

From 1978 to 1985 the motor recedes almost entirely from the record and what remains is acrylic, a square format (60 cm standard, then 120 cm), and a small set of operations on a grid (C7063). Couleur 1981 (W14, acrylic on canvas, 60 x 60 cm) is the mature formula, image-verified: a checkerboard of small squares in teal/green and magenta/purple, dark at the edges, glowing pink-red toward the centre, as if a light were behind the middle of the grid (C7064). Couleur 1983 (W15) is a gradient machine: concentric stepped diamond frames in small purple and magenta pixels, closing inward toward a plain white square in the centre -- the frame seems to advance and recede at once, and the painting is all frame, all transition (C7065). Couleur 1984 (W16) breaks the symmetry: a deep blue ground of small squares crossed by a broad diagonal band of pink-magenta squares running from bottom-left to centre-right, fading from pale pink at the lower end to deep purple where it meets the blue (C7066). Saturation metalice 1978 (W13, acrylic on canvas, 60 x 60 cm) makes saturation a series title in its own right, the metallic paint halfway between the flat acrylics and the chrome of Relations transformables (C7063). 4 Saisons (E) 1985 (W17, acrylic on panel, 120 x 120 cm) doubles the standard in each direction, four times the area, the Couleur system scaled up to a wall (C7067). The generic titles -- Couleur, Saturation -- say the system, not the picture, is the work.

**Takeaway:** fix the format, vary the operation. One grid, one pair, one move per picture -- the system is the author.

## M8 -- The late return to rotation: the circle closes

Fugas (W18, 1990) is the diagonal of 1984 become the whole composition: a black ground with diagonal bands of small square dots running top-left to bottom-right, one band glowing red-orange toward the top-right, another yellow-green toward the centre-left, the rest dim grey-white -- the most nocturnal of the image-verified paintings, the grid dissolved into dotted trajectories, like notes escaping a staff (C7068). Vitesse Bleu (W19, 1993) is "blue speed": a dark grey square panel from whose centre one vertical bar of bright blue light and two or three thin dark diagonal bars radiate, like the spokes of a turning wheel caught mid-rotation, a small horizontal blue-lit element at the bottom edge like a base or light strip (C7069). The medium is unattested, so no motor, lightbox or construction is asserted for it; the image strongly suggests a kinetic or light object, and that suggestion is not stated as fact (C7069). Movement (W20, 1994, lightbox with rotating colour circles and motor, 60 x 60 x 11.5 cm, signed, dated and titled on the verso "MOVEMENT, Demarco Paris 94") is the last dated work, executed the year before his death: every element of the practice in one 60 cm square -- the flat square format of the Couleur paintings, the motor of the 1967 reliefs, the coloured light of 1968, the circles the chrome crescents of 1970 only implied (C7070). The circle returns as the final form, and the last work is in effect the first work -- a light box, 1962 -- refined to a single rotating gesture.

**Takeaway:** end where you began, with one gesture left. The circle was there all along; the square was its frame.

## The aesthetic logic: the viewer as the motor

Four continuities hold the five phases together. The square: 54 cm in 1962, 60 cm again and again, 120 cm in 1985 -- the format barely changes in thirty years. The module: a small repeated unit -- square, dot, crescent, circle -- whose repetition, not its shape, does the work. The complementary pair, restated in every medium: gouache, plexiglass, chrome, acrylic, neon, black light. And the viewer as the motor: even in the motorised works, the consensus of the sources is that "form, color, texture, and rhythm were combined to produce virtual volumes and movements" -- the movement is in the perception, and the motor is only one way of triggering it (C7108). Demarco's wager is that a flat surface, given two colours set against each other in a regular module, will move for a still viewer; everything else in his practice -- the neon box, the mirror, the motor, the lightbox -- is a second way of cashing the same cheque. He is the founder who left: he helped found the collective programme and then kept its central question -- how does a surface move? -- as a solitary, lifelong research, answering it first in pigment, then in light, then in rotation, and finally in a single turning circle.
