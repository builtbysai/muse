## M1. The archive roll post

His early feed is dominated by backlog re-edited into time capsules. Captions literally date the material: "jan 2020 (1/2) 35mm [reupload]" (2021-12-10), "co summer 2021 Super 8 [reupload]" (2021-12-24), "sept 2021 35mm + miniDV" (2022-09-26). Posts are not timely; they are published when the roll is ready, and the carousel cover is usually the strongest single frame of the set. The 16-month silence from May 2025 to August 2026 broke the monthly cadence entirely, and the September 2026 return was itself backlog: Making Time 2023, 2024, and 2026 rolls published in one pass. The watchable behavior is release as event: the gap between capture and publication is part of the form, stated openly in the caption, so the feed reads like a diary being mailed in installments rather than a timeline being fed.

**Takeaway:** build the delay between capture and release into the work itself; date the material openly so the audience experiences the post as a letter arriving late.

## M2. The medium is the metadata

Captions habitually tag the film format as if it were the post's subject line: 35mm appears 21 times in the corpus, miniDV 4+, Super 8 5+, "reupload" 6 times, and vocabulary like "pan cel" (2026-09-23) names the presentation format outright. The caption does not describe the picture; it files it. Nikon F3 + a $5 reusable point-and-shoot; Ultramax 400, Ilford HP5, Gold 200, Tri-X 400 (PhotoLounge interview). The watchable behavior is a filing system made public: the viewer learns to read the medium tag first and the image second, which is why a post can be titled "iceland 2022 super 8 film" and need nothing else.

**Takeaway:** label the medium as the caption's headline and let the format tag do the describing; the viewer reads the stock name before the scene.

## M3. Overlay collage as the image

f01 (2021-11-19): translucent 35mm negatives layered over each other, basement party over party, arms and a pile of cans on white ground. He digitally mimics a double-exposed contact sheet and posts the collage itself as the work. The layers are same-scene ghosts: the same room stacked on itself until the party reads as an accumulation rather than a moment. This is the 2021 register, before the print collages take over in 2024, and it establishes the through-line: the photograph is never finished at exposure; the edit is a second exposure.

**Takeaway:** stack same-scene negatives translucently until the room accumulates; the edit is a second exposure, not a correction.

## M4. The miniDV and 35mm hybrid

Captions pair the two formats on one post ("35mm and miniDV" on the Trippie Redd post, 2021-11-29). f02 reads as a hybrid object: the low-angle red-stage-lit portrait of the rapper in a hood pointing at the camera, with two small inset strips at the right edge, a pink crowd still and a blue chain-link close-up, that read as miniDV stills embedded inside the 35mm frame. Two temporal textures live in one post: the crisp film portrait and the low-res tape artifact. The watchable behavior is the seam between them, the visible difference in resolution and color handling where the two media meet.

**Takeaway:** put two temporal textures in one frame and let the seam show; the resolution gap between the media is the subject.

## M5. Super 8 with the perforations kept

f03 (2021-12-24): a Super 8 frame with the perforation holes visible at the left, a mountain summit scene, girl in a pink jacket on a rock, half a friend's face at the right. f06 (2022-11-22): three frames of a Super 8 strip, perforations at left, a smiling man pressing his cheek against Stefani the cow in an Iceland pasture. He posts the film object, not just the image: the black sprocket strip is part of the composition, and the frames read as a sequence because the strip says so. The PhotoLounge interview confirms the Iceland 2022 trip was his first time abroad, and the cow photo is his self-named favorite, so the strip presentation also functions as a souvenir of the trip's novelty.

**Takeaway:** keep the film's sprocket edge in the frame; the strip turns three stills into a sequence and the object into a souvenir.

## M6. Light leaks as palette

f10 ("bleeding juniper" stills, 2024-01-23): two yellow Kodacolor film bags on gravel under heavy yellow and red leaks, a car door, red-cast portraits. The leaks are not accidents to crop out; they are the palette the collage is organized around. The watchable behavior is saturation pushed until the leak becomes the light source: the red cast unifies frames that would otherwise be a gravel close-up, a car detail, and two portraits. This is the film equivalent of a color grade, applied by the camera's own failure.

**Takeaway:** let the light leak carry the color grade; push the saturation until the camera's failure becomes the light source.

## M7. Print collage, taped

From 2024 onward the online signature is the physical assembly posted as the image: f09 (Red Bull, 2023-11-07) lays Matthew Law's record-store portrait into offset overlapping prints; f14 (Overmono, 2024-12-31) tapes two 4x6s at the seam, DJ above, hands-up crowd below, the masking-tape strip visible; f08 (2023-11-03) mounts a black-and-white documentary portrait with masking tape to white backing. The white border and the tape strip are his online presentation language, the Instagram equivalent of Omar-Abba's painter's tape but indoor, cleaner, and deliberately graphic. The watchable behavior is the construction showing: the seam, the overlap, the tape are left in because they say "this was arranged," and arrangement is the art after capture.

**Takeaway:** leave the seam, the overlap, and the tape in the frame; the visible construction tells the viewer the arrangement is the art.

## M8. Pan cels

f18 (2026-09-23): a wide, desaturated, stitched panorama of a riverside amphitheater, wooden stage structure, circular bench ring, yellow abstract sculpture, brick wall with string lights, overcast sky, captioned "@makingtimeisrad 2026 pan cel." The vocabulary matters: "pan cel" names a recurring format, the festival-ground equivalent of the Super 8 strip, a wide frame that holds the whole venue the way the sprocket strip holds the sequence. It appears in the return era, after the silence, as if the photographer came back needing to see the whole field at once.

**Takeaway:** stitch the venue into one wide named format; the panorama is the ground's equivalent of the film strip, holding the whole field at once.

## M9. Erasure and tape thumbnails

f19 (Boyharsher, 2026-10-08): the video thumbnail is a graphic object, not a still. The singer's face sits under heavy white brush-stroke erasure marks, an orange tape strip runs on black above, a handwritten "6" anchors the corner. The 15 videos in the corpus are thumbnails only to this research, so the mechanism is the cover treatment: mark the face, tape the frame, number the series. It is the same tape language as M7 moved onto motion, and it says the video is an object with a spine, filed like a roll.

**Takeaway:** treat the video thumbnail as a graphic object: erase the face, tape the frame, number the series; the cover is a file folder, not a still.

## Evolution

2021-11 to 2022-12 is the archival dump era: re-uploads, rap-artist portraits (Trippie Redd, Dominic Fike, Saweetie, Cage the Elephant, Wyclef Jean), the #wigglegram and #reto3d stereo experiments at soulstice, and it ends with the first physical product, the Edition Calendar 2023 (2022-12-02): 24+ removable 4x6 prints, the print logic already arriving. 2023 is the Iceland output and the documentary pivot (documentary portraits, Red Bull Bcone, music-video BTS for @lluk45, @rawdaisies, violenthighs). September 2023 to March 2025 is the festival house-photographer era: Making Time ∞, highbeam raves, BWC Sounds sessions, the schemantic.xyz animation, the eclipse with @jleggett.xyz assisting, DRATS shows, the "TOUCH GRASS" solo show. Then the 16-month silence. The September 2026 return is the same photographer with older material: backlog rolls, pan cels, erasure thumbnails. The arc is dump to product to residency to silence to archive, and the medium discipline never breaks: every era files its format.

## The aesthetic logic

The whole practice runs on the film object as a social contract. The perforation strip, the masking tape, the white border, the light leak, the miniDV inset, the format tag in the caption, the re-upload date: every one of them says "this is a thing that was made, by a person, with tools that fail in known ways, and it arrived late." The failure modes are the brand: the leak is the palette, the sprocket is the frame, the tape is the seam, the gap is the schedule. Underneath that contract sits a second mechanism, the residency: 16 posts for Making Time, photo credits that run from The FADER to WXPN to In Sheep's Clothing, the "TOUCH GRASS" show's flyer held in a blue-nailed hand. He is not a visitor to the scene; he is its filing clerk, and the archive roll is how a clerk publishes. What the generative pipeline can take from this is not film grain but the filing system: name the medium, date the material, show the seam, and let the release arrive late on purpose. The photograph earns its weight from the objecthood around it, not the other way around.

**Do not import:** the festival subject matter (Making Time, Overmono, the DJ circuit is his beat); the rap-portrait register (commissioned access, not a mechanism); the literal Super 8 sprocket (a film object's edge, not a generative texture); the DJ mixes (his other practice, tangential); the "photo + things" branding (his shrug, not ours).
