# Polyhop (Simon Alexander-Adams): Study Notes

**Site:** Simon Alexander-Adams, working as Polyhop, is a Detroit-based multimedia artist and designer specialising in real-time generative art, interactive installations and audiovisual performances. He earned an MA in Media Arts in 2015 from the University of Michigan School of Music, Theatre & Dance, where he experimented with designing new musical instruments, building interactive installations and directing multimedia performances; he started with Max and Processing, and his mentor and friend Carlos Garcia introduced him to TouchDesigner, which became his go-to tool. He played keyboards, synth and composition/production in the Detroit art-rock quartet saajtak for about a decade, and spent a month studying Carnatic music in India. He was a University Musical Society (UMS) Artist in Residence in 2016-17. From 2020 through 2024 he worked full time at ARTECHOUSE as an Interactive Multimedia Designer and Developer, focusing on software for large-scale installations; he went independent around 2025, expanding his TouchDesigner tutorials on YouTube and Patreon, working on local exhibits and installations in Detroit, and developing a new audiovisual project. His daily generative sketches, made in TouchDesigner and posted on Instagram as Polyhop, began in April 2018 and were "on year eight" in 2026. His birth date and birthplace were not established in any source read; do not assert them. [C37269]

**Relevance:** Polyhop helps investigate who decides the finished work when a system produces hundreds of outputs and the artist's only correction is keep-or-discard: his daily practice, his installations, and his fixed-seed ENSv2 cards all stage the question #839 left open -- when the system's reading is wrong and nobody corrects it, does the error become the record? The evidence is the ENS interview of 28 October 2026, titled "on the Art of Letting Go", in which he calls the balance of control "shepherding randomness"; the Dust physarum system, in which live audio analysis steers agents he does not individually direct; and the ENSv2 collection, in which a fixed seed and a fixed 1 June 2026 snapshot make every card final -- three systems in which the uncorrected output is released as the work, not repaired. [C37270]

**Doorway:** The 460th doorway. Harrison asked: when the recogniser is wrong and nobody corrects it, does the error become the record -- and who pays for the correction, the writer who must keep watch, or the reader who must keep doubting? Polyhop answers from the maker's side, and his answer is that the error becomes the record the moment the system ships it: in the ENSv2 collection a card computed once from a fixed 1 June 2026 snapshot with a fixed seed can never be re-rolled or corrected, and the only correction on offer is the holder's interpretation, which is never fed back into the system. In the daily practice the same answer holds at smaller scale -- a sketch that fails is not fixed, there is another chance tomorrow -- and in the installations the system's misreading ships live, in public, in real time, because "You're not going to create a foolproof system... But I also think that's part of the charm in some ways." The open question carried forward is: if the record is fixed and the doubt lives beside it, what must the display itself contain so the person reading it can tell the record from the doubt -- and who is responsible when the display does not show the difference? [C37271]

## M1 -- Shepherded randomness: the artist fixes the frame, the system decides inside it

**Subject:** Polyhop's whole practice, stated as a working method: the daily sketches, the installations, and the ENSv2 cards are all systems in which he chooses what the system can produce and what holds the outputs together, then lets the system decide the rest.

**Visual principle:** Tension between control and surrender, made visible as coherence-with-variation: every output in a series shares a recognisable frame (palette family, composition skeleton, material) while no two outputs are alike. The frame is what makes the variation legible instead of noise.

**Computational mechanism:** A two-level rule set. Level 1 (artist-fixed): the system design, the palette/composition frame, the parameter ranges, the decision to post or ship. Level 2 (system-decided): noise fields, simulation evolution, emergent pattern within the frame. The boundary between the levels is itself a parameter the artist sets per work -- in ENSv2 he fixes five trait mappings and a seed scheme, and the snapshot data fills the rest; in the daily sketches he fixes a system and lets one day's noise field differ from the next. As stated this is a design pattern, not a standalone simulation: it becomes executable only when instantiated in a specific piece (a palette table, a seed scheme, a parameter range), and no prompt below is built from M1 alone. [C37273]

**Artistic opportunity:** A piece can make the boundary itself the subject: show the viewer the fixed frame and the system's freedom inside it at the same time, so the viewer watches control being handed over rather than just its result. Most gallery pieces hide this boundary; Polyhop's practice invites showing it. [C37274]

**Takeaway:** The work is in deciding how much freedom to leave -- and that decision can be drawn. [C37275]

## M2 -- Physarum trail-following, steered by a live signal (Dust; Single Cell, Single Sky)

**Subject:** A slime-mould simulation (Dust, reused interactively in Single Cell, Single Sky): agents deposit trails on a field, sense the trails left by others, and follow them, so a small set of rules produces branching, vein-like networks.

**Visual principle:** Branching density against empty ground: thin luminous filaments fork like lightning or mycelium over black (the Dust still: hot magenta-pink filaments dissolving into electric blue), with the network's density readable at a glance. The trail is both the drawing and the memory of where agents have been.

**Computational mechanism:** Agents move on a 2D field, each sensing trail concentration ahead-left and ahead-right, turning toward the stronger trail, depositing trail as it moves; the trail field decays over time. Polyhop's own implementation adds two inputs: real-time audio analysis steers the motion, and external geometric patterns (hexagonal tiling, circle packing) are folded in as scaffolding the agents move over. The audio steering is a mapping from an analysed signal (bands/energy) to agent parameters (speed, turn angle, deposit rate) -- the signal does not draw the network, it biases the walkers that do. No parameters were obtained; a prompt built from this mechanism must state its own. [C37276]

**Artistic opportunity:** A network that visibly remembers and forgets: trails fade unless re-walked, so the drawing is a record of recent attention, not a fixed image. Steered by a generative rhythm instead of a recorded track, the network would pulse with its own beat -- a piece that grows its own veins to its own music. [C37277]

**Takeaway:** The trail is the memory; the signal is only the wind. [C37278]

## M3 -- One signal per layer: each visual element answers to a different voice (Tangled; Murmuration)

**Subject:** In Tangled, different layers of the music are isolated and analysed to drive different visual elements, "so everything on screen moves with a sound, in an abstract dance of color and form". In Murmuration, a live piano performance is reduced to a single activity measure (note count, duration, velocity in a time window), staccato passages are separated out to trigger faster visuals, and each note gets its own particle through a particle-and-attractor system.

**Visual principle:** Layered synchrony: several independent visual systems share one screen and one beat but move differently -- a Gerstner-wave mesh roiling, a ribbon sphere undulating in jagged quantised patterns, a Boids flock trailing blue and green -- so the eye can read which layer belongs to which voice. Contrast between layers (smooth mesh vs jagged ribbons vs swarming trails) is what makes the separation visible.

**Computational mechanism:** A bank of analysers, one per layer, each reducing a distinct input stream to a small set of control values (energy, onset, activity measure), each mapped to one visual subsystem's parameters. The subsystems are independent (mesh displacement, ribbon quantisation, flock steering) and share only the input clock. In Murmuration the generative music itself is modular: MIDI-clip modules in Ableton Live with follow behaviour that loops back or moves forward, randomised on every pass, with TouchDesigner launching a new module per section. [C37279]

**Artistic opportunity:** A piece with several visible voices, each honestly driven by its own stream, lets the viewer conduct by watching: the pleasure is in seeing which layer answers which voice, and in the moments when two layers coincide. It is the opposite of a single waveform driving everything. [C37280]

**Takeaway:** Give every layer its own ear. [C37281]

## M4 -- The body disturbs a calm system; stillness is rewarded (Moire Pool; Pisces Pool; Silhouettes I-IV)

**Subject:** A calm system -- a reflection pool (Moire Pool), a pool holding a school of virtual fish (Pisces Pool), four deterministic simulations (Silhouettes I-IV) -- that a visitor's movement disturbs and that settles when the visitor is still.

**Visual principle:** Disturbance against calm: ripples and scattered fish against a settling image; chaotic smoke-like effects against a slowly drifting field. The contrast is temporal as much as spatial -- the system has a visible relaxation time, and the viewer can watch the disturbance die away.

**Computational mechanism:** A stable base state (original imagery, a fish school holding formation, a reaction-diffusion field evolving slowly) plus a disturbance field injected at the visitor's position with a decay constant: each frame, disturbance = disturbance x decay + new input; the base system is attracted back to its calm state at a rate set by the decay. In Pisces Pool, reaching in ripples the pool and scatters the fish; stillness brings them close. In Moire Pool the explicit intention is that stillness is rewarded -- "if viewers stay still, they will see the settling of the original imagery". In Silhouettes, the visitor's outline shepherds growth and decay in a Game of Life variant. [C37282]

**Artistic opportunity:** Stillness as an input is almost unused in the gallery: most pieces reward touching and moving. A piece whose image only sharpens, resolves, or reveals itself after seconds of no input makes patience the interaction -- and makes the viewer's own restlessness visible as blur. [C37283]

**Takeaway:** The system notices when you stop. [C37284]

## M5 -- Geometric scaffolding under organic growth (Dust)

**Subject:** In Dust, Polyhop folds "external geometric patterns such as hexagonal tiling and circle packing" into his physarum simulation. The SEQUENCER gallery photo of Dust reads at that framing as pink/purple hexagonal cell-like rings with glowing dots -- the circle-packing layer dominating.

**Visual principle:** Order against organism: a regular lattice (hexagons, packed circles) gives the eye a grid to hold onto, while the organic network grows over and through it. The tension is between the perfect tiling and the imperfect growth that refuses to follow it.

**Computational mechanism:** A fixed geometric field (hex grid cells or a circle-packing layout, computed once per seed) is written into the same field the agents read: it biases agent sensing, deposit, or decay -- for example, trail deposits faster inside cells, or agents turn preferentially along hex edges -- without determining any single agent's path. Two rule systems share one field: one static and exact, one dynamic and emergent. Note the bias rule is this study's "e.g.", not Polyhop's stated rule: the source says only that the patterns were "folded in", not how. [C37285]

**Artistic opportunity:** The scaffold can be made visible on its own terms -- a piece that lets the viewer toggle, fade, or dissolve the geometry and watch the organism lose or keep its shape. The question "how much of the pattern is the grid, how much is the growth?" becomes something the viewer can test, not just admire. [C37286]

**Takeaway:** Put a perfect grid under a living thing and watch which one wins. [C37287]

## M6 -- Looping noise: a sine and a cosine tracing a circle at constant speed (Looping Noise tutorial; daily sketches)

**Subject:** The looping-noise technique from his tutorial "Looping Noise Part 1: Ending at the Beginning", recreating an Etienne Jacob Processing sketch: drive 4D noise with a sine wave on one parameter and a cosine wave on another, so the parameters trace a unit circle at constant speed and the animation loops seamlessly at any chosen length.

**Visual principle:** Seamless return: motion that never slows, stops, or reverses at the loop point -- the failure mode of a sine LFO alone, which "slows down, stops and reverses" at the turning point because sine waves do not have constant velocity. The visible effect is a texture or field that breathes forever without a seam.

**Computational mechanism:** Two noise parameters, p1 = sin(2 pi t / T) and p2 = cos(2 pi t / T), fed as the third and fourth dimensions of a 4D noise field; t advances at constant rate, so the parameter point travels the unit circle at constant speed and returns exactly to its start after period T. Any visual driven by the noise field (displacement, colour, density) inherits the perfect loop. Loop length T is a free parameter. [C37288]

**Artistic opportunity:** A perfect loop is a small miracle the viewer can feel but rarely name -- the piece never stutters, never jumps, and the eye can relax into it. A piece that exposes its own loop length (and lets the viewer change it while the seam stays invisible) turns a technical trick into a visible idea: the same field, breathing at different tempos, always returning. [C37289]

**Takeaway:** The circle, not the line, is what makes it loop. [C37290]

## M7 -- The fixed-seed record: snapshot data in, an uncorrectable card out (ENSv2)

**Subject:** The ENSv2 commemorative collection: five traits computed from a 1 June 2026 snapshot of a holder's ENS history -- Era x Archetype = 36 colour palettes, Depth (names held) setting compositional density, Gasveteran setting edge wear with circuit-like patterns and foil exposed by wear, Rarity (label length) setting finish -- plus a fixed seed for further variation. "Refreshing the page or claiming later won't give you a different version".

**Visual principle:** A legible ledger: the same layout on every card (wavy terrain band, branching network with faceted geodesic spheres, faint circuit background, worn edges) with palette, density, wear and finish varying by the data, so the viewer can read the record off the card. Density is a dial: the Depth cards show the same green/pink palette with the network growing steadily denser from Singular to Domainer while the terrain band stays fixed.

**Computational mechanism:** A deterministic mapping from a fixed input record to visual parameters: categorical inputs select a palette (Era x Archetype lookup table); a count input scales a density parameter; a history input scales a wear parameter (edge erosion, foil exposure); a length input selects a finish. A fixed seed supplies the remaining variation, and the whole computation runs once -- there is no re-roll, no correction pass, no feedback from the holder's reaction into the record. The mapping is total: every input record yields exactly one output, and the output is final. [C37291]

**Artistic opportunity:** This is the doorway mechanism. A piece can stage the moment a reading becomes a record: show the input, show the computation, freeze the output -- and then let the viewer annotate, doubt, or argue with it in a layer that is visibly *not* the record. The record stays wrong if it was wrong; the doubt is drawn beside it, never inside it. That separation -- record vs annotation -- is the visual idea no existing gallery piece stages. [C37292]

**Takeaway:** The card is final; the doubt has to live somewhere else. [C37293]

## M8 -- Deliberate rule-breaking as a marker of age (ENSv2)

**Subject:** In the ENSv2 collection, "names registered in earlier eras introduce increasingly complex palettes. For some of the earliest histories, Simon deliberately broke the rules he'd established elsewhere in the collection, bringing in inverted colors and unexpected contrasts". The Founding era is visibly the most complex and rule-breaking cell in the 6 x 6 grid.

**Visual principle:** Violation as signal: within a strict system, the one cell that breaks the rules reads instantly as special -- not because it is louder, but because the system around it is so consistent. Contrast between the rule and its breach is the whole effect.

**Computational mechanism:** A conditional override keyed to an input value: if input age (or rarity, or depth) passes a threshold, the mapping switches to a second rule set -- inverted palette, unexpected contrasts, higher complexity -- applied to that output only. The override is itself a rule (a rule about when to break the rules), so the system stays deterministic while producing an outlier. [C37294]

**Artistic opportunity:** A generative system that mostly obeys itself and occasionally, visibly, does not -- with the breach tied to something the viewer can understand (the oldest, the rarest, the most-worn) -- gives the viewer a moment of genuine surprise inside an otherwise orderly field. It is the opposite of random glitch: a meaningful exception. [C37295]

**Takeaway:** Break the rule only where the record earns it. [C37296]

## M9 -- Slow drift, then transition: deterministic systems that change colour over a fixed duration (Silhouettes I-IV)

**Subject:** Silhouettes I-IV: four systems, each drifting "slowly over the course of 10 minutes, moving through colors and variations before transitioning to the next". The systems are "wholly deterministic yet have the potential to generate organic and lifelike imagery".

**Visual principle:** Slow change against a stable image: the same reaction-diffusion maze or Game of Life field, seen over minutes, changes colour and variation so gradually that the viewer notices the change only by looking away and back. Time becomes a visible dimension, not a countdown.

**Computational mechanism:** A deterministic simulation (reaction-diffusion, fluid/particle, Game of Life variant) runs continuously while a separate slow clock drives a palette/variation parameter along a fixed path (for example hue rotation, feed/kill-rate drift) over a fixed duration; at the duration's end, the system switches to the next simulation and the clock resets. The simulation state carries across the colour drift but not necessarily across the transition. The 10-minute duration is a fixed parameter; the drift path is fixed; only the simulation's evolution is emergent. [C37297]

**Artistic opportunity:** A piece that rewards staying -- the longer you watch, the more the image has changed, and the change is the piece. Against a gallery of pieces that loop in seconds, a ten-minute drift with a visible transition is a different kind of attention, and the transition itself (one system handing over to the next) is a moment worth staging. [C37298]

**Takeaway:** Deterministic does not mean still. [C37299]

## M10 -- The room as an instrument: position along a line plays the panels (Blooming Strings; CIRCULEIGHT)

**Subject:** Blooming Strings: eight transparent OLED panels treated as "a single digital instrument" -- lidar tracks where visitors are, vines bloom and butterflies come to life on the nearest panels, each activation triggers a sound, so moving through the space "plays" the screens; entering or leaving sends a wave of butterflies and petals across all eight. In CIRCULEIGHT lidar captures the whole room so visitors' silhouettes shape the wall visuals.

**Visual principle:** Local activation against global wave: a bloom appears exactly where the visitor is (local, precise), while entry and exit trigger a wave across the whole field (global, sweeping). The contrast between the two scales is what makes the space feel like an instrument rather than a sensor.

**Computational mechanism:** A one-dimensional position input (distance along the panel line, or silhouette position in the room) is mapped to a per-panel activation value with falloff by distance; each panel has its own state (dormant to blooming to fading) with a trigger threshold and a decay; crossing a boundary (entering/leaving the tracked zone) fires a global wave event that propagates panel to panel with a fixed delay. Sound triggering is bound to the same activation events. [C37300]

**Artistic opportunity:** A piece that turns the viewer's path into a melody of blooms -- walk slowly and each panel flowers in turn; walk fast and the wave outruns you. The entry/exit wave gives the piece a beginning and an end that belong to the viewer, not to a timer. [C37301]

**Takeaway:** Walking is playing. [C37302]

## Mechanism note -- differential growth (Hoff), cited but not his

Anders Hoff's "shepherding random growth" -- nodes and edges with attraction, rejection, and curvature-biased splitting (curvature estimated as 1 - |dot(a,b)|, split only if a uniform draw is less than the curvature) -- is the source of the phrase Polyhop cites, "shepherding randomness". It is Hoff's mechanism, not Polyhop's: no source read says Polyhop uses differential growth himself. It is recorded here as provenance for M1's vocabulary only. No prompt in this study is built from it; see Rejections. [C37303]

## The arc: from a pool in the woods to a card on a chain, the system stays the subject

Polyhop's arc is a single question asked at three scales. At Michigan and in the early Detroit years (2013-2017) the question is bodily and small: a pool you can reach into, a silhouette that shepherds pixels, a dream machine that answers light with light -- systems a visitor can touch, built from Max, Arduino, Kinect and TouchDesigner, with the artist usually in the room. At ARTECHOUSE (2020-2024) the question moves to architectural scale: lidar rooms, 40-to-43-skyscraper facades, a 20 x 5 metre LED screen in Sunderland, science collaborators (NASA, the Society for Neuroscience, WHOI) supplying the data the systems run on -- and the visitor is now one body among many in a tracked room, not the sole player. Since going independent (2025-2026) the question turns inward and becomes about the record itself: Dust's physarum system is reused interactively in Single Cell, Single Sky, the daily practice enters year eight, and the ENSv2 cards turn on-chain history into a fixed, uncorrectable image -- the system no longer needs a room at all, only a snapshot and a seed. [C37304]

Three constants run through all three eras. First, the system is always the artwork: whether it is a slime mould, a flock, a reaction-diffusion field or a trait mapping, the rules are the piece and the image is their residue. Second, the artist's job is always the same decision -- how much freedom to leave -- stated as "shepherding randomness" and practised as a daily discipline of shipping what the system produced. Third, the viewer is always given a real role, never a spectator's seat: disturb the pool, walk the line of panels, play the piano the system listens to, hold the card whose record cannot change. The arc's end is the doorway's answer: the uncorrected output ships, and the only correction on offer is what the audience finds in it. [C37305]

## The aesthetic logic

Polyhop's aesthetic is the legible system on a black ground. The beauty is in a rule you can watch working: filaments that remember where they have been and forget where they have not, a mesh that roils to one voice while ribbons snap to another, a card whose density you can read as a number of names held. Nothing is hidden in a preference panel or a confidence score; the system is on the surface, where the viewer can see it and act on it. The palette is restraint itself -- hot magenta-pink dissolving into electric blue on black, white maze-lines on black, one saturated card at a time -- because the mark must be readable as a system's output, and decoration would break the reading. Slowness is not a mood here; it is the price of attention, paid in the 8 seconds of stillness before a pool resolves, the 10 minutes before a system hands over to the next, the 8 years before a daily practice becomes a body of work -- and the honest piece shows the price rather than hiding it. [C37306]
