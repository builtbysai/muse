## The life: the draughtswoman who made the city her drawing board

Carmela Gross was born in Sao Paulo on 16 February 1946 and trained at FAAP, 1965-1969, under Flavio Motta, whose course was founded on drawing understood as a project and as the construction of social forms of freedom and emancipation. That sentence is the whole career in miniature: drawing not as a picture but as a project, and the project as a way of freeing people in a place. She has never stopped working from it. (C13600, leg 1; C13601, leg 1)

Her own statement of programme, from her site, is the compass for everything that follows: "The common axis, beyond the diversity of contexts and proposals elaborated in each case, is the basic concept of working-in-the-city. The set of operations that involve everything from the conception of the work, through the production process, to its placement in the exhibition space emphasize the dialectical relationship between the work and space, between the work and the public/passerby. The works seek to engender new artistic perceptions that affirm a critical action and thought and bring out the semantic load of the place, whether it is a public space, an institution, or the moment of an exhibition." And on drawing itself: "It is present, it leads me, it is the beginning of any work I do." (C13616, leg 1)

The career runs in six phases, and the hinge is the same as the doorway: the student who taught children to draw in a public square became the professor who made the square, the avenue and the Bienal building itself into drawings.

(1) Training and the street classroom, 1946-1969. In 1966, still in her second undergraduate year, she co-created Arte na Praca with fellow students including Marcello Nitsche, Ana Cristina Rocco and Iza Ribeiro: free drawing workshops for children on Sundays in Praca Dom Jose Gaspar, Sao Paulo, behind the Biblioteca Mario de Andrade, running to 1971. She also took a first prize for sculpture in 1966 and showed at the IX Bienal in 1967, where the early sculpture "Nuvens" (Clouds) already showed the pop-art influence and the visual vocabulary of children that mark the early work. In 1968 came the intervention "Escada" (Ladder), a drawing made on an embankment on the outskirts of Sao Paulo, and participation in "Bandeiras na Praca General Osorio" in Rio, a collective banner demonstration. She graduated from FAAP in 1969, and the same year showed at the X Bienal her second Bienal: "A Carga" (The Load), "Presunto" (Ham), "A Pedra" (The Rock) and "Barril" (Barrel), made from raw and appropriated city materials -- mattresses, truck tarps, barrels, straw, plastic -- carrying connotations of threat and danger under the military dictatorship (1964-1985). "Barril" refers to the barrel used as a torture instrument. Also in 1969 she joined Paulo Mendes da Rocha's team for the Brazilian Pavilion at the Osaka International Expo (Expo '70). (C13602, leg 1; C13605, leg 1; C13606, leg 1)

(2) Teacher and multimedia experimenter under the dictatorship, 1970-1980. She worked 1968-1973 at the Centro de Educacao e Arte, an art school for children and for the training of art teachers, directed by Fanny Abramovich; taught at the Escola de Belas Artes de Sao Paulo 1971-1972; and joined the Universidade de Sao Paulo in 1972 as a teacher in the Departamento de Artes Plasticas, ECA-USP, where she taught undergraduate and postgraduate visual arts for over four decades. She belongs to the generation that began working in the late 1960s and early 1970s, years of resistance to censorship and state violence, working collectively in public space, streets and alternative venues with performance, happening, theatre, video and film, breaking with traditional art categories. In 1977 she held her first solo show of drawings and produced a video the same year, a black gouache composed of images shown orthogonally on screen, connoting a prison. In 1978 came "Carimbos" (Stamps) at the Gabinete de Artes Graficas: 80 works made by repeatedly stamping rubber stamps on 70 cm x 1 m boards to form fields of repeated images, with antecedents running back to the 1968 stamp experiments. (C13602, leg 1; C13603, leg 1; C13605, leg 1; C13607, leg 1)

(3) Postgraduate artist-researcher and the sky project, 1980-1989. The ECA-USP postgraduate programme in visual arts (PPGAV) was created in 1974 -- the first postgraduate programme in Brazil designed for artists, with the master's accredited in 1974 and the doctorate in 1980 -- under Walter Zanini, with Regina Silveira and Julio Plaza among the key artist presences. Gross, on the faculty since 1972, was one of its first artist-researchers. In 1981 she took her master's degree under Zanini; the thesis was the artwork itself, "Projeto para a construcao de um ceu" (Project for the Construction of a Sky), a set of 33 drawings in coloured pencil and India ink, shown the same year at the XVI Bienal. In 1982 she took part in "Artemicro", the microfiche exhibition at MAC-USP jointly organised by Silveira and Rafael Franca. In 1983 came "Quasares" (Quasars), exploring the dimensions arising from industrialisation and technology. In the late 1980s the work moved into painting, three-dimensional objects and painting-objects -- bulky works neither painting nor sculpture, attached to wall or floor, made of painted wood or cast aluminium. In 1987 she took her doctorate in arts under Zanini, thesis "Pintura/desenho", defended at MAC-USP. (C13604, leg 1; C13603, leg 1; C13607, leg 1; C13608, leg 1; C13609, leg 1)

(4) The city at urban scale, 1990-2002. In 1991 she won the Fundacao Vitae Fellowship and made costumes for Philip Glass's opera "A Queda da Casa de Usher"; she has made costumes and stage designs for theatre throughout her career. In 1994 came "Buracos" (Holes) for Arte-Cidade I in the former municipal slaughterhouse: 48 holes dug in a regular pattern across about 350 m2 of shed floor. In 1997 came "A Negra" (The Black Woman), first installed on Avenida Paulista: a ten-foot metal structure covered in layers of black tulle, on wheels, alluding to the invisibility of certain bodies in the urban landscape. In 2001 she made the permanent public work "Fronteira, Fonte, Foz" in Laguna, Santa Catarina, a triangular elongated square of about 1,600 m2 paved in black and white Portuguese mosaic. In 2002 came the turn to light as material and language as urban sign: "Hotel", a 3 m high, 15 m long fluorescent-tube sign spelling HOTEL in red on the outside of the Bienal building for the XXV Bienal; and "Eu Sou Dolores" (I Am Dolores), a 3 m high, 25 m long red fluorescent sign for ArteCidadeZonaLeste at the old Sesc Belenzinho building, its structure 313 bulbs. (C13613, leg 1; C13614, leg 1; C13609, leg 1; C13610, leg 1)

(5) Light, language and permanent works, 2003-2019. "Aurora" (2003) put the word AURORA in pink-violet tubular lamps on a wall, bathing the room in rosy light. "BleuJauneRougeRouge" (2004) was a permanent architectural intervention on the facade of the Ecole Rene Binet in Paris, demolished with parts of the building in the winter of 2015. "Cascata" (2005) is a permanent staircase of 16 superimposed slabs forming a stepped amphitheatre on the bank of the Guaiba river in Porto Alegre, designed to go unnoticed as artwork. "Carne" (2006) covered a whole city bus in layers of red adhesive; "Sul" (2006) was a 200 m2 luminous sign of 255 white fluorescent tubes hung in the Instituto Tomie Ohtake. The retrospective "Carmela Gross: Um corpo de ideias" (A Body of Ideas), curated by Ivo Mesquita, opened at the Estacao Pinacoteca in 2010 and won the Premio Bravo! for best exhibition in 2011; "Iluminuras", 76 rotating yellow beacons ringing the building -- the former DOPS political-police headquarters -- was made for it. In 2017 came "O Fotografo" (The Photographer) at Kunsthalle Bratislava, the permanent sign "Grande Hotel" at Sesc 24 de Maio, and "Radical Women: Latin American Art, 1960-1985" at the Hammer Museum. In 2019 the solo show "Roda Gigante" (Big Wheel) at Farol Santander gathered about 300 discarded objects tied with ropes to the atrium's columns and balustrades, forming an irregular web. (C13609, leg 1; C13610, leg 1; C13612, leg 1; C13618, leg 1)

(6) Recent work, 2020-2026. In 2021 she showed at the 34th Bienal, her eighth Bienal, with "Boca do Inferno" (Hellmouth), a panel of 160 monotypes made at the Fundacao Ibere Camargo print studio, named after the 17th-century nickname of the Bahian poet Gregorio de Matos. In 2023 the Instituto de Arte Contemporanea revisited the 1978 stamp series with more than 300 items of process documentation and 12 of the original 80 works. In 2024 "Quase Circo" (Almost Circus), curated by Paulo Miyada at Sesc Pompeia, gathered works from 1990 to 2024 plus two new ones -- "Bandeira Pivo" and the large installation "Gato", which realises in colour a Lina Bo Bardi sketch of the Sesc Pompeia walkways -- and a new iteration of "Roda Gigante" with about 320 objects. Her own CV lists group shows through 2026. She lives and works in Sao Paulo. (C13611, leg 1; C13612, leg 1; C13619, leg 1)

Her own framing, for the study's voice: drawing "is present, it leads me, it is the beginning of any work I do" -- and the work is always working-in-the-city, a dialectic between the work and the space, between the work and the public/passerby, bringing out "the semantic load of the place". On the stamps: "Each stamp, with its brand, is repeated many times on the same board, measuring 70 cm x 1 m, forming a pattern. I stamped one thing next to the other, until I formed this field of repeated images"; the stamps were "the matrix for thinking about the works". On "Barril", fifty years on: "As an instrument of torture, it [the barrel] was, in turn, already a residue of the North American oil industry. A metaphor reduced to a minimum, almost a non-metaphor, but which implied foreign domination and Brazilian dependence, including in the clandestine practices of the dictatorship" -- and the same statement, she adds, "presents a Brazil still submissive to the USA and with democracy threatened." (C13616, leg 1)

The through-line of the life is not a style but a scale that keeps growing while the tool stays the same: drawing as trace, sketch and project -- "a mark of gestures, thoughts, and processes that solidify in the work" -- runs from the Sunday workshops for children, through the stamps (a manual gesture replaced by mechanised stamping), the sky project (33 drawings as a thesis), to the staircases, holes, buses and light-signs that are drawings built at urban scale. The city is the material and the interlocutor throughout: raw found materials in 1969, the exposed guts of the Latin American city (cables, pipes, scaffolding) as explicit raw material, and from the 2000s the city's own communication forms -- neon, fluorescent signs, billboards -- taken up and subverted, so that the work and the passerby complete each other in the street. Politically, the work is a record of Brazil under and after the dictatorship: the barrel as torture residue, the DOPS building ringed with beacons, the "Boca do Inferno" monotypes as revolt against the contemporary Brazilian context. (C13617, leg 1; C13708)

## M1 -- The stamp as drawing machine: the manual gesture replaced by mechanised repetition

A scribble or stain is transposed into a rubber stamp and replicated mechanically, one stamp next to another, until a field of repeated images forms. "Carimbos" (1978, W04): 80 works on 70 cm x 1 m boards, each stamp repeated many times on the same board; the artist: the stamps were "the matrix for thinking about the works"; ICAA: "the artist replaced the sensual and manual gesture by mechanized stamping in a serial stroke". Antecedents run from the 1968 stamp experiments. Watchable: a field that builds stamp by stamp, one motif repeated until it stops being an image and becomes texture; the hand is present only in the first mark, the machine does the rest. (C13683, leg 2 C13624; leg 1 C13607, C13616)

**Takeaway:** a stamp earns its mechanism when repetition changes the mark's kind, not its count. If the field is one image copied eighty times, it is a print run; if the copying turns a scribble into a surface, the stamp is the drawing machine.


## M2 -- Drawing as frontier: the line that fences the body

The drawing leaves the paper and becomes a physical boundary on the floor or wall, conditioning where the spectator can walk. "Ilha I" (1995, W08): brass, steel and rubber band, diameter 210 cm, "the outlining of spaces on walls or the floor through a drawing configured as a physical frontier between the outside and inside of a space defined as closed"; drawing dematerialized into constructions "dealing directly with the spectator's bodily presence, his movement in space, and therefore his freedom". "Buracos" (1994, W07): 48 holes dug in a regular pattern across about 350 m2 of slaughterhouse floor -- the drawing is a void in the ground you must walk around. "Alagados" (2000, W13): the frontier moves from wall to floor. "Em vao" (1999, W12): a black-ribbon labyrinth among a colonnade that "conditioned the spectators' movements". Watchable: the spectator's path bends around a line on the floor; the line is a wall you can step over but do not. (C13684, leg 2 C13627, C13628, C13632, C13633; leg 1 C13609)

**Takeaway:** a line becomes a frontier when the body obeys it. If the line decorates the floor, it is a pattern; if the crowd routes around it, the drawing is architecture.


## M3 -- The hinge that flips furniture into weapon

An iron form hinged to the wall rotates between two readings: a schematic chair, then an aggressive set of rebars. "Feche a Porta" (1997, W11): 18 mechanical artifacts, each two closely symmetrical mirrored halves of cylindrical iron bars hinged to the wall, together forming a schematic drawing in space resembling a chair; when rotated, the unit is undone and the metal rods generate other shapes. "The hinge is a simple mechanism that allows you to easily revert a representation of power, such as a chair-shape, into an aggressive set of rebars." Companion: "Gancho 2" (1995, W09), iron, 63 x 53 x 0.5 cm -- "HOOK is both drawing and sculpture simultaneously"; its sharp edge suggests perforation and danger. Watchable: a row of chairs on a wall, then a push and the chairs come apart into spears; power is a hinge angle. (C13685, leg 2 C13629, C13631)

**Takeaway:** a hinge earns its mechanism when one rotation changes the object's politics. If the chair stays a chair, it is furniture; if a turn of the hinge turns it into rebar, the hinge is the argument.


## M4 -- Found city materials as a threat record

Raw and appropriated materials from the city carry the connotations of threat and danger under the dictatorship. The 1969 X Bienal set (W03): Presunto, A Carga, A Pedra, Barril -- mattresses, truck tarps, barrels, straw, plastic -- "exercises in registering an urban landscape charged with an ambivalence between opacity and morbidity"; the set referred to veiled or hidden urban elements, usually unseen by passersby. Barril: the barrel was often used as a torture tool; the artist: "As an instrument of torture, it [the barrel] was, in turn, already a residue of the North American oil industry. A metaphor reduced to a minimum, almost a non-metaphor, but which implied foreign domination and Brazilian dependence." Watchable: familiar city junk -- a mattress, a barrel -- staged so its menace is legible; the material is the evidence, not the medium. (C13686, leg 2 C13623; leg 1 C13605, C13616)

**Takeaway:** found material earns its threat when the object is already charged before the artist touches it. If the junk is only texture, it is assemblage; if a barrel reads as a torture instrument, the city supplied the meaning and the artist only framed it.


## M5 -- Light as the drawing unit: the city's own signs taken over

The fluorescent tube and the neon word become the unit of drawing, and the city's commercial signage is taken up and subverted. "Eu Sou Dolores" (2002, W14): a 24.95 m x 2.10 m luminous board, 313 red fluorescent bulbs, "an atmospheric element, in the limit of the incorporeal... indicating an individuality that gains consistency through transit"; the artist: "I AM DOLORES is the red light that transcribes the low lands of the enormous city". "Hotel" (2002, W15): a large illuminated HOTEL sign on the Bienal building, pointing both to the temporary character of the show and its privileged character. "Aurora" (2003, W16): the word AURORA in pink-violet tubular lamps bathing a room in rosy light. "O Fotografo" (2012, W18): a man drawn in red light in empty space, lamps as "the basic unit of drawing", built from standard lamp lengths after the Vitruvian Man, cables and transformers left exposed. Watchable: a word in light so large it names the whole building; a figure drawn in lamps that hums with visible cables; the sign that was supposed to sell you something now names you instead. (C13687, leg 2 C13634, C13635, C13636, C13638; leg 1 C13609, C13610)

**Takeaway:** a light sign earns its subversion when it uses the city's own vocabulary against the city. If the neon merely glows, it is a lamp; if the word in light names the passerby and the cables stay visible, the sign is the drawing.


## M6 -- The unbuildable project: the sky as a thesis

A drawing set becomes a project for something that cannot be built, and the project itself is the work -- even the thesis. "Projeto para a construcao de um ceu" (1981, W05): 33 drawings in coloured pencil and India ink, 0.70 x 1 m each, Gross's master's thesis under Zanini, shown at the XVI Bienal 1981; grown out of drawings, paintings, objects and stamps made 1976-1979 representing fragments of urban space and celestial spaces. "Quasares" (1983, W06): the series "explored the dimensions that arise as consequences of recent industrialization and technology". Watchable: thirty-three sheets of sky, each a fragment, hung as a construction manual for something no one can build; the thesis is a set of drawings, not a text. (C13688, leg 2 C13625, C13626; leg 1 C13603, C13607)

**Takeaway:** a project earns its unbuildability when the drawings are the deliverable. If the sky is a picture, it is a study; if thirty-three sheets are submitted as the thesis for a sky, the project is the work.


## M7 -- The veiled body on wheels: invisibility that moves through traffic

A body made invisible by the city is given wheels and sent back into it. "A Negra" (1997, W10): a ten-foot iron structure covered in layers of black tulle, on wheels, first installed on Avenida Paulista. Hammer: the piece "alludes to the invisibility of certain bodies in the urban landscape". The artist: "the object of iron and black nylon tulle, armed with wheels for traveling on the roads, on asphalt. I also see it as an object image that mixes the movement of streets, cars, sounds, building lights, shops and advertising, but it is still an object of desire that contrasts with the ever-changing city and the passersby -- by crossing, turning, walking, coming and going, and returning". Watchable: a black veiled figure rolling down an avenue among cars and billboards, turning, coming and going; the invisible body is the only thing moving at its own pace. (C13689, leg 2 C13630; leg 1 C13609)

**Takeaway:** a veiled body earns its wheels when it moves through the traffic that ignores it. If it stands on a plinth, it is a monument; if it rolls down the avenue, the street is the pedestal and the invisibility is the subject.


## M8 -- The discarded-object web: a drawing in space whose endpoints are ruins

Hundreds of discarded objects are tied with ropes to a building's columns and balustrades, forming an irregular web -- a drawing whose lines end in the ruins of a city. "Roda Gigante" (2019/2024, W19): about 300 objects at Farol Santander (2019), about 320 at SESC Pompeia (2024); "an 'impenetrable' Ferris wheel, a drawing in space, lines that have as their point of arrival (or starting point) the ruins of a city that is, at the same time, construction and dismantling". Companion: "Cascata" (2005, W17), a permanent staircase on the Guaiba riverbank "designed to go unnoticed as artwork", its edge in iron or CORTEN steel "so that it would be overlooked to clearly define the drawing lines" -- the drawing as infrastructure, meant to disappear into use. Watchable: a web of junk strung across an atrium, impassable, its lines meeting at broken things; or a staircase you climb every day without knowing it is a drawing. (C13690, leg 2 C13637, C13639; leg 1 C13610, C13612)

**Takeaway:** a web of discarded things earns its drawing when the lines end in ruins. If the objects are only hung, it is a mobile; if the ropes trace a ferris wheel that cannot turn, the city is the drawing and the ruins are its endpoints.


## The aesthetic logic: drawing is a project, the city is the material, the body finishes it

Gross's programme has three terms, and they never change, only scale up. First, drawing is a project, not a picture: a mark of gestures, thoughts and processes that solidifies in the work, a beginning that leads her -- "It is present, it leads me, it is the beginning of any work I do." A stamp is a drawing machine; a hinge is a drawing that moves; a neon tube is a drawing that glows; a staircase is a drawing you climb. Second, the city is the material: the barrel already carried the torture before she touched it; the slaughterhouse floor already carried the holes' meaning before she dug them; the avenue already carried the invisibility before she veiled the body and rolled it down it. She does not illustrate the city; she frames what the city already says, and the frame is the work. Third, the spectator's body is the last component: the line on the floor is only a frontier if the crowd routes around it; the ribbon labyrinth only exists as a steering of feet; the veiled body only matters because it moves at its own pace through traffic that ignores it; the neon word only lands because a passerby reads it and is named by it. The piece is unfinished until a body moves through it, and the movement is the reading.

That is why the same moves survive sixty years of changing supports: take a familiar thing, remove it from its use, put it where the body must negotiate it -- on the floor, on the wall, on the avenue, in light -- and let the place argue with it. The medium is chosen per problem, which is why the oeuvre runs from rubber stamps to fluorescent tubes without ever changing its question: what does a drawing do when it leaves the paper, and who completes it by walking through it? (C13711)
