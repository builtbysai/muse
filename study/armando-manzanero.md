# Armando Manzanero: Study Notes

**Site:** Armando Manzanero Canche (born Saturday 7 December 1935, Ticul, Yucatan, by the registration year, 1934 by the English Wikipedia; died Monday 28 December 2020, Mexico City, of COVID-19 complications, aged 85), Mexican composer, singer, pianist, actor and producer, called the Rey del Romanticismo, with more than four hundred songs by the lifetime counts and more than six hundred by the Sociedad de Autores y Compositores de Mexico (SACM) count at his death, more than fifty of them famous internationally, more than thirty albums, the first Mexican to receive the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award (January 2014), president of the SACM from September 2010 until his death, producer of Luis Miguel's Romance (1991), the composer whose songs Perry Como, Elvis Presley, Frank Sinatra and Tony Bennett sang in English, and the man who stood 1.54 metres tall and sang seated at the piano because the public identified him with the piano. He is not a singer in the sense of Pineda or Libertad, and that is the point of studying him here: he is the composer both of them duetted with, the man who wrote the songs they sang, the one who brings the song itself into the chain, because Pineda brings the trova and the ranchera and Libertad brings Peru and Manzanero brings the bolero that both of them needed. He was Maya by his own account, "Yo por mi raza soy maya, yo soy un indigena", he learned Spanish at four or five from a grandmother who spoke only Maya, and his surname Canche means a wooden bench, or, lengthening the vowel, a yellow stick. He is the composer the whole circle agrees on from the other side of the song: Pineda duets with him, Libertad trades verses with him, Luis Miguel owes him the bolero revival, and none of them has to explain why.

## M1. The piano traded for a sewing machine

He did not start with a piano. He started with a violin that had no bow. The Poniatowska interview gives it in his own words, and it is the mechanism stated as a childhood: he wanted to play the violin, but from the Casa Veerkampf to that faraway Yucatan a violin arrived WITHOUT A BOW, and the bow never arrived, and in the meantime his mother found a broken-down piano and changed her sewing machine for it, and he began to play the piano. The mechanism is the trade, not the gift: the instrument arrives broken and unwanted, the mother gives up the machine she sews with, and the boy gets the piano because the violin failed, so the career begins with a substitution, and the substitution is the career, because everything he does afterwards is a substitution of the same kind, a vals turned into a ballad, a bolero turned into a pop hit, a song written for one singer and carried by another. The trade has a second half: he studied music from the age of eight because his mother's great-aunt was the director of Bellas Artes in Merida, and his teachers were Chucho Zarzosa, Mario Ruiz Armengol and Vicente Garrido, so the broken piano was not left alone, it was taught. The first paid work is the mechanism's proof: at ten years old, on Sundays at nine in the morning, at the radio station of Manuel Araujo beside the Teatro Peon Contreras, he accompanied the children who sang, and they paid him with a voucher for a can of Nido milk, because the Yucatecans never drank milk, there were no cows in Yucatan, they drank chocolate with water, so even the payment was a substitution, a can of milk for a boy who drank chocolate. Watch the trade: when the violin arrives without a bow and the mother trades her sewing machine for a broken piano, and the boy plays it until the broken piano is the career, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** trade the sewing machine for the broken piano. The substitution is the career. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M1) also served ten years in the small rooms, but her small rooms were the school; Manzanero's small room was a radio station at nine on a Sunday morning, paid in milk, which is why his version is a trade and hers is an apprenticeship.

## M2. The ten years as someone else's pianist

He spent ten years playing behind other people, and the ten years are the mechanism. The Poniatowska interview gives the number in his own words: "durante 10 an~os no hice sino acompan~ar a otros", for ten years I did nothing but accompany others, and he names them, Lucho Gatica, Marco Antonio Muniz, Pedro Vargas, Angelica Maria, Olga Guillot, and adds Jose Feliciano and Raphael in the other accounts, and he counts the travel, "fui a Sudamerica 14 veces", I went to South America fourteen times, "a todos los lugares habidos y por haber donde existe el idioma espan~ol he estado un minimo de 10 veces", to every place there is where Spanish is spoken I have been at least ten times. The mechanism is the accompanist as the school: the boy who was found by Luis Demetrio getting his hair cut under a zapote tree, because Demetrio's pianist had not arrived in Merida, and who was offered work in Mexico City the same weekend, "El dia que quieras vivir en Mexico, yo te doy trabajo", learns the song from behind, from the piano bench, watching the singer take the applause, and the learning is the career, because the accompanist hears what the singer needs and writes it. The ten years end with a morning, not a contract: "una man~ana del an~o de 1967, amaneci y pense: 'Que voy a hacer? Yo tengo que ver por mi, ser mi patron, yo tengo que tener mi propia empresa, no puedo andarme ateniendo a nadie y ese dia, a los 31 an~os, decidi lanzarme en grande como compositor'". One morning in 1967 I woke up and thought, what am I going to do? I have to look after myself, be my own boss, have my own company, I cannot go on depending on anyone, and that day, at thirty-one, I decided to launch myself in a big way as a composer. The mechanism closes the same year with the first album, "A mi amor... con mi amor", released Wednesday 5 April 1967. Watch the decade: when the pianist who has played behind everyone for ten years wakes up one morning and decides to be his own boss, and the same year he records his own songs, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** play behind everyone for ten years, then wake up and be your own boss. The piano bench is the school. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M1) also started again from below, but her below was six years out of the media after arriving already famous; Manzanero's below was ten years inside the media, behind the famous, which is why his version is a shadow and hers is a disappearance.

## M3. The first song at fifteen

He wrote his first song at fifteen and other people have been singing it ever since, and that is the mechanism. "Mi primera cancion la compuse en 1950 y se llama Nunca en el mundo, que mas tarde grabo Fernando de la Mora." My first song I composed in 1950 and it is called "Nunca en el mundo", which Fernando de la Mora later recorded. The English Wikipedia adds the number that makes the mechanism visible: twenty-one versions in different languages have been recorded of it to date. The mechanism is the song as the thing that leaves: the boy writes it at fifteen, another singer records it, and then twenty-one versions in different languages, so the song does not belong to the boy, it belongs to whoever sings it, and the composer is the man who writes what others will carry. The first hit proves the mechanism a second time: "Voy a apagar la luz" in 1958, his first hit, recorded with Lucho Gatica, the singer he had accompanied, so the accompanist's song becomes the star's hit. Before that, Bobby Capo had popularized "Llorando estoy" in 1957. Then the sixties arrive in order: "Cuando estoy contigo" wins first place at the Festival de la Cancion in Miami in 1965, and "No", put at the top of the Latin American charts by Carlos Lico in 1966, so by the time he decides at thirty-one to launch himself as a composer, the songs have already been famous without him, sung by other people, and the launch is only the composer stepping out from behind the piano to claim what was already his. Watch the leaving: when the fifteen-year-old writes a song and another singer records it, and twenty-one versions in different languages follow, and the composer is still the man at the piano, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** write the song at fifteen and let others carry it. The song leaves; the composer stays. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M11) also carried one song as the spine of her career, but hers was a song she sang herself for thirty-four years; Manzanero's first song left him at fifteen and never came back, which is why his version is a departure and hers is a spine.

## M4. The absent "tu"

"Esta tarde vi llover" is the most famous song he ever wrote, and the "tu" in it does not exist, and that is the mechanism. The tvnotiblog biography gives the afternoon in full: one day at the end of October 1966, Armando was alone, a musician earning good money, but at that moment with nobody to eat with, and resigned to eating without company he went to a restaurant called La Fuente, at the corner of San Antonio (Eje 5 Sur) and Insurgentes in Mexico City, and sat in front of a window to enjoy a beautiful afternoon, because the rainy season was supposed to be over, and everyone walked confidently without umbrellas, and then the unexpected happened, it began to rain and the passers-by ran for shelter, and while he watched them, amused, the first line of one of the most famous romantic songs in the Spanish-speaking world came to his mind: "Esta tarde vi llover, vi gente correr y no estabas tu". The quien.com version adds the steak and the beer, and the singer's own words close it: "Estaba yo solo, ese 'tu' no existe. No existe. Imaginense, no existe nada. No tenia yo a quien extran~ar ni nada por el estilo; sencillamente estaba solo y la gente corria por la lluvia." I was alone, that "you" does not exist. It does not exist. Imagine, nothing exists. I had nobody to miss or anything like that; I was simply alone and the people ran from the rain. The mechanism is the absence as the subject: the greatest love song in his catalogue is written about nobody, by a man eating alone, watching strangers run from rain that should not have fallen, and the absence is what makes it universal, because anyone can put their own "tu" into a song whose "tu" was never anyone. The song has a second absence in it: it was a filler song, one of two songs written to complete his first album, "A mi amor... con mi amor", the other being "Felicidad", while the first singles were "Contigo aprendi" and "Adoro", so the most famous song on the record was the one written to fill the space. The afterlife proves the mechanism: Billboard placed it at the top of its list of the 50 best Latin songs between 1920 and 2015, Tony Bennett recorded it in English as "Yesterday I Heard the Rain" in 1968 and again bilingually with Alejandro Sanz on Duets II in 2011, Maria Dolores Pradera sang it with him in 2013, and on the night he died the SACM asked the whole country to sing it from their windows at eight in the evening. Watch the absence: when the man eating alone watches strangers run from the rain and writes a love song to nobody, and the nobody becomes everyone's somebody, and the filler song becomes the first song on the list, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** write the love song to nobody. The absent "tu" is the subject. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M11) also retitled a song and made it hers, but her retitling was an appropriation, "Yolanda" becoming "Te amo"; Manzanero's song was never anyone's, not even his, which is why his version is an absence and hers is a renaming.

## M5. The vals that became a ballad

"Adoro" began as a Peruvian vals and he turned it into a ballad when he recorded it, and the turn is the mechanism. The vaffausa survey and the Diario de Cultura piece agree on the story: in 1965, inspired by the Peruvian vals of Chabuca Granda and Luis Abanto Morales, whom he met during a visit to the Andean country, Armando Manzanero composed "Adoro", one of his most remembered songs and a milestone in his career that gave him world fame. The composer created it at first following the sonority of the vals criollo, but when he recorded it, he converted it into a ballad. The mechanism is the translation inside a single song: the Peruvian rhythm comes home to Mexico in his head, and when he sits at the piano to record it, the rhythm changes its clothes, the vals becomes a ballad, and the song that was born in one genre arrives in another, so the borrowing is not a copy but a conversion, and the conversion is what makes it travel. The travel proves the mechanism: months later RCA Victor took it to Europe, and it became known in Japan, Korea and Italy, and it was sung in more than five languages, by Juan Gabriel and Bronco in Mexico, Graciela Susana in Argentina, David Bisbal, Alejandro Sanz and Placido Domingo in Spain, Mina Mazzini in Italy and Simone in Brazil, and Manzanero himself sang it in Maya, the ancestral language of his home state. The first voice was not his: Carlos Lico sang it first, and put it with "No" at the top of the Latin American charts in 1966, so the song left him before he recorded it, as his songs do. The doorway to Chabuca Granda runs through this song and no other: the Peruvian composer behind Libertad's forbidden "Cardo o ceniza" is also the influence behind Manzanero's most travelled song, so the same woman stands at the root of two studies. Watch the turn: when the vals comes home from Peru and the composer sits down to record it and it comes out a ballad, and the ballad is sung in five languages and in Maya, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** bring the vals home and record it as a ballad. The conversion is the travel. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M6) also drew a map, but hers was the Black Coast, from Zana to Paris to Senegal, a coastline drawn album by album; Manzanero's map is a single song, Peru to Mexico in one conversion, which is why his version is a turn and hers is a coastline.

## M6. The song that crossed the language

"Somos novios" crossed into English and came back a standard, and the crossing is the mechanism. The Diario de Cultura piece says he composed it in 1960, the culturaocio piece says 1968, and the album "Somos novios" is 1968 in both, so the song was written before the album that carries its name, and the crossing came two years after the album: in 1970 the American songwriter Sid Wayne, who composed songs for Elvis Presley, versioned it in English, titling it "It's Impossible", and Perry Como performed it, and the version was nominated for a Grammy for Song of the Year at the 14th Grammy Awards, held Tuesday 14 March 1972, Manzanero's first and only Grammy nomination. The mechanism is the bolero as an export that does not need a passport: the melody is so plain and so exact that it survives the change of language, and the English words are new words, not a translation, so the song becomes two songs, one in Spanish and one in English, and both are the same song. The crossing has a second half that proves it: Elvis Presley put his voice to the English version, and the French version is "J'ai Mal de Toi", and the jazzmen took it too, Bill Evans, Abbey Lincoln and Tete Montoliu recording his melodies, so the bolero leaves the Spanish-speaking world and comes back as jazz, as pop, as a standard. The Billboard list of 2015 puts both "Esta tarde vi llover" and "Somos novios" among the 50 best songs in the history of Latin music, so the two crossings, the rain and the lovers, are the two pillars of the catalogue. Watch the crossing: when the bolero written in Mexico is sung in English by Perry Como and nominated for a Grammy, and Elvis sings it too, and the jazzmen play it without words at all, and it is still the same song, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** write the melody so plain it survives the language. The crossing is the proof. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M7) also took a song to Paris, but she took it herself and sang it back in French; Manzanero's song went by itself, in English, sung by other men, which is why his version is an export and hers is a conquest.

## M7. The height as the trademark

He was 1.54 metres tall without shoes, 1.58 with platforms, and the height is the mechanism. The Poniatowska interview gives the numbers in his own words, and the story that makes them a mechanism: only once did a man called Rubio, director of the nightclub in the Hotel Alfer, veto him for his height. Chabela Duran, whom he accompanied at the piano, wanted him to play solo in that place, and Rubio tested him, that he play different rhythms, that he read one score and then another, that he read music, that he accompany a modern romantic singer and then one of a different style, and he passed every test, but Rubio explained to Chabela Duran that he had no presence, that he needed a pianist of greater height because he looked very small, even though he played very well. The mechanism is the veto as the trademark: the one time his height cost him work, it taught him that the height was the identifier, "Me di cuenta de que en los restaurantes o en las reuniones decian: 'Mira, buscate a Manzanero. Mira, no te vas a equivocar. Es un muchacho bajito pero bajito. No hay pierde. Al mas chaparrito que veas, ese es Manzanero'". I realised that in restaurants or at gatherings they said, look for Manzanero, you will not go wrong, he is a very short boy, there is no way to miss, the shortest one you see, that is Manzanero. "En mi caso esta va a ser un privilegio; va a ayudarme en lugar de perjudicarme." In my case this is going to be a privilege; it is going to help me instead of harming me. The ABC newspaper said it first, on his first tour of Spain in 1968, a whole page describing him as "pequen~o de estatura pero indiscutible coloso de la cancion melodica", small of stature but indisputable colossus of the melodic song. The photographs give the mechanism its colours: am-02, the small man in the grey suit standing beside the grand piano under magenta light, the piano bigger than he is; am-08, the young man in the beige blazer towering over him, Manzanero short and smiling in his cardigan, the CD in the young man's hand. The stage habit closes it: "La mayoria de las veces canto sentado frente al piano porque el publico me identifica con el piano, pero desde luego ya en los espectaculos grandes, cuando hay mucha gente de por medio, me paro, me pongo a conversar con las personas y hablo entre cada cancion." Most of the time I sing seated at the piano because the public identifies me with the piano, but of course in the big shows, when there are many people in between, I stand up, I talk with the people and I speak between each song. Watch the height: when the shortest man in the room is the one everyone is looking for, and the piano is bigger than he is, and he sits at it because the public identifies him with it, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the height identify you. The smallest man in the room is the colossus. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M2) also refused a contract that asked for her body, blonde hair and a miniskirt; Manzanero's refusal was quieter, a veto he turned into a privilege, which is why his version is a trademark and hers is a refusal.

## M8. The producer who revived the bolero

In 1991 he produced Luis Miguel's Romance, and the bolero came back to life, and the production is the mechanism. The Spanish Wikipedia article on Romance gives the facts: Luis Miguel chose the bolero as his next project, the singer and composer Armando Manzanero was hired by WEA Latina to co-produce the album with Luis Miguel, the recording began on 24 August 1991 at Ocean Way Recording in Hollywood, California, with Bebu Silvetti on the musical arrangements, and the album was released Tuesday 19 November 1991. Manzanero chose twelve boleros from five hundred songs, including two of his own, "Te extran~o" and "No se tu", and Silvetti arranged them with thirty-two violinists under the American conductor Ezra Kliger. The mechanism is the old song given a new production: the boleros were written between 1944 and 1986, and Manzanero picked them the way a composer picks them, not the way a record company picks them, and the production dressed them in strings and in Luis Miguel's voice, so a generation that had grown up on pop heard the bolero for the first time and thought it was new. The sales are the proof: more than eight million copies worldwide by the Romance article's count, seven million by the other counts, thirty-two weeks at number one on the Billboard Latin Pop Albums chart in the United States, the first album in Spanish by a Latin artist to earn a gold disc in the United States, gold discs in Brazil and Taiwan as well, the best-selling album in Argentina by a non-national artist, and the two first singles, "Inolvidable" and "No se tu", at number one on the Billboard Hot Latin Songs chart and six months at the top of the Mexican charts. The revival became a series, Segundo romance in 1994, Romances in 1997, which sold more than four and a half million copies and won another Grammy for best Latin pop album, and Mis romances in 2001, from which "Por debajo de la mesa" and "Somos novios" come, and the series sold more than twenty-three million copies altogether. The friction is part of the mechanism and is carried as dramatized, not as documented fact: in the Luis Miguel series, Manzanero is the one who pushes for a second volume of boleros, Luis Miguel's lack of commitment angers him until he abandons the project, and Luis Miguel apologizes to him, and the collaborations continue. Watch the revival: when the composer picks twelve old boleros out of five hundred, and a young pop star sings them with thirty-two violins, and the bolero that was supposed to be dead sells eight million copies, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** pick twelve old songs out of five hundred and dress them in strings. The revival is the production. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M8) also refused the track, but her refusal was a small band of four that sounds like eighty; Manzanero's revival was a big band of thirty-two violins that sounds like a bolero, which is why his version is a production and hers is a refusal.

## M9. The duet as the second career

From 2001 on, the duet became his second career, and the duet is the mechanism. The Latin Grammy of 2001 is the date: he won the Latin Grammy for best pop vocal album by a duo or group for Duetos, in which he sings with Olga Tan~on, Alejandro Sanz, Ricardo Montaner, Lucero and Miguel Bose, and the Diario de Cultura piece says he won two Latin Grammys for his Duetos and Duetos I albums. Duetos 2 followed in 2002. The mechanism is the catalogue sung by others, in his company: he does not duet to harmonize, he duets to hand the song over, and the younger singer takes the verse that fits them, so the album is the composer's songs sung back to him by the voices he wrote them for. The Libertad trilogy is the mechanism at its longest: La Libertad de Manzanero (1995/1998), Armando la Libertad (1997/1998), and Desarmando a Tania (February 2014), plus the tribute Manzanero a tres pistas (2013), which took seven years of planning, and the Wikipedia description of the last one, carried in #356, is the mechanism stated as a conversation, the two voices trading verses by personality, each one taking the phrase that fits it best. The Poniatowska interview calls Libertad "su interprete favorita", his favourite interpreter, and lists Desarmando a Tania among the three discs he handed out at lunch, so the trilogy is not a contract but a friendship. The duet list runs on: Susana Zabaleta on "De la A a la Z" (2006, DVD) and "Amarrados" (2009); Francisco Cespedes on "Armando un Pancho" (2012/2013); Cafe Quijano on "Origenes: el bolero" (2012), singing "Quiero que mi boca se desnude" with them; Maria Dolores Pradera on "Gracias a vosotros" (2013), singing "Esta tarde vi llover" with her; Julieta Venegas on the same song and Edith Marquez on "No" (gluc.mx); Lisset on "Nada personal" and "Tentaciones"; Alejandro Lerner, twenty-five years of mutual collaborations, beginning with "No hace falta que lo digas". Watch the handover: when the composer who wrote the song at fifteen stands beside the singer who sings it now, and the verse goes to the voice it fits, and the album is the catalogue sung back to him, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** hand the song over in a duet. The catalogue sung back is the second career. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M5) also made the duet a conversation, trading verses with Manzanero across four albums; his side of the same conversation is the catalogue, the songs themselves, which is why her version is a conversation and his is a handover. Pineda (#357 M10) also made the duet a family tree, fourteen branches in one album; Manzanero's duet is not a tree but a road, twenty years of handovers, which is why his version is a career and hers is a genealogy.

## M10. The telenovela theme

His songs became the voices of the telenovelas, and the theme is the mechanism. The Spanish Wikipedia list gives the run: "Corazon salvaje" in 1977, with "Amanecer" and "Fue una vez"; "Apasionada" in 1993, with "No se tu" sung by Luis Miguel; "Dos mujeres, un camino" in 1993, with "Adoro" sung by Bronco; "Nada personal" in 1996, the duet with Lisset, one of his most remembered themes; "Mirada de mujer" in 1997, with "Dime", sung by Aranza; "Tentaciones" in 1998, again with Lisset; "Yo soy Betty, la fea" in 2001, with "Somos novios" sung as a duet with Olga Tan~on; "La hija del jardinero" in 2003, with "Somos novios" sung with Mariana Ochoa; "La hija del mariachi" in 2006, with "Adoro" sung by Adriana Bottina; "Vidas robadas" in 2010; "Quiero amarte" in 2013, with Noel Schajris, Samo, Jesus Navarro, Juan Pablo Manzanero and Carlos Macias; and "Nada personal" again in 2017, sung by Ximena Sarin~ana and Jesus Navarro. The mechanism is the song as the drama's voice: the telenovela needs a feeling it cannot say in dialogue, and the Manzanero song says it, so the song is heard every night in millions of houses, and the composer who wrote it at fifteen becomes the voice of the evening. The move between networks is part of the mechanism: when Televisa told him he was exhausted, he went to the rival network and composed one of the most remembered telenovela themes, "Nada personal", so the song is also the answer to being told he was finished. Watch the theme: when the drama stops and the song starts, and the whole country knows the feeling before the characters say it, and the song is Manzanero's, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the song speak for the drama. The theme is the voice of the evening. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M3) also made a forbidden song mass, but hers was made mass by a million and a half people buying the disc; Manzanero's songs were made mass by television playing them every night, which is why his version is a theme and hers is a sale.

## M11. The composer who refused the label

He spent his life writing boleros and asked not to be called a bolero writer, and the refusal is the mechanism. The vaffausa survey states it plainly: "A pesar de haberle dedicado su vida al bolero, el musico pidio que no lo encasillaran como bolerista", despite having dedicated his life to the bolero, the musician asked not to be pigeonholed as a bolero writer. His own words are sharper: "Yo soy musico, yo fui musico y voy a morir musico. No especifico los ritmos ni nada por el estilo." I am a musician, I was a musician and I am going to die a musician. I do not specify the rhythms or anything like that. And then the teachers he claims: "Todos mis lugares donde escucho musica estan impregnados de la musica que yo estudie; yo soy de Mozart, yo soy de Chopin, yo soy de Tchaikovsky, yo soy de todos esos compositores que escribieron melodia." All the places where I hear music are impregnated with the music I studied; I am of Mozart, I am of Chopin, I am of Tchaikovsky, I am of all those composers who wrote melody. The mechanism is the label refused from the inside: the bolero is the vehicle, not the cage, and the proof is the catalogue, a vals turned into a ballad, a bolero turned into a pop standard, a song turned into a telenovela theme, a symphony turned into a "Mosaico mexicano" with the Orquesta Sinfonica de Jalapa Tlen Huicani, a big band album, "Big Band Jazz de Mexico", and jazzmen, Bill Evans, Abbey Lincoln and Tete Montoliu, recording his melodies without words. The refusal has a second edge, the joke he made about the new music: "No creo que nadie pueda hacer el amor con una cancion de Maluma." I do not believe anyone can make love to a Maluma song. And the wish beside the joke, that he wanted to collaborate with Maluma and Sebastian Yatra, so the refusal is not a rejection of the new, it is a demand that the new meet the old standard, a melody. Watch the refusal: when the man who wrote four hundred boleros says he is of Mozart and Chopin and Tchaikovsky, and the jazzmen play his songs without words, and the bolero is still the bolero, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** refuse the label from the inside. The bolero is the vehicle, not the cage. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M4) also refused to be labelled, but her refusal was the voice, one voice in every genre; Manzanero's refusal is the catalogue, one composer in every rhythm, which is why his version is a catalogue and hers is a voice.

## M12. The guild president

He ran the composers' guild for ten years, and the presidency is the mechanism. In September 2010, after the death of Roberto Cantoral, he assumed the presidency of the directive committee of the Sociedad de Autores y Compositores de Mexico (SACM), and he held it until his death in December 2020, and before that he had been its vice president from 1982 to 2011. The Poniatowska interview gives the pride in his own words: he and Cantoral had just come back from Paris, from the ninety years of the Confederation Internationale des Societes d'Auteurs et Compositeurs, "y nos reconocieron como la sociedad internacional latinoamericana mas avanzada, con mejores sistemas de reparto y una manera de operar que solo nosotros tenemos", and they recognized us as the most advanced Latin American international society, with better distribution systems and a way of operating that only we have. The mechanism is the composer as administrator: the man who wrote the songs defends the men who write the songs, and the defence is forty years of struggle, "A traves de 40 an~os de lucha hemos tenido maravillosos apoyos de las autoridades, porque la musica siempre cuenta con un angel", through forty years of struggle we have had marvellous support from the authorities, because music always has an angel. The mechanism has a counterweight that makes it visible: he did not like to be called "maestro". Myriam Hernandez told La Tercera: "no le gustaba que le dijeran maestro, de inmediato me decia: 'acaso yo soy un carpintero, Myriamcita?'". He did not like being called maestro, he immediately said to me, am I a carpenter, little Myriam? So the president of the composers, the man who defended the guild for forty years, refused the title the guild would have given him, and the refusal is the same as the label refusal in M11, the man who will not be filed. Watch the presidency: when the composer who wrote four hundred songs sits in Paris defending the distribution system, and back home refuses to be called maestro, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** defend the guild and refuse the title. The carpenter joke is the presidency. Against the chain: Leon (#354) also fought the industry, but her fight was the long discography, album by album, in public; Manzanero's fight was the guild, forty years of meetings in Paris and Mexico City, which is why his version is an administration and hers is a career.

## M13. The museum he opened himself

He opened his own museum seventeen days before he died, and the museum is the mechanism. On Friday 11 December 2020, the governor of Yucatan, Mauricio Vila Dosal, and the federal secretary of tourism, Miguel Torruco Marques, inaugurated the Museo Casa Manzanero in the Paseo 60 shopping centre in Merida, with Manzanero himself present, cutting the ribbon, thanking the people of his home state, and the reports agree it was his last public appearance. The museum is a high-technology, multisensory space, built with a mixed investment of nine million pesos, with more than fifty objects, many of them donated by the maestro himself because they meant a great deal to him, and it is divided into three thematic axes that carry the names of his compositions, the first of them "Nunca en el mundo", which explains the origins of the bolero, what Merida was like at the beginning of the last century, and the first years of Armando Manzanero's life. The same day he inaugurated the "Tour de la Nostalgia", a route through the places of his childhood and his great anecdotes, the Parque de Santa Lucia, the Escuela Superior de las Artes, the Armando Manzanero and Peon Contreras theatres, the Palacio de la Musica, the Museo de la Cancion Yucateca. His words at the inauguration are the mechanism stated as a wish: "que bueno que en estos tiempos esten reconociendo a los autores, sobre todo a los autores vivos", how good it is that in these times they are recognizing the authors, above all the living authors. The museum is therefore not a tomb but a recognition in life, and the seventeen days that follow are the mechanism's proof: on Monday 7 December he had celebrated his eighty-fifth birthday with a party, on Friday 11 December he opened the museum, in October he had travelled to Miami for the Billboard Latin Music Awards tribute, where he received a standing ovation on a stage covered with Mayan symbols, and on Thursday 17 December he was hospitalized in Mexico City, and on Monday 28 December he died at 03:20 of a cardiac arrest, and his body was cremated and his ashes were transferred to Merida, where the house museum stands in his honour. Watch the museum: when the composer opens his own museum and says how good it is that they recognize living authors, and seventeen days later he is dead, and the museum is still open, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** open the museum yourself and ask them to recognize the living. The museum is the recognition. Against the chain: Pineda (#357 M12) also celebrated fifty years, but hers was a tour, rooms filled one after another; Manzanero's anniversary was a building, one room that stays open, which is why his version is a museum and hers is a road. Libertad (#356 M11) also came home to sing one song in Zana; Manzanero came home to open a museum in Merida, which is why his version is a building and hers is a song.

## M14. The song that outlives the singer

He kept working until the last year of his life, and the work outlived him, and that is the mechanism. His last album was "Tengo permiso", published in 2017, when he was eighty-two, with lyrics by his close friend, the doctor and writer Jose Antonio Ceballos, launched in a famous restaurant in Mexico City in the old way, with different artists interpreting the songs of the new disc before the public and the media, and Manzanero opening the act with "Sin noticias", a song about nostalgia for a past love. In an interview at eighty-two he refused to agree with those who said his time had passed, and said he would never in his life stop working: "No lo voy a dejar jamas en la vida." I am never going to leave it in my life. The mechanism is the refusal to stop, and it has two witnesses: the AARP interview of early 2019, "Yo tuve la suerte de que lo mio todavia perdura", I was lucky that mine still endures, and "A mi todavia me toco esa suerte, me toco ese tiempo en donde las canciones tenian una vida larga. Ahora no, ahora todo es muy vertiginoso, de mucha velocidad. Sale una cancion, desaparece, hay que sacar otra", I was still lucky, I got that time when songs had a long life. Now no, now everything is very vertiginous, very fast. A song comes out, disappears, you have to put out another. The legacy is stated by others: Gabriel Garcia Marquez said in the mid-1980s that Manzanero was "uno de los mas grandes poetas actuales de la lengua castellana", one of the greatest living poets of the Spanish language, after failing himself to write a bolero, "es lo mas dificil que hay", it is the most difficult thing there is; and Carlos Monsivais said in 1997, "El sitio de Manzanero solo podra ocuparlo Manzanero", Manzanero's place can only be occupied by Manzanero. The afterlife proves the mechanism: on the night he died the SACM asked the whole country to sing "Esta tarde vi llover" from their windows at eight in the evening; Libertad closed the Festival Internacional Cervantino 49 on Sunday 31 October 2021 before twenty thousand people in his memory and returned to the Zocalo on Monday 21 March 2022 with "Contigo aprendi. Homenaje a Armando Manzanero"; Merida's Palacio de la Musica held a tribute on the first anniversary of his death, Tuesday 28 December 2021; and on 9 January 2021 his compilation "20 Exitos Originales", first issued in 2007, entered the Latin Pop Albums chart at number ten. Watch the outliving: when the eighty-two-year-old releases a new album and says he will never stop, and a year after his death the country is still singing his song from its windows, and the compilation is back in the charts, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** never stop, and let the song outlive you. The long life is the luck. Against the chain: Libertad (#356 M12) also left a name as her legacy, the girls named Tania Libertad; Manzanero's legacy is not a name but four hundred songs, sung by other people in other languages, which is why his version is a catalogue and hers is a christening. Pineda (#357 M11) also kept one song and let it ripen; Manzanero kept four hundred and let them all ripen at once, which is why his version is a catalogue and hers is a spine.

## Evolution

1935-1950, the straw house and the sewing machine: born in Ticul, Yucatan, in a straw house that was later transformed, raised in the barrio La Cruz de Galvez in Merida by his maternal grandmother Rita Baqueiro Chi, who spoke only Maya, so he learned Spanish at four or five; his father Santiago Manzanero, musician and founder of the Orquesta Tipica Yucalpeten, and his mother Juana Canche, who played the jarana yucateca, introduce him to music early; at eight he begins music studies at the Escuela de Bellas Artes, where his mother's great-aunt was the director; the violin arrives without a bow, the mother trades her sewing machine for a broken piano, and he begins to play. 1950-1957, the first song and the piano bench: he composes "Nunca en el mundo" at fifteen in 1950; he begins his professional activity as a pianist in 1951; at ten he had already been paid with a Nido milk-can voucher for accompanying child singers on the radio; in 1957 Bobby Capo popularizes "Llorando estoy", and Manzanero becomes musical director of the Mexican branch of CBS International, and the next year pianist to Pedro Vargas, Lucho Gatica and Raphael. 1957-1967, the ten years behind others: he accompanies Pedro Vargas, Lucho Gatica, Marco Antonio Muniz, Angelica Maria, Olga Guillot, Jose Feliciano and Raphael, fourteen trips to South America, at least ten visits to every Spanish-speaking place; "Voy a apagar la luz" is his first hit in 1958 with Lucho Gatica; "Cuando estoy contigo" wins first place at the Festival de la Cancion in Miami in 1965; Carlos Lico puts "No" and "Adoro" at the top of the Latin American charts in 1966; one morning in 1967, at thirty-one, he decides to be his own boss. 1967-1970, the launch: "Mi primera grabacion" (1959 by the English Wikipedia, 1967 by the Spanish Wikipedia) and "A mi amor... con mi amor", released Wednesday 5 April 1967, with "Felicidad" and "Esta tarde vi llover" as filler songs; "A mi amor... con mi amor", "Manzanero el Grande" and "Somos novios" in 1967 and 1968; the first tour of Spain in 1968, ABC's whole page, "pequen~o de estatura pero indiscutible coloso de la cancion melodica"; "Somos novios" becomes "It's Impossible" in 1970, Perry Como's hit, Grammy-nominated Song of the Year at the 14th Grammy Awards, Tuesday 14 March 1972. 1970-1991, the festivals and the telenovelas: "Sen~or amor" wins first place at the Festival de Mallorca in 1978; "Corazon amigo" is honoured at the Yamaha Festival in 1982; "Corazon salvaje" in 1977, "Nada personal" in 1996, "Mirada de mujer" in 1997, "Tentaciones" in 1998; Billboard gives him the Premio a la Excelencia in 1993; the Premio Lo Nuestro a la Excelencia in 1993; vice president of the SACM from 1982. 1991-2001, the revival and the duets: Romance, released Tuesday 19 November 1991, eight million copies, the bolero revived; Segundo romance in 1994, Romances in 1997, Mis romances in 2001; La Libertad de Manzanero (1995/1998) and Armando la Libertad (1997/1998); the Latin Grammy for Duetos in 2001; the International Latin Music Hall of Fame in 2000; the Billboard Latin Music Hall of Fame in 2003. 2001-2014, the duet years and the guild: Duetos 2 in 2002; "De la A a la Z" with Susana Zabaleta in 2006; "Las mujeres de Manzanero" in 2008; "Amarrados" in 2009; the Latin Grammy Award for Musical Excellence in 2010; president of the SACM from September 2010; "Armando un Pancho" with Francisco Cespedes in 2012/2013; "Manzanero a tres pistas" in 2013; the Latin Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2013; "Desarmando a Tania" in February 2014; the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in January 2014, the first Mexican to receive it; the OEA Patrimonio Cultural de las Americas in 2015. 2014-2020, the late years: "Tengo permiso" in 2017, lyrics by Jose Antonio Ceballos; the Havana Malecon concert in 2018 at eighty-two; the AARP interview of early 2019, "Yo tuve la suerte de que lo mio todavia perdura"; the Billboard Latin Music Lifetime Achievement Award in October 2020, the standing ovation in Miami; the birthday party on Monday 7 December 2020; the Casa Manzanero inauguration on Friday 11 December 2020; hospitalized Thursday 17 December 2020; died Monday 28 December 2020 at 03:20 of a cardiac arrest; cremated, his ashes transferred to Merida. The arc is a song that keeps leaving: the boy writes it at fifteen, others sing it, the accompanist plays it behind others, the composer sings it himself, the producer gives it to Luis Miguel, the duet partner trades it with Libertad, the museum keeps it open after he is gone.

## Aesthetic logic

Manzanero's aesthetic is the song as the unit, sung by whoever can carry it, in whatever language it survives: the bolero, the ballad, the vals turned into a ballad, the telenovela theme, the duet, and the scarcity is the argument, because a composer with no costume, no mask and no character has only the melody to hold the room, and the melody has to be plain enough and exact enough to be believed in every language. The materials are a broken piano traded for a sewing machine, a violin without a bow, a Nido milk-can voucher, ten years behind other singers, a first song at fifteen, an absent "tu" in the rain, a vals from Peru, a bolero in English, a height of 1.54 metres, twelve boleros picked out of five hundred, thirty-two violins, a duet traded verse by verse, a telenovela theme every night, a guild defended for forty years, a museum opened seventeen days before death, and four hundred songs that outlived the man who wrote them. The palette is the one the photographs give: the light grey suit and pale blue shirt of am-01 to am-04 against the magenta and purple stage light of Cehegin; the black of the piano and the black suits of am-06 and am-07 against the blue stage light; the bronze of the bust and the white walls and blue base ring of am-05 against the deep blue Merida sky; the grey-brown cardigan of am-08 against the white wall and the wood parquet. Where Pineda's logic was one country sung by one voice in every genre, and Libertad's was two countries sung by one voice in one night, Manzanero's is every country sung by every voice in one song, and the difference is the carrier: Pineda carries the song, Libertad carries the country, and Manzanero carries nothing, because the song carries itself, which is why his version is a catalogue and theirs are a voice and a set list.
