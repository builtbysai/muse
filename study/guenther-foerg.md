Foerg is the painter who took modernism as his subject rather than his style. Born 5 December 1952 in Fuessen -- 9 December per GalleriesNow, the split carried above -- he studied at the Academy of Fine Arts in Munich 1973-1979 under Karl Fred Dahmen, and the dates and the teacher are unanimous across every source read. His student work was almost exclusively black monochrome in acrylic, the grey producing a milky, veiled surface. From 1977, after the death of his colleague Blinky Palermo, he pursued Palermo's European legacy of American Minimal Art. His first solo show was at Rudiger Schoettle, Munich, in 1980, with monochromes. He taught for half his career: Karlsruhe 1992-1999 (1992-98 per the Grasslin CV) and then professor at the Munich Academy 1999-2013, until his death. He died 5 December 2013, of cancer -- on his 61st birthday, if the majority birth date holds. [C27084]

The evolution is a grey arc with a grid for a spine. The black monochromes of 1973-76 give way to colour-field painting -- four strict vertical fields in W02, brown, gold, blue and matt grey, "emptied, purged" -- and from 1976 he experiments with lead and aluminium sheet as image carriers. The 1980s and 1990s belong to lead: sheets wrapped over wood and painted with acrylic, in three, four and six parts, the metal heavy and dull under pure pigment (W03, W07, W11). Bronze runs alongside: small bronzes in 1988, the fourteen bronze reliefs of Stations of the Cross 1989-1990, the freestanding leg Bein in 1993, the twelve-metre-high Tor und Stele in Rotterdam in 1994. The grid paintings begin in the early 1990s and become the central series, loosening into the spot paintings of 2007-2009, while architectural photography runs in parallel from the early 1980s to 2006. A Stedelijk seminar text says the grey paintings bracket the oeuvre, first works 1973 and final painting 2009 -- but that is search-summary-only, and a dated 2013 painting exists (W20, Fort Worth), so the 2009 claim is carried as a tension, not a fact. [C27085]

The institutional spine is steady: first solo Schoettle 1980, documenta IX 1992, Stedelijk solo "Couplet 4" 1995, Wolfgang Hahn Prize 1996, Munich professorship 1999-2013, then the posthumous retrospective "A Fragile Beauty" (Stedelijk, May 2018, then Dallas) and the estate with Hauser & Wirth from June 2018 -- Gagosian, named in the brief, is NOT a representing gallery; the site search finds no artist page, only a 2019 Geneva group-show inclusion. His lifetime galleries were Greene Naftali, Almine Rech and Galerie Max Hetzler. Architecture is biographical, not decorative: the Wittgenstein House, Casa Malaparte, Casa del Fascio, the IG Farben Building, Bauhaus Tel Aviv and Jerusalem, Fascist buildings in Italy -- photographed large and unembellished -- and the wall pieces that turn rooms into paintings, from the first red tempera wall in a friend's apartment in 1978 to the MMK Frankfurt stairwell in 1991 and the Swiss Re interiors in Zurich in 2000. The auction record is Untitled (1990), 1,331,250 pounds at Christie's, 12 February 2020. [C27086]

## M1 -- Lead as painting surface

Lead sheet is wrapped over wood and painted with acrylic, so the painting has a metal skin. W03 (MoMA, 1987, three parts, 254.6 x 210.2 cm) and W04 (SFMOMA, 1988, 240.0 x 160.0 cm) establish the format; W07 (1989, six parts, each 60.2 x 60.2 cm) is "a regiment of six chromatic panels aligned in formation; identical wooden structures wrapped in sheets of unprimed lead and painted with pure unadulterated pigment", with "parallel bands of crimson" unifying "quadrangles of black, charcoal grey, navy and off-white". W11 (1991, four parts, each 80 x 60 cm) has "fields of dappled cobalt and burnished chestnut" in "chromatic dialogue with planes of violet, saffron and buff", modulated by "the natural patina of the lead support, a shimmering hue between grey and blue". The lead is described as heavy, dull and inert, the sheet metal undulating, crinkling and furrowing and wrapping around the support to form a rounded edge. The watchable behaviour is the edge: the metal does not end at the picture plane, it folds round it, so the painting is an object with a back and a rim, not a window.

**Takeaway:** wrap the painting in a metal skin and let the edge fold round; the support must be visible as a weight. [C27087]

## M2 -- The grey/black monochrome as system

Grey is not a colour choice, it is the system the whole oeuvre runs on. The student work of 1973-76 is almost exclusively black monochrome in acrylic, the translucent grey producing a milky, veiled surface. W02, Color Field (Farbfeld) (1987), is four uniform vertical fields -- "elegant brown, glowing gold, garish blue, and matt gray" -- strictly ordered, with "no forms visible - the picture appears emptied, purged". W12, the suite of ten paintings of 1991, is each canvas "painted in varying monochromatic color fields", the suite staged at the Musee d'Art Moderne de la Ville de Paris. The watchable behaviour is the refusal: a field of one colour, held long enough that the eye starts inventing variation in it -- the veiled surface, the milky grey, the single matt field against its glowing neighbour.

**Takeaway:** paint one colour per field and hold it; let the eye invent the variation the painting refuses to supply. [C27088]

## M3 -- The grid / Gitterbild

The grid is a lattice of horizontal and vertical lines laid down in powerful single brushstrokes, enclosing areas that stay deliberately unelaborated. W15 (1995), the largest Gitterbild at nearly thirteen by twenty feet, has "clusters of ochre, sage, rust, and charcoal cross-hatchings on a neutral gray-toned background", enveloping the viewer in its "totalizing presence". W16 (1998, 250 x 420 cm per the catalogue details -- the essay on the same page says 450 cm wide, the split carried) is an "architectonic 'wall formation' with enclosed areas", with "seemingly ordered cubic areas in black and signal red tones: cadmium red or vermilion", and "spontaneously placed cubes" that "do not appear elaborated", the paint "a provokingly translucent structure". W18 (2005) has "its lattice of horizontal and vertical lines rendered in powerful, single brushstrokes of black, blue and green" over a background "filled by a subtly modifying yellow", with "clouds of blurred blue pigment" and "a verdant green" curving through the bottom left, the intersecting bars generating "a patchwork of window and net forms". The watchable behaviour is the hand inside the system: the grid promises order, and the single brushstroke, the blurred cloud, the spontaneously placed cube break it.

**Takeaway:** draw the grid in one stroke per line and let the hand break the order it promises. [C27089]

## M4 -- The spot painting

Colour arrives as patches placed with rapid strokes on a white-primed canvas, flat, with no illusion. W19 (2007, 289 x 400 cm, ALBERTINA) is the anchor: "a great vitality can be felt here. Forg guided his brush in a joyful and playful manner. The oil paints shine and glow on the white-primed canvas, on which the artist placed patches of different colors and textures with rapid strokes." The elements "remain flat in their effect and do not create illusions of reality". Against the lead paintings, where colour sits on a heavy metal skin, and against the grids, where colour is enclosed by lines, the spot painting lets colour sit free on white -- the ground does the work the grid used to do. The watchable behaviour is the placement: each patch lands once, fast, and stays where it landed.

**Takeaway:** place the colour once, fast, on white, and leave it flat; the placement is the composition. [C27090]

## M5 -- Rectangles that roam

A rectangle of colour is set loose on a square field, and the field is big enough that the rectangle reads as moving. W12, the 1991 suite of ten paintings, is the anchor: each canvas is a monochromatic colour field, and "rectangles of color roam across the series of square fields, suggesting an expanse beyond the frame". The suite references the architectural language of Le Corbusier's Unite d'Habitation, so the roaming rectangle is also a window, a balcony, a slab -- a piece of a building set loose on a wall. The watchable behaviour is the drift across the series: no single canvas holds the rectangle, it moves from one to the next, and the expanse it suggests is larger than any one frame.

**Takeaway:** let one rectangle roam across a series of square fields; the picture is bigger than the frame it sits in. [C27091]

## M6 -- The wall as painting

The wall itself is the support: coat a section of it and the room changes. W01, the first wall painting (1978), is the anchor: Foerg "coated a section of the wall in red tempera paint" in a friend's apartment, and in doing so "heightened one's perception of the wall, ceiling, and floor through an awareness of color as an element of space". The mechanism scales up without changing: the colourful wall piece for the central stairway of Frankfurt's MMK on its opening in 1991, with a bronze relief, and the colour design for all interiors of the 1920s Villa Bodmer plus two enormous tubes of raw metal in the entrance hall for Swiss Re, Zurich, in 2000. The watchable behaviour is the room, not the picture: the colour does not hang on the wall, it re-measures the wall, the ceiling and the floor around it.

**Takeaway:** paint the wall, not a picture for the wall; the colour must re-measure the room. [C27092]

## M7 -- Architectural photography, unembellished

Modernist buildings are photographed as they are found -- dilapidated, carelessly renovated, stubborn. W17, Bauhaus Tel Aviv - Jerusalem (photographed 2001, book 2002), is the anchor: photographs taken with a 35 mm camera and zoom lens, the buildings "presented in an unembellished way, sometimes dilapidated, often featuring careless renovations or additions - as monuments that have stubbornly defied the course of time". The subjects run from the 1930s and 1940s, largely by architects who had emigrated from Europe, and the series shows working-class housing developments, villas and studios. The same unembellished eye covers the Wittgenstein House, Casa Malaparte, Casa del Fascio, the IG Farben Building in Frankfurt, and Fascist buildings in Italy, 1980-2006. The watchable behaviour is the refusal to tidy: the photograph documents what time did to the ideal, and the ideal is still visible underneath the damage.

**Takeaway:** photograph the building as found, damage and all; the ideal must show through the dilapidation, not instead of it. [C27093]

## M8 -- Bronze as bodily fragment

Bronze is cast as a body in pieces -- a leg, a torso, a hand, a foot -- and the casting traces are left in the surface. W13, Bein (Leg) (1993, 151.5 x 57 x 42 cm, edition of 3, 2 cast), is the anchor: "at once softly nuanced and streaked with dramatic gouges, scratches and grooves, the bronze surface evokes the texture of skin while concurrently retaining the bulges and perforations that reveal the indexical signs of the casting process". The small bronzes of 1988 (W05, MoMA, 90.2 x 59.4 x 5.7 cm; W06, Art Institute of Chicago, 260 x 120 cm) establish the fragment early, and W10, Stations of the Cross (1989-1990), scales it to fourteen bronze reliefs and fifty-four monotypes. The watchable behaviour is the skin: the surface reads as flesh at one distance and as a casting at another, and the gouge is the evidence that a hand made it.

**Takeaway:** cast the body in fragments and leave the casting in the skin; the gouge is the signature. [C27094]

## M9 -- Monumental public bronze and stone

The fragment grows to street scale and becomes a gate. W14, Tor und Stele (Rotterdam, made May 1994, 12 x 100 x 6 m, bronze and stone), is the anchor, documented by Sculpture International Rotterdam / BKOR: "a grandiose gesture in rough bronze and glistening polished black marble" -- a bronze sculpture in the form of an obelisk with a flat top, a rectangular arch of black, smoothly polished stone, and rectangular areas of yellow-brown tiles set into the pavement, along which old-fashioned streetlamps are placed. The bronze stele shows traces of Foerg's handling of the bronze in its surface. The same public instinct put two-metre-long walls of mirrors in a Rotterdam tube station in 1988, demolished 1999. The watchable behaviour is the walk-through: the arch is a gate the viewer passes under, the stele a marker, the tiled pavement a path -- the sculpture is a place, not an object.

**Takeaway:** build the sculpture as a place -- a gate to pass under, a marker, a path -- at a scale the body has to walk through. [C27095]

## M10 -- The multi-part format

The painting is a regiment in formation, not a single field. W03 is three parts, W02 four, W07 six parts "aligned in formation", W11 four parts, W12 ten paintings, W20 two parts (object number suffix A-B). The part is the module: each panel carries its own field of colour, and the seams between panels are part of the composition -- the crimson bands in W07 unify the six panels and punctuate them at once. The watchable behaviour is the count: the viewer counts the parts before reading the picture, and the picture is the sum of the parts plus the gaps between them.

**Takeaway:** build the painting from counted parts in formation; the seams are part of the composition. [C27096]

## Aesthetic logic

Foerg's logic is modernism taken at its word and then weighed. The lead is heavy and dull and inert, and the pure pigment sits on it anyway; the grid promises order and the single brushstroke breaks it; the grey refuses variation and the eye invents it; the photograph refuses to tidy the building and the ideal shows through the damage anyway. Against Herold's cheap lath and caviar, Foerg's materials are industrial and architectural -- lead, aluminium, concrete, bronze, stone, the wall itself -- and the joke, where there is one, is not a pun but a scale: a leg cast in bronze, a gate twelve metres high, a grid thirteen by twenty feet, a wall painted red in a friend's apartment. The through-line is the grid, from the monochrome fields through the lead panels to the Gitterbilder and the spot paintings: a strict structure that the hand keeps loosening, one stroke at a time, until the structure is still there and the hand is visible inside it. [C27097]
