# Sergio Romagnolo: Study Notes

**Site:** Sergio Mauro Romagnolo -- born 16 December 1957 in Sao Paulo, lives and works in Sao Paulo; a Brazilian painter and sculptor of the Geracao 80 generation whose work since 1976 has been one operation repeated across two mediums: take a recognisable ready-made image or object -- a cartoon character, a VW Beetle, a drum kit, a rose -- and superimpose it on itself, remake it in a wrong material, turn it upside down, or pair it with something it never belonged with, so that recognition arrives first and understanding has to catch up. ## The life: the boy who painted cartoons and the man who melted cars

Sergio Mauro Romagnolo was born in Sao Paulo on 16 December 1957 -- the full name and the date from the Lattes CV and the Portuguese Wikipedia; one source (Bordallo Pinheiro) gives the order "Mauro Sergio Romagnolo", a single-source variant not used here (see Corrections 6). He took a private "Desenho de Publicidade" atelier course in 1975, and his first exhibition was a painting in a group show in 1976, the Salao de Arte Contemporanea de Goiania. He studied at Colegio Iade in 1976-77. In 1977 the CV lists him in the XIV Bienal Internacional de Sao Paulo twice over (Grupo IADe; performance "Serpentinando") plus "Imagem e Palavras" at CAYC in Argentina -- CV-only, single-source, flagged, not independently corroborated. (C15209, leg 1 C15000, C15001, C15002, C15004)

In 1980 he was admitted to Plastic Arts at FAAP in Sao Paulo, degree 1980-84, supervisor Walter Zanini. The teachers he names as decisive are Renato Vieira Filho, Regina Silveira, Julio Plaza, Nelson Leirner and Walter Zanini -- the same four who, per MoMA's account in #672, reformed FAAP's curriculum while Jac Leirner studied there. He taught Educacao Artistica in public and private schools while studying, 1980-84, and taught painting at FAAP ("Pintura IV") in 1985-86. What no source read gives is his FAAP graduation year; the 1984 that belongs to Catunda and Leirner must not be attached to him. (C15210, leg 1 C15001, C15003, C15004)

His own periodisation, given in the 2017 Newcity interview, is the spine of the whole career: painting only 1976-86, sculpture only 1986-96, both together since 1996. The 1983 shows -- "Pintura como Meio" at MAC-SP, "Arte Acesa" at the Painel Luminoso do Anhangabau, "Arte na Rua" -- put him in the painting-return moment before the movement had a name. In 1983 Leonilson had his first Tomas Cohn solo, the generation's calling card, and Leonilson and Catunda met that year. (C15211, leg 1 C15003, C15004, C15008)

1984 is the hinge year, and it comes as a pair. First, "Como vai voce, Geracao 80?", Parque Lage, Rio, 14 July-13 August, 123 artists -- the movement's landmark show, a return to subjective painting against the austere conceptual 1970s. Romagnolo confirms his participation in his own words: "Yes, 'Como vai voce, Geracao 80?' was an unforgettable art festival experience." The curation credit is disputed -- "Marcos Lontra" alone in one source, a trio "Marcus Lontra Costa, Paulo Roberto Leal e Sandra Magger" in another, with the spelling Marcos vs Marcus differing too (see Corrections 5). Second, "Sergio, Leda, Leonilson e Ciro" at Galeria Luisa Strina in Sao Paulo -- the four-artist show with Leda Catunda, Jose Leonilson and Ciro Cozzolino, Leonilson designing the poster, and a 1984 photograph of the four together, captioned left to right Leonilson, Romagnolo, Cozzolino, Catunda. He also wrote the 1984 catalogue text "A Sintese de Leda Catunda". The Strina gallery is the shared room of the whole cohort: Romagnolo's first solo there in 1986, Leonilson's solos in 1983, 1985, 1987 and 1989, Catunda's first solo in 1987. (C15212, leg 1 C15003, C15004, C15005, C15008; leg 3 C15102, C15103, C15108, C15110)

From the mid-1980s the programme turns physical. An acquisition prize at the Salao Nacional, MAM Rio, in 1985. First solo at Galeria Luisa Strina in 1986 -- and the first sculptures exhibited that same year, in fiberglass. The first car sculpture, "Carro Abandonado", an 80 cm fiberglass model, in 1987; a solo "Sergio Romagnolo - Esculturas" at Petite Galerie the same year; the XIX Bienal. In 1988 the first sculpture solo at Strina -- the first series in plastic moulded by hot-air gun. The 1986 painting solo and the 1988 sculpture solo must not be collapsed into one: the interview's "my first solo show at Galeria Luisa Strina" refers to the 1988 sculpture solo (see Corrections 2). In 1989 he curated "Arte Hibrida" for Funarte/MAM Sao Paulo, touring four cities, and made the religious works "Pieta" and "Coroa de Cristo". In 1990 "Sapatos e Flores" opened at Centro Cultural Sao Paulo -- the occasion, his own account says, was the birth of his first daughter Rita that year -- and he won the Premio Brasilia de Artes Plasticas. XXI Bienal Internacional de Sao Paulo in 1991. In 1993 "Eva", grey moulded plastic, and twelve "Profetas de Aleijadinho" figures. In 1994 "Linhagem", wood, now in MAM Rio (entered 1996). (C15213, leg 1 C15000, C15001, C15002, C15003, C15004; leg 2 C15050, C15052, C15053, C15058)

The 1990s are the academic decade. MA at ECA/USP 1995-98, dissertation "Esculturas: Rugas e Alegorias" (Sculptures: Wrinkles and Allegories), supervisor Regina Silveira, obtained 1999. Doctorate at ECA/USP 1999-2001, thesis "O Vazio e o Oco na Escultura" (The Void and the Hollow in Sculpture), supervisor Carlos Alberto Fajardo, obtained 2002. The two titles are not decoration: "rugas" (wrinkles) names the fingertip and wrinkle marks frozen into his heat-moulded plastic, and "o vazio e o oco" names the hollow at the centre of his sculpture practice. In 1996 "A Alma da Dobra" at Galeria Casa Triangulo opened the Casa Triangulo period (solos 1996, 2000, 2004, 2005, 2009, 2012) -- and, by his own periodisation, the year painting and sculpture began running together. Full-time professor at Faculdade Santa Marcelina 2000-05. Co-curator of the Panorama de Arte Brasileira at MAM-SP in 2001 is ICAA-only, single-source. Bienal de Liverpool in 2002, and a group show "POT" at Galeria Fortes Vilaca the same year -- the ONLY Fortes connection found; Fortes D'Aloia & Gabriel is NOT a representing gallery and must not be listed as one. (C15214, leg 1 C15004, C15007; leg 2 C15050)

The 2000s are the monumental decade. In 2003 "Fusca Grande" (160 x 160 x 400 cm) and "Fusca de Ponta-Cabeca", moulded plastic, and "Bateria com Pantufa". In 2004 he began the fiction "A Feiticeira e as Maquinas" and took part in the 20-year Geracao 80 re-edition "Onde Esta Voce, Geracao 80?" at CCBB. The Samantha series ran 2006-13. In 2007 he became professor at UNESP Instituto de Artes (full-time, still listed current) and began the research project "O objeto e imagens prontas como alegoria neobarroca" (The object and ready-made images as neobaroque allegory), running from 2007 to the present. The retrospective "O Corpo Denso da Imagem", Instituto Tomie Ohtake, 80+ works, curated by Agnaldo Farias, is dated 2009 by Wikipedia and the CV and 2008 by the SciELO essay, with the title varying between "da Imagem" and "das Imagens" -- unresolved; print "2008 or 2009" (see Corrections 1). "A Feiticeira e as Maquinas" showed at Casa Triangulo in 2012, 20 works per the CV (18 per a listing not read end to end). Premio Honra ao Merito Arte e Patrimonio, IPHAN, in 2013. (C15215, leg 1 C15003, C15004, C15011; leg 2 C15050, C15057)

The current gallery is Galeria Marilia Razuk: first show there 7 June-25 July 2014 (8 new acrylic paintings + 4 remodelled-plastic sculptures); 8th Premio Marcantonio Vilaca, Funarte, 2015; a solo at MARP (Museu de Arte de Ribeirao Preto) in 2016 tied to the Vilaca prize; "Sapatos e Flores" revived at Razuk 2 June-22 July 2017 (11 acrylic paintings + 5 bronze and moulded-plastic sculptures). A book launch for "A Feiticeira e as Maquinas" (Editora Cobogo) was announced for early 2018 with a patented 3-D viewing kit -- but publication is NOT verified, so do not assert it (leg 1 wins over leg 2 on this point; see Corrections 9). "Sergio Romagnolo e as Tres Dimensoes da Pintura" at Galeria Casa da Imagem, Curitiba, 10 November 2018-17 March 2019 (gallery news snippet only; CV year 2019 -- probably one show spanning the year boundary, do not split into two). Frieze New York with Marilia Razuk in 2019. "Estado Bruto" at MAM Rio in 2021 is gallery-news-snippet only, not in the CV as read, partially evidenced. "Efeitos Especiais" (Special Effects) at Marilia Razuk, 11 February-18 March 2023, curated by Jose Augusto Ribeiro, is corroborated by both CV and gallery page. The 40-year echo "Leonilson e a Geracao 80" at Pinakotheke, Rio, 31 July-23 September 2023, included Romagnolo among 19 artists besides Leonilson. "FULLGAS, Artes Visuais e Anos 1980 no Brasil" at CCBB in 2024 is CV-only for his participation; press seen does not name him. Group shows at Marilia Razuk in 2024-25 are CV-only. (C15216, leg 1 C15002, C15003, C15004; leg 2 C15051, C15054, C15056, C15058, C15062)

The Geracao 80 frame, as the sources give it: the 1984 Parque Lage show is the movement's landmark, a return to subjective painting against the "austere" conceptual 1970s. But the ICAA nuance matters for Romagnolo: his case shows Geracao 80 is not limited to the return to painting -- it encompasses sculpture too. Pontual's "Explode Geracao" groups Catunda, Romagnolo and Milhazes as the Rio names -- a critical grouping, not a statement that he lived in Rio. (C15217, leg 1 C15005, C15006, C15007)

The evolution, in his own three phases: painting only 1976-86 (large acrylics, 140-200 cm wide, overlay already the driver) -- sculpture only 1986-96 (fiberglass 1986-87, hot-air moulded plastic from 1988, bronze from c. 1995; small, shy, introverted subjects) -- both together from 1996 (overlay painting returns with Samantha 2006-13, city overlays 2014, shoe and flower overlays 2017; plastic scales up to the 400 cm Fusca, then back down to 22-48 cm shoe and flower objects). The same two operations run through both mediums throughout: (a) superimpose two views of the same thing; (b) remake a familiar object in a wrong material and/or wrong orientation or scale. (C15218, leg 1 C15003; leg 2 C15050, C15051, C15056)


## M1 The superimposed two-view overlay -- the "defective image"

The master mechanism, and the one that runs through both mediums for forty years: two views of the same thing, from different angles, laid on one surface so the figure doubles and will not settle. In "Duas rosas deitadas" (Two Laying Roses), 2017, acrylic on canvas, 125 x 160 cm, two views of the same roses from different angles are superimposed; the petals blur toward abstraction while the subject stays recognisable. His own phrase for it: "like a defective image". The same operation in 1985, in an untitled acrylic on canvas, 140 x 200 cm: overlapping different images and frames of the same film, an oscillating, tremulous figuration with virtual vibration -- the figure will not settle into one frame. And in the 2014 overlay paintings (Prague, Obidos, Orvieto, Paris, Rome, an orchestra concert, wrecked cars), the same place seen through different angles, overlaid on one canvas. The mechanism is not blur and it is not double exposure as decoration: it is two incompatible viewpoints held in one picture, and the viewer's eye keeps trying to resolve them into one object and failing. The defect is the point. In sculpture the same doubling appears as the remodelled ready-made -- the object seen twice, once as itself and once as its plastic ghost.

**Takeaway:** a picture that will not settle is watchable for longer than a picture that resolves. Two views of one thing, superimposed, make the eye work -- and the work is the picture.

## M2 The crammed composite recognition test

"Fred, Barney, Betty and Wilma", 1983, acrylic on plastic sheet, 139 x 196 cm: four Flintstones characters crammed into one composite figure on a transparent two-sided sheet. The viewer must separate four known silhouettes from one tangled outline. The mechanism is a recognition test run at the speed of looking: every silhouette is instantly familiar, but the composite refuses to be any one of them. The transparency of the sheet matters -- the figure has two sides, and the cramming is literal, physical, a sheet you could turn over. His own account: "I crammed all the four characters into one." The companion work from the same year, "Untitled (Batman Trapped)", 1983, acrylic on canvas, 101 x 160 cm, is the same test in a different key: a neo-expressionist "bad painting" of Batman, painted directly with no draft, special attention to the eyes -- recognition and painterly distortion at once. The character is never in doubt; the painting is.

**Takeaway:** cram several things everybody knows into one outline and the viewer cannot help but pull them apart. Familiarity is the material; the tangle is the work.

## M3 Motion as repeated figure and motion lines -- Duchamp over a TV character

"Samantha descendo uma escada" (Samantha Descending a Staircase), 2006, acrylic on canvas, 100 x 150 cm: Duchamp's motion lines over TV's Samantha (Bewitched) -- one figure repeated and shifted down the stairs, stillness and descent in one image. The series ran 2006-13, and the device is Duchamp's, lifted whole: the nude descending the staircase becomes a sitcom witch descending a staircase, and the joke is that the device still works. The motion lines are not illustration of movement; they are the movement, painted. A static canvas holds a descent that never arrives, because the figure is repeated rather than moved. The mechanism earns its watchability from the collision: high-modernist motion analysis applied to the most disposable image in the house.

**Takeaway:** repeat a figure with a shift and the picture moves without moving. Borrow the motion device from art history and point it at television, and both sides sharpen.

## M4 Hand heat-moulding of plastic, marks kept

The sculpture technique, and the one that gives the plastic works their hand. Plastic is heated with a hot-air gun and shaped by hand; fingertip and wrinkle marks remain in the cooled surface. The origin, in his own telling, is a small plastic head heated beside an alcohol-soaked cotton ball, lit -- a domestic accident turned into a method. "Menina de Saia" (Girl with Skirt), 1999, is the two-stage transfer at its clearest: a clay model of his daughter, remade in hot-air-moulded plastic, the fingertip and wrinkle marks frozen in the cooled surface. The "rugas" (wrinkles) of his 1999 MA dissertation title, "Esculturas: Rugas e Alegorias", are these marks -- the hand's trace kept as the sculpture's skin. The same hand-moulding makes "Bateria com Pantufa", the Fuscas, "Flor azul deitada" (2017, moulded plastic, 48 x 14 x 8 cm, a flower as a small plastic object) and "Sandalia Azul" (2017, moulded plastic, 22 x 18 x 11 cm, a shoe miniaturised into a wall sculpture). The material is cheap and industrial; the marks are personal and unrepeatable. That contradiction is the whole technique.

**Takeaway:** heat cheap plastic by hand and keep the fingerprints. The industrial material says disposable; the kept marks say somebody was here.

## M5 The monumental remake of a ubiquitous object in one cheap material

"Fusca Grande" (Big VW Beetle), 2003, moulded plastic, bright red, 160 x 160 x 400 cm: a full-scale everyday object remade in a single prosaic material, bright red where the original is ordinary. The idea came from a police dump yard where Fuscas lay upside down -- "they were so beautiful". The mechanism is scale plus material substitution: the most common car in Brazil, the car everybody has sat in, rebuilt at full size in plastic, in a colour no real Fusca wears. "Bateria com Pantufa" (Drums with Slipper), 2003, moulded plastic, 160 cm high, is the same operation at the same scale: a drum kit remade in silent plastic. The object is recognised before it is understood, and the understanding -- this is plastic, this is silent, this is red -- arrives second. The monumentality is not bronze and it is not marble; it is a cheap material insisting on the same footprint as the real thing.

**Takeaway:** take the most ordinary object in the room, rebuild it life-size in one cheap material, and it becomes a monument. Recognition first, material second, and the gap between them is the sculpture.

## M6 Inversion -- the same object, wrong orientation

"Fusca de Ponta-Cabeca" (Upside Down Fusca), 2003, moulded plastic: the same Fusca body, orientation wrong. The roof reads as floor, the wheels as sky. The mechanism is the cheapest possible transformation -- no remodelling, no new material, only a rotation -- and it changes everything the object means. The dump-yard pose is preserved as the finished pose: the cars he saw lying upside down in the police yard are not corrected, they are kept. Inversion works because the Fusca's silhouette is so familiar that the eye reads it instantly even upside down, and then has to re-read every part against its new position. The car is still a car; it is a car that cannot be driven, cannot be entered, cannot sit on a road.

**Takeaway:** turn the familiar object upside down and change nothing else. The shape stays legible and the meaning flips -- the pose is the work.

## M7 The incongruous pairing -- things that combine but do not combine

"Bateria com Pantufa": a drum kit remade in silent plastic, with a slipper tied on. His rule, in his own words: "The idea is to superimpose different things. Sacred and profane, musical instrument and shoe. Why? Because they never were together; they combine, but they do not combine." The mechanism is pairing without reconciliation: two things that never belonged together are put together and left unreconciled, so the viewer holds an ambiguous relation instead of a resolved image. The drums cannot sound; the slipper cannot be worn; the pair cannot be used. The religious works from 1989, "Pieta" and "Coroa de Cristo", are the same pairing in a different register -- the sacred reworked in a crude handmade register, sacred and clumsy coexisting -- but they are grade C, thinly evidenced, so the drums and the slipper carry the mechanism. The pairing is not a joke and not a riddle with an answer; it is a relation that stays open.

**Takeaway:** pair two things that never belonged together and do not resolve them. The viewer holds the ambiguity, and the holding is the experience.

## M8 Subtraction -- remove the props, let the contour carry the meaning

"Eva" (Eve), 1993, grey monochrome moulded plastic: earlier versions had apple and snake props; the final version carries all meaning in the body contour alone. The grey industrial colour cannot carry the semantics, so the viewer reconstructs them from form and text. The mechanism is removal as construction: take away everything that names the subject, and the subject must be carried by the shape of the body itself. The colour is part of the subtraction -- a grey that refuses to help, that will not do the work of skin or fruit or serpent. What is left is a contour that has to mean Eve with nothing but its own lines. The companion operation is serial: twelve "Profetas de Aleijadinho" figures, 1993, moulded plastic, twelve near-identical hieratic baroque prophets in cheap industrial plastic -- permanence undone by disposability, the sacred figure repeated until the repetition itself is the statement. (The prophets are a backup mechanism, M9, not one of the eight primary prompts.)

**Takeaway:** remove the props and the picture has to earn its subject from the contour alone. Subtraction is not emptiness; it is a demand placed on the shape.

## The aesthetic logic: the ready-made, overlapped and remade

Romagnolo's logic is neobaroque allegory by his own research-project title -- "O objeto e imagens prontas como alegoria neobarroca" -- and the title is accurate. The baroque part is the excess: too many views, too many characters, too much material, too big a car. The allegory part is that every object stands for something it is not: the Fusca stands for the dump yard and for ubiquity; the drums stand for sound they cannot make; Eve stands for a story her body has to tell without props. The ready-made part is the discipline: "Most of my production is based on pre-existing images and ready-mades." He does not invent forms; he takes forms everybody already knows and does one operation to them -- overlap, cram, repeat, melt, enlarge, invert, pair, subtract. One operation, done completely, is the whole work. That is why the three phases are one career and not three: the painting overlays the image, the sculpture remakes the object, and after 1996 both run together because they were always the same operation in two mediums.

The watchability follows from the operation, not from the subject. An overlay that will not settle keeps the eye moving. A crammed composite keeps the viewer separating silhouettes. A repeated figure keeps descending without arriving. A plastic car at full scale keeps being recognised and re-read. An upside-down car keeps flipping in the mind. A pairing that does not combine keeps the relation open. A contour without props keeps demanding its subject. In every case the work gives the viewer something to do, and the doing is slow enough to watch. The colour logic supports it: the bright red Fusca against the grey Eva -- one colour that shouts, one colour that refuses to help -- and the palette of the paintings, large acrylics in which the overlay blurs toward abstraction while the subject stays recognisable.

What he is not: not an accumulator in Leirner's sense (he does not count identical units; he overlaps recognisable figures), not a pop artist in the American sense (the cartoon characters are not quoted, they are crammed and distorted), not a minimalist (the work is excessive, doubled, too much). The closest kin in the studied corpus are Catunda, who also sews printed ready-made images into new wholes, and Leonilson, whose pop-figurative phase bears Romagnolo's mark -- but Romagnolo's operation is his own: the ready-made, overlapped and remade, one operation at a time.
