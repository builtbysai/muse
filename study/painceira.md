## The life: painter for five years, journalist for fifty

Eduardo "Lalo" Painceira was born in La Plata on 26 September 1939 -- the date comes from a single direct source, Jose Maria Pallaoro's blog caption ("Eduardo 'Lalo' Painceira (La Plata, 26 de septiembre de 1939)"), and is corroborated by every dated anchor: dead 8 May 2024 aged 84; "a los 30 anos" when interviewed in late 1970 (off by a year against the date -- he turned 31 on 26 September 1970 -- so the interview was earlier in 1970, or the age was rounded, or the phrasing is loose; the year is safe, the day and month are one-source). His parents' house was in Gonnet, just outside La Plata, and Communist Party cell meetings were held there; he had two brothers, one named Alfredo, who introduced him to Karl Jaspers; he had daughters and a grandson, Justino. His nicknames were Lalo, Rabito, Conejo (the Rabbit -- the title of his third book comes from it), and, around 2001, the alias Pablo Mugica.

He entered the Escuela de Bellas Artes, UNLP, as a painter, and was active in the student centre through the agrupacion AREBA (with Carlos Vallina, Sergio Labourdette, Nestor Mussotto), which ran the Centro de Estudiantes as a bastion of the reformist left. Hector Cartier's classes were the one room his generation shared -- Painceira called Cartier a "gran maestro que no recibio todavia el homenaje que le debe La Plata" -- but no source names Painceira individually as a Cartier or Vision pupil, and this study does not claim it (C9807). The informalists and the geometricos, he wrote, had "no... otro punto de encuentro que las clases que daba en la Facultad de Bellas Artes Hector Cartier"; both collectives "manteniamos posiciones sectarias y enfrentadas. Sin posibilidad de dialogo."

The founding night was Friday 7 October 1960, at the Salon Estimulo in the Museo Provincial de Bellas Artes (then in the basement of the Cine San Martin). Several young exhibitors discovered that night that they were not the only informalists in town, carried the night into the Bar Capitol around the corner, and when the Salon came down hung their pictures on the bar's walls. "Fue nuestro cafetin discepoliano; alli aprendimos, filosofamos, amamos, debatimos, creamos, compartimos y crecimos." Thirty-four days from first meeting to constituted group: "Habian transcurrido nada mas que 34 dias desde que nos habiamos conocido [...] y ya formabamos un grupo" (*El blues*, 2013: 273). Rafael Squirru baptised the group "Si" on an October afternoon; its motto was "Si, a los demas, ante la indiferencia." The first show as Grupo Si was 10 November 1960 at the Circulo de Periodistas; Lima in 1961; the Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires in December 1961 (16 artists, Squirru presenting); a joint presentation with the poets of the Grupo de los Elefantes on 7 October 1961. The group worked by day in a workshop in a quinta at Tolosa and spent the nights in tertulia at the Capitol, its nightly home "desde el 7 de octubre de 1960 hasta fines de 1962". Reunions followed in 1968 (*Remember... Grupo Si*, Alianza Francesa) and 1969 (*Movimiento Si*, Sociedad Hebraica Argentina, 12 participants including Painceira, Vigo invited). He was not among the seven exhibitors of the 1971 *Muestra Grupo Si* -- by then he had left painting and was in prison.

The break was political, in three steps, in that order: "Las discusiones comenzaron a tenirse con ideologia y eso genero en algunos, la necesidad de una praxis" -- he joined the Federacion Juvenil Comunista and the Frente Cultural of the Communist Party, and "esto determino su alejamiento del informalismo, luego del Grupo Si y finalmente, de la plastica." His militancy, in his own words, "habia comenzado como pintor, con Victor Grippo" -- Grippo was the man who "habia llevado a las charlas del bar el tema politico" -- and his cell was led by Mauricio Tenembaum. He entered the Cinematografia degree at UNLP (classmates Raymundo Gleyzer, Alejandro Malowicki); Ricardo Gil Soria later brought him to Peronism. By late 1970 he was already listed in *Quien es quien en La Plata* as "artista plastico, periodista, director teatral y escritor" -- no theatre production, company or date was found; record the title only.

Journalism from 1966 (revista *Juan*, Buenos Aires, with Paco Urondo), then staff chronicler at the daily El Dia de La Plata "donde ejercio varios anos" -- his method, in his own account: "Cuando iba a cubrir un hecho entrevistaba a la gente a la cual le creia, la que padecia lo que estaba ocurriendo. No al funcionario." In 2002 he co-founded La Pulseada, the magazine of the Hogar del Padre Cajade (No. 1, April 2002, opening with his interview of Carlos Cajade).

The hinge of his life as a writer was prison. Detained in mid-1971 in Recoleta, Buenos Aires, tortured under the Lanusse dictatorship at Coordinacion Federal, about a month in Devoto, then a year in Unidad 9, La Plata (cell 786, pavilion 15), for "causas judiciales que me inventaron". Released with the political prisoners' option to leave the country, he went into exile first in Peru (under Velasco Alvarado), then in Chile (under Allende), and returned 25 May 1973. He wrote in prison to stay free: "Escribo para cumplir con una especie de exorcismo... Escribo para trascender la carcel y sentirme libre." / "Los libros son fundamentales en la vida de un preso. Son ventanas sin barrotes que permiten volar, escaparse de la prision." The first part of *El limite de un conejo* is his prison writings transcribed as-is in Peru in 1972.

He called himself "marxista y cristiano" -- "Vengo del marxismo y era ateo... Dios se cruzo en mi vida" -- and later, "un cristiano muy anarquico": "Producto de mi cancer soy cristiano, un hombre de fe al haberme hecho amigo del inigualable Carlitos Cajade." Do not state cancer as the cause of his death in 2024; no source does (C9808). His three books are *Dar la vida. La resistencia de calle 30* (De la Campana, 2006 -- the 24 November 1976 attack on the Mariani-Teruggi house, calle 30 No. 1134), *El blues de la calle 51* (EPC, 2013), and *El limite de un conejo* (Ediciones EME, 2018), "el texto mas doloroso que escribio y al mismo tiempo el mas optimista". In 2017 the Concejo Deliberante de La Plata named him Personalidad Destacada de la Cultura; at the ceremony he said, "Soy hijo de una ciudad que hizo de la cultura uno de sus pilares," and "Cultura no es un comercio, no tiene que dar ganancias, es una inversion. Somos enfermos de esperanza y tenemos nuestras razones." A fourth book was announced c. 2019, title and theme withheld; no trace was found. He died in the early hours of Wednesday 8 May 2024, aged 84, in La Plata (UNLP notice, 8 May 2024, 15:08 UTC: "fallecido a la edad de 84 anos"; Diario Full: "En la madrugada de este miercoles"). His motto in the last interview: "La memoria siempre es resistencia, no melancolia."

## M1 -- Collage as multi-voice structure

*El blues de la calle 51* is subtitled "Collage", and the subtitle is the method: his own memoir mixed with other voices' testimonies, art-history passages on the vanguards, social history of the city, and lists of the music and films that formed the generation. Badenes: "mucho mas que un libro sobre un grupo de artistas plasticos identificados con el 'informalismo'. Es un relato con muchas voces que recupera climas y experiencias que la ciudad vivio hace medio siglo." His own account of the method (2013: 340): "Hasta aqui el relato se centro en el Grupo Si como una expresion de la vanguardia romantica de 1960, recurriendo a mi memoria y a la de mis companeros y amigos." The key chapter, "En busca del tiempo vivido" (pp. 267-340), explicitly organises his testimony against the materials of his personal archive. The Nimio authors warn that the book is oral history as much as history -- "la inexactitud de los recuerdos no debe ser considerada negativamente" -- and that its value is as collective memory, not as a fact-checked chronicle. Collage here is not a painting technique; it is a structure for letting many voices share one surface, and it is Painceira's own stated working method, in prose.

**Takeaway:** many fragments, many voices, one surface -- memoir, testimony, history and lists layered so no single voice owns the picture. A piece that tells one story in one voice has missed the mechanism; the collage must be structural, the fragments must stay visibly different from each other.

## M2 -- The bar-room hang: exhibition as cohabitation

After the Salon Estimulo came down, the group hung their pictures on the walls of the Bar Capitol, which became their headquarters: "Fue nuestro cafetin discepoliano; alli aprendimos, filosofamos, amamos, debatimos, creamos, compartimos y crecimos." The bar "no era atractivo: seducia por no cerrar nunca sus puertas", and the owners "nos prestaron sus paredes". The Capitol was their nightly home from 7 October 1960 to the end of 1962 -- not a gallery with opening hours but a room where the pictures lived with the people who made them, argued over, smoked over, drunk beside. Exhibition here is cohabitation: the work hangs where life happens, the audience is the same people every night, and the debate is part of the display. No individual work by Painceira in this hang is titled or described anywhere; the mechanism is the hang itself, not any picture in it.

**Takeaway:** pictures hung on a bar wall and lived with, not displayed in a white cube -- the room is the frame, the nightly argument is part of the show. A piece that presents its images in a clean gallery grid has missed the mechanism; the work must share the room with its viewers.

## M3 -- The founding night: thirty-four days to a group

The group formed fast, and the speed is the mechanism. Strangers at a salon opening discover they are not alone, carry the night into a bar, and thirty-four days later they are a constituted group with a name, a motto and a first show: "Habian transcurrido nada mas que 34 dias desde que nos habiamos conocido [...] y ya formabamos un grupo." / "Me resulta casi increible nuestra audacia, esa potencia que solo puede ser parida desde la fe y desde la seguridad en lo que haciamos." The formation is a count, not a mood: first meeting, discovery of shared position, nightly meetings, name (Squirru's "Si"), motto ("Si, a los demas, ante la indiferencia"), first show 10 November 1960 -- thirty-four days from strangers to a group that "asombro a todos, escandalizo a muchos y gusto a pocos". Squirru's 1960 line, quoted by Painceira: "Un signo del hombre actual, del hombre nuevo es su conciencia de equipo [...] el hombre se da plenamente fuera de si, se da, dandose."

**Takeaway:** a group that assembles itself in counted days -- strangers, one shared discovery, nightly meetings, a name, a first show -- the formation visible as a sequence, not a backstory. A piece that starts with the group already formed has missed the mechanism; the assembling must be the watchable event.

## M4 -- The perfect-square city and the slap

His most-quoted line about the group (*El blues*, 2013: 276): "En una ciudad creada desde el cuadrado perfecto, nosotros fuimos la provocacion, el cachetazo." La Plata is a planned city, drawn from the perfect square -- and the informalists were the slap in its face. The geometry/informalism opposition is his own later synthesis: "Mientras la geometria representaba la utopica esperanza de una organizacion nueva, el informalismo mostro el alma rota del tiempo que vivia... se complementan y representan las dos caras, el Yin y el Yan de la ultima vanguardia conocida hasta el momento del Modernismo." The revolt was against the official platense field, "clasico y conservador" and grounded in geometric abstraction: the painters "se volcaron hacia el informalismo materico y el expresionismo abstracto" -- matter laid down through stain, texture and gestural brushwork. The city is the perfect grid; the gesture is the break in it; and the two need each other, because without the square there is nothing to slap.

**Takeaway:** a perfect, regular grid -- the planned city -- and one gestural break in it, the slap that only reads because the grid is exact. A piece with no grid has nothing to break; a piece with no break has nothing to say. The grid must be exact and the slap must be visible.

## M5 -- Materic informalism: the group's toolkit, not his hand

This mechanism is flagged, and the flag is part of it. The materic toolkit -- marine varnish, paste paint, volcanic sand on cotton and jute, spatula and knife gesture, scraping on prepared chapadur, industrial discards (enamels, asphalt paint, corroded and burnt sheet metal, wood, wax, adhered papers, sand and sawdust loads, lacerations, cuts, perforations), heavy-matter zones next to bare zones, glossy next to matte, whole-body gesture, the exaltation of the mark and the imprint, improvisation and chance -- is documented by Monkes and Pino (TAREA 2017) for Paternosto and Puente, whose two MAMBA works are the illustrated examples. It is the Grupo Si shared toolkit, evidenced for the group, NOT for Painceira's own hand: no source describes his paintings individually, and no painting title, medium or size of his was found anywhere. Puente's remark that "nosotros con pocos recursos... hay algunas obras de ese tiempo que desaparecieron, se destruyeron. Incluso, yo tambien he destruido algunas de las mias" is Puente's, about his own work, and must not be transferred to Painceira. Any prompt that uses this mechanism must frame it as the group's language -- the room Painceira stood in -- never as "Painceira painted like this".

**Takeaway:** heavy matter on cheap support -- varnish, sand, paste paint, scraped board, industrial discards -- glossy beside matte, dense beside bare, the mark of the whole body. A piece that paints this toolkit in clean flat colour has missed the mechanism; and a piece that attributes it to Painceira's hand has missed the evidence. The matter is the group's; the hand is theirs, not his.

## M6 -- Jazz and calle 51: the city as soundtrack and setting

The book's title is a blues, and the jazz is not decoration: "con el jazz como cortina" -- with jazz as the curtain, the background the group lived against. *El blues de la calle 51* maps the city street by street as the scenography of a generation: the bars (La Cosechera, Bristol, Teutonia, La Paris, El Parlamento, El Cabildo, Sorrento, Costa Brava, Capitol, Adriatico, Tirol Chopp, La Modelo, Rivadavia, La Aguada, Bacci), the cinemas (Astro, Ocho, Paris, Mayo, Rocha, San Martin, Select, Belgrano, Roca, Guemes, America), the independent theatres (La Lechuza, Teatro Nuevo, Los Duendes, the university theatre, CLIMN, Comedia de la Provincia), the Club de Ajedrez at 6 and 54 where Rodolfo Walsh began *Operacion masacre*. The music of the sixties as it reached La Plata -- Joan Baez, John Lennon, Serrat, Atahualpa Yupanqui, Daniel Viglietti, Mercedes Sosa, Violeta Parra -- and the new cinema (Fellini, Truffaut, Antonioni, Resnais, Richardson and Osborne; Torre Nilsson, Murua, Kuhn, Favio). The city is the subject as much as the paintings are, and the street -- calle 51 -- is the spine the book hangs on. The later erasure is part of the mechanism too: "La historia y la transformacion de la ciudad borro a los bares que nos cobijaban: el Capitol, el Adriatico y el Tirol Chopp... De aquellos anos solo queda un bar, el de la esquina, el Parlamento, al que jamas fuimos en los anos sesenta."

**Takeaway:** a city mapped as a soundtrack and a street plan -- bars, cinemas, theatres, one street as the spine, jazz behind everything -- the place doing the work the pictures used to do. A piece that shows the paintings without the city has missed the mechanism; the map must be there, and the music must be audible in the structure.

## M7 -- Memory as resistance: writing as freedom

The late method, and the one that is entirely his own. From *El limite de un conejo*: "La memoria siempre es resistencia, no melancolia." From the prison writings, transcribed in Peru in 1972: "Escribo para cumplir con una especie de exorcismo... Escribo para trascender la carcel y sentirme libre." / "Los libros son fundamentales en la vida de un preso. Son ventanas sin barrotes que permiten volar, escaparse de la prision." The mechanism is writing as an act that does something physical: the prisoner writes to get out of the cell while staying in it; the old man writes to get the dead back into the room while knowing they are gone. The Christmas 1971 passage from Unidad 9 -- a greeting to "mi familia de sangre, a esos viejitos mios y a mis hermanos, cunadas y sobrinos" -- is the same act at its smallest: words sent out of a cell (cell 786, pavilion 15) to people who will visit tomorrow. Memory here is not looking back; it is a tool for getting through the wall in front of you.

**Takeaway:** writing that does physical work -- words as windows without bars, memory as resistance rather than melancholy, the sentence that gets the writer out of the cell. A piece that treats memory as nostalgia has missed the mechanism; the remembering must push against something, and the wall must be visible.

## M8 -- The archive in manila envelopes

The raw material of the 2013 book is his personal archive, visited by the Nimio authors in his house in 2016: a room "atiborrada de libros y de fotografias", his desk with a window onto Avenida 19 overlooking a plaza, and the archive itself -- newspaper articles he himself wrote announcing the group's shows, clippings tracking his ex-colleagues, photographs of his own paintings, Grupo Si leaflets, the Vigo-designed catalogue *Expone movimiento si*, images of the 2007 *no-arte-si* show -- "un registro excepcional y significativo del Grupo Si, con materiales unicos y originales". Collective memory reconstructed through one member's autobiographical account: the archive is not a museum's collection but a man's envelopes, kept for fifty years, in the order he kept them. The book's chapter "En busca del tiempo vivido" (pp. 267-340) works directly from these materials. Custody of the archive after his death in 2024 is unknown; the Nimio authors visited his house in 2016, and no source says who holds it now.

**Takeaway:** a collective history rebuilt from one man's kept envelopes -- clippings, leaflets, photographs, his own press notices -- the archive as a room, not a vault, with the keeper's order still visible. A piece that presents the history clean and sorted has missed the mechanism; the envelopes, the clutter, the hand that kept them must show.

## Evolution: painter, then the break, then the chronicler

1959-60 La Plata: Bellas Artes student, painter, student-centre activist, one of the three who show the first informalist pictures in the city. 7 October 1960: the founding night, Salon Estimulo, then the Capitol; 34 days to a group; first show 10 November 1960; Lima 1961; MAMBA December 1961; the Capitol as nightly home to the end of 1962. Mid-1960s: the three-step break -- informalism, then Grupo Si, then painting itself -- driven by politicisation (Federacion Juvenil Comunista, Frente Cultural, the cell led by Mauricio Tenembaum, Grippo bringing politics into the bar), and the entry into cinematography. 1966: journalism, revista *Juan* with Paco Urondo. 1971-73: detention, torture, Devoto, Unidad 9, exile in Peru and Chile, return 25 May 1973. 1973-2024: fifty years as journalist and chronicler -- El Dia, La Pulseada from 2002, three books (2006, 2013, 2018) that are all memory-work, and the 2013 book as a return to painting only as history: he writes the group, he does not paint again. There is NO evidenced "late painting" to contrast with the early work -- the late work IS the books. Any prompt imagining a late Painceira painting style would be inventing it. What cannot be claimed: any dated stylistic sequence for his paintings (no titled painting found); any visual description of a single painting of his (no image viewed); any solo show or prize (none found); any degree (none documented).

## The network: the trio, the pair, the room, the anchor

Dalmiro Sirabo (#640): fellow founder; the trio member whose answer went to geometry -- began informalist, ended a geometric constructivist, museologist and subdirector of the Museo Provincial de Bellas Artes (tenure dates unknown), his works in the Museo Pettoruti, MUMART and MACLA. He did not build a museum; do not say he did.

Horacio Elena (#641): fellow founder; the trio member whose answer went to the children's book -- some 235 illustrated books, first in 1966, settled in Sitges in 1975, Spanish citizen 1980. The one who left La Plata permanently, against whom Painceira's staying is defined.

Cesar Paternosto (#638) and Alejandro Puente (#639): fellow Grupo Si members, the "geometria sensible" pair, the group's principal exponents after a brief passage through abstract expressionism from 1960. Painceira is ABSENT from the 1967 *La vision elemental* signatories and the May 1968 exhibitors -- documentary absence (he had left painting), not a quarrel. The link runs through the shared 1960-62 group and the shared Cartier room, not through any later collaboration; none was found.

Hector Cartier: the common teacher, the shared room. His Vision classes (1956-65) were the one point of encounter between the informalists and the geometricos, who otherwise held "posiciones sectarias y enfrentadas. Sin posibilidad de dialogo." Painceira is the chronicler of that classroom and of the sectarian split, and his book is itself cited in the Cartier scholarship. He is not an evidenced Cartier pupil (C9807).

Cesar Lopez Osornio (#642): the anchor, NOT a Grupo Si member and NOT a fellow founder -- A1: "Si bien este ultimo artista no participo de dicho grupo." The link to Painceira is institutional and historiographic (the Vision chair Lopez Osornio held; the MACLA as the institution that holds the generation's work, including Sirabo's 2007 show and Elena's 2008 solo show), not a personal encounter -- none was evidenced. ## Aesthetic logic

Painceira's aesthetic is the logic of the man who stopped painting and kept the painting alive in words. Pick one night -- 7 October 1960, a salon opening, a bar around the corner -- and let everything else change around it: the city it happened in (La Plata, drawn from the perfect square), the room it happened in (the Capitol, doors never closed, pictures on the bar walls), the people who were there (sixteen names, three of them the founding trio), and the job the night is asked to do fifty years later (a book, a collage, a room full of manila envelopes). The informalist gesture is the through-line, but it is a gesture remembered, not a gesture performed: the slap in the face of the perfect square, the heavy matter on cheap support, the jazz behind everything -- all of it reaches the study second-hand, through his prose, because the paintings themselves are not in the record. That is why the book, not any canvas, is the central work, and why the mechanisms that transfer are structural (collage, cohabitation, formation, the grid against the slap, the city as soundtrack, memory as resistance, the archive) rather than visual (no palette, no motif, no handling attributable to his hand). A Painceira that invents his paintings -- a late style, a signature colour, a titled canvas -- has kept the name and sacked the evidence; the paintings are the one thing the record does not give, and the study must say so plainly. The contrast with his two fellow founders is the study's spine, and it is a contrast inside one bar: Sirabo took the informalist night into geometry, Elena took it into the children's book and out of the country, and Painceira took it into prison, into journalism, and finally into the book that remembers the night for all three of them. Same generation, same city, same bar -- and from that shared room, three opposite mechanisms: the geometry, the book, and the memory.
