## M1. The aristocratic interior as a decaying organism

What it is: a room, a palazzo, a hotel, a castle, filmed so completely that its furniture, fabrics, candles and dust are the plot. The interior does not decorate the story, it digests the people in it. The camera moves slowly across objects before it finds faces, people are framed by doorways, mirrors and windows, and the room outlasts its occupants. The Leopard's Salina palazzo and its ball, the Grand Hotel des Bains in Death in Venice, Ludwig's castles, the two-floor apartment in Conversation Piece, the Hermil house in The Innocent, and the watchable behaviour is a camera that treats a chandelier as a character and a doorway as a sentence, and a house that is still standing, still furnished, still lit, after the people who owned it have gone.

**Takeaway:** the room must digest the people, and an interior that only frames the action has not used the mechanism.

## M2. The long take as social observation

What it is: the camera holds a shot long enough for a social situation to develop, contradict itself and resolve, without cutting away to a reaction. A dinner, a ball, a boxing match or a walk runs in real time while several small dramas cross inside one frame. **Takeaway:** the shot must outlast the reaction, and a long take that only shows one thing happening has not used the mechanism.

## M3. The family as the unit of history
