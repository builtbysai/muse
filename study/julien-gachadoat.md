## The life: forty years of code

The biography is unusually well documented for a plotter artist, because he tells it himself in a long Verse conversation (November 2022, read end to end) and the Galerie Data SINUSOIDES press kit (April 2024, 906 lines) repeats it with dates. The study does not fill the gaps those sources leave.

Julien Gachadoat was born in 1975; no source found states where (C2111). He lives and works in Bordeaux. He started coding at age 8, on a very old computer with 4kb of RAM that his teacher father brought home: "I was playing video games, but I was also compelled to do coding and so started coding without knowing what it meant at the time. I have been doing this for years now. Forty years now..." He grew up in the demomaking culture of the late 1990s, the avant-garde scene of visuals generated by computer code, and programming languages have been his creative tool ever since.

Before the plotter work made him known, he co-founded the interactive digital art studio 2Roqs with Michael Zancan (study #593), working on numerous interactive installations (C2113: single-sourced to the Domestika bio; no founding date established). He won an early prize for the digital work "Gravity": the Liedts-Meesen Foundation's New Technological Art Award in 2010, jury prize and public prize, 5,000 euros, exhibited at Update_3 / Body Sound at the Zebrastraat in Ghent, 17 April - 20 June 2010. What "Gravity" itself was -- medium, behaviour -- is not described in any source read (C2118, C2160); do not describe it beyond "digital work".

His stated philosophy, repeated across gallery, publisher, and course bios: "To leave a unique mark, aesthetically palpable, that is not in defiance, but rather in aid of the digital." The Galerie Data version adds: "Leaving a unique, physical and palpable trace of art, not in spite of digital technology, but thanks to it." He "unites" on paper "the computer and the pencil, the rigor of computer code and the poetry of art, moved by its errors, its irregularities, its share of the improbable."

Teaching is a parallel practice, not a side job: more than 15 years teaching creative coding in schools in France and abroad, and at university level (C2114: single-sourced to Domestika; no schools named). His Domestika course "Intro to Creative Coding: Create Graphic Objects" (2022) has 8,051 students and a 4.9 rating. He runs public "Recoding" workshops in which participants recode pioneer works of the 1960s-70s in p5.js and plot the results on modern and vintage plotters.

## The turn: the plotter years

The turn has a date and a machine. As told by Mark Webster in the Pathways book text (Vetro Editions, 2024): in 2014 Webster collaborated with one of his students to build a drawing machine called SAM; a few years later SAM was shown in a workshop and installation organised by Gachadoat in Bordeaux, and it was there that Webster saw "the beginnings of Julien's own interest in plotters." Gachadoat's plotter work then became a long-running artistic journey devoted to drawing with the machine.

The lineage he claims is explicit and historical. The Galerie Data press kit places him in the line of Vera Molnar ("Interruptions", 1968-69; "Hypertransformations", 1975-76), Georg Nees, Manfred Mohr's 1971 plotter work, Michael Noll, John Roy, Jean-Paul Delahaye. He names Molnar as a central influence, and at the end of 2023 he met her in person -- she died in December 2023, so the meeting was among her last collaborations -- and worked with her using his "DiceGL" software, dice rolls driving a pen plotter, producing the 2023 drawing series "Deux generations pour un sommaire" ("Two generations for a summary"). The DiceGL protocol detail (how dice values map to drawing parameters) comes only from his own site (C2116, C2171).

The second lineage thread is Frieder Nake. In 2013 Gachadoat co-organised with Mark Webster a lecture and workshop by Nake in France. Nine years later the three sat down in the Verse conversation that titles this study's method: Nake's line, "Think the image, don't make it... We don't make the images. For that, we have the machine. We think the images." Gachadoat's own version of the same sentence is the one the study is built on: "I don't draw anything, I just code, adding some process and ideas, and I'm not sure about the results I will get."

He entered crypto art in 2021, selected by ArtBlocks in May 2021 with his first project "Radiance" in July 2021, then invited by Casey Reas to -GRAPH on Feral File in November 2021. He returned for +GRAPH in 2023 with "Mineral". His own words on entering the space: "I was curious about that space... With the ability for the collector, to not see what he is going to buy. For ArtBlocks, I thought it was quite incredible at the time, and I still think it is." Note the correction the legs had to make: he has no evidenced fxhash profile (C2112, C2179). The brief's "fxhash-era peer" is cohort context -- the 2021-2023 generative-NFT wave -- not an evidenced fxhash catalogue.

## The practice: the executor, not the hand

Three load-bearing ideas organise everything he makes, all in his own words or in the words of the people who write about him.

First, the computer as performer. After Vera Molnar, he calls the machine "this unique performer" / "this peerless executor", used "to explore the formal possibilities that arise" / "to navigate the field of possible motifs". The plotter is the same idea made physical: the machine that thinks the image while he thinks the system. The contrast with Pierre Braun, his SINUSOIDES partner, is the point: Braun reduces the number of points on a curve to give the line an organic aspect; Gachadoat uses a filling algorithm to produce calculated results, tracing "with maximum functional economy". The machine is outstanding precisely because it is not a hand.

Second, simple rules plus random numbers. Mark Webster's reading: simple graphic elements laid out on a grid, randomness varying position, size, orientation, line length -- the same grammar as Molnar and Mohr. The tools are Processing and p5.js; the materials are an AxiDraw, a 0.5 mm black uni-pin fine-line felt-tip pen, Fabriano bristol 250 g paper; unique pieces, usually 21 x 29.7 cm or 29.7 x 42 cm. Monochrome geometric elements, spatial repetition, grids, sinusoidal functions, filling algorithms. Random number sequences introduce the unpredictable. He also works with vintage plotters (Roland DXY in the 2026 Recoding workshops), an industrial robot arm (Universal Robots at AATB, Arles, 2019; C2214: single-sourced), silkscreen printing, and "Live Plotting": drawing on paper as you code, the plotter running while the program runs.

Third, the serial etude. Works arrive as named series with many iterations (Radiance 512, Structures 256, Umwelt and Mineral 30 each, Sato sleeves in runs of 60-100). He also publishes books that are themselves serial systems (Pathways 121, Noir Orion 63). The practice rhythm is serial, etude-like: one algorithm, many iterations, sometimes bound as a book.

## The evolution: from sine waves to the circle, via the city and the map

The arc has five phases, and the constants are the three pillars of the practice: a grid, a simple rule, and random numbers. What migrates is the primitive, and then the distribution.

Phase 1, the plotter decade before the chain (2010-2020): "Gravity" wins a prize in 2010, then the record goes quiet until the 2020 gallery shows (Lignes, Galerie Metavilla, Bordeaux; Graphic waves, DIDAM, Bayonne; Algorithmes, La Conciergerie; GENERATIVE, Galerie Data). The early work is black ink on white paper, one system per drawing: sine waves (Double, Slate), subdivision meshes (Lignes), radial bursts. The machine is a desktop AxiDraw; the aesthetic is monochrome, geometric, explicitly historical.

Phase 2, the chain years (2021-2022): the same systems go on-chain, and the edition becomes the work. Radiance (512, ArtBlocks) turns the maze into a mint; Umwelt (30, Feral File) turns subdivision into a plotter-drawing bundle (first collector of each edition receives the physical plot); Haze (4, objkt) turns hatching into a tribute to Herbert W. Franke. Nothing about the line changes; what changes is the distribution: the software holds millions of unseen compositions, and the first collector gets the physical plot. The cover work (Memory Scale -- Soft Power, 2021, for the Audiobulb label) and the sleeves (Sato, 2022-2023, for Chapelle XIV) show the same systems applied to music packaging -- generative drawing as applied art.

Phase 3, the systems get bigger and more social (2023): Structures (256) builds a whole isometric city out of cell types; Pathways (121) builds a fictional map out of 121 tiles and then a book out of the map; Mineral (30) moves from geometry to simulated ecologies; DiceGL hands the random source to real dice and to Vera Molnar herself. The work stops being a single drawing and becomes a protocol, a map, a collaboration.

Phase 4, the break with black-on-white (2024-2025): Curls and Superspective invert to white ink on black paper; Superspective leaves paper for enamel on lava stone; Hypercombination adds circles and arcs to the maze family; Deambulations and the Delahaye recodes put the line on a yellow ground. The isometric cube, the quarter-turn square, and the circle become the new primitives, replacing the sine wave of 2020.

Phase 5, the algorithm becomes the collectible (2025-2026): Noir Orion sells the algorithm itself to Ragnar Digital, inscribed on Ethereum, with the book of 63 drawings as its evidence; Recoding turns the practice backward, re-writing John Roy's "Segrid" (1973) and Delahaye's 1985 figures in p5.js and teaching others to mutate them. The arc runs from drawing with code, to minting the drawing, to selling the code, to teaching the history of the code.

## M1 -- Subdivision plus per-cell fill

The lifelong engine. A square is partitioned into smaller areas; each area is filled by its own rule. The rule changes per work; the engine does not.

Umwelt (2021, -GRAPH) is the dated proof, and the clearest: "a subdivision algorithm combined with a curved-line drawing algorithm. Each image partitions a square into a series of smaller areas and fills those areas with a dense group of curved lines; the spacing of the lines, their orientation, and their precise curvature are defined by harmonic functions that create rhythms within a spatial geometric melody" (Feral File press coverage). The inspected image matches exactly: a black field of white curved mesh, the square visibly cut into rectangular cells, each cell carrying its own curve direction and density, some cells near-empty, some saturated.

Radiance (2021, ArtBlocks, 512) is the same engine with a different fill: the inspected testnet render shows a deep blue field holding a square subdivided into regions, each region filled with nested right-angle line paths -- a maze of parallel white lines turning at 90 degrees, dense in some cells, sparse in others, with occasional small spiral-like knots (C2163: no prose description of the algorithm was found; the maze rule is inferred from the image; mint palettes may vary).

Structures (2023, Plottables, 256) is the engine grown into a city: the inspected image shows a blue field holding a dense isometric city on a diamond grid. Each grid cell holds ONE structure type, drawn in white line: a stack of offset cubes, a staircase, a Gaussian bell hill rendered as a wireframe dome, a stack of flat plates, or a bare triangle outline (C2168: the cell-type reading is inferred from the single image; the exact type list and selection rule are unstated in prose).

Hypercombination (2024, for "Interthinking", Budapest) is the engine with a wider motif set: the inspected image shows a black square tiled with cells, each cell holding a different geometric motif in white line -- nested squares, concentric circles, quarter-circle arc fans, dense parallel-line grids, tiny squares (C2173: no prose mechanism; visual reading from one image).

**Takeaway:** divide the field first, then give each cell its own rule. The subdivision is the composition; the fill is the variation. A field with one rule everywhere is a texture; a field with one rule per cell is a city.

## M2 -- The sine wave as primitive

The 2020 primitive, and the one the Galerie Data press kit documents in full mechanical detail. The "Cascades" algorithm (2020-2023): vertically modulated sinusoidal waves whose amplitude and frequency vary from top to bottom of the drawing; the interstices between two sinusoids are filled with vertical stripes whose orientation and density follow spatial rules plus random numbers; noise functions add micro-variations. Randomness introduces irregularities and break zones, so the spaces between strokes are deliberately irregular, producing a rhythm of compression and dilation that may evoke mountainous reliefs. The inspected Sinusoides image matches: a framed black-on-white plot of horizontal wavy contour bands forming mountain-like reliefs, dense at the peaks, signed "v3ga".

"Double" (2020) is the same family with the filling switched on lightly: a sinusoid moving up and down in a regular pattern, modulated by a non-linear function so the waves do not follow a predictable trajectory; a second sinusoidal function modulates the frequency of the oscillations; the filling function is light, producing a dense but airy surface. "Slate" (2020) is the same family with the filling switched off: the overall structure is based on a sinusoidal function with NO internal shape filling; the spacing between the lines and the covering with perpendicular lines gives the impression of a grid, or honeycomb structure, reminiscent of natural structures. The contrast with Double is the point: same sine family, filling switched off (C2162: Double and Slate mechanisms rest on the press kit alone).

Memory Scale -- Soft Power (2021, cover for the Audiobulb label) is the wave applied to a regular line field: the inspected image shows a black rectangle holding parallel horizontal white lines displaced by a wave function -- the lines bend up and down into a smooth undulating surface with a central bulge, breaking into zigzag teeth at the left and right edges where the displacement exceeds the frame (C2165: mechanism inferred from the cover image only).

**Takeaway:** one wave is enough if you modulate it twice -- once for amplitude, once for frequency -- and let noise break it. The break zones are the drawing; a perfect sine is a diagram.

## M3 -- The dual renderer: draw once, emit twice

He wrote a custom SVG renderer with functions mirroring the p5.js drawing API (rendererP5 / rendererSVG), so every line drawn to the canvas is simultaneously written as SVG for plotting (reported by Ahmad Moussa / Gorilla Sun, April 2023, quoting Gachadoat's own shared code; the Gorilla Sun page was read only in part, lines 0-372 of 572). The screen and the plotter are not two outputs made in sequence; they are one drawing act with two bodies. He shares code openly on GitHub: recodes of Jean-Paul Delahaye's books in p5.js, "p5LearnableProgramming", a compilation of computer and computer-art history links, live-plotting code ("Drawing on paper as you code").

This is the technical fact behind the whole practice's distribution: the same code that shows a composition on screen in under a second is the code that drives the AxiDraw for hours, and the same code that mints 512 Radiance outputs is the code that plots 30 Umwelt drawings. The renderer is what makes the edition possible -- the NFT and the plot are not two versions of the work, they are two emissions of one drawing act. The divergence between them is the point Velitchkova makes from the other side (her screen shows a detail, her paper the whole); Gachadoat's version is cleaner: the two emissions are simultaneous, and the difference between them is only the medium's patience.

**Takeaway:** build the renderer, not the picture. If the code emits screen and plotter together, the work can live in both worlds without being translated -- and translation is where generative work usually loses its nerve.

## M4 -- Dice as the random source

DiceGL (2023) is the random source made physical and historical at once. Real dice drive a pen plotter, in a protocol built with Vera Molnar: the drawing "Deux generations pour un sommaire" was created with Molnar using the DiceGL software. The inspected photo shows the protocol as a still life: a white sheet on a wooden table carrying a small black-on-white plotter drawing of nested-square maze motifs (the Radiance / Hypercombination family); two red dice at the top right, a black pen, a punched-card strip, and a red-and-white striped book spine reading "VERA MOLNAR" at the bottom. The dice are the random source; the plotter is the hand. The lineage is explicit: Molnar's own dice-based chance procedures, re-run through his plotter (C2171: the exact protocol -- how dice values map to drawing parameters -- is unstated in sources found; the photo shows dice plus plotted sheet only. C2116: the DiceGL detail comes only from his own site).

The mechanism matters because it answers the question every generative system has to answer: where does the randomness come from? Most systems take it from a seed, invisible and unarguable. DiceGL takes it from a throw, visible and embodied, and from the artist who invented the procedure in the first place. The dice are not a gimmick; they are the random source with a history attached, and the history is the point -- two generations, one summary.

**Takeaway:** make the random source visible and give it a lineage. A seed is a number; a die is a decision with a history. The viewer should be able to see where the chance came from.

## M5 -- The edition as the work

The chain years change nothing about the line and everything about the distribution, and that change is itself a mechanism. Radiance (512, ArtBlocks, July 2021) turns the maze into a mint; Umwelt (30, Feral File, November 2021) turns subdivision into a plotter-drawing bundle; Mineral (30, Feral File, 2023) turns simulated ecologies into the same bundle; Structures (256, Plottables, 2023) turns the isometric city into a mint; Haze (4, objkt, 2022) turns hatching into a tribute. The software holds millions of unseen compositions; the edition is the work's public form, and the first collector gets the physical plot. The edition sizes come from his own site; the Feral File and ArtBlocks project pages were not read directly (C2117, C2164).

The applied-art wing of the same mechanism: the Sato sleeves for Chapelle XIV (2022: 60 silkscreen + 10 plotted; 2023: 100 signed AxiDraw plotted sleeves) and the Memory Scale cover put the same systems on music packaging. The sleeve counts are edition counts; the system does not care whether its output is a gallery plot or a record sleeve. That indifference is the mechanism: one algorithm, many distributions, and the distribution is chosen after the system, not before it.

**Takeaway:** the edition is not packaging around the work; it is the work's public form. Design the system so its output count is a parameter -- 4, 30, 256, 512 -- and let the count change what the work means. (Not folded into a prompt this run; the edition is the distribution discipline behind JGA1 and JGA5.)

## M6 -- The map and the book as the sum

Pathways (2023, with Vetro Editions, released on Verse, London) is the mechanism at its largest: 121 unique NFTs connected in a grid to form a larger artwork. The 121 tiles together reveal the topography of a fictional map made of imaginary geometric landscapes and abstract parametric urban areas; the map holds several neighbourhoods with unique features depending on their localisation, giving the viewer a "promenade in the world of Gachadoat's patterns" (Vetro/Verse). The collector's edition: 121 unique covers forming an 11 x 11 grid of books at Verse; the standard edition: a soft cover showing the whole grid. Physical books released November 2023. The inspected image is the standard-edition book cover: a black cover with the 11 x 11 grid of tiny monochrome map tiles, axes numbered 0-10, "PATHWAYS" and "VETRO" on the cover, "J. GACHADOAT" down the side. Each tile is one NFT; the map is the sum, not any single tile (C2169: Vetro Editions and his site say 121 unique NFTs; a 2023 newsletter reported 879 pieces minting at $60 each -- the 121 figure is used here; the 879 figure is unreconciled).

Noir Orion (2025, RRose Editions) is the same mechanism at book scale: a sketchbook of 63 drawings generated by an algorithm exploring the gradual transformation of a circle (see M8). The book is the system bound: the algorithm's parameter space, printed in order, from a bare circle to starbursts. The book is not a catalogue of the work; it is the work, in the only form that shows the whole parameter space at once.

**Takeaway:** build the tile, then build the sum. The map is not 121 pictures; it is the one picture that only exists when all 121 are together. A system whose outputs add up to something larger than any one of them is a system worth building.

## M7 -- Simulated ecologies

Mineral (2023, +GRAPH, Feral File, 30 editions) is the turn from geometry to artificial life. Casey Reas's per-artist line: Gachadoat is "engaging with ideas of artificial life and rendering the traces of unfolding simulated ecologies." The specific simulation rules are unstated in every source found (C2170) -- the prose mechanism ends there, and the study does not invent the rest. What the inspected image shows: a black field of white linework forming a fractured mineral terrain -- large polygonal facets (Voronoi-like cells) with empty black interiors, zones of fine stipple dots, zones of parallel hatching, and a central triangular mesh. The visual vocabulary is fracture plus fill: each shard gets its own fill treatment.

The word "traces" does the work in Reas's line. The system is not drawn; it unfolds, and the drawing is the trace it leaves behind. That is the real contrast with Velitchkova, and the corrected doorway's point: her system recreates a recognisable thing and breaks it; his system grows a picture and leaves the trace of the growth. The mineral is not depicted; it is the record of a process that behaved like growth. Orbe (undated on his site) is the same vocabulary wrapped around a perfect orb: the inspected image shows a huge white concentric-ring disc (polar grid of radial spokes and concentric circles) dominating a black field, framed at the corners by fractured mineral shards, stipple fields and hatch zones (C2175: date and medium unstated).

**Takeaway:** let the system unfold, then draw only the trace. The picture should read as something that grew, not something that was drawn -- fracture, stipple, and hatch as the record of a process, not as decoration.

## M8 -- The circle takes over

Noir Orion (2025 book; 2026 algorithm NFT) is the late primitive, and the proof is an image that is itself the argument. The algorithm explores the gradual transformation of a simple shape, the circle: a Gaussian function determines the subdivision depth of the circle, the number of decorators applied, and their distribution along its perimeter; randomness then determines the characteristics, including the subdivision values, the decorators chosen, and their parameters (Ragnar Digital, read end to end). The inspected image is the proof: an 8 x 8 contact sheet of 64 circles on black, reading left to right, top to bottom, from a bare circle, to one chord, to shard subdivision, to dense hatched and stippled decorators, ending in radial starbursts. The sheet IS the algorithm's parameter space made visible.

The 2026 step is the mechanism's endpoint: the algorithm itself as an NFT on Ethereum (custom contract), acquired by Ragnar Digital; the algorithm contains all the book iterations plus almost infinite variations (C2115: book and NFT single-sourced to his own site; RRose Editions / Ragnart pages not located). The book of 63 drawings is the evidence; the algorithm is the collectible. Where the chain years sold the drawing, Noir Orion sells the code -- and the contact sheet is the receipt, showing the buyer exactly what the code's space looks like.

**Takeaway:** show the parameter space, not the favourite output. A contact sheet that reads bare circle to starburst is the algorithm made legible, and legibility is what makes an algorithm collectible.

## M9 -- The inversion

After years of black ink on white paper, the line flips. Curls (2024) is white ink on paper (Fedrigoni Sirio Ultra Black 280 g), 42 x 59.4 cm, unique piece: four squares make a quarter-turn from the corners to the centre of the composition, in a slightly asymmetrical rotational movement; their placement in space overlaps in a geometric choreography of lines (Forged in Data press kit). The mechanism is a rotation plus overlap rule applied to four squares; the "curl" is the visual result of the quarter-turns (C2172: prose only; no image inspected).

Superspective (2025) is the same inversion as depth: white ink on paper (Fedrigoni Sirio Ultra Black 280 g), 40 x 40 cm, unique piece, randomly placing cubes in isometric perspective on a two-dimensional grid; their superimposition creates a rhythmic composition of lines, creating an illusion of depth and movement (Forged in Data press kit). The companion works "Solid Superspective #1" (100 x 100 cm) and "#2" (50 x 50 cm), 2025, are enamel on lava stone -- the same isometric vocabulary leaving paper entirely (C2174: prose only; the enamel-on-lava-stone process unstated).

The yellow ground is the second inversion, quieter: Deambulations (2024, L'Artichaut, Bordeaux) puts the line on a yellow ground -- the inspected image shows a yellow ground holding a black-line blob of subdivided cells, elongated petal/leaf cells radiating and branching, dense at the centre, with hatched sub-cells, organic, like a cracked seed or plant cross-section -- and the Delahaye recodes do the same, a black wireframe heightfield with sharp Gaussian-like peaks on a yellow ground. Two yellow-ground exceptions in a monochrome career, both late, both organic where the black-on-white work is geometric.

**Takeaway:** invert late, and invert completely. White on black is not a palette swap; it is the same system proving it survives its own negative -- and when it leaves paper for lava stone, the system has proved it is not the medium. (Not folded into a prompt this run; the inversion is the palette discipline behind JGA2 and JGA7.)

## M10 -- Recoding as method

The practice turns backward in 2026 and makes the turn into a method. MAGBa3 -- Origens (Palo Alto, Barcelona, 8 July - 28 August 2026): nineteen artists each reinterpret a work from Computer Graphics and Art magazine (1976-1978). Gachadoat's contribution recodes "Segrid" (1973) by John Roy. The companion workshop "Recoding: Tracing the Origins of Generative Art", 10 July 2026, is a four-hour p5.js session starting from a contemporary recoding of Segrid, with participants making their own iterations and mutations. A second Recoding workshop runs at Stereolux, Nantes, 2026. The inspected image is an install photograph only: a visitor looking at a framed black-and-white plot of a dense pixelated/mosaic circular composition ("Hyper prototype") on a white wall at Palo Alto (C2177: the Segrid recode's exact output title and parameters are unstated).

The recodes on his own site go further back: Jean-Paul Delahaye's 1985 books "Dessins geometriques et artistiques avec votre micro-ordinateur" and "Nouveaux dessins geometriques et artistiques", re-written in p5.js (C2176: which Delahaye figure is recoded is not named on the page; the filename suggests figure 198). ## The aesthetic logic

The aesthetic is the executor's trace: monochrome line, simple rules, random numbers, the machine's precision doing what a hand cannot -- and the hand's absence doing what precision cannot. The line is always the same instrument (0.5 mm uni-pin, or its white-ink inversion) on the same paper (Fabriano bristol, or black Sirio, or lava stone), and the variety comes entirely from the rule, never from the material. That restraint is the argument: one pen, many systems.

The work is quiet where the field is loud. Where Velitchkova's systems glow and saturate, Gachadoat's stay black on white, white on black, or -- twice, late -- black on yellow. The quiet is structural, not decorative: the systems are small (a sine, a subdivision, a dice throw, a Gaussian), the outputs are mostly air (Radiance's maze cells hold empty space beside dense knots; Umwelt's cells go near-empty beside saturated ones), and the meaning arrives as a trace -- of a wave's modulation, of an ecology's unfolding, of a circle's subdivision. The viewer does the last step, reading the rule out of the trace.

The evolution's direction is from the wave to the circle, and from the drawing to the code. The sine wave of 2020 becomes the Gaussian of 2025; the plot of 2021 becomes the algorithm of 2026. The system did not get more complicated; the thing it sells got more abstract. That is the whole practice in one image: the rules stay simple, the trace stays legible, and what changes is how much of the work the code is allowed to be.
