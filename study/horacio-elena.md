# Horacio Elena: Study Notes

**Site:** Horacio Elena Romero, born La Plata, Argentina, 1940 (no day or month found anywhere; the year is attested by three independent sources and carried with a caveat, see C9786), death year unknown, Argentine painter, sculptor and children's-book illustrator, the platense who took the Vision teaching out of the gallery and into the children's book -- from the informalist Grupo Si, which he co-founded in 1960, to fifty years of parallel tracks in Sitges: painting and sculpture on one side, some 235 illustrated children's books on the other, the rounded female figure the constant that runs through both, modelled in texture, cut in pure colour on wood, cropped into fragments, and set looking at the sea (C9781, C9786).

## The life: La Plata texture, the road, the book, Sitges

Horacio Elena Romero was born in La Plata, Argentina, in 1940. The day and month are not found anywhere. The year itself was the subject of a flag carried from study #640 -- "birth year not found anywhere, do not state one" -- and this study overturns it: three independent sources give La Plata, 1940 -- Visit Sitges (the official town tourism site, on the 2018 retrospective): "La carrera artistica de Horacio Elena (La Plata, 1940) comienza a principios de los anos sesenta..."; the Universidad Nacional de Cordoba library, "Red de relaciones": "Elena Romero, Horacio (Argentina, 1940)"; and the Basque library authority record (Liburubila): "Elena, Horacio (1940-)". The caveat is stated plainly: his own biography gives no year at all (it opens "Nace en La Plata, Argentina" with no date), and none of the three sources is a primary record. The year is consistent with the rest of the record -- the 2018 retrospective spans 1962-2018, a career starting at about twenty-two, and Painceira's "Ninguno en el grupo llegaba a los 30 anos" for the 1960 Grupo Si fits a twenty-year-old in 1960. Print 1940 with this attestation, not as unqualified fact (C9786).

His training, in his own words: "Cursa estudios de Arquitectura y Cinematografia en La Plata, Plastica con Alfredo Kleinerth y Vision con Hector Cartier. Grabado en madera en la Escuela de Bellas Artes de Bahia, Brasil." No institution is named for the architecture and cinematography studies, no degree is claimed, and Kleinerth is attested nowhere else found this run -- a name from Elena's own bio only. The Vision line is the one that matters: Cartier (1907-1997) taught Vision at the Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes, UNLP, 1956-1965, bringing Gestalttheorie into the La Plata curriculum -- Koffka and Arnheim in the syllabus, "the study of the psychological basis of the integration of line, space, colour, value and texture, and also the relation figure-ground, closure, good form" (Grassi). Sirabo studied colour and composition with Cartier alongside Sitro, Ambrosini and Trotta; Lopez Osornio was also Cartier's student and then held the Vision chair 1963-1975. The informalists and the concrete artists shared Cartier's classes and did not speak to each other -- "Ambos colectivos manteniamos posiciones sectarias y enfrentadas. Sin posibilidad de dialogo" (Painceira) -- and Elena sat in that room on the informalist side.

The founding scene is 7 October 1960, Salon Estimulo, Museo Provincial de Bellas Artes, then in the basement of the Cine San Martin, La Plata. Young artists who thought they were alone as informalists discover each other; that night they move to the Bar Capitol round the corner, which becomes their headquarters -- "Fue nuestro cafetin discepoliano; alli aprendimos, filosofamos, amamos, debatimos, creamos, compartimos y crecimos" (Painceira). When the Salon came down they hung the pictures in the bar. Galvez is explicit that Sirabo, "junto a Horacio Elena y Lalo Painceira, exhibio los primeros cuadros informalistas en la ciudad de La Plata". The group's first show as Grupo Si follows on 10 November 1960, Circulo de Periodistas, La Plata -- two different events, do not conflate them. The motto, per Nessi: "Si, a los demas, ante la indiferencia". The group shows in Lima (1961), at MAMBA (December 1961, sixteen artists), and at Tres Arroyos (1962); then it dissolves -- Yurkievich, writing from Paris in 1975, calls it "el consiguiente exodo de la mayor parte del grupo". Elena is at the 1968 and 1969 reunions per Gustavino's rosters, is absent from the 1971 Muestra Grupo Si, and returns for the 2001 reunion, "El Informalismo Platense de los 60", at MUMART La Plata and the Centro Cultural Borges.

Early in the 1960s he travels for two years through Brazil and Peru, "con su companera" -- with his companion, unnamed in every source -- and in Brazil and Peru, "en contacto con otras corrientes pictoricas, su obra evoluciono hacia la figuracion". He studies wood engraving at the Escola de Belas Artes da Bahia. Dated traces: individual shows in 1964 at Galeria M. Querino, Bahia, and Galeria del Periodico Comercio, Manaus. Back in Buenos Aires he makes "figuras con madera de colores muy puros como el rojo, el azul y el amarillo" -- figures in wood of very pure colours, red, blue, yellow.

In 1966 he illustrates his first children's books: Aire Libre, by Maria Elena Walsh, and Mi amigo Gregorio. The date carries a tension (C9787): his bio says the work was done in 1966; the book as published is dated 1967 (first edition, Estrada, Buenos Aires). Print both; the work-versus-publication reading is an inference, flagged as such. Mi amigo Gregorio is unidentified beyond the title -- no author, publisher or catalogue hit. That same year he shows in "Jovenes Artistas Plasticos", La Plata; 1967-68 brings shows at Galeria Arte Nuevo and Van Riel in Buenos Aires, Federico V in La Plata, and the MNBA.

In 1969 he travels to Spain on a Museo del Prado scholarship -- Elena-only, no independent confirmation found; the Prado's published becarios lists do not reach back to 1969 -- and shows in Madrid and Seville at the Instituto de Estudios Hispano-Americano. In 1975 he settles in Sitges, the coastal town near Barcelona, and in 1980 acquires Spanish citizenship. From then on he combines painting and sculpture with children's-book illustration -- 235 books by his own count -- plus advertising and book-cover design. The publishers evidenced are La Galera and Parramon in Barcelona; the authors illustrated include Walsh (1966), Roald Dahl (El Superzorro, 1977, singled out by the Biblioteca Nacional de Espana), Nicolas Guillen (1977), Enric Larreula (1992), Pilar Ramos (2003), and Camilo Jose Cela. No death has been found; his portfolio site lists exhibitions dated 2026 and was recently updated. The honest statement is: no death found, last positive trace the actively maintained personal site with 2026 entries; do not state a death year.

The late painting, as the 2018 retrospective describes it: Metamorfosis, late 1980s to 2003, oils, watercolours and drawings, "plasma su ira ante los horrores de la dictadura argentina... un proceso sumamente elaborado y visceral, donde expresa de manera dolorosa el terror y la oscuridad de una epoca" -- a retrospective, exilic response made a decade-plus after the 1976-83 regime, not contemporary protest. Fragmentos, the sensuality and carnality of a series "inspirada en la obra que Horacio Elena habia trabajado a principio de los anos sesenta en Argentina" -- the early-60s rounded female figure recovered, thicker and cropped. Musas, watercolour on handmade paper, the work in progress at the time of the retrospective. And his 1960s drawings, reinterpreted in digital format. Two public sculptures stand in Sitges: Mujer mirando el mar, donated to the town in 2007, and Torso num. 5, 2009 (the years assigned by list order only -- flagged inference).

## M1 -- Texture as the picture: informalist matter + rounded female volume

Visit Sitges on the early work, the piece the retrospective opens with: Informalismo "caracterizado por trabajar con grandes texturas totalmente abstractos", and "en este trabajo inicial combina la figura femenina de formas redondeadas con el volumen". The mechanism: heavy material texture does the abstraction, and a rounded female figure supplies volume inside it -- figure emerging from matter, not drawn on it. Contrast with Sirabo (#640): Sirabo's figure is a flat achromatic snap against a plain ground; Elena's early figure is modelled, volume inside texture. The texture is not background; it is the medium the figure is made of.

**Takeaway:** a heavily textured, near-abstract field in which one rounded female volume slowly declares itself. A piece that paints a clean outline figure on a textured backdrop has missed the mechanism; the figure must be in the texture.

## M2 -- Pure-colour wood figure: red / blue / yellow as structure

"Realiza figuras con madera de colores muy puros como el rojo, el azul y el amarillo." (Visit Sitges) The mechanism: three unmixed primaries on wood do the structuring -- colour is not applied to a form, it is the form's division. Flat planes of pure colour on a hard, warm ground, no blending, no modelling. The wood support matters: the figure is built from colour planes the way a carpenter builds from boards. This is the closest Elena comes to his Grupo Si colleagues' flat colour, but his is figurative where theirs is geometric. The Bahia wood-engraving training sits behind it, but no source links a specific engraving to these figures -- do not merge them.

**Takeaway:** a figure built from flat planes of pure red, pure blue, pure yellow on wood; hard edges, no gradient, the colour boundaries are the drawing. A piece that shades or blends the primaries has missed the mechanism.

## M3 -- The fragment: carnality by cropping the body

The Fragmentos series: "la sensualidad y la carnalidad de la serie 'Fragmentos'... recuperar la carnalidad de la figura femenina de los anos sesenta para volver a elaborarla en fragmentos figurativos de pincelada mas gruesa." (Visit Sitges, on Mujer mirando el mar) The mechanism: the body is never shown whole -- a torso (W12 is literally a torso, the fifth), a fragment -- and the thicker brushstroke does the carnality that a complete nude would merely illustrate. Cropping plus impasto equals sensuality; completeness would kill it. The sculpture is the three-dimensional Fragmentos: the same carnality "tambien la traslada a la escultura".

**Takeaway:** show a fragment of a female body (torso, shoulder, hip) cropped by the frame, painted with a visibly thicker stroke than the rest of the picture. A piece that shows the whole body has missed the mechanism; the fragment must feel like more body, not less picture.

## M4 -- Visceral darkness: terror painted as process, not scene

Metamorfosis (late 1980s-2003): "a traves de oleos, acuarelas y dibujos, plasma su ira ante los horrores de la dictadura argentina. Es un proceso sumamente elaborado y visceral, donde expresa de manera dolorosa el terror y la oscuridad de una epoca." The operative words are proceso, elaborado, visceral: the mechanism is accumulation and working-over -- oils and watercolours and drawings, three media on one subject -- darkness built up in layers rather than depicted in a scene. No individual Metamorfosis image is described anywhere, so no motif may be invented: no hooded figures, no cells, no specific iconography.

**Takeaway:** darkness built by layering and reworking across media; a picture that feels worked-over and visceral rather than illustrative. A piece that invents a scene of the dictatorship has missed the mechanism; terror is density, not depiction.

## M5 -- The sea-gaze and the handmade ground

Two linked facts. Mujer mirando el mar is a woman looking at the sea, placed where the sea actually is (San Sebastian beach, Sitges) -- the sculpture's gaze has a real object; the viewer stands behind the gaze and shares it. And Musas is "acuarela sobre papel hecho a mano" -- watercolour on handmade paper, where the paper's own texture and absorbency are part of the image. The mechanism in both: the work borrows a real outside -- a real sea, a real paper surface -- instead of inventing its ground. The location wording differs between sources (bio: "Plaza Ermita de San Sebastian"; Visit Sitges: "playa San Sebastian") -- both are recorded, not reconciled.

**Takeaway:** a female figure seen from behind or in profile, gaze fixed on a real horizon the viewer can also see; or watercolour allowed to pool and bleed on a visibly fibrous handmade sheet, the paper doing half the drawing. A piece that invents its ground has missed the mechanism.

## M6 -- Illustration as integration: one child threading text, image, song and play

Aire Libre (W03): a single child character, Luciano, threads together poems, stories, songs and plays; patriotic symbols are shown through reproductions of artworks with full references; the supplement "Pergamino Amarillo" ("Recreo") "rescata la importancia del juego". The UNC analysis: "se plasma la concepcion del arte como integrador de lenguajes, en el que todos los generos y manifestaciones pueden acercarse". The mechanism: the illustration does not decorate a text -- it links heterogeneous materials (poem, song, play, artwork reproduction, game) into one continuous reading, with a child guide walking the reader through. The page is a small museum, not a backdrop.

**Takeaway:** an illustration that is a hinge between unlike things -- a small child figure leading the eye from a poem to a picture-within-the-picture to a game. A piece that decorates one text with one picture has missed the mechanism; the hinge is the work.

## M7 -- The return: redrawing the 1960s in the present tense

"Los dibujos que el artista realizaba en los anos 60... vuelven a ser interpretados en formato digital." (Visit Sitges) And Fragmentos and Mujer mirando el mar both explicitly recover early-1960s work ("inspirada en la obra que... habia trabajado a principio de los anos sesenta"; "recuperar la carnalidad de la figura femenina de los anos sesenta"). The mechanism is return-as-method: the same motif drawn twice, decades apart, the second version thicker, more fragmented, in a new medium -- the difference between the two versions is the work. What the digital reinterpretation changes is not stated (re-drawn? scanned and recoloured? animated?) -- do not elaborate beyond the sentence.

**Takeaway:** a motif shown in two states -- a light 1960s line drawing and its later, thicker, cropped, digital or painted reworking -- side by side, so the viewer reads the interval, not either image alone. A piece that shows one version has missed the mechanism.

## M8 -- The trip as hinge: travel rewrites the picture

The two-year Brazil/Peru trip "con su companera" is the hinge from informalism toward figuration (Visit Sitges: "en contacto con otras corrientes pictoricas, su obra evoluciono hacia la figuracion"), with the Bahia wood engraving feeding the later pure-colour wood figures. The mechanism: a journey that changes the work's language, shown as before/after -- the same hand, a different picture after the road. FLAG (C9790): this mechanism is coordinator-derived from leg 1's trip evidence, not drawn from a single observed picture. Use it only as a before/after structure: the informalist texture on one side, the figurative pure-colour wood on the other, and the road between them the only explanation offered.

**Takeaway:** two panels, same hand, one before the road and one after -- texture and rounded volume giving way to figuration in pure colour. A piece that shows only the after-state has missed the mechanism; the hinge is the change.

## Evolution: texture, the road, the book, the fragment, the sea

Early 1960s La Plata: informalist texture plus rounded female volume (M1) -- the figure already inside the matter. The two-year Brazil/Peru trip, early 1960s: the hinge toward figuration (M8), wood engraving in Bahia. Back in Buenos Aires: figuration, pure-colour wood figures (M2). 1966: the first children's illustrations, then fifty years of parallel tracks -- illustration (235 books by his own count) alongside painting and sculpture, not in sequence. Late 1980s-2003: Metamorfosis (M4), the exilic reckoning with the dictatorship, darkness as process. 2009: Fragmentos and the two public Sitges sculptures (M3), the early-60s figure recovered thicker and cropped. Around 2018: Musas, watercolour on handmade paper (M5), and the 1960s drawings reinterpreted digitally (M7). The constants: the female figure (rounded, then fragmented, then a torso, then a woman looking at the sea), pure colour, and the double life -- gallery work and children's books carried together for five decades. ## Aesthetic logic

Elena's aesthetic is the logic of the figure that refuses to leave, carried through every change of address. Pick one simple thing -- a rounded female body -- and let everything else change around it: the matter it is made of (heavy informalist texture, then pure colour on wood, then thick impasto, then bronze or stone in a public square), the scale it is shown at (a fragment, a torso, a woman against a real sea), and the audience it is made for (a gallery wall, a child opening a school reader). The Vision teaching is the through-line: figure against ground, closure, good form -- but where Sirabo took it into the sign and Puente into the system, Elena took it into the picture a child can walk through. The illustration is not a lesser half of the career; it is the same mechanism in a different room -- one child threading poem, song, play and picture into a single reading (M6) is the same hinge as one figure threading texture, colour and fragment into a single body. That is why the double life lasted fifty years: the two tracks are one practice, the figure kept alive in two places at once. An Elena that paints a pretty nude has kept the subject and sacked the mechanism; the figure must emerge from texture, be divided by pure colour, be cropped into a fragment, or be set against a real horizon -- and the return must be visible, the 1960s drawing redrawn thicker, because the interval is the work.

The contrast with Sirabo is the study's spine, and it is a contrast inside one friendship, between two men of the same city who shared a group, a teacher and a bar. Sirabo's generator is a sign repeated until it becomes a language: the figure-ground snap, the ambiguous depth, the leaning stack, the mirrored warning. Elena's generator is a figure repeated until it becomes a life: the rounded volume in texture, the pure-colour wood figure, the cropped fragment, the woman looking at the sea. Sirabo stayed on the plane and made the plane a sign; Elena left the plane for the book and the square and made the figure a companion -- for a child reader, for a passer-by on a beach. Both asked what Vision teaching was for, and Sirabo answered with perception, Elena with accompaniment. That is why the two studies belong together, and why Elena is the complement, not the follower: same generation, same city, same rooms, the same 1960 founding night -- and from that shared room, two opposite mechanisms.
