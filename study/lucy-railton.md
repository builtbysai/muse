# Lucy Railton: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-01. ig-artist-study #254.

**Site:** lucyrailton.com; @lucyrailton (15,375 followers, public, no verified badge). British cellist, composer, curator; London to Berlin.

Lucy Railton is the cellist of the Kit Downes duo Subaerial and the composer of Blue Veil, a solo cello album in just intonation with no electronics. Her practice treats tuning, rooms, and loudspeaker rings as the actual composition, and the cello as the sensor that reports on them.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Kit Downes study (#253): Downes's deepest duo partner, the cello on Subaerial (SN Variations SN9, 2021), a guest on his Dreamlife of Debris (ECM, 2019), and the owner of the richest visual world in the Downes-adjacent network: acousmonium rings, cathedrals, smoke stages, and trios where video and light outrank the musician.

## M1. The acousmonium as instrument

Forma (Portraits GRM, 2020) is an 8-channel, 23-minute multichannel tape and cello piece. She premiered it at the Akousma Festival, Maison de la Radio, Paris (2019-06-01), seated alone on a bench at the center of the hall, ringed by horn loudspeakers on stands (red and white horns), grand piano to her left, Hammond organ to her right, cello leaning by the bench (f05). The caption names it plainly: "Premiering my new multichannel piece 'Forma'." She went on to perform it on the acousmonium at Elevate Graz (2020-02-08), Le Mans Sonore (2022-01-23) and Sonic Acts (2022-10-08). Les Portails (2024) was composed the same way: f17 shows the GRM studio desk, a DAW session of colored multitrack regions, captioned "creating new work ('Les Portails') for the acousmonium" (2024-12-23); it premiered at INA GRM's Presences Electronique, November 2024. The mechanism: speaker diffusion treated as performance rather than playback. She composes FOR the ring and then plays inside it; the number and placement of speakers shape the piece as much as any note.

**Takeaway:** spatial diffusion as playable material; the performer inside the ring, not in front of it.

## M2. Just intonation as compositional engine

Blue Veil (Ideologic Organ, 18 April 2025) is 40 minutes, 7 tracks, solo cello in just intonation, no electronics or guests aside from barely audible sine waves used to heighten the low end. Recorded at Eglise du Saint-Esprit, Paris. She described the experience of playing it as "standing next to a guitar amp" (Stephan Kunze interview, quoted at exystence.net); she responds in real time to the sudden harmonies of her cello (Pitchfork, quoted at exystence.net). Her own formulation: the work studies "the interaction of sound (tone, colour, intonation, timbre), space and perception" through "tuning practices and long-durational form" (nottinghamcontemporary.org). Co-produced by Kali Malone and Stephen O'Malley, from the Does Spring Hide Its Joy sessions (three hour-long variations with Malone's tuned sine oscillators and O'Malley's guitar, composed in empty Berlin halls, March to May 2020). The mechanism: the overtone series does the composing; the musician steers by listening. One caveat from the research leg, kept here: the exact tuning schemes per track are not public, so this study claims just intonation and overtone-driven form, not specific ratios.

**Takeaway:** tuning as engine: build the scale, and the harmonies write themselves.

## M3. The trio show as a triangle of shifting priorities

Not A Word From Me (premiered CTM Berlin, February 2025) is a live show built from three disciplines: cello/electronics (Railton), video (Rebecca Salvadori), light (Charlie Hope). The title comes from a Corner Dancer track about withholding communication. The Crack Magazine interview (2025-10-07) is unusually explicit about process: only eight minutes of film in the whole show; blocks of colour and black screens used as material; lighting worked structurally and sculpturally, with reflector structures giving a membrane-like quality; projections serving as light source; Railton moving between stage positions (mic and amp, violin, behind the desk). "We're really challenging the audience to be involved in the performance... by asking them to tune their perception." The corpus gives the images: f14, the CTM premiere, a tiny silhouetted cellist before a giant orange-red screen carrying white projected text ("I was thinking about the light spinning on its axis / locked in its own cycle / everything can move in relation to that"); f16, Culturgest 2026, her body nearly dissolved into amber stage smoke (lights by Charlie Hope). No single discipline accompanies the others; leadership rotates.

**Takeaway:** the richest watchable system in the corpus: three disciplines taking turns leading, nobody the accompanist.

## M4. Venue as instrument

A thirteen-year pattern of sacred and raw spaces: Skalholt Cathedral, Iceland (the Subaerial recording, September 2017); Eglise du Saint-Esprit, Paris (Blue Veil recording and debut, 2025-04-09); Yodobashi Church, Tokyo, its soaring triangular concrete lattice in blue-violet haze (Does Spring Hide Its Joy, f11, 2023-06-12); Nottingham's City of Caves (2022-12-11); a raw concrete parking structure for a Glenn Branca soundcheck crowd (f03, 2013-07-29). And 5 S-Bahn (Boomkat, 2020): recorded in Berlin lockdown with mics on her back wall, responding to sounds outside the window. The mechanism: each room is an oscillator she plays by choosing it; the reverb tail becomes part of the composition. Read honestly: that she chooses these spaces for their acoustics is the research leg's inference; the observed fact is the pattern, running unbroken from 2013 to 2025.

**Takeaway:** the room is an oscillator you play by choosing it.

## M5. Improvisation as material, selection as composition

Subaerial was edited from a 3-hour improvisation session to 42 minutes, with the chosen passages left untouched (acloserlisten.com). Peter Zinovieff asked her to improvise on objects he placed before her and used those improvisations as his compositional vocabulary (thequietus.com; RFG Inventions for Cello and Computer, PAN, 2020; debuted at Berlin Atonal). RFG is Rowntree's Fruit Gums: they structured the piece through sweets. Her own line: "I rarely make music according to a specific concept, theme or narrative... I'm really interested in the material of sound and the emotional content of that material" (Crack). The mechanism is two-stage: generate freely, then edit ruthlessly. The composition IS the selection. One gap kept honest: the exact Subaerial editing criteria are not documented.

**Takeaway:** generate freely, edit ruthlessly; the composition is the selection.

## M6. Extreme dynamics as material

Her words: "Exploring extremes is what's always excited me, you know, silence obliterated by noise. That's when I feel most creative. I'm as excited by the sound of machinery as I am by music." (Crack). The corpus anchors the poles. Paradise 94 (Modern Love, 2018, LOVE 108LP), her solo debut: amplified cello and industrial noise, collaborations with Beatrice Dillon and Kit Downes; filigree autobiographical collage on one side, raw phased cello glissandi on the other. Blue Veil (2025): near-motionless just-intonation drone. Between them, RFG Inventions (cello plus computer, PAN, 2020). The through-line across the poles is the research leg's synthesis; the poles and the quote are evidenced. The mechanism: the whole span from near-silence to industrial noise treated as one instrument, with the poles explored as separate albums years apart.

**Takeaway:** the whole span from near-silence to industrial noise is one instrument.

## M7. Dematerializing the performer

A consistent recent visual language across the corpus: the Blue Veil cover dissolves her face behind soft blue light (f13, 2025-03-05); the Culturgest performance photo dissolves her body into amber smoke (f16, 2026-07-30); the Not A Word From Me stage reduces her to a silhouette before a giant glowing text screen (f14). The body is present but optically thinned: light, haze and projection do the visual work while the cello does the sonic work. Earlier frames show the opposite (f02, the playful 2013 promo shot with paper-flower headpiece), so this is a direction she arrived at, not a starting point. Honest reading: that this is a deliberate aesthetic program is supported by its consistency across three different photographers and two years, but it remains an inference.

**Takeaway:** presence through absence: the performer as light event.

## Evolution

- **2008-2013: the organizer.** Royal Academy of Music, London (graduated 2008); New England Conservatory, Boston, where improvisation "broke" the ethics of classical music for her and changed her direction (bigearsfestival.org). 2008: founds the new-music series Kammer Klang at Cafe Oto, curates it for ten years. 2013: co-founds the London Contemporary Music Festival with Aisha Orazbayeva, Sam Mackay, Igor Toronyi-Lalic; co-directs to 2016; programs Catherine Lamb, Philip Corner, Glenn Branca, Ellen Fullman, Ed Atkins. Session work: Bonobo, Jamie Cullum, Bat for Lashes, Laurel Halo. The corpus opens here: team photos behind an opened piano (f00), a Branca crowd in a parking structure (f03), an Ed Atkins screening (f01).
- **2014-2018: the ferment gap.** Absent from the corpus; the web fills it: Bach trio recordings (2012, later Three Or One); the Skalholt Cathedral session (2017); then Paradise 94 (Modern Love, 2018): amplified cello vs industrial noise, the first solo statement.
- **2019-2021: the multichannel turn.** GRM residency and the Forma premiere at Akousma (2019); RFG Inventions with Zinovieff (PAN, 2020); 5 S-Bahn (Boomkat, 2020), the lockdown window piece; Lament in Three Parts (Takuroku, 2020); Subaerial (SN Variations SN9, 2021) with Downes; Three Or One (ECM New Series, 2021) with Fred Thomas and Orazbayeva.
- **2022-2023: the network widens.** Fragments of Reincarnation with Michiko Ogawa (Another Timbre, 2023): one 45-minute movement for sho, Hammond organ and cello, combining different tuning systems. Does Spring Hide Its Joy with Kali Malone and Stephen O'Malley (Ideologic Organ, 2023): three hour-long variations from empty Berlin halls. Corner Dancer (Modern Love, 2023): cello, viella, Buchla, TR-808, fan, horsehair whips; "harmonizing with her cello through scrims of noise."
- **2025-2026: resolution.** Blue Veil (Ideologic Organ, 18 April 2025): the first solo cello album, 7 tracks in just intonation, Paris church, produced by Malone and O'Malley; described as a career resolution of her tuning practice (bigearsfestival.org). Not A Word From Me premieres at CTM (2025); Les Portails at INA GRM (2024); the Venice Music Biennale lineup (2025); film scores (False Positive with Yair Elazar Glotman, A24, 2021; Salon de Belleza, 2026).

## Aesthetic logic

Everything in Railton's practice points away from the instrument and toward what the instrument reveals: the tuning system (Blue Veil), the room (Saint-Esprit, Skalholt, Yodobashi), the diffusion ring (Forma), the audience's own perception (Not A Word From Me). The cello is a probe, not a voice. Improvisation generates the raw material; selection writes the score. The body withdraws while the light stays. And she curated before she composed: the networks she built at Cafe Oto and LCMF over a decade became her collaborators. The goal is never expression. It is to make the audience hear the room they are sitting in.
