## M1. Reconstruction, not design

What it is: a costume designer's work is from story and culture, and is more similar to reconstruction rather than design, in Tosi's own words, and the designer rebuilds a world that existed, and invention is allowed only where the record is silent, which is Medea, and the research system is paintings first, because a conversation piece or a genre painting shows both the architecture of clothes and how they were worn, the attitude, gait and gestures that expressed class and gender, and then drawing details by hand to learn the period, and then fabric archaeology with Umberto Tirelli from 1960 and 1963, and the watchable behaviour is a garment that looks as if it was found in an attic and not made in a workshop, and a film whose costumes are so exact they are routinely mislabelled online as authentic period garments, which happens to The Leopard and to Sparrow thirty years later, and the audience must not notice the design, only the person.

**Takeaway:** the garment must be rebuilt from evidence, and a period piece that invents its silhouette from a mood board has not used the mechanism.

**Takeaway:** the ageing must be a timeline of operations, and a distressed texture applied in one pass has not used the mechanism.

## M3. The narrow loom rule
