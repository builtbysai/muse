## Evolution: five phases, and the afterlife

PHASE 1 - THE DEBUNKER, 1983-1989: the thesis on fascism's campaign against the mafia, built on the papers of Cesare Mori, Mussolini's "iron prefect". The first line of the resulting book asserts the mafia is an idea, not an organisation. Leonardo Sciascia reviews it in Corriere della Sera on Saturday 10 January 1987 and Monday 26 January 1987, and uses its thesis against the "professionals of anti-Mafia". Steinberg in the London Review of Books, 17 August 1989: Duggan "learned how to debunk myth from Denis Mack Smith". Early renown, then the thesis is undermined by the revelations of the repentant mafioso Tommaso Buscetta, the one serious reversal of his career, and he never disowned the argument, which "continues to raise important theoretical questions" (Guardian obituary).

PHASE 2 - THE POPULAR HISTORIAN, 1986-1994: he condenses Finley and Mack Smith's Sicily into a single volume in 1986, and A Concise History of Italy (1994) returns him to his first love, medieval history, and becomes his most widely read book, 40,000 copies sold, used in schools, translated into Spanish, Italian, Chinese, Polish, Japanese and more. The pattern is set: big subjects, general readers, lucid prose, "moving, like his mentor Mack Smith, from one large subject to another" (Reading obituary).

PHASE 3 - THE BIOGRAPHER, 1990-2002: a decade of archival research in several countries for Crispi. The question shifts from "what was the mafia?" to "how is a nation made?": Crispi's career hinges on effecting "moral" as well as "material" cohesion (REF). The instruments of nation-building are named, collective memory, national icons, the army and schools as educators, expansionist foreign policy, and war as a tool of national integration, which "challenged long-held assumptions about the character of the Italian liberal state" (REF) and drew a furious objection from Giuseppe Giarrizzo in L'Acropoli, August 2002. Il Giornale on Sunday 29 July 2001 tried to enlist the book for the claim that only a charismatic leader could unite Italy; Duggan replied on Saturday 22 September 2001. The English biography, 777 pages, is the first full-length academic biography of Crispi; the Italian edition, Creare la nazione, runs to 995 pages.

PHASE 4 - THE SYNTHESIST, 2001-2008: The Force of Destiny, research 2001-2006, extends the nation-building question across the whole sweep from 1796 to Berlusconi, inspired by Alberto Banti, the title taken from Verdi's opera La Forza del Destino, which Duggan loved to work to. The tone darkens: the conclusion is "infused with melancholy" (Modern Italy obituary), the enterprise of nation-building possibly counter-productive. The Italian reception, 2008-2011, is a public event: an hour-long debate on RaiUno on the evening of Friday 5 December 2008, a Rome presentation in January 2009 by Giuliano Amato and Tullio De Mauro debated by some forty historians, a Turin debate led by Ezio Mauro, a Milan debate led by Sergio Romano, 22 Italian newspaper reviews, and Ernesto Galli della Loggia in Corriere della Sera on 7 January 2011 calling the book damaging to Italy's international standing, a "caricature" of the country's history. ## Aesthetic logic

Duggan's work is beautiful in the way a debunking is beautiful, and that is the point. Mack Smith's mechanism is the sentence landing like a verdict and the scene freezing on it; Gramsci's is the fragment arriving out of order and the idea returning in a different notebook; Duggan's is the archive opening and a person stepping out of it, and the beauty, where there is any, is in the patience: ten years in the archives for one life, hundreds of diaries for one regime, one prefect's filing cabinet for one campaign. His aesthetic is the prefect's report, the street sign, the letter to the Duce, the diary entry, the schoolteacher's life told in three lines, the tower that rises while its foundations thin. He begins by saying a thing does not exist, the mafia is an idea, and ends by showing how an idea is lived, the Duce's face touched for luck on a wall. The melancholy is the through-line: the harder the nation is made, the less it is believed, and the historian's job is to watch the making, document the believing, and record the gap.

**Takeaway:** let the archive open and the person step out, and the piece should be patient, documentary, and melancholy, the effort rising as the belief falls.
