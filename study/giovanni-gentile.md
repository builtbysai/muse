The watchable behaviour is a figure that exists only while it moves; the moment it stops, it thins out and is gone; nothing pre-exists the act, the stage is built by the acting.

**Takeaway:** keep the figure moving, and the piece should let the stage be built by the acting.

## Evolution

Phase 1, Formation, 1875-1903. Castelvetrano, Trapani, Pisa, 1893, Scuola Normale, one of four exceptional students, Florence perfezionamento, Campobasso, Naples. Teachers: Alessandro D'Ancona, Amedeo Crivellucci, Donato Jaja, the last Spaventa's disciple, who gives him his vocation. Laurea 1897, Rosmini e Gioberti, 1898. Meets Croce 1896; the correspondence runs 1896-1923, nearly a letter a day at its height. La filosofia di Marx, 1899, Dal Genovesi al Galluppi, 1903, La Critica founded 1903, first issue Tuesday 20 January 1903. The joint revolt against positivism, a battle the two friends also fight against their own earlier selves. Marries Erminia Nudi, Thursday 9 May 1901; six children: Teresa, 1902, Federico, 1904, twins Gaetano and Giovanni, 1906, Benedetto, 1908, Fortunato, 1910.

Phase 2, Palermo system-building, 1906-1916. Professor of history of philosophy at Palermo 1906-1914, ordinary professor 27 March 1910. The villa circle at Giuseppe Amato Pojero's, the Biblioteca filosofica: actualism is born here, first in Le forme assolute dello spirito, 1909, then the memoir L'atto del pensare come atto puro, December 1911, then La riforma della dialettica hegeliana, 1913, and the Sommario di pedagogia, 1913-14. Croce's public letter in La Voce, Friday 17 October 1913, declares the dissent; Gentile replies 11 December 1913; Croce rejoins in December. Pisa 1914-1919, theoretical philosophy, succeeding Jaja; the war years produce Teoria generale dello spirito come atto puro, 1916, I fondamenti della filosofia del diritto, 1916, and the first volume of the Sistema di logica, 1917. The philosophy is complete in its bones by 1916; everything after is application, politics, or consequence.

Phase 3, War and the turn to politics, 1914-1922. Interventionist, at first with strong doubts, then convinced the war is a call of the Risorgimento's destiny; Croce is against it, and the difference hardens into the second great break. War as the completion of the Risorgimento; support for D'Annunzio's Fiume 1919. Rome chair of history of philosophy, called Wednesday 24 October 1917, Caporetto day, in post from 1918; Giornale critico della filosofia italiana founded 1920, the philosophical separation from Croce made institutional. Rome city councillor 1920, assessore supplente for antiquities and fine arts 1921. Mazzini is revalued: from inept dreamer to apostle, "maestro dell'odierno fascismo". Signs the 1919 Gruppo Nazionale Liberale romano manifesto with Luigi Einaudi and Gioacchino Volpe.

Phase 4, Minister and fascist ideologue, 1922-1929. Minister of Public Education Tuesday 31 October 1922 to 1 July 1924, on Croce's recommendation, Croce had refused; senator from Sunday 5 November 1922 to 5 August 1943. The Riforma Gentile, 1923: five decrees, the liceo classico at the peak, state exams, religious instruction in elementary schools, Italian-only schooling, the limits on women teachers. Joins the PNF in May 1923 with the letter to Mussolini of Thursday 31 May 1923, "Mi son dovuto persuadere che il liberalismo... non e oggi rappresentato in Italia dai liberali... ma per l'appunto, da Lei". Resigns Saturday 14 June 1924, four days after the Matteotti murder, Tuesday 10 June 1924, motivated by political opportunity, not protest, Sasso. Chairs the Commissione dei Quindici, later dei Diciotto, for reform of the Statuto Albertino. Grand Council member 1925-1929. Manifesto degli intellettuali fascisti, Tuesday 21 April 1925. Heads two constitutional commissions 1925. Che cosa e il fascismo, 1925; La dottrina del fascismo, 1932. Promoter of the 1931 university oath. Publicly opposes the Lateran Concordat, Monday 11 February 1929, and loses.

Phase 5, The encyclopedia and cultural power, 1925-1943. The Institute founded Wednesday 18 February 1925; the Enciclopedia Italiana, thirty-five volumes 1929-1937, is the work to which he gives, Sasso says, the liveliest part of his energy, "quasi per intero". Normale: regio commissario 1928-1932, director 1932-1943, dismissed June 1936 by Education Minister Cesare Maria De Vecchi, reinstated by Mussolini. Istituto Nazionale Fascista di Cultura founded 1925, president to 1937. Bocconi vice-president, 1934-1944 per en and ca wikis, see C6. Istituto Italiano di Studi Germanici inaugurated 1932, president 1934; IsMEO inaugurated 1933, president; Istituto mazziniano Genoa 1934; Domus Galilaeana president 1941; Centro nazionale di studi manzoniani regio commissario 1937, president 1938; director of Nuova Antologia; Lincei national member 1932. Acquires the Sansoni publishing house 1936, after the break with Laterza. The school disperses: Carlini toward Catholic spiritualism, Spirito toward corporativism and problematicism; Croce re-emerges. The 1938 racial laws: private help, public silence. Silence in the war years 1940-43.

Phase 6, Late turn and death, 1943-1944. Son Giovannino, a valued physicist, dies Sunday 30 March 1941, the same calendar day, three years later, on which the death threats will arrive. La mia religione, Florence, Tuesday 9 February 1943: "Io sono cristiano... io sono cattolico... dal giugno del 1875." Discorso agli Italiani, Thursday 24 June 1943. Fall of Mussolini Sunday 25 July 1943; the letter to Leonardo Severi, and Severi's harsh reply, Wednesday 4 August 1943; Gentile resigns the Normale directorship. Writes Genesi e struttura della societa at Troghi, August-September 1943, Avvertenza 25 September 1943; shows the manuscript to the antifascist Mario Manlio Rossi: "I vostri amici possono uccidermi ora se vogliono. Il mio lavoro nella vita e concluso." First refuses Biggini's RSI offer; meets Mussolini at Lake Garda, Wednesday 17 November 1943, for about two hours; adheres to the RSI; president of the Reale Accademia d'Italia from November 1943, transferred to Florence, Palazzo Serristori; revives the Lincei; directs Nuova Antologia; speech Sunday 19 March 1944 praising Hitler as "Condottiero della grande Germania"; Ricostruire, Corriere della Sera, Tuesday 28 December 1943. Assassinated Saturday 15 April 1944.

Posthumous afterlife. "Rimozione", removal, after 1944, attenuating only in recent decades. The first studi gentiliani conference, Santa Croce, 15-17 April 1955. The Fondazione Giovanni Gentile per gli studi filosofici, founded immediately postwar to publish the Opere complete at Sansoni, directed first by the son Federico; seat from 1982 at the Sapienza philosophy faculty, president Gennaro Sasso. Opere complete: plan 55 volumes in 4 sections plus Epistolario I-XIV; Sansoni from 1955, continued by Le Lettere from 1987; 49 volumes published by 2000, DBI, Opera Omnia completed 1999. Augusto Del Noce is the chief revaluator: Giovanni Gentile. Per un'interpretazione filosofica della storia contemporanea, Il Mulino, Bologna 1990, posthumous, presented Rimini 26 August 1990, Gentile rediscovered in the 1960s at his greatest decline; actualism as a philosophy of praxis on the Marxian model, the idealist version; Gramsci's genesis traced to Gentilian actualism rather than Crocean historicism; Gentile as the terminal philosopher of modern immanentism. Gennaro Sasso: between the philosophy of the act in act and fascism "non c'e, ne ci puo essere, alcun nesso", there is not, nor can there be, any link; the political adhesion was philosophical "a parole" but "nelle cose no", in words but in things no, Le due Italie di Giovanni Gentile, 1998. Norberto Bobbio: a "periodo d'infatuazione gentiliana" 1927-1931, detachment culminating in the RSI adhesion; the philosophy "una cattiva filosofia", a bad philosophy, but no moral condemnation of the man. 1994: a postage stamp for the 50th anniversary of the death, the only leading figure of the regime so honoured by the Republic. Istituto di studi gentiliani, Rome, 1980s, president Antonio Fede. Mimmo Franzinelli, Il filosofo in camicia nera, Mondadori 2021. Emanuele Severino esteems the philosophy; Nicola Abbagnano called him "forse l'ultima piu vigorosa figura del Romanticismo europeo", perhaps the last most vigorous figure of European Romanticism.

## Aesthetic logic

Gentile's aesthetic is the act made visible, and the visible is always a passage, never a thing. Nothing in his work sits still long enough to be painted: the figure exists only while it moves, the fact is already a corpse, the portrait walks out of its frame, the book is written by thousands of hands at once, the signature erases what it records, the room goes dark instead of the body. His is an art of thresholds crossed in one direction, the gate that sorts, the letter that shortens, the voice that knows it will not be obeyed, the act that stops mid-sentence, and the piece that follows him must therefore be built from verbs that end, not from images that stay. The palette follows the same law: the schoolroom in chalk and ink, the decree in black on white, the encyclopedia in paper cream and printer's ink, the manifesto in newsprint grey, the villa circle in warm lamplight, the Campidoglio in stone and dusk, the Salviatino road in late-morning light that never finishes arriving. Colour must arrive as a state change, red draining to gold, grey mass splitting back into currents, the third panel swallowing the other two, never as decoration laid on top. His time is the present that never concludes, so the loop must never resolve: the figure must keep thinning, the names must keep accumulating, the crowd must keep answering from inside the chest, and the end must come from outside, from hands that do not think, because inside the act there is no ending, only the next gesture becoming the ground for the next.

The variants: it.wikipedia's photo caption gives directorship 1928-36 and 1937-43; sns.it says director "from 1936". Resolution: De Vecchi dismissed Gentile in June 1936 and Mussolini reinstated him, DBI Sasso, which explains the apparent 1936-37 gap; Torrini's "prima commissario e poi fino al 1944 quasi ininterrottamente direttore", almost uninterruptedly, covers both the 1936 dismissal and the August 1943 resignation.

C6. Bocconi vice-presidency: 1930 vs 1934-1944. ADOPT 1934-1944, with the 1930 variant noted. en.wikipedia infobox and ca.wikipedia employer list give 1934-1944; it.wikipedia body says "Nel 1930 diventa vicepresidente". The DBI mentions Bocconi among his activities without dates. Two sources against one; the 1930 date may reflect an earlier board role.

C7. Academy of Italy presidency: 25 July 1943 vs November 1943. ADOPT November 1943. it.wikipedia and Le Lettere give November 1943; DBI Sasso: nominated president after the November 1943 meeting with Mussolini, the Academy having been transferred to Florence. en.wikipedia's infobox date, 25 July 1943, is the day Luigi Federzoni's presidency ended, the fall of Mussolini, not the day Gentile took office.

C8. Doctrine of Fascism authorship: Gentile part 1 and Mussolini part 2 vs Gentile and Volpe. ADOPT the standard attribution: Gentile wrote part 1, "Idee Fondamentali", Mussolini part 2, "Dottrina politica e sociale", the whole signed by Mussolini alone, it.wikipedia, Torrini: the entry "Fascismo", "scritta da lui, ma firmata da Mussolini". de and nl wikis say Gentile and Gioacchino Volpe were the authors, no Treccani source supports Volpe's authorship.

C9. Fanciullacci death: 17 July 1944 vs 1947. ADOPT Monday 17 July 1944, en.wikipedia, Bruno Fanciullacci page. The 1947 date from thisdayinhistory.ai is wrong.

C10. Date of the killing: 15 April 1944, not 14 April. ADOPT Saturday 15 April 1944. Agreed by DBI Sasso, Torrini, en and it wikis, EBSCO. Historica Fandom's "executed on 14 April" is wrong.

C11. Emilio Gentile dates: id.wikipedia contradicts itself, infobox Bojano 31 August 1946, text Bologna 4 June 1946. ADOPT Bojano, Saturday 31 August 1946, it and en wikis. Do not use id.wikipedia for Emilio's dates.

C12. en.wikipedia phases list: "definition of Actual Idealism in his work Theory of the Pure Act (1903)". WRONG. The 1903 work is La rinascita dell'idealismo, Naples prolusione, 1903; the Teoria generale dello spirito come atto puro is 1916. Do not use 1903 for the Teoria.

C13. Rome chair: called 24 October 1917 vs in post 1918 vs 11 November 1917. ADOPT called Wednesday 24 October 1917, Caporetto day, Torrini, in post from 1918, DBI Sasso: "nel 1918 egli passava all'Universita di Roma". en.wikipedia's 11 November 1917 is supported by neither Treccani entry and is not used.

C14. Minister resignation: 14 June 1924 vs 1 July 1924. ADOPT resignation presented Saturday 14 June 1924, four days after the Matteotti murder, Tuesday 10 June 1924, DBI Sasso, formal end of tenure Tuesday 1 July 1924, en and it wikis. Successor: the liberal Alessandro Casati, a great friend of Croce. Motive per Sasso: political opportunity, to clear Mussolini's path, not protest, against Seton-Watson's reading.

C15. Rome theoretical philosophy chair: 1925 vs 1926. ADOPT 1925. DBI Sasso: in 1925 he passed to the chair of theoretical philosophy left free by Bernardino Varisco. en.wikipedia's 1926 is not supported by the DBI.

C16. Genesi e struttura della societa publication: 1944 vs 1946. ADOPT 1946, posthumous, Sansoni. Spanio, Treccani: "pubblicata postuma, nel 1946"; EBSCO bibliography: "first published in Italian in 1946"; archive.org holds the 1946 Sansoni scan; it and en wikis agree. The DBI's own works list gives "Firenze 1944", an error in the DBI list, 1944 may reflect the year the manuscript was completed, September 1943, or a printing confusion. The 1946 date is adopted everywhere in these notes.

C17. PNF enrollment: May 1923 vs June 1923. ADOPT May 1923. DBI Sasso: "Nel maggio 1923... il G. si iscriveva al Partito nazionale fascista"; the adhesion letter to Mussolini is dated Thursday 31 May 1923. EBSCO's "did not officially join the Fascist Party until June, 1923" is not supported by the Treccani entries.

C18. Che cosa e il fascismo: 1924 vs 1925. ADOPT 1925. Lecture at the Salone dei Cinquecento, Florence, 8 March 1925; Vallecchi, Florence 1925, it.wikipedia, search results. EBSCO's "(1924; what is Fascism?)" is wrong.

C19. Racial laws 1938: "openly criticized the government" vs public silence. ADOPT Sasso: no public dissociation. EBSCO claims Gentile "openly criticized the government in 1938, when it inaugurated a series of anti-Semitic decrees". DBI Sasso says the opposite: "niente egli disse in quegli anni in difesa di provvedimenti che non potevano non ripugnargli profondamente, in pubblico non se ne dissocio", while his private interventions for Jewish colleagues are documented, see M17. EBSCO's claim is unsupported and is not used.

C20. EBSCO: "appointment to the University of Naples" in 1903. WRONG. Gentile held no Naples university chair. He taught at the liceo Vittorio Emanuele in Naples from November 1900, obtained the libera docenza in theoretical philosophy in 1902, and pedagogy 1903, and took an appointment at the Istituto superiore di magistero Suor Orsola Benincasa in 1904, DBI Sasso. Do not use EBSCO's Naples appointment.

C21. DBI volume 53 publication year: 1999 vs 2000. ADOPT 2000. Treccani itself and it and en wikis give volume 53, 2000; de.wikipedia cites "Rom 1999". The Treccani imprint is authoritative.
