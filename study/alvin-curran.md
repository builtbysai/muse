## M1. The found sound as material: the world is already music

Any sound in the world is already music, and the composer's job is to gather it, not to invent it, and Curran says it plainly, I realized music is just sound, any sound, all sound, everywhere, all the time, and I began to collect environmental sounds because to me, they were, de facto, MUSIC, moreover, music that belonged to everyone. The gathering is the work: years of going everywhere, looking for stuff, staying up at night and waiting for some sound that I knew was out there, and the list is flocks of birds taking off, water, wind, frogs, cicadas, bees, love-making, children at play, junk objects, and above all raucous Italy. Natural History of 1982 presents the unedited field recordings with no composition at all, the purest form of the mechanism, and the Maritime Rites foghorns are indigenous American found music par excellence. **Takeaway:** go out and listen for the sound that is already there, and the piece is the gathering. Against the chain: Rzewski also took found material, M1, but his found material is a tune everyone knows, a song with words and a history, and Curran's found material is a sound nobody owns, a foghorn or a frog, and the difference is between a tune you borrow and a sound you collect, and Cage also took the world as music, but Cage's world is a silence you frame, and Curran's world is a noise you gather, and the difference is between a frame and a basket.

## M3. The contact mic on junk: any vibrating object is an instrument

## M16. The new common practice: polystylism as a principle

The refusal of a single style, stated as a theory, and Curran says it plainly, in the new common practice, you can do anything, you can speak any language, and you can speak multiple languages, and my thoughts concerning the new common practice came about due to my unexpected teaching activity that began in 1990 at Mills College, and his own bio says his music-making embraces all the contradictions, composed and improvised, tonal and atonal, maximal and minimal, in a serene dialectical encounter. The behaviour is just when you think you have figured out what is going on in one of his pieces it totally changes direction: a Feldman-esque harmonic landscape turns into a Thelonious Monk cover, a formal trio for violin, piano and percussion turns into a chaotic free-for-all with toys and theatrics, and I find that when you really start to look around, you do not see any distinctions anymore, all of these techniques, all of these languages, all of these alphabets, all of these worlds of sound, are available to everybody now, and the engine underneath is layering, we are obsessively polyphonic, we think in layering, many, many, many different materials going on simultaneously to create magical complexities or magical simplicities, and that is the Carter lesson, kept, while the Carter style was abandoned. **Takeaway:** speak every language at once, and the change of mind is the piece. ## Evolution

## Aesthetic logic

The world is the material, and the room is the instrument, and the tune is the guest, and that is the logic that runs through all sixteen mechanisms, and the piece is not an object but a place you enter, and the found sound is the place's weather, and the tape collage is the place's map, and the contact mic is the place's ear, and the foghorn is the place's voice, and the radio is the place's road, and the piano cycle is the place's walk, and the sampler is the place's shelf, and the shofar is the place's memory, and the computer is the place's clock, and the crowd is the place's population, and the garden is the place itself, and the detuned piano is the place's flaw, and the multiplied voice is the place's weather, and the new common practice is the place's law, that every language may be spoken here. The early MEV years are the same logic in its loudest form, a room so open that anyone must play, and the solo tape years are the logic turned inward, a garden you wander in alone, and the radio years are the logic stretched across a continent, six rooms that cannot hear each other, and the piano years are the logic at its simplest, one person at a piano playing a tune anyone could learn, and the large public works are the logic at its largest, a city as an instrument, and the music is not about him, it is about what happens between the sound and the place, and between the player and the room, and between the tune and the noise, and when the situation changed, the music changed with it, all the way down to the notes, but the world never left the room, and the room never left the world.

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. ROME ARRIVAL: 1964 vs 1965. Curran's own CV of 2024 and official bio both say living in Rome since 1964. The Oteri interview says the Berlin year was 1965 and after that year in Berlin in 65, I hopped in Joel Chadabe's Volkswagen and we drove to Rome. The MACRO exhibition text and the Center for New Music say since 1965 and by 1965. The 1964 date in his own CV most likely counts the Berlin DAAD year or is a rounding. USE 1965 for the Rome arrival, and mention 1964 only as the Berlin year. Fatal to since 1964 as an arrival date.

C2. "STUDIED WITH MILTON BABBITT" IS WRONG FOR CURRAN. The Center for New Music says he studied with Milton Babbitt and Elliott Carter in the early 60s. His own bio names Ron Nelson, Mel Powell and Elliott Carter. The Oteri interview is explicit: I was a student of Elliott Carter. Richard was a student of Mel Powell. Frederic had studied with Milton Babbitt. Babbitt was Rzewski's teacher, not Curran's. Do not repeat the Center for New Music line. Fatal to any sentence naming Babbitt as Curran's teacher.

C3. MILLS APPOINTMENT: 1980 vs 1991. The Center for New Music says in 1980, Curran was named the Darius Milhaud Professor of Composition at Mills College. His own bio says from 1991 to 2006 was the Milhaud Professor of Composition at Mills College, and AllMusic agrees, beginning in 1991. The 1980 date would overlap his Accademia years of 1975-1980 and is unsupported anywhere else. USE 1991-2006. Fatal to Mills from 1980.

C4. INNER CITIES START: 1991 vs 1993. The Center for New Music says the series was begun 1991. Wikipedia says since 1993, the Room40 track list dates Inner Cities 1 to 1993, and Gabriella Smart writes Alvin commenced No 1 in 1993.
