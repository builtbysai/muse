## The question: what does a rule draw when nobody needs to read it?

Govindarajan's systems are open at the far end: the picture is not finished until someone else can enter it. McWhae's systems are closed at both ends. There is no dataset, no user, no second rendering, no lesson -- only a rule, repeated hundreds of times until a picture appears. That is the whole question he hands the gallery, and it is the opposite pole from #822 on purpose: if a picture needs no one to read it, what makes it worth looking at? His answer, across every phase of the work, is the same: one simple rule, one distinctive mark, repeated at a scale where the repetition itself becomes the picture. The rule is never hidden and never complicated -- a flow field, a spiral, a packing, a set of parallel lines that turn at right angles. The interest lives in what the repetition does at the edges: where the flow bends around a target, where the spiral's arms crowd each other, where a circle is revealed only in fragments, where a packed field jostles. [C35900]

## M1. Flow-field target circles: the flow bends, the circle refuses (genart-019)

The saved reference, viewed directly: a square frame, edge to edge, on a flat deep violet-purple ground. The field is drawn as thick rounded capsules -- short fat strokes with rounded ends -- sweeping in broad curves across the whole frame, plus five to six concentric-ring targets (bullseyes) scattered through the field, some cropped by the edges. Every mark, capsule and ring alike, is itself striped lengthwise in the same rainbow banding: outer blue, then magenta, red, orange, yellow core. Nothing is muted. Density is high: the ground shows through only as thin gaps between marks. The largest bullseye sits right of centre, a second large one at lower left. The caption reads "More circles in a flow field", c. March 2022. [C35903]

The mechanism is two orders in tension, stated as rules (leg formalisation -- no code or process post by McWhae has been found; Bohet keeps artist code server-side by design, so every rule in this study is reverse-engineered from the images, never his documented procedure). Field: a 2D angle field, a base direction slowly rotating with y plus two to four vortex terms, each with centre, radius of influence 0.25-0.45 of canvas width, and strength plus or minus 1.5-3.0 radians. Capsules: 250-450 walkers stepping along the field for lengths 0.03-0.22 of canvas width (log-normal: many short, few very long), width 0.008-0.02, round caps, stopping early if they come within one capsule-width of a target's edge -- the flow must not cross a target. Targets: three to five circles, radii 0.08-0.16 of canvas width, centres at least 2.2 radii apart, each four to six concentric rings in equal widths, colours taken in order from the same ramp as the capsules. Colour: one ordered ramp of five to seven hues, a capsule's base colour indexed by its starting y so colour bands sweep across the canvas instead of scattering. The signature mark is the triple stripe: capsule in colour A at width w, then a thinner capsule (0.55w) in colour B, then a core line (0.2w) in colour C, all along the same path. Density guard: reject any new capsule whose path comes within 0.5w of an existing one, keeping the ground visible as thin dark seams. [C35904]

**Takeaway:** the targets are the only perfect geometry in the image, and the flow bends around them -- capsules near a target curve to follow its circumference. That bending is the picture: a loose order (the flow) acknowledging a strict order (the circle) without touching it. A builder who drops the triple stripe has a generic flow field; a builder who drops the targets has wallpaper. [C35905]

## M2. Spiral arms with bead chains: momentum you can count (genart-023)

The saved reference, viewed directly: a square frame on a very dark plum ground, near-black. One large spiral dominates: thick curved bands radiate and curl out from a vortex centre at lower left, fanning across the whole frame to the top right. Each band is a flat solid colour -- red, orange, yellow, olive green, teal, blue, purple, magenta -- and each band is studded along its length with large solid dots in a contrasting colour (teal dots on red bands, maroon dots on yellow, olive dots on blue). Between the thick bands run thin dotted lines and hairlines in the same colours. The dark ground is visible only in wedges between bands, most of all at top right. The caption reads "Spiral Code", c. June 2022. [C35906]

The mechanism is radial momentum, stated as rules (leg formalisation). Origin: a vortex point P, on or just outside the canvas (here near lower left, roughly (0.15, 0.85)). Arms: 10-16 arms, each a logarithmic spiral r(t) = r0 exp(b t), theta(t) = theta0 + a t, with tightness a 0.8-1.6 radians per e-fold and b chosen so the arm reaches the canvas edge in 1.5-2.5 turns; drawn as a thick stroked path (width 0.02-0.06 of canvas width, tapering by 40 percent along its length), cropped at the canvas edge, no fade. Colour: one flat colour per arm from a six-to-eight colour palette, adjacent arms differing by at least two palette steps so neighbours never merge. Beads: dots placed along each arm at arc-length intervals 0.03-0.08 of width (jittered 30 percent), dot radius 0.35-0.6 of local arm width, dot colour either the arm's colour darkened or a contrasting palette colour -- in the image the beads are often a different colour from their arm, which is what makes them pop. A bead is placed only if its distance to the previous bead on the same arm exceeds 1.8 times its radius. Secondary dotted filaments: 20-40 thin dotted lines following the same spiral family with smaller a, filling the gaps between thick arms. At most one arm may terminate early (under 60 percent of the others' length). [C35907]

**Takeaway:** the dots are speed markers -- large where arms are thick and slow near the centre, small and dense where arms thin out -- so the spiral reads as acceleration, not just rotation. And this is the most animatable McWhae system: arms can grow outward over time and beads can travel along them, which no other candidate offers as naturally. A slow 30-60 second growth loop would be a real piece, not a slideshow. [C35908]

## M3. Hidden circles: the shape exists only where something passes over it (genart-024)

The saved reference, viewed directly -- the flagship Camp 2 exemplar, and the strongest work in the study: a square frame, edge to edge, on a near-black ground, very dark violet-black. The image is built from four to five vortex centres -- "hidden circles" -- each one a set of concentric dotted rings: a small open crescent or hook at the very centre, then two to three broken rings of dots and short dashes spiralling outward. The largest vortex sits centre-left; another is cut off at top right; a third at lower left; a fourth, smaller, at lower right. Between and around the vortices run short rounded capsules, some straight, some gently curved, in magenta, gold or yellow, cream or white, and violet -- many capsules are two-tone (a magenta outer capsule with a gold or cream inner line), and some are pure white hairline arcs. Thin white hairlines and dotted trails fill the gaps and trace the flow around the vortices. Density is medium-high but the black ground breathes: genuine dark gaps, especially top-left and bottom-centre, which is what lets the circles read as "hidden" -- you find them by following the dotted rings, not by a filled disc. Palette is disciplined: essentially magenta plus gold or yellow plus cream or white plus violet on black, no green, no blue, no orange-red. The caption reads "Purple and Yellow Hidden Circles", c. April 2022. The BRIEF's description of this image checks out, with two small corrections: white is a full fourth ink (hairlines and capsule cores), and violet is present as a capsule colour alongside magenta, gold, and cream. [C35909]

The mechanism is occlusion as the whole idea, stated as rules (leg formalisation). Ground layer: 60-120 horizontal-ish capsules (length 0.08-0.30 of width, width 0.01-0.025) in loose horizontal bands, direction jitter plus or minus 12 degrees, triple-striped as in M1, plus 200-400 tiny dots (2-5 px) scattered in short horizontal runs of three to eight dots. Hidden circles: three to five circles, radii 0.12-0.22 of canvas width, centres at least 2.5 radii apart. Each circle has a ring set: three to five concentric rings at radii 0.35, 0.55, 0.75, 0.9, 1.0 of r, ring width 0.008-0.015, colours alternating yellow, white, magenta. Reveal rule (the mechanism): a ring arc segment is drawn ONLY where it intersects a ground-layer capsule or dotted filament -- compute the union of all ground marks, dilate by one capsule-width, and clip each ring to that mask; sample each ring at 1-degree steps and draw the segment at angle alpha only if the point lies within one capsule-width of a ground capsule's path. Dotted orbits: one to two dotted rings per circle (dots 3-6 px, angular spacing 4-8 degrees, radius jitter plus or minus 2 px) drawn under the same reveal rule. Arc fragments must be long enough to read: enforce a minimum visible arc of 15 degrees -- visible fragments in the image run 20-120 degrees. Colour discipline: rings yellow and white only; ground capsules magenta, purple, white; no other hues -- the restriction is load-bearing, because with a full rainbow ramp the reveal effect disappears into noise. [C35910]

**Takeaway:** this is the most conceptually strong candidate in the study, because the mechanism IS the subject: a shape that exists only where something else passes over it. The eye completes the circles from fragments -- a Gestalt closure effect -- which is why the image feels clever rather than merely decorative. It also has the one interaction in this set that the system genuinely earns: drag to move a circle (or to move the flow), and the revealed arcs change live as the mask moves -- the circle is literally discovered by moving things over it. [C35911]

## M4. Concentric arc tiling: colour as a function of one variable (genart-031)

The saved reference, viewed directly: a square frame, and a complete break from the capsule and dot language of the first three saves. A tunnel or dome of chunky rectangular blocks -- thick flat tiles with slightly rounded corners, overlapping like bricks -- arranged in concentric arches rising from a warm centre at bottom-centre to the top edge. Colour runs as a smooth vertical gradient through the arches: orange and golden yellow at the centre-bottom, through red and magenta, to purple and royal blue at the top and outer edges. Ground is dark navy, visible as thin gaps between blocks and as a flat band across the very top. No dots, no capsules, no hairlines, no circles. The blocks vary in length (some long horizontal slabs, some short chunks) and are offset from arch to arch, so the dome reads as stacked, slightly irregular courses. Dense -- the blocks fill about 90 percent of the frame. The caption reads "Celestial", c. August 2022. [C35912]

The mechanism is radial temperature, stated as rules (leg formalisation). Core: a centre C on or just below the bottom edge (here bottom centre, about (0.5, 1.05)); all geometry is polar around C. Rings: 14-22 rings, radii from 0.06 to 0.75 of canvas width, ring spacing 0.03-0.05 of width, increasing slightly with r (times 1.02 per ring). Tiles: each ring divided into arc segments of random angular length 4-14 degrees (shorter near the core, longer outside, scaled by (r/r_max)^0.5), with a dark gap of 1-3 degrees between segments; tile radial thickness 0.7-0.9 times the ring spacing, so a dark seam remains between rings; tile edges are straight radial cuts, corners square, not rounded -- that is what distinguishes it from the capsule pieces. Radial jitter: each tile's radius offset by plus or minus 0.15 times the spacing, angular position jittered plus or minus 1 degree, capped so no tile overlaps its neighbour ring's tile by more than 0.2 times the spacing. Colour: colour equals ramp(radius) only -- blue through violet through magenta-red to orange, sampled at five to seven stops, no per-tile random colour; tiles at the same radius share the same colour family, with lightness jitter plus or minus 8 percent. Ground: solid dark navy showing through all gaps; the rings stop at r_max, leaving a dark sky band of 8-15 percent of canvas height at the top. [C35913]

**Takeaway:** the most architectural and calm of the candidates -- it reads as a rose window or a kiln cross-section -- and the only one whose colour rule is a pure function of one variable (radius), which makes it extremely controllable. A builder can swap the ramp and the core position and get a whole family (sunset dome, furnace, eye). It is also the easiest to verify in a harness: ring count, gap fraction, and colour-by-radius are all countable. [C35914]

## M5. Perpendicular roads: one family of lines, one right angle (Bohet, c. 2023)

"Perpendicular" was viewed in three marketplace-hosted copies -- the Bohet original file (output 1546) was unreachable from this sandbox, so none of the three is claimed to be it. What the copies show is a discipline turn, and a different system from everything before it. Portrait format, flat ground. Sets of parallel lines that turn at right angles -- like roads or ribbon cables seen from above -- each set in one flat colour (red, cream, blue, green, lavender), forming nested U-turns, L-shapes, S-curves, and straight vertical and horizontal bands. The sets are spaced apart with generous negative space between them. One variant is sparse on charcoal, with a solid red horizontal bar anchoring the bottom right -- the airiest McWhae work inspected, the opposite pole from "Field Trip"'s density. A second variant, on muted sage green, runs the same system at maximum density: thick multi-line pathways in teal, purple, yellow, dark blue, and red weave, loop, and intersect without crossing, filling the whole frame like a labyrinth, with small clusters of solid purple squares (2x2 and 3x3 blocks) acting as nodes or markers. A third variant, on pale pink, reduces the palette to two colours -- pink ground, violet-blue ribbons -- and lets the lines meander in loose loops and figure-eights, with small dashed-line segments scattered in the negative space. Flat colour throughout, no gradients, no dots, no circles. [C35915]

The mechanism is the turn, stated as rules (leg formalisation, rebuilt from leg 2's inspection -- an earlier formalisation of this work as two crossing capsule families with an over-under weave was WRONG and is struck; it contradicted the viewed images, which show one system of parallel lines turning at right angles, not two families crossing). One family of parallel polylines per colour-set, line spacing constant within a set; each line turns only at 90 degrees, at turn points set by a slow noise field or by a fixed turn interval with jitter; lines never cross within a set, and sets are separated by a minimum gap (the negative space is a parameter, not an accident). Square nodes: small solid squares, singly or in 2x2 and 3x3 clusters, placed at a subset of turn points or dead ends, in a single accent colour per output. Density is the master variable: sparse (sets few, gaps wide, ground dominant) to maze (sets many, gaps narrow, ground a thin network). Palette: one flat colour per set, two to five colours per output, never a gradient inside a line. [C35916]

**Takeaway:** the right angle is the whole point -- any other angle would read as a tangle, 90 degrees reads as deliberate. And the density variable is the composition: the same rules produce an airy architectural drawing at one setting and a packed puzzle at the other. The sharpest statement of McWhae's range: the 2022 flow fields are the most alive, and this is the most restrained, and both come from the same instinct of one rule repeated hundreds of times. [C35917]

## M6. Vortex-attractor capsule field: the invisible force, made countable (Camp 2)

This is the BRIEF's Camp 2 mechanism, formalised from genart-019 and genart-024 rather than copied from either image: a near-black field bent around three to five invisible vortex attractors, short rounded capsules lying tangent to the local flow, bunching and curving sharply near each vortex, with dotted accents tracing the highest-curvature regions. Ground near-black (#0d0b14); a four-stop neon ramp -- magenta, gold, cream, violet. [C35918]

The mechanism, stated as rules (leg formalisation). Attractors: three to five vortices, centres placed by Poisson-disc sampling (minimum separation 0.3 of width), each with strength plus or minus 1.0-2.5 and core radius 0.06-0.12 of width; signs mixed (at least one clockwise, one counter-clockwise) so the field has both kinds of swirl. Field: angle theta(x,y) equals a base angle plus the sum over vortices of strength times a Gaussian falloff times the tangential unit, sigma 0.15-0.30 of width. Curvature kappa(x,y) equals the magnitude of the gradient of theta, computed numerically on a grid -- a real, computable quantity, and it drives the dots. Capsules: 400-700 short capsules (length 0.02-0.06 of width -- SHORTER than M1, because brevity is what lets them follow tight curvature without looking like bent noodles), width 0.006-0.012, round caps, triple-striped, each drawn tangent to theta at its centre. Length rule: L equals L_max times (1 - 0.5 kappa-hat), where kappa-hat is normalised local curvature -- capsules shorten where the field bends hardest, which is what makes the vortex cores read. Colour: index from the ramp by the angle theta mapped to ramp position, so colour bands follow the flow direction. Dotted accents: wherever kappa-hat exceeds 0.75, place dots (2-4 px, cream or gold) along the local flow direction at 4-8 px spacing, in runs of 5-15 dots, capped at about 300 total so they stay accents, not a second field. Interaction (earned): drag adds a temporary vortex at the pointer (strength plus 2.0, sigma 0.12, lifetime 1.5 seconds fading out); the capsules re-lay in real time around it. The field is the piece, and touching the field changes the piece. Never click-to-reload as the primary interaction; reseed is a secondary key or button only. [C35919]

**Takeaway:** the most buildable and most dynamic candidate: the field plus curvature plus dots is a complete, self-contained system with a countable acceptance test (capsule length must anti-correlate with curvature; dots must sit only in high-curvature regions; vortex count must be recoverable from the image by eye). It generalises McWhae's static prints into a living piece without inventing a new visual language -- same marks, same ground, same ramp, plus motion the originals imply but do not have. [C35920]

## M7. Dotted orbit rings: a circle made of nothing but gaps (genart-023 / genart-024)

The lightest system in the set, drawn from the dotted-ring texture of the two 2022 saves: bare dotted circles -- rings made of nothing but evenly spaced dots, some perfect, some wobbling -- floating over a dark field with only a few capsule accents. A ring of dots is perceived as a circle even though no line is drawn (closure and good continuation), and the gaps between dots make the circle feel lighter and more tentative than a solid ring. Varying the dot size along the ring (growing then shrinking) gives a sense of a pulse travelling around the orbit. Where two dotted rings intersect, the dot patterns interfere and make a moire-like shimmer. [C35921]

The mechanism, stated as rules (leg formalisation). Orbits: four to eight circles, radii 0.05-0.25 of width, centres placed with minimum separation 1.8 times the sum of each pair's radii, so rings may just touch but never deeply overlap -- in the images, dotted rings touch at most at one point. Dots: for a ring of radius r, place N equals 2 pi r divided by spacing dots, spacing 8-16 px, angular jitter plus or minus 0.02 radians, radial jitter plus or minus 1.5 px (small -- the wobble must stay subtle; in genart-024 the dotted rings are nearly perfect circles). Dot radius 2-5 px, constant within a ring, or modulated sinusoidally with angle for the pulse variant -- use the pulse on at most two rings per piece. Colour: one colour per ring, from the same ramp as the capsules (yellow, white, magenta in genart-024); adjacent rings must differ in colour. Ground texture: three to six short capsule accents only (length 0.1-0.2 of width), placed to balance the composition -- the dots must dominate; if capsules exceed about 15 percent of the mark count, the piece becomes M1. Optional pairing rule: rings come in concentric pairs sharing a centre (radii ratio 1 to 1.35-1.6), one thin solid ring and one dotted -- the pair reads as a single object with two voices. [C35922]

**Takeaway:** the only candidate where the dark ground is the majority of the image, which makes it the best fit for a slow piece -- a ring drawn dot by dot gives a natural 20-40 second loop with a clear beginning, middle, and end. The dot-by-dot drawing is inherently animatable, and the animation is the mechanism, not a slideshow over a finished picture. [C35923]

## The arc: capsule to bar to road to packed shape to block

What stayed constant across 2022-2024: the square or portrait frame, edge to edge, no borders, no text inside the work (except "Stencil"'s promo lettering); flat, opaque colour, no shading, no soft gradients except inside "Field Trip"'s bars; dark grounds dominating (near-black, navy, dark purple, charcoal), with pale grounds as the exception ("Perpendicular" sage and pink, "Stencil" #3's yellow-green); circles recurring in every phase (bullseyes in 2022, pie-wedge and striped circles in "Field Trip", round "Stencil" outputs); high saturation throughout -- he never goes muted or pastel as a main palette. [C35924]

What changed is the unit, and each swap re-tunes palette, density, and ground. 2022 Instagram: the capsule (rounded stroke) and the dot (dotted rings, dot-studded bands) -- organic, curvy, vortex-driven, rainbow-everything, every mark carrying the full spectrum at once. "Field Trip" (November 2022): the gradient bar (straight or curved) and the wireframe grid -- the mark goes rectangular, glossy, engineered; the rainbow moves inside the bar (gradient along each bar) while the composition stays dark; curves survive only as arcs and spirals; a pie-wedge circle appears, a circle with a bite taken out. "Perpendicular" (c. 2023): the parallel-line road -- the mark becomes a set of lines that turn together at right angles; the unit is the bundle, not the stroke; palette disciplines to one flat colour per line-set, two to five colours per output, with real negative space. "Stencil" (January 2024): the packed shape (square, circle, triangle, quarter-circle) -- the mark is a filled tile placed by physics ("dump them in a bucket"), not drawn by a moving point; many outputs are two to three colours total (#89: grey plus orange on black; #235: lime plus purple plus black; the checker: pink plus blue). "Block Party": the isometric block -- the mark gains a third dimension (extruded, wireframe) while staying flat-coloured. Its date is unknown in every source consulted, so its place in the sequence is left open -- do not place it after "Stencil" without evidence. The through-line, stated as interpretation: McWhae keeps the same generative instinct (one simple rule, repeated hundreds of times) but keeps swapping the unit, and each swap is a new picture. The 2022 flow fields are the most alive; everything after is more architectural. [C35925]

## Aesthetic logic: dark ground, saturated mark, one rule repeated

Three constants. First, the ground is dark and the mark is saturated -- near-black or deep violet against full-chroma capsules, dots, bars, roads, and blocks; nothing is drawn in mid-tones, and the ground is never mid-tone either (the BRIEF's Camp 2 rule, and it holds across every phase). Second, colour is assigned by position, never at random per mark: by starting y in M1, by arm in M2, by radius in M4, by flow angle in M6 -- the ramp is the ordering device, and a mark's colour tells you where it sits in the system. Third, occlusion is a hard rule, not a blend: capsules are separated by ground gaps; where two systems meet, one is clipped by the other (M3's reveal rule is only the purest form of it). What is absent throughout: soft gradients as ornament (except inside "Field Trip" bars, where the gradient IS the mark), photorealism, text as image, decoration for its own sake. The work trusts one rule, repeated at scale, to carry the picture -- and when the rule is a social one (who the code is for), that is someone else's study. [C35926]
