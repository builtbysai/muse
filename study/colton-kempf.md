## M1. Separate the audio, then re-score

The foundational mechanism of the whole practice, and the one his own write-up states as a rule: the original audio is always separated from the video first. The 30-plus hours of family home video (1996-2010, shot by his father, digitised and kept only where Kempf himself appears) become 18 audiovisual works through three working directions: music for video, video for music, or both at once. Sometimes the original audio is woven back in; sometimes it is replaced; sometimes both get re-edited together. The mechanism is exact and it is not editing hygiene: pulling the soundtrack off is how he discovers what the image can say. The watchable behavior is the home movie as instrument: the same domestic footage sounding tender, funny, or haunted depending on what gets put under it, and the knowledge that none of the sound is the sound it came with.

**Takeaway:** always pull the original audio off first; the re-scoring is the work, not the finishing.

## M2. Mismatch as expression

The emotional engine behind the method, stated in his own vocabulary. Editing sound and image into deliberate non-alignment, his citation is Wollen (2002), to produce tension and yearning, "parts longing to be made whole again". The mechanism matters because it turns a technical move into a feeling: the mismatch is not a mistake to be corrected but the affect the piece is after. In the home-movie works the audience knows the footage is real family material, and the deliberately unaligned sound makes the realness ache rather than reassure. The watchable behavior is the almost-right: sound and image close enough to recognise each other, far enough apart that the gap does the emotional work.

**Takeaway:** cut the sound and image to almost-but-not-quite meet; let the gap carry the feeling.

## M3. The making-of inside the track

The most distinctive habit in the practice, and the one no one else in this study series does. While making the 18 home-movie works he recorded himself narrating the process out loud, and sometimes kept the commentary inside the final audio. The voice explaining what he is trying to do becomes part of what he has done. The mechanism is process made audible: the tutorial track that never got mixed out, the annotation that survives as content. The watchable behavior is a piece that tells you how it was made while you are watching it, and refuses to pretend the making was ever separate from the made.

**Takeaway:** record yourself narrating the process, then keep the narration in the mix.

## M4. Ornate cursive title cards over location footage

The signature visual device of the a C c f film (2024), and the one with the cleanest frame evidence. Performer names appear in gold/yellow ornate script laid over venue exteriors: "Giulio Matheson" over the graffiti-covered PIEHOUSE van (f03), "BWU" over the hand-painted "FUCK ENGLAND!" placard (f04), "House on the Strand" over the open doors of Matchstick Piehouse (f06), the film's own title "a C c f" in gold cursive on pure black (f01). The mechanism is stated in his Sonic Scope article: he is "playing with and breaking traditional music video aesthetics, in which the name of a single artist is often written in ornate cursive at the outset". He keeps the convention and breaks the casting, one ornate name per act in a film about a whole scene. The watchable behavior is credit as decoration: the names drift over brick arches and spiked fences, and the venue gets named in the same typographic language as the performers, which is the film's thesis about hierarchy in one title treatment.

**Takeaway:** put every name, even the venue's, in the same ornate script over the location footage.

## M5. Super-montage: one track suturing many views

The compositional mechanism of the a C c f film, and its strongest claim about hierarchy. The montage sequence at 07:43-10:40 blends many performance clips through Premiere transparency overlays, all held together by a single performer track; interview audio is then laid under visuals it was never recorded with, and the whole film runs on the rule that visuals show the audience's perspective while audio carries the performers'. The mechanism is exact: one sound track suturing dozens of views, then voices deliberately crossing into images that are not theirs. The watchable behavior is the crowd rendered as one continuous surface: the distinction between who performs and who watches dissolves inside the blend, and the ending (credits as many faces plus an improv recording) is designed so the ending feels like a beginning. The paradigm shift came from the scene itself: a founder told him to make "a CROSSOVERS film", not "a film about CROSSOVERS", and the edit is that distinction made technical.

**Takeaway:** hold dozens of views together with one track, then let voices wander into images that are not theirs.

## M6. "Fly in the face" haptic closeness

The shooting grammar of the documentary work, named in his article after Marks's haptic visuality. The a C c f film was shot on a 50mm prime, observational and deliberately close: performers feel the camera's presence even when the audience never sees the camera's position. The mechanism is trust instead of reflexivity: closeness as a contract with the subject, not a trick played on the viewer. The still form is f18 ('NX Radio with Monty'): hands cueing vinyl on a turntable in warm lamplight, the frame close enough to be inside the gesture. It sits opposite the void-stage of #261 on purpose: where Attrill isolates her subjects against a surrounding dark, Kempf pushes the lens into the room until the frame is almost too close, and lets the intimacy be the shot.

**Takeaway:** shoot close enough that the subject feels the camera; let trust be the cinematography.

## M7. Found-footage rescoring as research

The laboratory register, and the one that makes the method visible as pedagogy. The A/V Composition Studies (May 2024, MA Audiovisual Cultures): Study 1 re-scores Lumiere's Serpentine Dance (1896) with Yak's 'Stampede' (2019) (f19, the blue-fabric dancer centered on black), Study 2 cuts a Peter Pan (1953) montage under his own 2022 music, Study 3 treats the Portrait of a Lady on Fire bonfire scene as a loudness-plus-pitch study. The mechanism is the classic test image made audible: early cinema and Disney used as neutral footage so the sound-image relationship can be isolated and varied, like a chemistry set. The watchable behavior is the famous image estranged by a new track: you know the Serpentine Dance, and you have never heard it like this. It is the same move as M1, reduced to a controlled experiment, and it is where his research-assistant and journal-editor lives show through.

**Takeaway:** take a famous silent image and re-score it like a controlled experiment; let the track do the estrangement.

## M8. Format grain as material

The texture system that keeps every register honest, and the mechanism that carries the sound-first logic into the image itself. Beatriz (SparkBoom's first film, 2024) mixes digital with archive super-8 (f15: red light leak, tree silhouettes, moon); Mixed Feelings for Alice Black Gallery (2024) is commissioned archival kaleidoscope in VHS (f16: scanline-degraded mic silhouette against a pink-orange bloom); 5,500 Miles (2025) is the US road doc in lo-fi camcorder (f17: "Cigarettes Sold" sign, direct-to-camera wave); his film photographs carry visible date stamps (f11: "24 8 18"). Each register gets the texture of its era; the nostalgia is engineered, not accidental. The sound equivalent is his Sonic Scope Issue Six "Video Hum" essay: the mains hum of early video art treated as a constant anchoring presence under unstable images, "like the sacred drone of Om", the essay opening on black with only the hum, sound elevated above image. Grain for the eye, hum for the ear: the format's noise floor is part of the composition.

**Takeaway:** give each register the grain of its era, and treat the format's noise floor as an instrument.

## Evolution

- **1996-2010: the archive.** His father shoots the family home video; it is digitised later and sits on a hard drive, 30-plus hours.
- **The MA years (Goldsmiths, MA Music: Audiovisual Cultures):** the archive becomes the major project: 18 audiovisual works, a 16-minute performance video demonstrating 9 techniques, and a live set in the EMS 7.1.4 Room ending in the attempted destruction of a home-movie tape. Modules produce the A/V Composition Studies and 'NX Radio with Monty' (Ethnographic Film and Music Research, Barley Norton); 'a C c f' films the CROSSOVERS nights at Matchstick Piehouse Deptford (October 2022 to August 2024).
- **May 2024: the partnership and the share.** He posts a year of video work; on May 9 he founds SparkBoom Productions with Iso Attrill ("Started a production company with @isoaattrill"). The first SparkBoom film profiles Beatriz Moura: digital plus super-8, his sound design and score, his father's voiceover debut.
- **2024-2025: the registers fill in.** 'Mixed Feelings' (Alice Black Gallery commission, VHS kaleidoscope); Valais and US road-trip photo work; '5,500 Miles' (SparkBoom road doc, 2025-01); Sonic Scope Issue Six "Video Hum" (2025-02); 'Alter Ego' (August 2025): sound and music for Kyle Beattie's dark Inverness MTB short, all natural sounds made into a soundscape, bagpipes by Gavin Bruce, Shaff 2026 selection; the PureGold performance of the home-movie practice (August 2025).
- **2026: Margate.** The move shows up in the work: the February DRATS Philly prints pair the crowded 2022 street register ('The Stage', 'Remnants') with the new minimal seascapes ('Purpling': one contrail in dusk gradient; 'Solo Dream': one figure mirrored on tidal flats). He leads the free Art and Design Saturday Club for teens at the Margate School of Art, runs a crowdfunder and a margarita fundraiser at High Dive (poster by Iso), and the Sonic Scope Issue Seven 'a C c f' commentary publishes October 5.

The arc: an archive inherited from his father, a master's degree that turns the archive into a method (separate, re-score, mismatch), a production company that applies the method to other people's footage, and finally Margate, where the crowded-frame street practice gives way to near-empty skies and tidal flats. The through-line never moves: sound first, image second, the negotiation between them always audible.

## Aesthetic logic

Everything Colton Kempf makes is a negotiation between a picture and a sound that did not start together, and the negotiation is the art. The family archive only became a practice when he pulled the audio off; the ethnographic film only became a CROSSOVERS film when one track was allowed to hold dozens of views and voices were allowed to wander into images that were not theirs; the mountain-bike short only exists as a score of natural sounds under footage he did not shoot. He is the inverse of the director's instinct: instead of staging a world and then sounding it, he takes a world that already exists and re-sounds it until it means something else. The handmade is in the sound, not the set: the narrated process kept in the mix, the hum held under the image like a hand under a table, the grain of each format left in the picture as a receipt for the era it came from. Where his partner stages small lit worlds against the dark, he starts with the whole noisy world and edits what you hear. The Margate turn is the logic made visible: fewer people in the frame, more sky, one contrail, one figure on the flats, as if the negotiation is finally happening between the image and almost nothing, and the almost nothing is winning.
