## M1. Two lanes, one feed, never blended

The grid runs two parallel products and refuses to cross them. Lane one: lo-fi phone-video skate clips (f01 "Wonky Muni Nights" 2022, f03 "Feels nice to be skating" 2023, f05 the Muni dusk grind with the caption "They really boutta get rid of this place"). Lane two: grainy 35mm black-and-white street frames (f02 the snowball-thrower captioned "Shoot, process. #streetphotography #35mm", f06 the tight Chinatown elders, f08 the Odunde toddler, f09 "Genesis" at the crosswalk). Film gets the considered frames; phone video gets the motion. The watchable behavior is the refusal to blend: the skate lane never borrows the film lane's grain, and the film lane never borrows the phone lane's shake. Both lanes were present from the first four posts in 2022, so this is not an evolution, it is the constitution.

**Takeaway:** run two render lanes that never share a grade (held grainy B&W frames for the art, jittery vertical phone motion for the community proof); the separation is the identity, not a phase.

## M2. The MUNI longitudinal: a spot as a multi-year subject

The Municipal Services Building skate plaza at 1401 JFK Blvd (tagged "#muni" in 6 posts, tagged Philadelphia 37 times) is a documentary subject across four years, not a backdrop. f05 (2023-05-24) is already a closure lament: "They really boutta get rid of this place." f12 (2025-10-17) formalizes it: "Excerpts from 'MUNI' In progress...", B&W film, a skater mid-trick over a ledge, face cropped out, dreadlocks flying. f13 (2026-04-17) is the elegy: "Farewell to the Kickout.", color film, skaters sitting on a curb while a cop stands across the street under a 10'-0" clearance sign. Security and police appear in-frame as the story, not as interruption. The interview gives the mechanism its sentence: "When you have something so strong happening, you don't want to leave it, especially if you know there's an ending." The watchable behavior is spot-death as subject: the same ledges photographed across years while the conditions of the spot visibly harden.

**Takeaway:** pick one spot and photograph it across years as the ending approaches; let security and police appear in-frame as the story; the subject is the spot's life, not any single trick.

## M3. Print as proof: the darkroom print posted as the post

f04 (2023-05-04) is not a photograph, it is a photograph OF a 16x20 fiber-based darkroom test print (#4): a B&W portrait of a braided kid seen through a scratched Chinese-store window, the print border and the test-strip process visible in the frame. The caption is a confession, quoted whole: "Took me an entire 5 sheets of 16x20 and getting the right exposure to realize that my camera lens missed focus... trying to learn to be more intentional with my work and slow down which includes using all the fstops when necessary and looking through the viewfinder lol." He calls himself an "amateur darkroom printer" and the interview states the perceptual consequence: "The darkroom changed the way I see...I now think in terms of printing and how the image will look on its own, printed." The watchable behavior is the failure kept in public: the missed focus, the five wasted sheets, the test strip all stay in the post. The print is the proof that the process happened.

**Takeaway:** post the artifact, not the file (the print with its border, the test strips, the wasted sheets); keep the failure in-frame and name it in the caption; let the darkroom, not the feed, be where the image is finished.

## M4. The timestamp route: walking as the method

"I treat it as if it's a timestamp...whatever I find interesting, I capture it." The method is a loose daily route through Center City starting at "the park," and the method has a transit rule: "taking the train presents opportunities he would otherwise miss completely if he were to drive" (roundtable). Parades are scheduled congestion: "that's one of the few times you can get a congestion of people in the city...there's a lot of energy, and it's our version of [New York's] tourist season." The watchable behavior is the route as camera: frames arrive at walking speed, subjects are whatever the route yields that day, and the rare congestion event is shot for density. f17 (2026-10-10, "Photo Larp #canonp") is the route made visible: a phone-video walk down a Center City sidewalk, Walnut St storefronts, pedestrians ahead, the camera just walking.

**Takeaway:** give the piece a daily route, not a subject; walk it at train speed, never car speed; shoot the rare congestion for density; the route is the composition.

## M5. Teaching as the second product

The bio link is not a portfolio, it is a product: "30 Days of Seeing- A Street Photography Field Guide" on Payhip, announced September 2026 (f16, the cover: "Slow down. Look closer. Find more. Everyday places, extraordinary photographs. By Nazir Wayman"). The announcement caption states the creed: "I believe becoming a better photographer has less to do with finding extraordinary places and more to do with learning to see the ordinary ones. 30 days. 30 assignments. A camera and your attention." The watchable behavior is pedagogy as product: the assignment, not the image, is what he sells. It sits naturally beside the roundtable testimony that he "doesn't typically experience creative blocks since there is always life in the streets" - the guide is the formalization of a method that never runs dry because the street never does.

**Takeaway:** make the assignment the product (30 days, ordinary places, attention as the only gear); teach the method, not the portfolio.

## M6. Close-range intimacy: kids and elders, wide lens, short distance

The street portraits are shot at conversational distance or closer, wide lens, direct engagement. f06 (2023-09-13): a tight B&W close-crop of two elderly Asian pedestrians mid-conversation in Chinatown, faces filling the frame, heavy grain. f08 (2024-06-11): a toddler on an adult's shoulder at the Odunde festival, direct gaze, soft window light. f09 (2024-11-29, "Genesis"): harsh-sun B&W street portrait of a person in a hoodie at a crosswalk, a script "Genesis" tattoo on the chest, layered pedestrians behind. f10 (2025-03-26): two kids with beaded braids hanging from scaffolding poles in an X composition. The subjects are overwhelmingly kids and elders, overwhelmingly Black Philadelphians, and the interview gives the method its source: "I try to do it all because I grew up in rec centers, playing with kids and [immersing] myself in the city." Community trust is the working method; the camera gets close because the photographer belongs. The watchable behavior is the held gaze: subjects look back, unguarded, because the distance was earned before the frame.

**Takeaway:** shoot only at conversational distance with a wide lens; subjects are kids and elders who look back; the closeness is earned by belonging, never by zoom.

## M7. Camera as character: gear named like a cast

Cameras are named in captions the way other photographers name collaborators. The Canon P rangefinder is "affordable, compact, and quiet," used "when I" need to move unnoticed (f17, "Photo Larp #canonp"). The Nikon F5 has a "louder shutter... too attention-grabbing for candid shots," so "I use it when I'm in places I'm welcome" - the shutter's social consequence is stated outright: "With an SLR, you get a little bit more wary." The Contax G2 is "this has to be the sharpest camera I ever put my hands on" (f18, golden-hour color frame, woman pushing a cart, long shadows and lens flare). The Leica M4-P, the Rolleiflex, Ilford HP5 pushed - each named with a hashtag like a credit line. The watchable behavior is the instrument changing the image: quiet camera for unnoticed work, loud camera only where welcome, sharpness as a named event. Gear is not decoration; it is the cast, and each cast member gets different scenes.

**Takeaway:** name the camera in the caption like a cast member; let the instrument change the image (quiet rangefinder for unnoticed work, loud SLR only where welcome); treat the shutter's social consequence as part of the exposure.

## M8. Social-media asceticism: the feed as a business tool

From the roundtable: he has "deleted his accounts multiple times to reclaim his mental space and prioritize authenticity" and now views social media "as a business tool rather than a means to fit in." This is visible as absence: the 68-post grid is thin for 15,390 followers, the earliest post is 2022 despite a practice that clearly runs longer, and the cadence never spikes for engagement (4/13/13/12/20 per year, 2022-2026). The grid reads as an archive with the business end up front: the Payhip guide in the bio, the Puma partnership reel (2026-07-17, "shoot the @puma"), the atmos Philadelphia launch (2023-09). The watchable behavior is restraint as strategy: no engagement bait, no trend formats, no reposts of virality. The feed is kept small on purpose, which is why every frame in it reads as chosen.

**Takeaway:** keep the feed small on purpose; treat the grid as an archive with the business end up front (guide, partnerships), not as a growth machine; let restraint be the strategy.

## Aesthetic logic

The darkroom is the whole theory: "The darkroom changed the way I see...I now think in terms of printing and how the image will look on its own, printed." Everything downstream is a consequence. The two lanes stay separate because only one of them is meant to be printed. The timestamp philosophy is anti-curation with a purpose ("I treat it as if it's a timestamp"), and the purpose is stated plainly: "I take photos because memories are important...documentation is crucial, especially for future generations." This is why a spot's closure gets a four-year longitudinal and why the missed-focus print gets posted: the archive is the point, and the archive keeps the failures. His favorite corner is Broad and Walnut, and he learned from Ray Metzker that "people have been photographing this corner for years" - the aesthetic logic is custodial. He is not hunting the extraordinary; he is keeping the ordinary on record, at walking speed, on film, with the print as the proof that it happened. Eugene Richards, Mary Ellen Mark, Jeffrey Stockbridge are the named lineage: documentary photographers who stayed with their subjects. The skate lane is the same logic in motion: "Excerpts from 'MUNI' In progress..." is a documentary that happens to be about a ledge. Slow down. Look closer. Find more. The guide's tagline is the practice's engine, stated as an instruction because it was always one.
