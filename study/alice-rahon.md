# Alice Rahon: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #331.

**Site:** Alice Marie Yvonne Philippot, called Alice Rahon (Chenecey-Buillon, France, 8 June 1904 - Mexico City, September 1987, age 83), French-born Mexican poet turned painter; the surrealist exile who never stopped being a poet. Hat designer for Elsa Schiaparelli, Man Ray model, first woman published in the Editions Surrealistes (1936); married Wolfgang Paalen 1934, divorced 1947, took her mother's maiden name and started over; began painting in Mexico at 35 by scraping the paint off Paalen's discarded palettes with a nail. Sand, crushed volcanic stone, marble dust, dyed gravels worked into oil; sgraffito figures scratched through to the layer below; feathers, butterfly wings, leaves, shells, lapis, burnt cotton glued to the canvas. The 1944 first solo at the Galeria de Arte Mexicano; thirty years of exhibitions in Mexico, New York, Los Angeles, Paris, Beirut; a 1969 fall down the Pecanins gallery stairs that made her a recluse; the last painting 1975; the Bellas Artes retrospective 1986; rediscovery from the 2009 MAM retrospective on. Identity anchors: the poem-painting (poeme-tableau); the cave wall as ground; the scratch as composition; the invocation as subject; the renamed self; the homage as genre; the miniature theatre and the lost film.

**Doorway:** Hop from the manuel-felguerez study: from Felguerez's machine-thread to the surrealist household the Ruptura learned its automatism from - with a correction riding the hop. #330 closed with the machine as process: weld the mural, define the alphabet, vary inside limits. #331 was supposed to be the woman who taught the Ruptura how to pour (Carrillo's study #327 records "dripping as plastic automatism (Rahon/Paalen line)"). The research says: she never poured. What the Museo del Palacio de Bellas Artes actually credits to "Alice Rahon y Wolfgang Paalen" is plastic automatism "como el chorreado o dripping" - the PAIR, and her own documented hand is the scrape, not the pour: sgraffito, sand, incision, the nail through wet paint. The pourer of the household was Paalen (fumage, invented 1936, candle smoke - Tate, Sotheby's). So the doorway carries a revision, not a handoff: from Felguerez's rules to Rahon's matter, and the drip line to Carrillo runs through the couple's shared automatist practice, with Rahon's contribution being subtraction and Paalen's being the airborne mark. Felguerez ordered chaos after throwing it; Rahon never threw anything - she scratched away what was already there.

## M1. Sgraffito as the signature automatism

The origin story (substack, end-to-end): she began painting by randomly scraping the surface of Paalen's discarded palettes with a nail. The accident became the method. IU Eskenazi (end-to-end) describes the mature form in Boite a musique III (1945): sgraffito, "incising lines into the painting's surface of sand-infused oil paint to reveal contrasting layers of color below." Observed in the viewed Boite a musique III: a dark sanded ground worked over with yellow-white incisions - stick figures, fronds, triangles, a crescent, and a full X-row scratched along the bottom edge like a signature in code. Observed in L'Eclipse (1947): golden lines carved through teal-black to draw the eclipse city. Observed in Chats nocturnes (1957): cat silhouettes exist only as scratches in a black-green night. Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M2) was additive-only - "I can't erase anything"; Rahon composes by SUBTRACTION, removing paint to draw. Felguerez (#330 M6) threw chaos then ordered it; Rahon lays the field and excavates the image. **Takeaway:** Compose by removing; the drawing is what the scratch reveals. Watch the surface: when the figures are incisions in a dark matter-ground, the mechanism is on.

## M2. Matter as cave wall

The material catalog (poetryalquimia + Andrade via the Zaragoza handout, end-to-end): fine sands, marble dust, dyed sands and gravels, worked into oil so the ground has the granular quality of a cavern wall. Artforum on Fiesta de Abril (1945): "the artist emulated the granular quality of cavern walls by adding sand (as she frequently did in many other pieces, sometimes applying crushed volcanic stone in its stead) to her oil paint," then scratched pictographic figures into the thickly encrusted surface. Observed in the viewed works: the encrusted dark grounds of Boite a musique III and L'Eclipse carry their figures like petroglyphs. The cave is Altamira, visited with Paalen in 1933 and 1934 at Miro's recommendation - "I think I am a cave painter," she told a reporter (Frieze). Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M1) built up paint and carved into it; Rahon loads the paint with geology BEFORE the image exists, so the carving always hits earth. **Takeaway:** Make the ground geology before it is image. Watch the ground: when the paint holds sand and the figures sit in it like rock art, the mechanism is on.

## M3. The optical-mix rule

Her own account of what Paalen taught her (inverarte bio, sourced to the 2009 MAM catalog): "as a painter, I owe everything to him [...] from him I learned not to mix the color beforehand but to put it in such a way that it blends in the eye of the beholder." Unmixed color juxtaposed so the mixture happens in the viewer. Observed in the 1951 Self-Portrait: the torso is a stippled field of thousands of tiny unmixed marks - ochre, teal, rust, cream - that read as continuous tone at distance. Observed in Balada para Frida Kahlo (1956-66): the golden floating city glows because translucent orange and yellow strokes lie side by side, never blended. Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M3) built luminosity with Renaissance glazing, thin transparent layers fused wet; Rahon's luminosity is pointillist and dry, mixed only in the eye. **Takeaway:** Mix in the beholder's eye, never on the palette. Watch the tone: when the color vibrates from unmixed neighbors, the mechanism is on.

## M4. Pictographic inscription

The vocabulary comes from two rock walls: Altamira and the Wrangell, Alaska petroglyphs sketched on the 1939 trip. Heckscher (end-to-end) on La Conjuration des Antilopes (1943): "three silhouette-like figures, a central humanoid form flanked by two antelope, are formed entirely by white spots. Parallel rows of black dots, representing antelope tracks, meander around and between these figures." The figure is a dot-cluster, not a contour. TheArtStory on Self-Portrait and Autobiography (1948): sgraffito figures arranged along a zigzag line "from the bottom to middle frame," the journey Europe-Alaska-Mexico told as glyphs; the bottom-to-top progression reads as autobiography. The X-row along the bottom of Boite a musique III (viewed) works as a text line in an undeciphered script. Against the chain: Garcia Ponce (#328) tore fragments that already had a past; Rahon's fragments are glyphs that never had a contour. **Takeaway:** Build figures out of dots and scratches, never outlines. Watch the mark: when the animal is a constellation of spots and the signature is an X-row, the mechanism is on.

## M5. The invocation (poeme-tableau)

Her 1951 Willard Gallery catalog text (quoted by Heckscher and inverarte, end-to-end): "At the beginning of time painting was magic; it was a key to the invisible. In those days the value of a work lay in its powers of conjuration, a power that talent alone could not achieve. Like the shaman, the sibyl and the wizard, the painter had to make himself humble, so that he could share in the manifestation of spirits and forms." She called her early paintings "Poeme-Tableau" (invaluable). The MOCA 2019 exhibition took its title from this doctrine: "Poetic Invocations." The painting is not a picture of a spirit; it is the act of conjuring one. Observed in Balada para Frida Kahlo: a city that is visibly an apparition, translucent, rising out of dark water - the invocation made visible. Against the chain: Felguerez (#330 M8) used technical titles as triggers over geometric paintings; Rahon's whole canvas is the trigger, and the title (La Conjuration des Antilopes) names the spell being cast. **Takeaway:** The painting is a spell, not a picture. Watch the apparition: when the image is translucent and rising, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The shapeshifter identity

Mary Ann Caws (LRB, quoted passages): Rahon "casually changed her date and place of birth (1904 in Besancon, not 1916 in Brittany), her name and nationality, sexual orientation and artistic genre." Born Alice Marie Yvonne Philippot; published poetry as Alice Paalen; divorced in 1947 and took her mother's maiden name Rahon, becoming a Mexican citizen in 1946; poet until 1941, painter after. The 1916-in-Brittany birthdate she gave out is still printed by auction houses (Christie's, Bonhams lots read "1916-1987"). The falsification is not vanity - it is the same operation as the paintings: scratch away the given surface, inscribe the chosen one. TheArtStory notes she kept her first name "as an homage to Lewis Carroll's Alice in Wonderland" - the girl who goes through the glass and comes back renamed. Against the chain: Gerzso (#329) signed one name for sixty years and distilled toward one wall; Rahon re-founded the author with each life chapter. **Takeaway:** The signature is a costume; change it when the work changes. Watch the name: when the biography has to be corrected against the artist's own telling, the mechanism is on.

## M7. Homage as genre

A substantial share of her named works are dedications: Celestial Shadow to Giorgio de Chirico; El Gato Herido (Para Frida Kahlo) 1946, painted the same year as Kahlo's Venado Herido, the cat standing in for the deer ("a love of cats is something she shared with other Surrealists," TheArtStory); La balada de Frida Kahlo 1956, reworked a decade later, with "Frida aux yeux d'hirondelle" written directly on the canvas; El tucan y el arco iris o Homenaje a Wolfgang Paalen 1967, "a luminous canvas with heartbreaking references to Rahon's first husband, and the toucan they kept briefly as a pet" (Wendi Norris). Homages also to Miro, Neruda, Rivera. The dedication is not a caption - it picks the subject: grief chooses the animal (the cat for Kahlo, the toucan for Paalen). Observed in the viewed El Gato Herido: the wounded cat is drawn in thin fragile lines, the blue bandage-rectangle the only saturated patch. Against the chain: Felguerez (#330 M8) titled with physics and refused illustration; Rahon titles with the names of the dead and illustrates the mourning. **Takeaway:** Dedicate the painting to the dead; let grief pick the subject. Watch the dedication: when the animal in the painting is the person being mourned, the mechanism is on.

## M8. The vertical rain

Las verticales del sueno (1959, viewed): the whole canvas is vertical dripping strokes - green-teal reeds of paint running downward, with small geometric kites (orange diamonds, red triangles, a golden arc) suspended among them like caught things. Her river series - El Nilo, Rio Papaloapan, Rio Papagayos, Encuentro de Rivieras (titled, not image-viewed) - makes water the recurring subject; she was "a very strong swimmer" (en.wiki), and her poem "Al Iztaccihuatl" speaks of "los limites chorreantes del oro inhabitable" (the dripping limits of the uninhabitable gold). This is the honest Rahon drip, and it matters for the doorway correction (C6): her drips are DRAGGED vertical strokes, gravity-assisted brushwork, not poured decantations. No source shows her pouring. Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M3) let gravity draw rivulets as pure direction; Rahon's verticals are still brush-drawn, the drip as gesture rather than surrender. **Takeaway:** Let the river fall straight down, by hand. Watch the vertical: when the strokes run like rain and the geometry hangs in them, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The nocturnal sgraffito city

A repeated nocturne: turquoise night grounds etched with trees, buildings, and cats. Chats nocturnes 1957 (viewed): a black-green night scratched with cat silhouettes, their eyes small red puncta. Gatos en el valle 1957 (substack, end-to-end): "the painting's background is a vivid shade of turquoise with trees etched into the middle and either side of the frame... it isn't easy to decipher whether the image contains four cats or two cats in motion." L'Eclipse 1947 (viewed): the eclipse disc hangs over an incised city. The cats are nahuales - "shape-shifting guardian spirit beings in Mesoamerican Indian belief," "enormous transparent entities whose bodies appear in the sky or envelop buildings or entire cities" (TheArtStory). The night is not background; it is the medium the scratch reveals. Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M4) made the wall the protagonist and the dent its plot; Rahon's wall is night itself, and the plot is whatever the scratch lets glow through it. **Takeaway:** Scratch the city into the night. Watch the dark: when the ground is night and the drawing is light carved out of it, the mechanism is on.

## M10. Found-matter assemblage

INBAL (end-to-end): "Era afecta a las manipulaciones minusculas de la materia, pegaba plumitas, hojitas secas, papelitos encontrados en la calle, alitas de mariposas y algodoncitos quemados" - she was fond of tiny manipulations of matter: little feathers, dry leaves, paper scraps found in the street, butterfly wings, burnt bits of cotton. TheArtStory's materials list: sand, sea and snail shells, leaves, lapis lazuli, onyx, wood, feathers, volcanic ash, gold dust. Poetryalquimia: fine sands, marble dust, bits of silver or colored paper. The walk becomes the painting: what the street and the garden give, the canvas keeps. Observed indirectly: the shimmering, uneven surfaces of the viewed works, where paint, sand, and incision share one skin. Against the chain: Felguerez (#330 M2) built his vocabulary from industrial chatarra - gears, wire, wrecked machines; Rahon's salvage is naturalia, gathered not welded. **Takeaway:** Stick the walk to the canvas. Watch the surface: when feathers and street paper share the paint's skin, the mechanism is on.

## M11. The miniature theatre and the lost film

The theatre line runs parallel to the painting: Juggler (1946), a 27-inch marionette; the 1946 Orion ballet - script, articulated puppets, cosmic choreography after India, revived in 2009 by the Laboratorio de la Mascara from her notes; the 1947 experimental film Le Magicien (Les Magiciens / The Magician) with husband Edward Fitzgerald - costumes, puppets, screenplay by her, direction by him, financed in part by chutney she cooked and sold, screened for friends and at the Willard Gallery in New York, then lost (only a few stills survive; sources conflict on whether it was finished). MOCA 2020 premiered Lea Petrikova's After the Magician, a film about the lost film. Against the chain: Felguerez (#330) staged happenings for a thousand people with Jodorowsky; Rahon's theatre is miniature, domestic, and vanished - the work that exists only as a screening nobody can repeat. **Takeaway:** Build the theatre small; let the film disappear. Watch the scale: when the puppets outnumber the audience and the film survives only as stills, the mechanism is on.

## M12. The household correction (the drip's true address)

The load-bearing finding of this study. MPBA 2025 (end-to-end): Carrillo "retomo de ellos [Alice Rahon y Wolfgang Paalen] una reconfiguracion del espacio pictorico alejada de la perspectiva tradicional, varias tecnicas de automatismo plastico (como el chorreado o dripping)" and took up collage "como habia hecho anteriormente Rahon." Note the grammar: the dripping is credited to the PAIR; the collage is credited to Rahon alone. UNAM Perseo (end-to-end): the automatism principles were "introduced in our country by Wolfgang Paalen, the husband of Alice Rahon." Paalen's fumage - invented 1936, candle smoke on canvas, documented by Tate, Sotheby's, Weinstein, adopted by Dali and Matta - is the airborne automatist mark of the household. Rahon's own automatism is the nail, the sand, the scrape. Fritz Bultman's "it was Wolfgang Paalen who started it all" (quoted in the 2013 Paalen catalog) is about the New York drip conversation, not about Rahon. So: the Ruptura's drip line runs through the Rahon-Paalen household's shared automatist practice; assigning the pour to Rahon personally is the myth. Her verifiable gift to Carrillo is the collage and the reconfigured space; the drip is the couple's. Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M3) carries "the Rahon/Paalen line" as stated; this study refines the line's address without breaking it. **Takeaway:** Credit the household, not the myth. Watch the attribution: when the source says "Rahon y Paalen," do not shorten it to "Rahon."

## The engine in one paragraph

Rahon never stopped being a poet; she just changed the instrument from the pen to the nail. The engine: lay a geological ground (sand, ash, dye), incise the image by removing matter (sgraffito), inscribe it with a private pictography learned from cave walls and petroglyphs, and leave the color unmixed so it blends in the viewer's eye. The subject is always an invocation - a conjuration of the invisible, in her 1951 words - and the recurring cast is small: cats, birds, moons, rivers, cities seen as apparitions, the dead addressed by name. She renamed herself the way she painted: scratch away the given surface (Philippot, 1904, poet) and inscribe the chosen one (Rahon, Mexican, painter, cave). The work is Mexican not by iconography but by matter and by company: the sand is local, the exile circle is the subject's address book, and the abstraction she ventured into in the 1940s - when abstraction "was not accepted in Mexico" (Arcq) - made her one of the country's first abstractionists alongside Merida, Paalen, and Gerzso. In one line: a poet who learned to paint by scratching, who made the canvas a cave wall and the painting a spell.

## Aesthetic logic

INBAL's phrase is the key: "Su obra fue siempre un ejercicio de ingravidez" - her work was always an exercise in weightlessness. Everything floats: the dot-figures, the apparition cities, the suspended kites in the vertical rain, the heron in blue. The weightlessness is earned by the heaviness of the ground: the heavier the sand, the lighter the apparition. Her own Altamira hypothesis (via poetryalquimia) completes the logic: the cave paintings "fueron hechas por mujeres que debieron quedarse en reposo" - made by women who had to rest - which is to say, made by someone like her, immobilized, working from the bed and the garden. The immobilized body makes weightless images. The poetry never left: the paintings are "impressions," in her word - "making present something, even if it wasn't directly representing it" (Borowitz via TheArtStory). Breton's "marvellous" - "the eruption of contradictions within the real" (Aragon via TheArtStory) - is the thermostat: the real (sand, feathers, street paper) erupting into the marvelous (the conjured city, the wound that is a cat).

## Load-bearing corrections (14)

C1. BIRTH YEAR 1904 (8 June, Chenecey-Buillon). Her own later claim - 1916 in Brittany - is still printed by Christie's and Bonhams ("1916-1987"). Use 1904; the falsification is material (M6).
C2. BIRTH NAME Alice Marie Yvonne Philippot. "Philppot"/"Ivonne" are source typos.
C3. PAALEN DIVORCE 1947, not 1946 (1946 is the citizenship year; alicerahon.org chronology).
C4. FITZGERALD MARRIAGE 1947-1960 (alicerahon.org). Canadian director/scenographer for Bunuel.
C5. FUMAGE IS PAALEN'S. Invented 1936 (Tate, Sotheby's, Weinstein, Wikipedia). Never attribute it to Rahon.
C6. NO POURING BY RAHON. No source documents it. Her drip is dragged verticals (M8). The MPBA "chorreado o dripping" is credited to the Rahon/Paalen pair; UNAM credits the automatism's introduction to Paalen. The doorway premise is revised, not confirmed.
C7. DEATH September 1987 (month certain; the 27th is single-source, poetryalquimia).
C8. FIRST SOLO 1944 GAM. The 1940 show was the group International Exhibition of Surrealism (3 watercolors).
C9. "FIRST US SOLO IN 45/55 YEARS": MOCA 2019 says 55 years since Houston 1964; Wendi Norris 2022 says 45+. Gallery-PR rounding; carry both.
C10. SAND/SGRAFFITO NOT HER INVENTION. TheArtStory: adopted mid-1940s, "first used by Rufino Tamayo." She pioneered their poetic/surrealist use.
C11. THE FILM (Le Magicien, 1947): sources conflict on finished-vs-lost-before-completion. Screened at Willard NY; only stills survive.
C12. SABLIER COUCHE illustrator: Miro (institutional sources). The Picasso version is a single unconfirmed line.
C13. 1955 Paris solo at Galerie La Cour d'Ingres, owned by her sister Geo Dupin.
C14. She "objected to having the term 'abstract' applied to her work" (TheArtStory); she called them "impressions." Qualify the abstractionist label.
