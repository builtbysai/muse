# Bruno Hellenbosch: Study Notes

**Site:** Bruno Hellenbosch, a Brussels engraver and artist ("graveur et artiste bruxellois") -- no birth year, no birthplace, no school and no teacher established in any source read (see C3916). A painter first who developed, in parallel, a monumental woodcut practice; Fred Penelle's other duo partner, the only collaborator who shares his medium. With Penelle he formed The Two Jimies in 2008 and made live frescoes before an audience from collages of enlarged woodcuts plus painting and drawing, an ephemeral work at each performance (C3902). His largest known undertaking is a woodcut Decameron, 100 plates in a ten-part series, built with the Michael Woolworth workshop in Paris from 2019 (C3909). Latest dated activity: "Nos geantes", a group show at the Centre de la Gravure, La Louviere, January-June 2024 (C3914); nothing dated 2025 or 2026 was found.

## The life: what the record does and does not say

Nothing is known from the sources read about Hellenbosch's life before 2008 -- no childhood, no family background, no schooling, no teacher. The record starts when he joins Penelle to form The Two Jimies, and even that start is told through Penelle's biography, not his own: there is no Wikipedia page for Hellenbosch in any language, no solo biographical page was found, and the one profile that should fill the gap -- the Bruzz piece of 3 March 2021 -- could not be opened by any leg or by the study author, its existence confirmed only through French Wikipedia's reference list (C3915, C4032). The only usable bound on his age is indirect: the Prix de la Gravure et de l'Image imprimee he won in 2007 is reserved for artists aged 25-45, which would put his birth year roughly between 1962 and 1982 -- an inference from the prize rules, not a biographical fact (C3906). This study states no birth year.

What the record does give is a practice in two registers, held in parallel. He is described as a painter first, who "in parallel to his activity as a painter" developed a monumental woodcut practice in recent years (C3908). He won the Prix de la Gravure at La Louviere in 2007 and "since then has kept on carving plates, exploring ever larger formats" (C3905, C3908). His gallery is Galerie DYS, 84 Rue de l'Arbre Benit, Ixelles, Brussels, where he has had solo shows in 2009, 2010, 2012 and 2021 -- the first three listed with no title and no work list, the last titled "Woodcut" (C3904). The 2021 show is the one the March 2021 Brussels culture listing describes in full, and it is the fullest account of his method anywhere in the record (C3908).

The arc after the duo is a turn from the ephemeral to the monumental. The Two Jimies circuit runs 2008-2013 (C3903); the Decameron begins at the Woolworth workshop in January 2019 and is still the subject of a two-artist show at Tournai in late 2023 (C3909, C3960). Named single works appear only from 2020 -- Fra Angelico, Triptyque -- and the work extends into a self-published book in 2023 and into co-curating the same year (C3962, C3963, C3964, C3961). The most recent dated activity is the "Nos geantes" group show, January-June 2024, where he exhibited alongside Penelle himself and some forty other print artists (C3914). Current activity after 2024 is unverified, not asserted as inactive.

## M1 -- The live fresco: made in front of an audience

The first mechanism is the one that makes Hellenbosch the #609 pick, and it is the print-native mirror of Mecaniques Discursives. In 2008 he and Penelle formed The Two Jimies and made frescoes directly in front of an audience, from collages of enlarged woodcuts plus different painting and drawing techniques, forming an ephemeral work at each performance (C3902, C4001). The fresco is built live, watched, and then it is gone -- the next performance makes a different one from the same vocabulary. This predates Mecaniques Discursives (2011) by three years: the same cut-enlarge-paste grammar, the same wall scale, the same live, in-situ making -- but with paint and drawing in front of an audience instead of video projection (C3902). The circuit is the evidence: White Hotel and Nuit Blanche in Brussels in 2008; Slick Dessin in Paris, Galerie d'Ys in Brussels and Nuit Blanche at Bozar in 2009; Mediatine, Fete de l'Iris, PixHell in Namur, Galerie Anarto in Antwerp and the White Hotel 5th anniversary in 2010; Jubilation Heroique at the Centre Wallonie-Bruxelles in Paris in 2012; Nuit Blanche, Artour in La Louviere and Plage des 6 Pompes in La Chaux-de-Fonds in 2013 (C3903). No image of a Two Jimies fresco was found in this run -- all four catalogue entries rest on prose -- so the mechanism is known from the definition, not from a picture. The collaboration did not end in 2013 in any stated way; 2013 is simply the last dated appearance, and the Centre de la Gravure's Chroniques show of 2018-2019 records Penelle's print overflowing onto the walls "through painting with Bruno Hellenbosch" (C3959), so the two were still working a wall together five years later.

**Takeaway:** build the image in public, once, and let it vanish. The recombination is the event; the audience watches the vocabulary get dealt, and the wall is only where it happens to land.

## M2 -- The image-bank: everything at once

The second mechanism is the vocabulary itself, and it is horror vacui as an encyclopedia. He draws live from the imagery of art history, integrating iconographic elements from Renaissance painting and from the peoples of Papua: from the unexpected meeting of gargoyles and shrimps, African masks and the Hydra of Lerna, an Annunciation and an Egyptian ankh (key of life), a portrait of Basquiat and the Facebook logo, comes the feeling of facing the whole history of humanity at once (C3907). He is inspired by the great texts of literature since Gilgamesh: a universal encyclopedia of human accomplishment in all that is derisory, funny and brilliant (C3907). The March 2021 listing compares the spectator's wonder to that once stirred by temple frescoes, church fronton details and manuscript illuminations -- "when the reading of an image opens a door onto something that goes beyond Man" (C3907). A London group-show text from 2011 describes the same sensibility from the outside: "intricate universes that bring to mind fairy story, gothic landscapes suffused with an overgrown, overblown, out-of-control nature" (C3913). The inspected Decameron total view confirms it at the level of the mark: a dense black-and-white field of hundreds of motifs -- a large bird, masks, a shrine, a deer, a shrimp, a church, framed portraits, fragments reading LEX and MAY -- with no empty ground and no hierarchy between the sacred and the trivial (C3969). Nothing is explained and nothing is given a key; the density is the meaning.

**Takeaway:** fill the field until every motif is the same size. When a shrimp and an Annunciation share a wall at equal weight, the viewer stops ranking and starts reading.

## M3 -- The monumental matrix: five metres of wood

The third mechanism is scale, and it is the whole direction of his solo practice. In January 2019 he arrived at the Michael Woolworth workshop in Paris with the first part of a ten-part woodcut series: he had carved 100 wood plates creating an immense fresco of the Decameron, the medieval story written by Boccaccio, to be produced as a limited edition for its first presentation in May 2019 at the Fine Arts Museum of Caen, a production by Allia Editions (C3909). The workshop's January 2020 diary records day 5 of 9 of the first editioned delivery: Boccaccio's book "is split into 9 days", and Hellenbosch "carved a 5M x 2M woodcut mural in ten pieces for each day", each print 105 x 75 cm, printed at 10 copies (C3910). The two diary entries disagree -- 100 plates in a ten-part series against 9 days times 10 pieces, and "9 days" against Boccaccio's famous 10 -- and this study does not resolve them (C3910). What is not in dispute is the move: from the ephemeral fresco that vanishes after the performance to a mural five metres wide, cut in ten pieces a day, editioned at ten copies, and still the subject of a show four years later at Tournai, where the school programme describes "on many wood panels full of symbols, a host of references from Van Eyck to Ghostbusters, a picture-trail to scrutinise" (C3957). The inspected Decameron matrix close-up shows what five metres is made of: black carved wood with pale wood showing where cut, a giant grinning skull, a domed cathedral, a Star of David, crosses, a keyhole and key, a bicycle wheel at the bottom edge, small human figures (C3969).

**Takeaway:** make the plate the size of a wall and cut it in pieces a day can finish. Monumental scale built from daily units is a schedule, not a miracle.

## M4 -- The matrix as object: scars on show

The fourth mechanism is the one the 2021 "Woodcut" show made visible, and it is the most directly portable of his solo devices. The engraved matrix, worked and inked, becomes sculpture and painting, tool and result: it carries the physical marks of the gesture, of what is removed so that something else appears, and the gallery wanted to show the matrix itself, with its moving and noble scars (C3908). The show also presented pulls closer to monotype -- offprints of the matrix reworked, redrawn and redrawn again (C3908). This is Penelle's dictionary (M1 in #608) with the emphasis moved from the image to the tool: the block is not a means to an edition but the object itself, exhibited with its cuts visible, and the prints are not editions but reworkings, each pull a new drawing on the same scarred ground. The Decameron matrix close-up is the evidence at the level of the surface: the pale wood showing where the gouge has been is the drawing, and the black that remains is the ground (C3969).

**Takeaway:** exhibit the tool, not just the print. A matrix with its scars visible is a complete object; a pull reworked until it is a monotype is a second work, not a copy.

## M5 -- The sacred frame, profaned

The fifth mechanism is the collision inside the frame. Fra Angelico, a woodcut of 160 x 120 cm dated 2020, is a black ground with white incised lines: a central arched Annunciation scene, a winged angel facing a robed Mary, ringed by a dense symbolic border of African masks, a chameleon, an eye, small framed portraits, and the words INRI and HELL (C3962, C3969). Triptyque, a woodcut dated 2021, is a stark black-and-white triptych of the Crucifixion and related Passion scenes, white incised lines on a solid black ground: the centre panel the crucified Christ beneath the inscription INRI, two robed mourning figures at the foot of the cross, the side panels a building with a cross-marked door and a hilltop Golgotha with a large robed female figure (C3963). The film synopsis names the same move in one line: a universe "where old and new, the beautiful and the trivial, Durer's shadow and a Lidl logo come together" (C3912). The frame is always the sacred one -- the arch, the triptych, the Annunciation, the Crucifixion -- and the profanation is always inside it, at the same scale, in the same white line: the supermarket logo, the cartoon animal, the pop fragment. The Tournai programme's "from Van Eyck to Ghostbusters" is the same sentence in curatorial form (C3957).

**Takeaway:** keep the sacred frame and fill it with the profane at equal weight. The arch does the reverence; the Lidl logo does the joke; neither cancels the other.

## M6 -- The book as a portable fresco

The sixth mechanism is the book, and it is the fresco made portable. A mon seul desir, self-published in 2023, comes with a head edition including a print, sold through the Saint-Martin bookshop (C3964). The inspected cover is silver-grey with a large X over black-and-white woodcut fragments visible in the four triangles; the head-edition print is an abstract black-and-white woodcut on cream paper, biomorphic black shapes over dense small text-like marks, pencil-signed and numbered at the bottom (C3969). The title is courtly love -- "to my only desire" -- and the object is the opposite of courtly: a self-published book with an X across its cover, the woodcut broken into fragments and reassembled by the cover's geometry. It is the Two Jimies collage reduced to the size of a book and the Decameron image-bank reduced to an abstract field: the same cut-and-recombine grammar, but the wall is now a page and the audience is one reader holding it. The print in the head edition is the matrix-as-object of M4 at book scale -- a complete image that is also a fragment of a larger vocabulary.

**Takeaway:** when the wall is unavailable, shrink the fresco to a book. The collage grammar survives the scale change; the X on the cover is the new wall.

## M7 -- The painting that never left

The seventh mechanism is the register the woodcut story keeps pushing aside, and it matters because it is the first one. He is a painter first, and the monumental woodcut developed in parallel to painting, not instead of it (C3908). The only painting in the catalogue with an inspected image is c-cedille: a collage painting of three cows on a ground of newspaper and dictionary paper, with French text fragments (TABLE DES MATIERES, EPO, FOODS) and blue-green painted passages (C3965, C3969). It is the one work in the catalogue that is not black and white. The cows are ordinary, the ground is printed paper, the paint is a flat blue-green wash -- the same cut-and-paste grammar as the woodcuts, but the cut material is the daily newspaper and the dictionary, and the colour is allowed in because paint, unlike the woodcut, does not have to choose between black and white. The painting is the reminder that the woodcut's starkness is a choice, not a limit: the same hand, the same image-bank, with colour switched on.

**Takeaway:** keep one register in colour. A practice that is black and white by choice needs a coloured work beside it, or the choice stops reading as a choice.

## M8 -- The repeat stage: same room, new fresco

The eighth mechanism is the venue as a frame that stays while the image changes. The Two Jimies did not tour new rooms each time; they returned to the same ones. The White Hotel hosted them in 2008, again in 2009 (as Galerie d'Ys) and again for its 5th anniversary in 2010; Nuit Blanche in Brussels hosted them in 2008, 2009 (at Bozar) and 2013 (C4002). Paris got two visits, Slick Dessin in 2009 and the Centre Wallonie-Bruxelles in 2012; the circuit closed at Plage des 6 Pompes, a Swiss street-arts festival, in 2013 (C4002). The repeat stage is the mechanism: the audience comes back to the same room to see what the vocabulary does this time, and the room's memory of the last fresco is part of the new one. It is the live-fresco version of Penelle's dictionary -- the same archive, dealt again, in a room that remembers the last deal. The solo practice keeps the same logic at a slower tempo: the Decameron returns to Caen in 2019, to the Woolworth workshop in 2020, to Tournai in 2023 -- the same hundred stories, cut again, in a new room (C3909, C3954, C3960).

**Takeaway:** return to the same room. A repeated venue turns each new work into a variation, and the audience's memory of the last one is free material.

## The aesthetic logic: the whole of human history on one wall

Hellenbosch's logic is the encyclopedia as a wall, and the wall as a place where everything is the same size. The Decameron is the right book for him because it is already his method: a hundred tales told in ten days by ten young people hiding from the plague, every register -- sacred, bawdy, tragic, comic -- told in the same voice, in the same room, while death waits outside Florence (C3909, C3960). His wall does the same thing in images: the Annunciation and the Facebook logo, the Hydra of Lerna and the shrimp, Durer's shadow and the Lidl logo, Van Eyck and Ghostbusters, all cut in the same white line on the same black ground, all the same size (C3907, C3912, C3957). The black ground is the great leveller: on white paper a hierarchy would creep in, but on black, cut white, everything is equally a hole in the dark.

The evolution runs in one clean arc: collaborative, live and ephemeral (2008-2013) -- monumental and solo (2019-2023) -- named single works, book and curating (2020-2023) -- group presence (2024). The early solo shows are untitled and undescribed, the duo's frescoes survive only as a definition, and the biography itself is missing; the record thins as you go back, and the work that survives best is the work that got biggest. Colour is essentially absent from the woodcut side -- black ground, white incised lines, the only exception seen being the blue-green wash of the collage painting c-cedille -- and the scale is the signature: five metres of wood, cut ten pieces a day, editioned at ten, is a practice that measures itself in days and metres rather than in editions. The honest summary of the record is also its sharpest description, from the film synopsis: very large wood engravings, a universe where old and new, the beautiful and the trivial come together (C3912).
