## Core engine

WALK THE INSTRUMENT. Dozens of long steel wires (13 to 30 meters, era-dependent) are tensioned across a room and attached perpendicularly to wooden resonators so they vibrate longitudinally in just intonation. The performer excites them by drawing rosin-coated fingertips lengthwise along the wire while walking the room's full length. Pitch, overtone content, and timing are governed by bodily locomotion through space. Her notation is half temporal score, half walking choreography: positions on the floor are marked the way other composers mark notes.

## Evolution (early vs recent)

- **1980, origins:** "Streetwalker", a metal skirt rigged with guitar strings running to her shoes, contact mic on the body. The BODY is the sound source before the room is. She already thinks like a builder (BFA sculpture background).
- **1980-1985, invention and debut:** The LSI is built in St. Paul then Brooklyn. First piece composed for the Echo festival at Het Apollohuis, Eindhoven, November 1984; residency and debut LP in 1985 (Apollo label, with Arnold Dreyblatt performing on "Woven Processional"). The era is austere solo drone: one performer, one fixed installation, walking the wires.
- **1985-2005, refinement:** Cassettes, "Body Music" (1993), "Change of Direction", Ort (2004), the first time she SINGS on a record. Collaborations with Oliveros, Dreyblatt, Scelsi influence, dance commissions ("Staggered Stasis"). Still one installation played by her or by ensembles she teaches.
- **2006-2019, duo era:** Theresa Wong enters as collaborator (Roulette workshop 2006); "Harbors" (Room40, 2020). Workshops and teachability become a mode: the LSI becomes a host instrument other people can inhabit. Trombone duets, split LP with Eleh, Barn Owl collaboration.
- **2020-2026, maximal and percussive era:** "Elemental View", 136 strings, six movements, three performers (The Living Earth Show: Travis Andrews, Andy Meyerson). Newly invented percussion tools: the box bow, the shovelette, and the shoveler, which strike or bow groups of 3, 6, or 9 strings at once (frame f18: Meyerson gripping the wooden shoveler). "Soundless" with Wong, co-composed 2024-26, runs both players through 4-channel amplification so the room becomes a dimensional sound field (Meyer Sound labs involved). Installations grow into industrial halls, churches, Tadao Ando's Bourse de Commerce rotunda (frame f03: strings stretched low across the dome floor, performer dwarfed). A 2026 commission for Splinter Reeds ("Social Fabric") may be her first major work decentering the LSI.
- **Through-line:** Every decade the string count and installation scale grow, and she invents tools that do more at once (one string, then a hand of strings, then percussive groupings). The gesture, walking with rosin, never changes.

## What she builds (the instrument, technically)

- Steel music wire stretched 13-30 m across a performance space, tuned in just intonation. The wire is clamped perpendicular to a wooden resonator box or soundboard bridge; excitation is LONGITUDINAL (along the string's axis), not transverse, which produces the organ-like, slowly blooming overtone texture no plucked string makes.
- The bow is a fingertip coated in liquid rosin (rosin powder dissolved in isopropyl alcohol; frame f01 shows her making it at a workbench before Munich performances). The finger is drawn along the wire like a bow, never plucked. Frame f15: rosin-white fingertips pinching the strings, subtitled "like floating, or surfing, or flying."
- The room is the sounding body. Each installation is tuned to the space: church halls (frame f17: vertical strings hanging beside a chandelier, mirrored floor doubling them), a concrete rotunda (frame f03), an industrial truss hall (frame f12: install in progress, strings from ceiling trusses, flight cases).
- Maintenance is visible craft: frame f06 shows go-bar clamps bending during a soundboard bridge glue-up. The instrument is constantly remade.
- Found-object percussion in the early era: the Water Drip Drum (1985 LP), water dripping into an amplified aluminum pan, pitch-bent by foot pedal.

## Observed mechanisms (for prompt work)

1. **The walk as score:** floor position selects harmony; her notation marks positions. Watchable as a body traversing, sound morphing with distance.
2. **Rosin-finger bow stroke:** fingertip drawn along the string axis like a bow; sustained organ-like overtones; never plucked.
3. **Longitudinal vibration:** wires vibrating along their length against perpendicular resonators; nodal rosettes (f05 album art).
4. **Architecture as resonator:** the room is tuned to each install; scale dwarfs the performer (f03, f12, f17).
5. **The shoveler / box bow:** wooden tools striking or bowing 3, 6, 9 strings at once; percussive rhythm inside pure drone (f18).
6. **Water Drip Drum:** water dripping into an amplified pan, pitch-bent by foot pedal; found-object percussion inside a drone piece.
7. **Duet infiltration:** Wong's cello or guitar "fits snugly inside" the LSI drones; Soundless runs both through 4-channel amplification so the room becomes a dimensional field.
8. **Rosin craft as ritual:** making liquid rosin, glueing bridges; maintenance as visible performance labor (f01, f06).
