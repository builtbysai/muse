# Manuel Felguerez: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #330.

**Site:** Manuel Felguerez Barra (Valparaiso, Zacatecas, 12 December 1928 - Mexico City, 8 June 2020, age 91, Covid-19), Mexican painter, sculptor, muralist and printmaker of the Generacion de la Ruptura; the Ruptura's machine-man. Hacienda boy turned scrap-welder: two months at San Carlos (1948), Paris 1949-56 under Ossip Zadkine (who let him watch Brancusi work), first solo 1958 at Galeria Antonio Souza, then thirty years of welding junk into murals (Mural de Hierro 1961, Canto al oceano 1963), the Espacio multiple exhibition 1973 (Octavio Paz prologue, Gran Premio Sao Paulo 1975), the computer project La maquina estetica with engineer Mayer Sasson (Harvard, Guggenheim 1975), a 1990s return to baroque relief painting, and a late career of urban monuments (Puerta 1808, Crisalida, Agenda 2030 at the UN). Founder-donor of the Museo de Arte Abstracto Manuel Felguerez in Zacatecas (decided 1997, opened 8 September 1998); his ashes rest in its memorial. Three marriages: Ruth Rohde (1951-1959), Lilia Carrillo (1960-1974), Mercedes Oteyza (1974-2020). Never depended on art sales; thirty years a professor (UNAM researcher 1977-1990); "productor y vendedor de placer estetico." Identity anchors: the mural assembled, not painted; junk as vocabulary; the eight-element ideogram alphabet and its containment rules; chaos thrown, then ordered with precision; the mirror that reproduces space; the picture inside the picture; the machine as process, never product; variation inside limits; the city as habitat, the museum as the will.

**Doorway:** Hop from the gunther-gerzso study: from the Ruptura's architect to its sculptor-abstractionist. #329 was the man who built the Ruptura's geometry as architecture: masonry stacked course on course, additive-only, the wall as protagonist, the dent as plot. #330 is the man who welded the Ruptura's geometry out of junk, ran it through a mainframe, and refused to found a school. Gerzso never erased; Felguerez never repeats. The traveling correction: no "Exposicion Solar" exists anywhere in the record (see C1); the real hinge exhibitions were El espacio multiple (1973) and La maquina estetica (1975). Also traveling: he was Lilia Carrillo's widower (#327), keeper of her archive, and the reason the MAAMF museum holds a Carrillo room at all.

## M1. The sculpted mural (murales de desecho)

His early career's signature (all sources): the mural is not painted, it is assembled. Mural de Hierro (1961): a 30-meter wall of welded chatarra (scrap iron) on colored cement for the Cine Diana on Paseo de la Reforma; the press called it a provocation because it referred to nothing, not the Revolution, not the people. The inauguration was a happening directed by Alejandro Jodorowsky: more than a thousand people, a choir declaiming poetry. "Organizada la chatarra con una impecable orden, testimonia la memoria de la ciudad" (BiCentenario): the junk is organized, not dumped. Observed in the viewed photo: a continuous diagonal composition of welded steel chunks, biomorphic and geometric at once, lit warm. Two iron retablos and the grand mural went to the Diana; a chatarra curtain to the Casa de la Paz (1964); the same procedure at the Deportivo Bahia (Canto al oceano, 1963: oyster, abalone and mother-of-pearl shells, wire, reinforced concrete, 500 by 800 cm, opening celebrated with a multi-day happening, a hundred actors and dancers under Jodorowsky, 3000 attendees). Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M1) built up paint and carved INTO it; Felguerez skips the paint entirely and builds the mural OUT of the material. **Takeaway:** Weld the mural; do not paint it. Watch the surface: when the wall is an assemblage of industrial parts held in impeccable order, the mechanism is on.

## M2. Junk as vocabulary (the postwar roads)

Nexos (end-to-end): as a boy he took mechanical objects apart and reassembled them easily. In 1947, at nineteen, he walked from Frankfurt to Rome through devastated country, and the wrecked tanks, cars and trucks on the roadsides "like scrap iron" left the decisive impression. Back in Mexico he started working with industrial waste and chatarra, recycling them into murals and public projects. The junk vocabulary precedes any composition: gears, machine parts, wire, shells. Del Conde: his "tecnica" is "la metamorfosis que convierte los materiales virgenes en una composicion acabada" (the metamorphosis converting virgin materials into a finished composition). Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M8) stacked stone courses as masonry; Felguerez stacks salvage. **Takeaway:** Collect the material vocabulary before composing; let the scrap teach the form. Watch the vocabulary: when the composition is made of recognizable machine parts in geometric order, the mechanism is on.

## M3. The eight-element ideogram alphabet

The Maquina Estetica rules, from Felguerez's own book as quoted by Rita Eder (DGCS, end-to-end): (a) every design lives in a horizontal 6 x 8 unit format; (b) each of the four invisible margins of the containing rectangle must be touched by at least one of the integrating simple geometric elements; (c) for graphic representation the form sits in an 8 x 10 unit total (one unit of background around; 7 x 9 for painting); (d) the eight elements: the rectangle (vertical, horizontal, or square); the circle; the isosceles triangle (ALWAYS up); the semicircle in four positions; rectangle-plus-semicircle in four positions; rectangle with two blunt quarter-circle corners in four positions; rhombus with vertical parallels and diagonals equivalent to half an isosceles triangle; rhombus with horizontal parallels. His own reasoning: "a traves de la aplicacion de la teoria de Identificacion de Sistemas, se puede llegar a elaborar un programa que permita... la produccion 'infinita' de nuevos ideogramas, disenos con todas las caracteristicas de estilo que definen la obra plastica de un autor." The result is a program that could "predict" his paintings for the next million years (origenarts). Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M1) drew two diagonals as the lawful skeleton and built masonry on it; Felguerez's law is not a skeleton but a VOCABULARY with containment rules, and the style is what the rules emit. **Takeaway:** Define the alphabet and the grammar first; the style is what the rules emit. Watch the margins: when geometric elements always touch the frame edges and the triangle never points down, the mechanism is on.

## M4. Rule-bound infinity, and why he quit

Two quits are documented. Del Conde: he realized the production of new ideograms reflecting all his formal constants would have meant an infinite production of designs, "y la vida no solo es finita, sino corta" (life is not only finite but short). So he abandoned the computer experiments. Origenarts quotes the second quit: he "sentia que sus dibujos salian hechos con regla" (felt his drawings came out made with a ruler) and wanted to appreciate the imperfection of natural hand-drawn strokes. The computer, he said, "es un instrumento como cualquier otro." Crucially, the machine never delivered paintings: "lo que se obtenia no eran cuadros, la computadora me proporcionaba el diseno y la estructura. Se trataba de dibujos, ideogramas que me proporcionaban la idea y los usaba para hacer una pintura o una escultura." Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M12) ended in white dissolution; Felguerez ends the computer chapter in a declaration of hand imperfection and finite life. **Takeaway:** Let the machine propose; never let it finish. Watch the handoff: when the plotted design is only the structure and the painting is made by hand on top of it, the mechanism is on.

## M5. The multiple space (few elements, three dimensions)

El espacio multiple (MAM, 1973, at the instigation of Fernando Gamboa, catalog prologue by Octavio Paz): Del Conde describes it as predominantly geometric and rigorous: "Del plano pasaba al relieve, y de alli a la tridimension exenta, precisamente 'espejeando' una serie de elementos. No eran muchos elementos, sino que la combinacion entre ellos determinaba su variedad." Few elements, mirroring them, combination as variety. Paz on the show: "El espejo, que es el instrumento filosofico por excelencia: emisor de imagenes y critico de las imagenes que emite, ocupa un lugar privilegiado en los objetos plasticos de Felguerez: es un re-productor de espacios." The germ of the idea was already in the 1972 computer phase: a system "auto-productivo" where the author constantly participates (en.wiki). The works were polychrome reliefs; it won the Gran Premio at the XIII Sao Paulo Bienal 1975. Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M2) accumulated additively and never subtracted; Felguerez transmutes the SAME few elements across dimensions: plane, relief, free-standing object. **Takeaway:** Take one vocabulary and run it through plane, relief, and free space. Watch the transmutation: when the same few shapes appear as painting, then as relief, then as sculpture, the mechanism is on.

## M6. Chaos thrown, then ordered (with precision)

His 2018 interview formula (designhunter, full text): "La creacion es un caos que regresa al orden: Aviento la pintura, creo un caos y despues con toda precision le doy orden" (I throw the paint, I create chaos, and then with total precision I give it order). Juan Villoro's gloss on the same duality (from the Trayectorias/Limite de una secuencia catalog, via del Conde): "El sentido de orden convive con una acendrada rebeldia, la disciplina de las formas con el vertigo de las texturas y las grafias delirantes." Observed in the viewed Sin titulo (Museo Espacio): a ground of ochre and gold, violent black scribbled loops underneath, broad white ribbon strokes laid over in decisive curves, cyan-teal margins at the frame edges. The chaos is real but bounded; the order is applied after, precisely. Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M8) meditated long and painted swift; Felguerez throws fast and orders slow. **Takeaway:** Throw the chaos first; order it with total precision after. Watch the layers: when gestural violence sits under a geometric discipline that was clearly applied second, the mechanism is on.

## M7. The picture inside the picture (relief as truth, shadow as paint)

Del Conde's reading of the late reliefs (Landucci 2001 show): the constant is "el efecto del cuadro (disfrazado o incompleto siempre) dentro del cuadro"  -  the painting-inside-the-painting, sometimes trompe-l'oeil, sometimes real. Sombra del tiempo (160 x 180): a small canvas appears pasted INSIDE the canvas, its lower edge slightly frayed and lifting with an almost imperceptible painted shadow, "como una especie de pelicula adicional." Pacto secreto (160 x 190): the duplication is REAL this time and carries other elements in relief: wire mesh, cables, pieces of stiffened cloth  -  the technique of the 1960s murals returning. Tierra baldia (130 x 145): an irregular square projects a FICTIVE (painted) shadow on the support; Tierra de fuego (100 x 120): a small irregular red zone "chorrea hacia abajo" (drips downward), with the color "en balance dirias que exacto con las formas." Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M5) drew the crack and haloed it; Felguerez peels the painting itself, and sometimes the peel is real wire. **Takeaway:** Make the edge an event: let a layer lift, really or fictively. Watch the lift: when a smaller rectangle floats on the canvas and you cannot tell at first whether the shadow is painted or cast, the mechanism is on.

## M8. Titles as triggers (never illustrations)

Del Conde again: the paintings are "baptized" when their basic traits are already set, or titled after finishing; either way "los titulos pueden actuar como disparadores" (the titles can act as triggers)  -  but "Felguerez no procede jamas como ilustrador de algo" (he never proceeds as an illustrator of anything). The 1976 Maquina estetica oils prove the doctrine in titles: La energia del punto cero, Limite de una esencia, Cubo en busqueda de su identidad, Homenaje a Lobachevski, El automata de Hagelberger. Concepts, physics, mathematics, automata  -  names over geometric works that refuse to illustrate them. Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M7) titled with myth and site names (Mitologia, Yaxchilan); Felguerez titles with science and machines. Both refuse illustration; his trigger-words are technical. **Takeaway:** Baptize the painting with a technical concept; let the title trigger and the painting neutralize. Watch the label: when the name is physics and the canvas is geometry, the mechanism is on.

## M9. Variation inside limits (the recognizable machine)

His 2019 declaration to Pilar Garcia (MUAC Trayectorias, via Nexos): "siempre se reconoce cuando una obra es mia. Presumo no solo de variar, sino de variar dentro de unos limites." (You always recognize when a work is mine. I pride myself not only on varying, but on varying within limits.) Juan Garcia Ponce's critical version: "las formas de Manuel Felguerez tanto en la pintura como en la escultura son siempre reconocibles, le pertenecen de una manera inevitable, una y otra vez, en su diferencia, estas formas lo muestran... crean al artista que las crea" (the forms create the artist who creates them). Villoro: "Las matematicas y la geometria siempre han estado presentes en la imaginacion de Felguerez. Sus lienzos y esculturas reiteran formas como talismanes... La proporcion del cuadro y su colorida simetria son calculados en forma racional. Luego, este cuidado universo se desordena y cobra vida con pinceles y chisquetes temperamentales." Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M7) kept one small human thing alive inside every field; Felguerez keeps one vocabulary alive and varies it inside declared limits. **Takeaway:** Publish your limits; vary inside them so the signature reads. Watch the signature: when an anonymous work is instantly his, the mechanism is on.

## M10. Experiment as media-forcing

Juan Garcia Ponce's rule for him (via del Conde): "En las obras de Felguerez la experimentacion nunca es meramente formal, nunca se sacia a si misma, sino que es antes que nada una manera de forzar a sus medios expresivos, de obligarlos a ir mas alla, de trascenderse a si mismos para hacer posible el encuentro de esa forma..." (experimentation is never merely formal; it is a way of forcing his expressive means, obliging them to go beyond themselves). Del Conde's gloss: technique for Felguerez is "la metamorfosis que convierte los materiales virgenes en una composicion acabada." Process over finish: "el aspecto acabado o inacabado de una obra no cuenta, lo que cuenta es el proceso"  -  and he knows when to leave a work alone ("es certero en su propia mirada"). Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M15) finished when the thunder announced it; Felguerez stops because the media have been forced far enough, not because the object is done. **Takeaway:** Force the medium past itself; the finished state is a side effect. Watch the material: when paint, wire, shell and scrap are all coerced into the same geometric order, the mechanism is on.

## M11. The machine as thread (process, never product)

Nexos: "le interesaba entender la maquina como un proceso y como hilo conductor en su obra" (he was interested in understanding the machine as a process and as the thread of his work). The machine obsession runs the whole life: childhood disassembling of mechanical objects; the 1960s Club de los crononautas with Rene Rebetez and Jodorowsky (science-fiction fantasies as art talk, with Monsivais and Gironella around); the mobile La maquina del deseo (1973) for The Holy Mountain; the plotter ideograms; the title El automata de Hagelberger; the chrysalis of Crisalida (2014), where a whole Volkswagen was cut into small pieces and rebuilt as a metamorphosis of technology with NOTHING added or removed ("en este objeto todo esta presente, sin necesidad de eliminar o agregar piezas"). Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M11) kept theatre as his persisting stage; Felguerez kept the machine as his persisting process. **Takeaway:** Treat the machine as a thread of process, never as a finished object. Watch the machine: when it appears as a word, a drawing, a program, or a rebuilt car  -  anything but a product  -  the mechanism is on.

## M12. The city as habitat (and donation as economy)

The late career is urban infrastructure: Puerta 1808 (2007, Reforma/Juarez/Bucareli, viewed: white peeling slab with the black curved blade and the circular cutout); Muro de Calaveras (2009-2014, viewed: repeated pierced steel skull-cells, nearly half a kilometer of pedestrian wall); Ecuacion en Acero (2010, SEP); Crisalida (2014); Agenda 2030 (2018, 2x5m at the UN in New York). The economy behind it: "nunca dependio de la venta de arte para vivir" (en.wiki); thirty years a professor; MUAC Trayectorias got 38 donated sculptures plus the archive; the Zacatecas museum got the collection; "ya todo esta donado de antemano" (everything is already donated in advance, 2020 interview). The protest of 1968: he withdrew Mexico 68 from the Olympic Village entrance over the student repression, and only donated it to the MAM years later; the public named it "el barco" and he adopted the name. Against the chain: Gerzso (#329 M13) ended distilling toward the wall itself; Felguerez ended occupying the city and giving the work away. **Takeaway:** Build for the street; donate the studio. Watch the placement: when the work is urban furniture with his signature on it and nobody bought it, the mechanism is on.

## The engine in one paragraph

Felguerez started as a sculptor who wanted machines, became a painter who welded murals, then a programmer who fed his own style into a mainframe, then a baroque relief-painter, then the city's monument-maker. The engine never changes: fix a small geometric vocabulary, define strict containment rules (touch the margins, triangle always up), then vary inside those limits forever. The work is Mexican not by subject but by material fact: chatarra from the postwar roads recycled into murals, shells and wire into the Bahia, the tzompantli rebuilt as pierced steel, a Beetle cut up and rebuilt as a chrysalis. Experiment is how he forces the medium past itself; the computer episode (1975-77) proved that rules can generate infinite designs and that a finite life should stop at infinity. Paz said his propositions enter through the eyes and the touch, not the ears, and that the mirror in his work is a reproducer of spaces; del Conde showed the same few elements transmuted from plane to relief to free-standing object, espejeando themselves. In one line: a machine-thread run through a small alphabet of shapes, from welded scrap to plotter paper to city monuments, always within limits, always signed.

## Load-bearing corrections (14)

C1. NO "EXPOSICION SOLAR." The brief offered it as a 1971-72 anchor. No source in EN or ES shows any Felguerez exhibition or work under that title (two quoted searches). The real hinge shows of that window: El espacio multiple (MAM 1973, catalog prologue by Octavio Paz) and La maquina estetica (project 1975, Harvard/Guggenheim). Never use the anchor.
C2. SAN CARLOS DURATION. Institutional summaries say four months (1948). Felguerez himself, in his final 2020 interview: "estuve dos meses." Carry his own figure.
C3. BIRTHPLACE. Officially registered at Orizaba 150, Mexico City (es.wiki). Felguerez always claimed the San Agustin del Vergel hacienda, Valparaiso, Zacatecas. Carry both; the hacienda is his identity claim.
C4. BIRTH YEAR. 1928 in every institutional source (INBAL, Nexos, both wikis). The artehoy acervos page and its obra-publica flyer print "1929" - a single-gallery house error. Use 1928.
C5. NAME SPELLING. The Spanish spelling accents the e (Felguerez). The unaccented form in the brief and older sources is typographic sloppiness; this file is ASCII-only, but note the correct spelling has the accent.
C6. HOMENAJE A UXMAL DATE. artehoy flyer: 1961 (Museo Nacional de Antropologia). Nexos: 1964. Conflict carried.
C7. CARRILLO MARRIAGE YEAR. en.wiki infobox prints 1959. The Art Story narrative and the #327 load-bearing research: divorced Rohde 1959, married Carrillo in Washington in 1960. Use 1960.
C8. MAQUINA ESTETICA DATING. Project developed 1975 at Harvard (Guggenheim); the 12 oil paintings of the series are 1976 (Centeno, CENIDIAP); the UNAM book 1975-76. Do not collapse El espacio multiple (1973 show) into it.
C9. MURO DE CALAVERAS DATE. artehoy flyer: 2009-2014 (Museo Nacional de Antropologia). It is a dated range, not undated.
C10. "KINETIC" SCOPE. Do not claim a large kinetic oeuvre. Documented machines: La maquina del deseo (1973 mobile, The Holy Mountain, later at Paramo), Geometria suspendida (2003 suspended mobile), Crisalida (2014, deconstructed-rebuilt VW, static). His machines live in titles, ideograms, and the computer program.
C11. MAYER SASSON. Teresa del Conde prints "Mayer Sazon." All other sources: Mayer Sasson, Harvard systems/mathematics engineer. Use Sasson.
C12. MUSEUM DATING. Decision 1997 (governor Arturo Romo Gutierrez approached him at the door, not the office). Opened 8 September 1998. Not "opened 1997." Donation condition: dedicated to abstract art of his generation and after.
C13. FIRST SHOW VS FIRST SOLO. The 1954 Instituto Frances de America Latina show sold out entirely and earned him the French scholarship - but the first individual exhibition is 1958 at Galeria Antonio Souza. Do not backdate the solo.
C14. OSAKA NAMES. Eleven artists commissioned by Fernando Gamboa for Osaka 1970; MAAMF holds the "Los murales de Osaka" room. Sources print "Arnaldo Cohen" (Ibero) - the artist is Arnaldo Coen.
