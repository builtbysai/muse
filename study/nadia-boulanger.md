## M1. Strict counterpoint as the ground

What it is: species counterpoint and fugue as the non-negotiable base of all composition teaching, and Berkeley's "rigorous demands in strict counterpoint", in Dickinson's words, and Boulanger made Berkeley refrain from composing original music during his first year, according to Chandos, so the craft came first, and it is the ground of everything she taught, and Wikipedia's pedagogy section lists "traditional harmony, score reading at the piano, species counterpoint, analysis, and sight-singing", and her own 1904 first prizes included fugue, and the 1908 Prix de Rome scandal was literally about a fugue, because she submitted an instrumental fugue where a vocal fugue was required, and the watchable behaviour is two independent lines entering, obeying and diverging, and a fugue subject stated, answered, inverted and returned, and the piece is a discipline made audible.

**Takeaway:** the counterpoint must be the ground, not the ornament, and a piece that only decorates with fugue has missed the mechanism.

## M2. La grande ligne, the long line

What it is: Copland's report of her "one all-embracing principle", "the creation of what she called la grande ligne, the long line in music", and Copland's gloss is "a sense of forward motion, the feeling for inevitability, for the creating of an entire piece that could be thought of as a functioning entity", and it is the single test she applied to every score, and a piece either has a long line or it does not, and the watchable behaviour is a single melodic arc spanning the whole piece, and every local event a station on one unbroken trajectory, never a bead on a string, and the eye follows one line across the frame.

**Takeaway:** the long line must carry the whole piece, and a piece of beautiful episodes with no line through them is a necklace, not a grande ligne.

## M3. Analysis before composition

What it is: the student score read against the whole repertoire before a note is added, and her first comment on a score placed it beside Bach and Chopin, "these measures have the same harmonic progressions as Bach's F major prelude and Chopin's F major Ballade. Can you not come up with something more interesting?", in Wikipedia's report, and the lesson is comparative, and the student's bar is only interesting once it is shown to be different from the masters' bars, and the watchable behaviour is a motif shown, then shown again inside Bach, then inside Chopin, and the difference between the three is the lesson, and the screen becomes a comparative anatomy table.

**Takeaway:** the comparison must produce a difference, and an analysis that only finds similarities is a catalogue, not a lesson.

## M4. Fixed-Do solfege and ear-training discipline

What it is: sight-singing with fixed-Do solfege as daily drill, and she came third in the 1897 solfege competition at age nine and won first prize in 1898, and by twelve she knew the whole of Bach's Well-Tempered Clavier by heart, in Monsaingeon's account via Wikipedia, and Annette Dieudonne taught the rudiments and solfege to her beginners for decades, and the ear is trained before the hand, and the watchable behaviour is a line sung before it is played, and the ear leading and the hand following, and notes arriving already heard.

**Takeaway:** the ear must lead, and a line the singer cannot hear before playing is a line the composer has not yet heard either.

## M5. The Wednesday class as a listening laboratory

What it is: from autumn 1904, a Wednesday afternoon group class in analysis and sight-singing at 36 rue Ballu, continued almost until her death, seventy-five years of Wednesdays, and new students mixed with older ones, and the work equally difficult for all, in EBSCO's words, and Elliott Carter recalled that students who would not cross Paris through the February 1934 riots showed they did not "take music seriously enough", in Wikipedia's report, and the watchable behaviour is a room, a score, a group sight-reading together, and the same passage heard by beginners and masters at once, and the gap between their hearings made visible.

**Takeaway:** the class must mix the levels, and a class that separates beginners from masters hides the hearing that is the lesson.
