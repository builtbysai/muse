## M1. The fugitive's song

The outlaw is the singer, and the singer is the mechanism. He escapes first to Los Angeles, and then he goes on horseback upward, toward the high cordillera, to near the Atacalco pass, and the horseback is the fleeing, and the fleeing is the hiding, and the hiding is the cordillera, and the cordillera is the song, and the song is the mechanism. There, by the high lakes, by the Lago Laja, he lives with the arrieros who earn their living as cuatreros, stealing Argentine cattle to bring it across those hidden passes onto Chilean soil, and the living is the knowing, and the knowing is the song, and the song is the arrieros, and the arrieros are the mechanism, because the singer does not observe the outlaws, he hides among them, and the hiding is the singing, and the singing is the outlaw, and the outlaw is the singer, and the singer is the mechanism. The lyric says it plainly, and the plainness is the proof: "Los Angeles, Santa Fe, fueron nombres del infierno... hasta su casa llegaba, la ley buscando al cuatrero", and the law comes to the house looking for the cattle thief, and the looking is the song, and the song is the law, and the law is the house, and the house is the flight, and the flight is the mechanism. The pass itself is disputed in the press, and the dispute is carried, not settled: the N~uble papers place Atacalco in the village of Pinto, on the north bank of the Diguillin, at the start of the foothills, far from the border with Argentina, and La Tribuna corrects them, and the correction is the geography, and the geography is the pass in Antuco, at the northern end of the Bio Bio province, inside the Laguna del Laja National Park, reached by intricate cordillera roads or by crossing the lake in a boat, and the reaching is the hiding, and the hiding is the song, and the song is the pass, and the pass is the mechanism.
