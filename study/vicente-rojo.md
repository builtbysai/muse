# Vicente Rojo: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #326.

**Site:** Vicente Rojo Almazan (Barcelona, 15 March 1932 - Mexico City, 17 March 2021), Mexican painter, sculptor, and graphic designer, co-founder of Ediciones Era (1960), art director of Imprenta Madero (1962-1984), designer of some 700 books and covers. Octavio Paz's lifelong design partner: the executing typographer of "Blanco" (1967), the visual author of "Discos visuales" (1968), the designer of Vuelta. Painter of six named series (Senales, Negaciones, Recuerdos, Mexico bajo la lluvia, Escenarios, Escrituras) built on a single instrument: geometry as alphabet. Identity anchors: the letter T as a balanced event; negation as a working method; the series as a system; geometry as a second language; the cover as a first painting.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Octavio Paz study: from the poet who built machines on paper to the designer who built the machines with him. The correction travels with the hop: Rojo did NOT design "Blanco"; Paz designed it entirely himself, and Rojo with Joaquin Diez-Canedo executed the typography and jointly chose to stay uncredited. "Discos visuales" is the reverse half of the partnership: the visual part is Rojo's, and when Paz proposed a joint signature, Rojo declined it, crediting himself only as the author of the drawings. Paz's aphorism on him is the study's compass: "Riguroso como un geometra y sensible como un poeta," rigorous as a geometer and sensitive as a poet. #325 ended with the reader promoted to operator; #326 is the man who set the reader's table.

## M1. The T as formal event

"Negacion" (1971, Coleccion Blaisten, viewed): a deep teal-green field, about #1E5A4C, fills the lower two-thirds like a stage. Across the upper third a grid of horizontal and vertical bars forms a flattened T, a portico: bar segments in mottled greens, ochre yellow, pink, orange, gray, each cell carrying small drawn marks, circles, crosses, arrows, scribbles, drips, with visible underdrawing and black outlining. "Negacion no.9" (1972, viewed): the same composition solved monochrome, black white gray, a vertical column of small dark triangles like a harlequin strip descending from top center, crossed by rough horizontal bands, vertical streaks dripping downward, the surface sanded scraped smudged into weathered stone. Two solutions, one glyph. Rojo isolated the capital T because among the alphabet's letters it alone "brought together the vertical and the horizontal in perfect balance" (MAM group label). The meeting of the two strokes forms a square, and the square permits the diagonal. The Negaciones ran to about 200 large paintings, late 1960s to mid 1970s, each only a T, each discarding the last. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M6) made meaning a function of rotation angle; Rojo makes the composition a function of one letter, and the angle is fixed at ninety degrees. Chucho (#323 M1) made fragility the wager on tissue; Rojo makes balance the wager on canvas. **Takeaway:** Find the one glyph that holds your opposites in balance and solve it two hundred ways. Watch the meeting point: when the cross of the T makes a square, the mechanism is on.

## M2. Negation as engine

The series is called Negaciones because each T negated the previous one: "the new solution implied its automatic discard, so a new T had to be invented to be discarded in turn." Rojo's 1974 list reads like a working method stated as poetics: "Negaciones. Banalidades. Homenajes. Lugares comunes. Expropiaciones. Infantilismos y otras inutilidades. La negacion bien entendida empieza por uno mismo," negation well understood begins with oneself. The two viewed Negaciones prove the method visually: 1971 chromatically generous and busy with marks; 1972 reduced to a gray weathered field, the color denied, the glyph surviving the denial. Against the chain: Soriano (#324 M11) crossed into color once and never came back; Rojo crosses back, color given, color taken, the series advancing by refusal. **Takeaway:** Make the discard the composition; each piece exists to make the next one necessary. Watch the palette: when a later work negates an earlier one instead of developing it, the mechanism is on.

## M3. Series as system

"Rojo worked in series, in accordance with a 'general theory of systems' in which the unitary parts form a whole and are interrelated. In this way, when viewing one of the works in these series, we would also be viewing all the others" (MAM group label). He painted fifteen or sixteen pieces simultaneously, and "the origin of one series is sometimes seen in a previous one." "Senal sobre fondo violeta" (1967, viewed full-size) was made the year he first worked multiple paintings at once: a large violet-magenta shield field, about #8E3D8B, filling the lower two-thirds, a dark gray-black vertical bar crossed by a shorter horizontal bar forming the T sign-post at its center, above it a gray-green ground and a large ochre-rust triangular roof, about #A06228, with blue-gray underpainting, and a horizontal ochre band with small dark oval marks crossing behind the column. The surface in the detail crop is weathered gold-ochre and slate blue, fine white scratches, cracked aged paint, the hand preserved in the tool marks. The piece is not a singleton; it is one node of the Senales field. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M1) counted 584 lines and each verse was a station; Rojo's series are stations too, but each work is the whole system seen from one angle. **Takeaway:** Build fields, not pieces; the viewer should sense the siblings. Watch the bench: when paintings are made in parallel and read in parallel, the mechanism is on.

## M4. Destruction as construction

In the 1960s Destruccion/Geometrias paintings the canvas becomes "a witness of actions, such as tears, erasures and folds": a glued rectangular mat folded at the corners into triangles yields an inverted T; half-spheres emerge from a surface lattice. The trigger was seeing Rauschenberg take the 1964 Venice Biennale Grand Prize, and the method became a diptych discipline: the left side a highly structured geometry, the right side destroyed and rebuilt with cross-outs, added elements, torn canvas. The viewed Negacion no.9 carries the residue of this method a decade later: the surface eroded like stone, highlights where the paint was sanded away. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M5) made the image a non-invertible operator, stone equals feather; Rojo inverts the canvas itself, the structured side and the destroyed side the same painting. **Takeaway:** Build the order, then unbuild it in the same frame; the violence is part of the geometry. Watch the edge: when a tear or fold is the composition, the mechanism is on.

## M5. The geometric alphabet

"He usado la geometria como un lenguaje. Para mi, las formas geometricas (cuadrado, triangulo, circulo, cubo, cono o la maravillosa esfera) son las palabras que sirven para desarrollar un lenguaje vivo o leido: configuran mi alfabeto." "Codice 2" (1991, viewed): a 4x4 grid of sixteen chunky glyph-signs on ochre-gold paper, about #C8952A, rows strictly alternating magenta-pink and teal, each glyph like a stylized letter or pre-Columbian codex sign, an M, a W, spirals, houses, ladders, printed in a rough crayon texture that lets the paper show through, the ochre ground dotted with tiny embossed dimples. The MAM retrospective's verdict on him is a tool inventory: "an art of square and protractor, and T-square, and the T as a ruler." Against the chain: the whole chain has treated tools as subjects only late (spite's instrumentarium, the toolbox below); Rojo started with drafting tools as the brush. **Takeaway:** Let the ruler be the style; geometry is not the subject, it is the language. Watch the straightedge: when a protractor arc reads as a word, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The secret alphabet

The late Escrituras series: "pense en intentar una escritura propia. Se trataria de un alfabeto secreto." The Casa de Letras paintings (2014) carry it: in "Alfabeto lineal V," three vaguely typographic forms articulate against a background of muted stripes that in different points coincide and collide with the figures, each lit in active reds, oranges, yellows. The "Codice 2" viewing confirms the glyph grammar years earlier: signs that behave like letters, rows that behave like text, and no language resolves. Asemic writing before the term was fashionable, and rooted: the pre-Columbian codex read as an alphabet the artist never learned. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M7) put position to work as meaning, the word "Niego" falling apart as "Ni/Ego"; Rojo's secret alphabet refuses the fall, the letters never arrive. **Takeaway:** Write a text nobody can read and everybody can; the asemic page is a mirror. Watch the reading: when the eye scans for meaning and finds only behavior, the mechanism is on.

## M7. Rain as diagonal

"Mexico bajo la lluvia" (1985, gouache, viewed): an all-over mosaic of thousands of tiny triangles packed in diagonal rows, dominant turquoise-teal about #1FA8A0 with peppered orange, emerald, slate, gray accents, even and meticulous, shimmering like static. "Mexico bajo la lluvia 108" (1982, 100x100 cm, viewed): the same system at monumental scale, sweeping diagonal bands from upper-left to lower-right in hot red, vermilion, magenta, pink, about #E03050 to #F04080, interlaced with fine slivers of teal blue violet breaking through, the pattern bleeding to all edges in a vibrating moire field. The criticism's line: "Vicente Rojo teaches us that the rain is diagonal. Between the vertical and the horizontal, the parallel deluge." The origin is a memory, rain over the Valle de Cholula seen from Tonanzintla in his youth, executed in 1981-1989, about fifty rains eventually. Weather as pure geometry: no clouds, no drops, only the slant. Against the chain: Soriano (#324 M7) drew women's bodies as one line; Rojo draws weather as one angle. **Takeaway:** Reduce a phenomenon to its vector; the diagonal is the weather. Watch the slant: when rain is geometry and nothing else, the mechanism is on.

## M8. The designed book

Some 700 books and covers designed for Era and others. The Cien anos de soledad first-edition cover (1967): his work, the Denver essay notes, "was seen by millions." The MUNAE 2023 piece composes a Negaciones "T" out of twenty Era book covers: the product line becomes the painting. The cover is the work's first painting; the spine, the paper stock, the ink are compositional decisions with the same weight as the glyph. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M2) made the book a machine the reader operates; Rojo makes the book an object the reader receives, and the receiving is the reading. **Takeaway:** Design the container as the first line of the work; the cover is not packaging. Watch the shelf: when the book's outside is already the painting, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The uncredited typographer

The "Blanco" story, corrected against every assumption: Paz sent Joaquin Diez-Canedo an exact typographic scheme in October 1966; Diez-Canedo asked Rojo for help; the two interpreted Paz's scheme together; then, Rojo's own words, "Joaquin y yo eramos igual de discretos, y el libro salio sin nuestras firmas como tipografos," they were equally discreet, and the book came out without their signatures as typographers. Total technical command placed entirely at the service of another's vision, on purpose. Against the chain: the chain's machines have been authored objects (#325 M3, the Discos with their red case); this mechanism is authorship withheld, the strongest signature being the absent one. **Takeaway:** Sometimes the mastery is invisible on purpose; the unsigned work is still the work. Watch the credit: when the craftsman disappears into the author's scheme, the mechanism is on.

## M10. Cut-and-paste origin

"At four years of age, in Barcelona, I found myself with pencils and colored pencils in my hands; with papers, scissors and glue. From that moment I discovered that I really like cutting, pasting, and playing with those elements, which I keep to this day." The viewed "Mascara #1" (1988) proves it decades on: a dark mahogany and rust ground with a slate-teal band, near the top a "crown" of small house-like shapes, triangles on squares, in magenta violet teal pink; two pale rings like metal eyelets as eyes; below a teal scalloped form like a mustache with a pendant tab; a strip of exposed corrugated cardboard along the bottom edge; the paint dry and scrubbed, cardboard fibers showing. "Mascara #2": a rust ground, a city-skyline band of small rectangles under a dark strip, a pale distressed band pierced by two square black eye holes, bright pink semicircles as cheeks flanking a small inverted purple triangle, heavy impasto and scraping. Scissors-and-glue as a lifelong pictorial syntax, persisting into the R42 collage and the Lluvias de noviembre serigraphs on amate bark paper. Against the chain: Chucho (#323 M1) built fragility into paper; Rojo builds houses out of it. **Takeaway:** Keep the childhood tool; the scissors are the style. Watch the edge: when the cut cardboard shows, the mechanism is on.

## M11. Painting private, sculpture relational

"Si la pintura es el aspecto mas privado de Rojo, la escultura esta en relacion con la ciudad, plazas y edificios, pensadas en terminos relacionales" (Rita Eder): if painting is Rojo's most private aspect, his sculpture is in relation with the city, plazas and buildings, conceived in relational terms. The vocabulary does not change; the scale does. "Volcanes" (2003) outside the Museo Memoria y Tolerancia; the SRE fountain for Legorreta; the Anti-Mural at CENART; "Jardin Urbano" (2018), geometric lines and circles carved in volcanic stone across ten panels at the Museo Kaluz, moving "as if the wind pushed it, evoking a natural landscape," one of his last large works. Against the chain: the chain keeps the work at hand-scale; Rojo's T goes up to building height and the T still holds. **Takeaway:** Scale the vocabulary, not the system; the city is a bigger canvas. Watch the street: when a traffic-sign glyph becomes a plaza, the mechanism is on.

## M12. The toolbox self-portrait

The late self-portraits accumulate not works but the means of work: watercolors, scissors, tin soldiers, toy airplanes, rubber stamps, stencils outlining the word ROJO, glasses, a blackboard, acrylics, oils, inks, compasses, pencils, crayons, spirals, brushes, rules, "rules and more rules." Explicitly modeled on the 1968 Duchamp suitcase-book made with Paz, but inverted: the suitcase holds not final works but potential works. The artist painted as his own instrument case. Against the chain: Soriano (#324 M8) treated the theater as a second studio; Rojo treats the studio as the portrait. **Takeaway:** Portrait the instruments, not the face; the toolbox is the biography. Watch the frame: when the contents are all means and no ends, the mechanism is on.

## M13. Memory as material

The Recuerdos series was "born of his attempt to abandon a difficult childhood." Late, the memory returned on its own schedule: the 2019 "80 anos despues" paintings of his father's Ipanema voyage, Bordeaux to Veracruz, June-July 1939, sunsets, sea scenes, ship parts, sand and beach; "Jardin de ninos" (1968, with Jose Emilio Pacheco) recovering "the children killed by the bombers." Childhood war re-entering abstraction decades later, not as confession but as material. Against the chain: Soriano (#324 M6) drew death as a perfect curve without solemnity; Rojo lets the war in through the alphabet, the bombers arriving in a book for children. **Takeaway:** Let the old memory arrive when it arrives; do not schedule the past. Watch the date: when a 1939 memory surfaces in 2019, the mechanism is on.

## M14. The city as sign-case

In a 1965 UNAM film Rojo drives through Mexico City reading it as "signs, arrows and signs to follow with your eyes." The Senales series (1966-1972) converts that urban semiotics into painting: Tapies' grammar of symbols, the street's typography abstracted into the T vocabulary. The viewed "Senal sobre fondo violeta" is the mechanism literal: a sign-post T planted in a violet field, a roof above it, the road-marking band behind, the city compressed to one sign. Against the chain: Paz (#325 M10) built the magazine as a periodic machine; Rojo built the city as a readable page, and drove through it reading. **Takeaway:** Read the street as a text and paint the text; the sign is already the composition. Watch the drive: when a traffic arrow becomes a painting, the mechanism is on.

## Aesthetic logic

The typographer's discipline never leaves the painting. Geometry is the second language, the T the first word, negation the grammar, the series the book, and the secret alphabet the last page. Rojo's systems move in one direction across the decades: from the found city-sign to the balanced glyph to the rain's diagonal to the unreadable letter, always narrowing the vocabulary and deepening the field. He is the chain's great systematizer: where Soriano extruded a line into bronze and Paz extruded a poem into a field, Rojo extruded the printing shop into a life, and the shop kept perfect order. The uncredited typographer of "Blanco" and the painter of two hundred negations are the same man: the one who believed a letter could hold a life.

## Evolution

Barcelona 1932, Civil War childhood, the scissors-and-glue discovery at four, the 1939 exile rupture; Mexico 1949 at seventeen, Mexican nationality the same year; La Esmeralda, the decisive bond with fellow exile Miguel Prieto, "the best typographer in Mexico," first publishing job, the visual democracy of typography; 1950s design work for Artes de Mexico, Mexico en la Cultura, the Revista de la Universidad, laying out Cuevas's "La cortina de nopal"; 1958 first solo at Galeria Proteo, early "paisajes, animales y construcciones" settling into abstraction; 1960 co-founding Ediciones Era (Espresate, Rojo, Azorin), art director of Imprenta Madero 1962-1984; 1961-62 space investigations; 1964 the Rauschenberg shock at Venice and the first T in the paintings; Senales 1966-1972; the Paz years 1966-1968, Blanco's uncredited typography, Discos visuales, the Duchamp suitcase-book, Jardin de ninos; Negaciones late 1960s to mid 1970s; Vuelta's design 1976; Barcelona return 1978, the Paseo de Sant Joan sketches; Paris 1980; Mexico bajo la lluvia 1981-1989; Codices, Escenas, the Escenarios review of everything 1990s; Volcanes 2003; Casa de Letras 2014; Jardin Urbano 2018; the 1939 voyage remembered 2019; death March 2021, two days after his 89th birthday. Through-line: the alphabet gets shorter and the paintings get deeper.

## Study method note

The brief's load-bearing premises were all re-footed: Rojo did not design "Blanco," he executed its typography uncredited; the "Paisajes" series does not exist as a named series (six-series canon: Senales, Negaciones, Recuerdos, Mexico bajo la lluvia, Escenarios, Escrituras); the Libertad bajo palabra and Laberinto covers are not evidenced as Rojo's; Era was founded 1960, not 1949; the Garcia Ponce "four years older" claim is wrong (both born 1932); one design-encyclopedia entry was rejected as confabulated. Ten distinct works were inspected first-hand as images; the Cien anos cover failed to load and is carried second-hand; one expected work image (paz3-thumb) proved to be an exhibition text panel. No early work, no monograph end-to-end, no Recuerdos works found: carried as honest gaps.
