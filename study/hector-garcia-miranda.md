# Hector Garcia Miranda: Study Notes

**Site:** Hector Garcia Miranda, reported 1930-2018 -- the dates are secondarily attested only (APPL cemetery notice, citing geometrica.es; the Madrid gallery text) and no museum, foundation or BnF source read for this study confirms them, so they are printed here as reported, not as fact -- Argentine painter of geometric abstraction who worked in Paris from 1959, co-founder of the Centre de Recherche d'Art Visuel in July 1960 who left it with the Molnars and Hugo Demarco before it renamed itself GRAV in 1961, the founder who walked away from the group and then walked away from painting itself: his visual career runs 1956 to 1966, a single decade, and after that he spent his life making Andean folk music as founder and artistic director of Los Calchakis. ## The life: the founder who left twice -- the group, then painting

Hector Garcia Miranda was born on 7 October 1930 in the province of Buenos Aires, Argentina (C7401). At the end of primary school his drawing teacher, Anibal Zorzoni, took him as a pupil and at age 14 placed him as an apprentice in an advertising agency, drawing furniture sketches for advertisements (C7402). He entered the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes in Buenos Aires (named in the Madrid gallery text as the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes Manuel Belgrano), where he later stayed on as a teacher of design and engraving until 1959 (C7403). Before leaving Argentina he exhibited at the Museo de Cordoba and in the Peuser and Pizarro galleries, was selected by Argentina for the 1957 Sao Paulo Biennial, and won the gold medal at the Salon d'automne de San Fernando the same year (C7404).

He emigrated to Paris in 1959 (C7405). There he took classes with the British printmaker Stanley William Hayter and was selected the same year for the Argentine section of the First Paris Biennale (C7405). His work already showed geometric abstraction and the influence of Victor Vasarely, who supervised his work regularly from his arrival; Vasarely's recommendation letter of 1 October 1959 says Garcia Miranda "etudie les arts plastiques sous ma direction, je suis en intervalles reguliers son travail et son evolution" (C7406).

In 1960 Morellet, Stein, Garcia Miranda and Yvaral exhibited together under the name Motus at Galerie Azimut in Milan, then Padua and Brussels; Horacio Garcia Rossi, Hugo Demarco, Sergio Moyano, Julio Le Parc and Francisco Sobrino joined for the Brussels show (C7407, C7502). The Motus members renounced signing their works, attributing them to the group, and declared they pursued plastic research with no commercial aim (C7407).

A few months after the Motus shows, in July 1960, the group became the CRAV (Centre de Recherche d'Art Visuel). The founding signatories were Hugo Demarco, Hector Garcia Miranda, Horacio Garcia Rossi, Julio Le Parc, Vera Molnar, Francois Molnar, Francois Morellet, Sergio Moyano Servanes, Francisco Sobrino, Joel Stein and Jean-Pierre Yvaral -- eleven names as usually counted (C7408). On the eleven-vs-twelve count, see Corrections: the Degruson text lists "Sergio Moyano, Servanes" as two names, which would make twelve, and the #626 study's twelve-name roster is not confirmed by this run. The aim was to create a research group working free of all aesthetic, social and economic pressure, building production on a geometric theoretical base, across the study of light, colour and movement -- work that falls under Op-art and kinetic art (C7409).

Dissensions arose quickly over anonymity and over the shared premises on rue Beautreillis in Paris -- were the works individual unsigned pieces or works claimed collectively by the group, and was the rue Beautreillis space a laboratory or a press and critics showroom? Vera and Francois Molnar, Hector Garcia Miranda and Hugo Demarco left the group over these questions (C7410, C7503). In 1961 the CRAV became the GRAV, around six artists: Horacio Garcia Rossi, Julio Le Parc, Francois Morellet, Francisco Sobrino, Joel Stein and Yvaral (C7411). Garcia Miranda was therefore a CRAV co-founder who never belonged to the GRAV. The group debuted collectively in its first year at Galerie Denise Rene in Paris (C7412, C7505); the statements later collected as the GRAV manifesto were edited by Galerie Denise Rene for the collective "El Movimiento" at the Moderna Museet, Stockholm, in 1961 (C7412).

The GRAV took part in the Nouvelle Tendance exhibition at the Pavillon Marsan (Louvre, Paris), alongside the Italian Gruppo N and Gruppo T and the Spanish Equipo Cronica; Garcia Miranda stood out there for pieces made of geometric structures in corrugated cardboard (C7413, C7462). He then took part in the Musee d'Art Moderne de Paris exhibition of summer 1965, which brought together the most important Latin American artists close to the Ecole de Paris; critics praised his good taste and elegance in using optical effects to create the geometric forms of his compositions (C7414, C7462). In 1962 he took part with other Argentine artists in "30 Argentins de la Nouvelle Generation" at Galerie Creuze, Paris (C7412).

The Madrid gallery text dates his last geometric piece, a gouache on cardboard, to 1966, "the year in which the artist decided to break with the GRAV, which would finally dissolve in 1968" (C7415). This wording is imprecise -- he had already left the CRAV in 1960-61 -- but 1966 is consistently the end date of his geometric production: the 2019 show covers only 1959-1966 (C7419). In parallel he was a musician. He founded the Andean folk group Los Calchakis with his wife Ana Maria Miranda (singer, "Huayta") -- sources disagree whether the founding year was 1960 or 1961 -- and was its founder and artistic director, a baritone who played siku, moceno, tarka, pinkillo, bombo and other percussion (C7416, C7507). The group's first record, "Les Andes a Paris", was recorded in 1966, just after the group was formed, and in 1972 Los Calchakis played the soundtrack of Costa-Gavras's "Etat de siege", composed by Mikis Theodorakis (C7417). The group went on to make more than 40 records and became one of the most important performers of Latin American folklore in Europe (C7416).

Garcia Miranda died on 23 October 2018 in Paris and is buried at Pere Lachaise cemetery, in the grave of the lyric singer Ines Baldassari, known as Chabal (1910-1997) (C7418). The Galeria Jose de la Mano show of 21 February - 6 April 2019, "GARCIA MIRANDA [1959-1966]. Un argentino silenciado del GRAV", was described as the only solo exhibition of historical reclamation ever devoted to him, with thirteen works dated 1959-1966 tracing his most intense period of geometric production (C7419).

His network is the CRAV room and the music room. Demarco is the fellow founder and fellow leaver, the Argentine who left beside him (C7503). Morellet is the Motus co-exhibitor who stayed into the six-name GRAV -- the contrast case (C7502, C7503). Los Calchakis, with Ana Maria Miranda, is the second network: the man who left the group in 1960 founded a folk group the same year, and music -- not painting -- filled the rest of his life (C7507). No dated one-to-one Demarco-Garcia Miranda collaboration or joint exhibition was found; the evidenced link is the shared founding plus the shared exit, and it must not be overstated (C7503, C7551).

## M1 -- The progression: a series, not a subject

The earliest work found, Progresion No.1 (W01, 1956, acrylic on wood, 30 x 81), is already fully geometric and serial: the title names a progression, not a subject (C7451). Continuite (W03, 1959), viewed as the work itself, is the same idea grown up: a tall vertical composition of grey and black stepped horizontal bands, cut by thin vertical red lines running the full height; the bands shift in tone from light grey at the edges to near black at the centre, producing a flickering, receding rhythm (C7456). The title fits the unbroken verticals and the serial band progression. The mechanism is the series as the subject: the picture is one step in a progression, and the progression -- not any single arrangement -- is the work. No 18 (W02, 1959, gouache on card, 19.5 x 50 cm, signed and dated on the verso "Paris / 7 de Mayo 1959") is a long horizontal strip, the same serial logic in a different orientation (C7452).

**Takeaway:** name the progression, not the picture. One module, stepped through its values, and the series is the work.

## M2 -- The still centre: an empty square inside the vibration

The two 1965 works viewed as the work itself share one device: a still point at the centre of a vibrating field. Untitled (W06, 1965) is a grid of red, grey and white triangles with fine black hatching, arranged in rows around a plain central square; the triangles point in alternating directions so the field reads as a set of folding fans or spinning blades, with the empty centre acting as a still point (C7458). Caporal (W07, 1965) is a dense diamond lattice of dark blue and black squares, each carrying a small pale mark, with a bright blue diamond-shaped field at the centre and small red dots at the lattice intersections; the pattern produces a strong moire-like vibration, the most aggressively optical of the three viewed works (C7457). The mechanism is the contrast: the vibration is strongest because something in the middle does not move. The eye has a place to rest, and the rest makes the movement readable -- exactly the GRAV manifesto's "unstable structures only perceived in the field of peripheral vision" and "virtual movement" (C7468).

**Takeaway:** put a still point in the middle of the vibration. The empty square is what lets the eye feel the field move.

## M3 -- The anonymous form: no signature, no author

The Motus doctrine, carried into the CRAV, was that members renounced signing their works, attributing them to the group, and declared they pursued plastic research with no commercial aim (C7407). The GRAV manifesto's version is that form is "an anonymous element distributed over the surface" (C7468). The mechanism is a refusal of authorship as a working rule, not a pose: if no work is signed, the only thing that can be judged is the system, and the system is the research. It is also the reason so few of his works carry individual titles -- most are untitled, and the catalogue reads as a series of medium-and-date entries rather than named pictures (C7468). And it is the reason the split happened: the anonymity question -- individual unsigned pieces or works claimed collectively by the group -- was one of the two questions that broke the CRAV (C7410). The doctrine that made the group was the doctrine that broke it.

**Takeaway:** remove the signature and the system has to carry the work. Anonymity is a test of the mechanism, not a style.

## M4 -- The cheap material: corrugated cardboard as structure

At Nouvelle Tendance, Pavillon Marsan, Garcia Miranda "stood out for his pieces formed by geometric structures of corrugated cardboard" (C7462). This is his only attested move from flat painting into relief or object, and his only attested use of a cheap, industrial material (W18). The mechanism is the inversion of the painter's hierarchy: the cardboard is not a poor substitute for canvas, it is the structure itself -- the corrugation is the geometry, the cheapness is the point. A material that comes with its own regular ridges needs no drawn grid; the structure is already there, and the artist's job is to cut and stack it. No exact year, count, title or dimension for the individual pieces is attested, and no image of them was viewed in this study -- the mechanism is documented in prose, not in a seen object, and is stated as such (C7462, C7552).

**Takeaway:** let the material carry the grid. Corrugation is a ready-made module; the work is in how you cut it.

## M5 -- The square at scale: format as the unit

The middle period changes scale, support and dimensionality together. Formats grow: from the 19.5 x 50 cm gouache of 1959 to square canvases and tablex panels of 60 x 60 cm (W04, c. 1959/1960, acrylic on canvas) and 78 x 78 cm (W05, 1962, acrylic on tablex, signed and dated on both sides), the largest individually dimensioned work found (C7453, C7454). Supports diversify: acrylic moves across canvas, board and tablex, and a collage on board appears (W14, the only collage in the record) (C7461). The mechanism is the square as the unit of the system: a square format has no direction to favour, so the module can repeat in any direction and the eye reads the system, not the composition. The growth in size is not ambition, it is the module getting room to repeat -- the 78 x 78 cm panel is the same system as the 19.5 x 50 cm strip, given a larger field to run in.

**Takeaway:** fix the format square and let the module repeat. Scale is the field the system runs in, not the ambition of the picture.

## M6 -- The two lives: vibration to the eye, vibration to the ear

He founded Los Calchakis with his wife Ana Maria Miranda in 1960 (W19), ran it in parallel with painting through the CRAV years, and after 1966 it is the only documented output, with more than forty records to the group's credit (C7469) and a dated composer credit as late as "Les Flutes indiennes" in 1974 (W20, C7470). The Del Infinito text frames the two paths as one project -- vibration carried to the eye in painting and to the ear in Andean music, "light as active matter" on one side, the pulse of the Andes on the other (C7468) -- but the record shows a clean break in medium: painting ends in 1966, music continues for decades until his death in Paris on 23 October 2018. The mechanism is the same driver in two senses: a repeated module (a triangle, a diamond, a flute phrase) stepped through its values until the field vibrates. The career is not two careers; it is one mechanism with the sense changed. The first Los Calchakis LP, "Les Andes a Paris", was recorded in 1966, the same year as the last geometric work (C7417) -- the handover year, when the vibration moved from the eye to the ear.

**Takeaway:** the same driver works in two senses. When the picture stops, the rhythm keeps going -- change the sense, not the system.

## M7 -- The short decade: ten years, then silence

The visual career is 1956 to 1966, and the public record of it is thin: one 1956 painting, the thirteen-work 2019 show, three APPL-illustrated works, and the Nouvelle Tendance reliefs (C7459, C7462). No museum holding, no auction record, no catalogue raisonne, no dated visual work after 1966, no exhibition after 1966 (C7470). The 2019 show, "GARCIA MIRANDA [1959-1966]. Un argentino silenciado del GRAV", is the only solo reclamation show ever devoted to him (C7419). The mechanism is the archive as the work's second life: a career that lasted ten years and then stopped is recoverable only because one gallery, fifty-three years later, gathered thirteen panels and gouaches and printed a text. The study's catalogue is honest about what it is: 7 individually identified visual works, 10 show-group entries known only by medium and date-group, 1 relief group, 2 musical works -- and the thirteen-work total of the 2019 show is the anchor that holds the accounting together (C7459, C7552). The silence after 1966 is not a gap in the record; it is the record. He stopped painting.

**Takeaway:** a short career is a complete career if the record is honest. Count what survives, name what does not, and let the silence stand.

## M8 -- The leaver's fork: leave the group before it renames itself

The defining event is the split: four founders -- Vera and Francois Molnar, Hector Garcia Miranda and Hugo Demarco -- left the CRAV over anonymity and the rue Beautreillis space, and in 1961 the CRAV became the GRAV around the six who remained (C7503). The mechanism is a fork taken early: leave before the rename, and the name never attaches to you. Morellet stayed and became GRAV; Garcia Miranda left and became, first, a painter without a group, and then a musician without a canvas. The fork is also the reason his name is hard to find: he is not in the six-name GRAV roster, not in the manifesto signatures, and the gallery texts that call him a "GRAV founder" are using the name loosely for a group he had already left (C7503, C7505). The doorway's D2 failure is the same fact from the other side: the sketch said he stayed, and the record says he left. The lesson for the study chain is that the fork is the mechanism -- the exit is the work, the way Molnar's four-month membership was the work in #626 (M4 there), except Garcia Miranda's exit was followed by a second exit, from painting itself.

**Takeaway:** leave before the rename and the name never sticks. The exit is the decision; the second exit is the career.

## The aesthetic logic: progression, still centre, cheap structure -- then silence

Four continuities hold the short career together. The vocabulary never changes: stepped bands, triangles, diamond lattices, red points, from Progresion No.1 (1956) to Caporal (1965). The method never changes: one module, stepped through its values, serial comparison -- the progression is the subject. The criterion never changes: the field must vibrate, and the vibration is strongest where a still point sits at the centre. And the sense changes once: the vibration moves from the eye to the ear in 1966, and the painting stops. What changed is the locus of the work -- from the panel, to the relief, to the record -- while the driver never changes: a repeated module stepped through its values until the field moves. Garcia Miranda's wager is the opposite of Molnar's: Molnar left the group to measure seeing, and kept the question for thirty years; Garcia Miranda left the group over its direction, kept the question for six more years, and then let the question go and played music instead. He is the founder who left twice -- first the group, then the painting -- and the programme he built kept producing nothing visual after 1966, because he had moved it to another sense.
