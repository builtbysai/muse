## M1. The apprentice copy (study the master by re-painting him)

Gorky learned by copying, and the copying was the career, not the juvenilia. In Boston and New York he worked through Cezanne, landscapes and still lifes in 1927, and Picasso's Synthetic Cubism in the late 1920s, and Miro and Kandinsky and Leger and Ingres, painting "in the manner of" each in turn, and his own account to Julien Levy in the winter of 1932-33 states the method plainly, "I was with Cezanne for a long time... and now naturally I am with Picasso", and Devaney's reading, via Artsy, states the motive, not merely to copy but to understand, to emotionally engage and empathise with these artists' creative forces by literally getting under the layers of their painting, by reapplying and appropriating them, and in doing so discovering his own artistic identity, and Harold Rosenberg's verdict is that a style truly modern must be capable of mirroring the paintings and carvings of all times and places, and Gorky was a great connoisseur of painting. The procedure is to pick a master, paint inside his structure with your own subject, and keep what survives the transplant, and Staten Island of 1927 is the proof, painted from nature on a hillside Gorky sought out because it reminded him of L'Estaque, rigid architectonic geometry of private houses dominating the composition while a warm joyful palette makes a New York borough look like the South of France, and the danger, which the critics of the 1930s named, is derivation, and the payoff, which Breton named in 1944, is a personal idiom built out of other people's grammar, and he was still doing it in the mid-1940s, studying Poussin's Triumph of Bacchus at the Durlacher Gallery and borrowing its structural scaffolding for Agony, the eye entering at the left and moving along a succession of vertical lines and triggers. **Takeaway:** pick a master, paint inside his structure with your own subject, and keep only what survives the transplant, so the copy becomes a method, not a phase. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M2) also began in automatism and then worked the edges for months, and his is a fast start and a slow finish, and Gorky's is a slow apprenticeship and a fast release, which is why Onslow Ford's spontaneity is planned at the end and Gorky's is planned at the beginning, and the planning is the drawing, and the drawing is the copy, and the copy is the study.

## M2. The photograph as armature (The Artist and His Mother)

Take a photograph, the Van photograph of c. 1912, a boy standing beside his seated mother, commissioned by his mother and sent to his father in America, and use it as a scaffold, and then remove everything photographic from it. Gorky pares the composition to loosely brushed passages and masklike faces and stark gazes, leaving areas, notably the hands, unfinished, and he opens a negative space between the two figures, and he angles the boy's feet away from his mother, and he expands the dark rectangle behind the mother's head into a Madonna-like cloth of honour, and he omits the patterned frock of the photograph, and he reworks the canvas intermittently for a decade, from c. 1926 to 1936, and makes a second version, c. 1929 to 1942, now in the National Gallery of Art, and the Whitney's text states the result, the unfinished quality instils "a haunting inaccessibility - a formal analogy, perhaps, for memory itself", and the Tate press release states the purpose, the two versions act as memorials to his lost childhood and confrontations with exile, and the picture is likened to Ingres for simplicity of line, to Egyptian funerary art for pose, to Cezanne for flat planar composition, to Picasso for form and colour, and the procedure is subtraction from a document, the less the picture resembles its source, the more it remembers. **Takeaway:** take a document, use it as a scaffold, and subtract from it until the picture remembers more than the document shows, so the unfinished hands are the memory, not the failure. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M3) unwound a landscape mark by mark until only inner structure remained, and his is an unwinding of a place, and Gorky's is an unwinding of a person, which is why Onslow Ford's ends in an inscape and Gorky's ends in a mother who is also a rectangle, and the rectangle is the cloth, and the cloth is the honour, and the honour is the distance.

## M3. The squared-up transfer (drawing to painting)

Gorky developed paintings from drawings through gridding, and the improvisation happens in the drawing and the painting is its enlargement. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M2) also began without preconception and then worked the edges for months, and his is one gesture worked slowly, and Gorky's is a hundred drawings worked into one painting, which is why Onslow Ford's automatism is a single throw and Gorky's is a campaign, and the campaign is the grid, and the grid is the rehearsal, and the rehearsal is the freedom.

## M4. The layered impasto ("hundreds and hundreds of layers")

In the 1930s and early 1940s Gorky built paintings up in layer upon layer of paint until the canvas was, in his own phrase to MoMA, "hundreds and hundreds of layers of paint to obtain the weight of reality", and it was a running joke that visitors were dared to lift his canvases, which were impossibly heavy, and the procedure is accumulation as realism, reality is heavy, so the painting must be heavy, and the cost was material, expensive paint in the depths of personal poverty, and the motive was partly fear that the war would cut off art supplies from Europe, and partly a "seemingly pathological need to make the object more real", and the shift to diluted washes in the mid-1940s is the release from this procedure, and the 1947 late works abandon purely linear design and thin painting to cover the canvas fully again, building layer upon layer of tones until, per Schwabacher, he achieved the most sumptuous colour of his entire career, and the layer count is the dial, thin wash for air and water, heavy impasto for weight and reality, and the dial is turned by the subject, not by the fashion. **Takeaway:** build the painting in layers until it weighs what the subject weighs, and thin the paint when the subject is air, so the weight of the canvas is the weight of the memory. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M1) poured enamel flat and cut the dry skin to reveal wet enamel beneath, and his is a revealing of layers, and Gorky's is an accumulation of layers, which is why Onslow Ford's picture is a cut and Gorky's is a weight, and the weight is the reality, and the reality is the layer, and the layer is the memory.

## M5. The colour veil (thinned washes, 1944)

Dilute the paint with turpentine, Matta's 1942 advice, use more turpentine to loosen up the paint, and wash it unevenly across the canvas in films, and then brush in evocative but indistinct forms, and the result is a painting with no spatial recession and no identifiable forms, only a suggestion of landscape. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M1) poured enamel flat and cut the dry skin to reveal wet enamel beneath, and his is a cut that reveals, and Gorky's is a wash that conceals, which is why Onslow Ford's picture opens and Gorky's picture veils, and the veiling is the looking, and the looking is the film, and the film is the milkweed.

## M6. Automatic drawing (line first, interpretation after)

The Surrealist technique as Gorky practised it is two-stroke, an uncontrolled first mark and then a controlled reading. The Foundation biography states the introduction, Matta introduced him to the Surrealist technique of automatic drawing and encouraged him to improvise, and Breton's 1924 definition, quoted in the 1981 Waldman retrospective catalogue, states the theory, "Pure psychic automatism by which one intends to express verbally, in writing or by other method, the real functioning of the mind. Dictation by thought, in the absence of any control exercised by reason", and Elaine de Kooning, who watched him draw in the summer of 1943, wrote that he drew non-stop with lines that built a labyrinth, "in a frenzy with lines and shapes spilling from him out of control", but the drawings were then studied and squared and transferred, and Mougouch states the release, Matta encouraged him and admired his work, he wanted him to stop practising, to let himself go, and it took time, but a year or so later, in Virginia, far from New York, he was able at last to do just this, and the 1941 lectures gave him the theory, Onslow Ford explaining automatism, and the Virginia summers gave him the practice, and the procedure is to draw without preconception, let the hand move, then read the result, and the reading is the second half of the work, not an afterthought. **Takeaway:** draw first without preconception, then read what the hand made, and only then decide what it is, so the interpretation follows the line instead of leading it. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M2) also began in a random gesture and then worked the edges for months, and his is a fast start and a slow finish, and Gorky's is a fast hand and a slow reading, which is why Onslow Ford's finish is in the paint and Gorky's finish is in the grid, and the grid is the reading, and the reading is the line, and the line is the thought.

## M7. The title as free association (the Breton titling game)

Between visits and dinners at Gorky's studio and in the Connecticut countryside, Breton and Gorky titled the canvases for the March 1945 Levy debut, and the procedure was a game of stacked translation. Gorky would stare at his paintings and ramble off a train of thought in English while Mougouch translated into French for Breton, and Breton would choose a phrase from her translation, which she then translated back into English for Gorky, and the Hauser essay states what the game was, "This stacked rupture of non-native languages represented a kind of Surrealist game in itself, in which words were a site for estrangement, play and formation all at once", and the results are absurdist analogies, The Leaf of the Artichoke Is an Owl, and The Liver Is the Cock's Comb, and personified nature, The Horns of the Landscape, and word play, Water of the Flowery Mill, flour against flower, and reminiscence, One Year the Milkweed, and How My Mother's Embroidered Apron Unfolds in My Life, and Breton called the result a "hybrid" of direct observation and past impression, and the mechanism is that the title is not a label but a second artwork, made by translation, and it does half the painting's work, it tells the viewer what to feel while refusing to tell them what to see, and the painting does not need to show an artichoke leaf or an owl, because the title does the seeing. **Takeaway:** title the picture by translation and free association, not by description, so the title tells the viewer what to feel and refuses to tell them what to see, and the picture and the title are two artworks that never meet. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M8) named his pictures for the voyage and the SEED, and his is a title that names a place, and Gorky's is a title that names a feeling, which is why Onslow Ford's title is a map and Gorky's title is a pun, and the pun is the game, and the game is the translation, and the translation is the title.

## M8. The biomorphic line (thin black line and watery plume)

The mature visual signature is stated most clearly in The Liver Is the Cock's Comb, "an abstract landscape filled with watery plumes of semi-transparent color that coalesce around spiky, thorn like shapes, painted in thin, sharp black lines, as if to suggest beaks and claws", per Auping via Wikipedia, and the procedure is to lay down washed colour first and then draw over it with a thin, sharp, elegant black line that is simultaneously a contour and a beak and a claw and a thorn and a stem, and Wikipedia's legacy section states the formula, "He used twisted but elegant lines to bring in 'biomorphic' forms in his abstract paintings along with an overlay of colours to create a complex landscape of lines and colours on the canvas", and the line is the skeleton and the wash is the flesh, and in Agony the same line becomes incisive and pulled, per The Art Story, "the incisive pulling of the lines and forms", and in Charred Beloved the same line becomes char, and the line's thinness is the point, a thick line would be a shape, and a thin line is a nerve, and the warm reds and yellows and oranges against the cooler blues and whites and earth tones are the plume, and the black line is the beak that holds the plume together, and the painting works because the line draws and the colour feels, and neither does the other's job. **Takeaway:** wash the colour first, then draw over it with one thin sharp black line that is a beak and a claw and a stem at once, so the line draws and the colour feels, and a thin line is a nerve, not a shape. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M6) reduced art to line, circle and dot, and his is a language of three marks, and Gorky's is a language of one line and one wash, which is why Onslow Ford's picture is a glyph and Gorky's picture is a body, and the body is the line, and the line is the nerve, and the nerve is the plume.

## M9. The serial drawing campaign (one theme, one hundred attempts)

Gorky's working unit was the series, not the single picture, and the procedure is to take one theme, draw it until the theme is exhausted, and then paint the survivors. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M7) painted in the instant and disappeared into the mark, and his is one instant repeated, and Gorky's is one motif repeated, which is why Onslow Ford's repetition is a speed and Gorky's repetition is a siege, and the siege is the drawing, and the drawing is the variation, and the variation is the motif.

## M10. En plein air observation fused with memory (Virginia and Connecticut)

From the summer of 1943 Gorky drew out of doors daily at Crooked Run Farm in Virginia, and later in Connecticut, milkweed pods and fireflies and apple orchards and cows and running water and the change of seasons, and Sweeney's Harper's Bazaar account of April 1944 states the decision, Gorky "decided to put out of his mind the galleries of Fifty-seventh Street and the reproductions of Picasso, Leger, and Miro, and 'look into the grass,' as he put it", and the result was a freshness and personalization of idiom he had never previously approached, and a new vocabulary of forms, and Mougouch states the fusion, "Gorky's sight was inventive. Against the chain: Onslow Ford (#385 M3) gave up his trees to become a surrealist, and his is a landscape unwound until only inner structure remains, and Gorky's is a landscape looked at until memory joins it, which is why Onslow Ford's ends by abandoning the place and Gorky's ends by doubling it, and the doubling is the grass, and the grass is the milkweed, and the milkweed is the wheatfield.

## M11. The mural as public abstraction (Newark Airport, 1936-37)
