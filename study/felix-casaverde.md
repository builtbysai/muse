## M1. The single guitar as the whole band

The reduction is the invention, and the reduction is the mechanism. A lando in the ordinary form needs a small crowd: a solo voice and a chorus answering it, a cajon and a bongo and a cencerro and a tumbadora, a first guitar and a second guitar, and the crowd is the sound, because the lando is a dance and a dance needs feet, and Casaverde takes the crowd away and leaves one guitar, and the one guitar has to be the crowd, and the being-the-crowd is the mechanism, because the guitar must carry the bass and the harmony and the melody and the rhythm at once, and it carries them by refusing to choose between them, and the refusal is the technique in M3 and M4, and the technique is the sound. The proof is the second solo album, "Memorias de Felix Casaverde - Guitarra Negra" of 1990, which the Oxford entry describes as acoustic and instrumental in its entirety and arranged for one guitar and one cajon, and the proof is the suite itself, "Cuatro tiempos negros jovenes", which is written for one guitar and one cajon and nothing else, and the cajon is the only companion the guitar allows, because the cajon is the drum the guitar learned to answer, and the answering is the duet, and the duet is the band. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the working life: he spends his life accompanying singers, Chabuca and Baca and Eva and Libertad and Olga Milla and Carmina Cannavino and the rest, and then he makes two albums in which there is no singer at all, and the no-singer is the statement, because the guitar that accompanied everybody can also stand alone, and the standing alone is the proof that the accompaniment was never subordinate, it was a reduction waiting to happen. Watch the reduction: when the chorus leaves, and the percussion leaves except the cajon, and the second guitar leaves, and the one guitar stays, and the lando still sounds like a lando, and the dance still moves, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** take the crowd away, and let one guitar be the crowd. The reduction is the band. Against the chain: the Kipus (#364 M2) also built their sound from guitars, but theirs was a weave of two guitars that answer each other, and the answering needed two hands; Casaverde is one guitar that answers itself, which is why their version is a dialogue and his is a monologue that contains the dialogue. Chabuca (#361) also reduced, but hers was a reduction of the orchestra to a small group; Casaverde is a reduction of the group to one instrument, which is why her version is a chamber and his is a solo.

## M2. Four rhythms in one suite

Four rhythms live in one piece, and the living-together is the mechanism. "Cuatro tiempos negros jovenes" is a suite for guitar and cajon composed across the 1970s, more than seven minutes long, and its theme is a personal synthesis of four rhythms of the Peruvian coast: the zapateo, the marinera limen~a, the lando and the festejo, and the synthesis is the mechanism, because the four rhythms are not played one after another as a medley, they are folded into one another, and the folding is the composition, and the composition is the argument, because the argument is that the four rhythms are one music, and the one music is the coast, and the coast is the man, and the man is the guitar. The lando section is about half the piece, and the half is the proof that the lando is the centre, because the suite is named for four rhythms and gives half its time to one of them, and the giving is the emphasis, and the emphasis is the identity, and the identity is the lando, and the lando is the guitar's home. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the first solo album, "Somos Adu" of 1986, which the Oxford entry describes as arrangements in the rhythms of festejo, panalivio, valse, tambarria, zapateo and marinera, six rhythms where the suite has four, and the six are the same move at a larger scale, because the album is a suite of suites, and the suite is the album in miniature, and the miniature is the proof that the mechanism scales. Watch the synthesis: when the zapateo ends, and the marinera begins, and the lando begins, and the festejo bursts in, and the guitar does not change hands, and the cajon does not change hands, and the four rhythms are one piece, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** fold four rhythms into one piece, and the piece is the coast. The synthesis is the argument. Against the chain: Baca (#363) also gathered rhythms, but hers was a gathering of forms across the Caribbean and Latin America, cumbia and bolero and plena y bomba; Casaverde is a gathering of four rhythms from one coast, which is why her version is a diaspora and his is a home. The Kipus (#364 M5) also carried a catalogue of regions, but theirs was a map walked town by town; Casaverde is a map folded into one room, which is why their version is a road and his is a suite.

## M3. The fingertip, not the nail

He plays with the fingertip only, and the only is the mechanism. The Vortex article's transcription work gives the technique in exact terms: on the right hand, Casaverde uses exclusively the pad of the finger for tone production on the strings, not the nail, not the pick, and the pad is the sound, because the pad is soft and the softness is the warmth, and the warmth is the voice, and the voice is the guitar singing instead of plucking, and the singing is the mechanism, because a guitar played with the pad does not attack, it touches, and the touching is the sensuality in M7, and the sensuality is the style. The rest of the technique follows from the pad: the timbre range of the strings along the neck is limited to the sul tasto characteristic of the region between frets 15 and 19, the right hand usually parked at the middle of the sound hole and the wrist turned slightly down, and the parking is the mechanism too, because the hand does not travel, it stays, and the staying is the steadiness, and the steadiness is the solemnity, and the solemnity is the lando at 60 to 70 beats per minute, slow enough for poetry to run like speech. And the arpeggio is replaced: every p-i-m-a formula, thumb-index-middle-ring, is substituted by tying the strings in a single attack that opposes thumb and index, a ligature, and the ligature is the mechanism, because the four fingers do not take turns, the thumb and the index argue, and the argument is the rhythm, and the rhythm is the lando, and the lando is the guitar. The left hand is difficult on purpose: the fingering holds the strings pressed the maximum time possible within the bar, above all to keep the bass voice alive even when the written figure is short, and the holding is the mechanism, because the bass must not die between notes, and the not-dying is the continuity, and the continuity is the suite. Watch the fingertip: when the nail never touches the string, and the hand stays high on the neck, and the thumb and the index tie the strings in one attack, and the bass stays pressed after the figure ends, and the sound is warm and slow and solemn, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** play with the pad, and let the pad be the voice. The fingertip is the sound. Against the chain: Eva (#362) also sang with a voice that carried three bloods, but hers was a voice that carried; Casaverde is a hand that carries, which is why her version is a throat and his is a fingertip. Oscar Aviles, the classic vals guitarist of Chabuca's four, is the contrast the chain supplies: the vals hand against the lando hand, and the difference is the pad.

## M4. The open chord with no third

The chord has no third, and the having-no-third is the mechanism. The introduction of the lando in "Cuatro tiempos negros jovenes" sits in the harmonic region of Bm7 as tonic, and its base chord is Bm5, a chord of the type 1-5-8 inside a pentatonic scale, B, C sharp, E, F sharp, A, with no third and no seventh, the note B doubled on the third and fourth strings, and the doubling is the mechanism, because the chord is hard and solemn at once, and the hardness is the missing third, and the solemnity is the doubled root, and the two together are the sound of a music that refuses to say whether it is major or minor, and the refusal is the identity, and the identity is the lando, and the lando is the guitar. The article names these power chords, tricords in root position with the third omitted, and notes their presence in rock and pop as a tonal counterculture against the hegemony of the V-I and I-VIm progressions, and the counterculture is the mechanism at the level of history, because Casaverde refuses the dominant-to-tonic preparation symbolically, his trills finishing on the sixth degree, the note G, refusing to prepare the tonic, and the refusing is the politics, and the politics is the refusal in M9, and the refusal is the man. The harmonic sequence runs Bm5, Bm5/A, GMaj7/13, Bm5/11/F sharp, Fdim, C7/9/11, a base chord interrupted by passing tensions on the way to the dominant, and the interruption is the mechanism, because the chord never arrives cleanly, it arrives through tension, and the tension is the journey, and the journey is the suite. Watch the open chord: when the third is missing, and the root is doubled, and the sound is hard and solemn, and the dominant is refused, and the chord never resolves the way the textbook says it must, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** leave the third out, and let the root do the talking. The omission is the harmony. Against the chain: Manzanero (#358) also built his songs on harmony, but his was an extension, ninths and elevenths added to a full chord; Casaverde is a subtraction, the third taken away, which is why his version is an addition and Casaverde's is an omission. Chabuca (#361) also broke the vals structures toward jazz and bossa nova, but hers was a breaking by the composer; Casaverde is a breaking by the chord, which is why her version is a writing and his is a voicing.

## M5. The descending bass as sorrow

The bass walks down, and the walking-down is the mechanism. In the introduction of the lando, the basses run a descending path, B, A, G, F sharp, configuring a variant of the Phrygian tetrachord, and the descending is the mechanism, because a bass that walks down is a rhetoric, and the rhetoric has a name in the Western learned tradition, catabasis or descensus, an archetype used to express sadness and grief, and in the modal Phrygian world of the Spanish flamenco dances the same progression, Bm, A, G, F sharp, is called the Andalusian cadence, IVm, III flat, II flat, I, and the naming is the proof that the sorrow is old, because the same four steps have carried grief from the baroque to the flamenco to the lando, and the carrying is the tradition, and the tradition is the guitar. The mechanism repeats at every scale of the piece: the first variation states a motif in the progression I minor, VII, VI7, in 6/4, which Casaverde says belongs to the classical form in which past generations played the style, and the second variation confirms it with a heavier, almost continuous bass marking, and the last variation reaches the peak of the rubato, phrasing in one bar a dotted quarter, two quadruplets and a triplet whose last beat anticipates the next bar's note, and the anticipation is the mechanism too, because the sorrow does not wait for the bar, it arrives early, and the arriving-early is the syncopation, and the syncopation is the lando. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the life: the Air Force years, the prison, the refusal, the return to civilian life, are a descent and a return, and the return is the guitar, and the guitar is the ascent after the descent, and the ascent is the suite. Watch the descent: when the bass steps down B, A, G, F sharp, and the chord refuses the tonic, and the next variation states the same descent heavier, and the last note of the bar arrives before the bar ends, and the sorrow is the shape of the steps, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** walk the bass down, and let the descent be the grief. The steps are the sorrow. Against the chain: Sosa (#359) also sang grief, but hers was a grief imposed by a dictatorship, an arrest on a stage; Casaverde is a grief built into four bass notes, which is why her version is an event and his is a progression. The Kipus (#364 M2) also made the strings cry, but theirs was a crying of the introduction before the voice; Casaverde is a crying of the bass under the melody, which is why their version is a prelude and his is a foundation.

## M6. The economy of sound

He leaves notes out, and the leaving-out is the mechanism. The Vortex article names the ethos plainly: an ethos of musical complexity from the austerity of compositional elements, and the austerity is the mechanism, because the lando for solo guitar is built by economy, not by minimalism in the strict sense, but by a continuous and homogeneous formal structure, triadic and scarce harmonies, and an absence of melodic lines that are not needed, and the absence is the expression, because the silence does more work than the sound, and the work is the poetry, and the poetry is the slowness, and the slowness is the tempo, and the tempo is 60 to 70 beats per minute for the lando, slow enough that the poetry runs almost like speech, without periodic metre, and the running-like-speech is the mechanism, because the guitar talks before it sings, and the talking is the voice, and the voice is the man. Chabuca taught him the economy, and the teaching is the proof: her phrasing freed the accentuation of words from any metre, becoming almost spoken, while keeping the usual agogics of the coastal rhythms, and her performances gave the silence the leading role, with results often more expressive than constant musical emission, and the elision produced a performance that stressed the ways of concatenating a music before the profusion of its harmonic and melodic possibilities, and the concatenating is the mechanism, because the music is the joints, not the pieces, and the joints are the silences, and the silences are the guitar. The polymetre is the economy at the level of time: in Chabuca's "Lando" the singing seems to sit in simple quadruple time, the guitar stays in compound quadruple avoiding always accenting the first beat, and the oboe crosses the theme in compound binary time, and the three times run at once, and the running-at-once is the mechanism, because the economy of each line makes room for the others, and the room is the silence, and the silence is the piece. Watch the economy: when the note is left out, and the silence stays, and the tempo is slow, and the phrase runs like speech, and the guitar and the voice and the oboe keep different times without colliding, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** leave the note out, and let the silence carry it. The omission is the expression. Against the chain: Baca (#363) also built an aesthetic from witnesses and fragments, but hers was a harvest of testimonies; Casaverde is a harvest of silences, which is why her version is an archive and his is an elision. Libertad (#356) also sang with a voice that crossed into opera, but hers was a crossing by range; Casaverde is a crossing by restraint, which is why her version is a reach and his is a holding-back.

## M7. The sensual treatment

He gives the lando a sensual guitar treatment, and the sensuality is the mechanism. The phrase is Casaverde's own, carried by the thesis through Chocano and Llorens: he adds to the landos of Chabuca what he describes as a "tratamiento guitarristico sensual" to the lando pattern for cajon developed by Caitro Soto, in order to formalize "una unidad" in Chabuca's experimental landos, which eliminated the extra Latin percussion used by Peru Negro, and the elimination is the mechanism, because the 1970s tendency was to adapt rhythmic patterns and add Afro-Cuban instruments to representations of the Afro-Peruvian, and the Chabuca group does the opposite, it clears the sound space of the larger percussion and uses small acoustic percussion, the cajon, the cajita, the spoons, the cencerro, and the clearing is the sensuality, and the sensuality is the closeness, and the closeness is the unity, and the unity is the guitar and the cajon as one thing, and the one thing is the sound. The thesis's witnesses agree on the words: "unidad", "limpieza", "sensualidad", "cercania", "libertad", unity, cleanliness, sensuality, closeness, freedom, and the agreement is the proof, because a producer of Afro-Peruvian projects says that listening to "Cada cancion con su razon" she does not know where one ends and the other begins, like a contact dance, and the not-knowing is the mechanism, because the guitar and the cajon and the voice have stopped being separate instruments, and the stopping is the unity, and the unity is the performance. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the solo albums: "Memorias de Felix Casaverde - Guitarra Negra" is one guitar and one cajon, the unity reduced to two, and the two are the sensuality, and the sensuality is the closeness, and the closeness is the piece. Watch the treatment: when the big percussion leaves, and the small percussion stays, and the guitar answers the cajon pattern instead of accompanying it, and the listener cannot tell where the guitar ends and the cajon begins, and the sound is close and clean and free, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** clear the space, and let the guitar and the cajon be one body. The sensuality is the unity. Against the chain: Eva (#362) also leaned on the cajon, but hers was a second voice beside the first, a partner; Casaverde is a guitar that becomes one body with the cajon, which is why her version is a partner and his is a unity. Peru Negro, the contrast the thesis supplies, also played the Afro-Peruvian, but theirs was an adding of Latin percussion; Casaverde is a taking away, which is why their version is an addition and his is a clearing.

## M8. The laboratory at Chabuca's house

Chabuca's house was a laboratory, and the laboratory is the mechanism. Felix Casaverde, first guitarist of the Afro-Peruvian stage, says that Chabuca stimulated musical creation in laboratories she ran at home, proposing exercises of many kinds, above all envisioning images, and the envisioning is the mechanism, because she would say, "A ver Don Felix, piense en Lima antigua, piense en el Centro de Lima, piense en una calle empedrada, piense en una banca, piense en un farol... Toque!", think of old Lima, think of the centre of Lima, think of a cobbled street, think of a bench, think of a lamppost, touch, play, and the playing is the answer to the image, and the answer is the music, and the music is the street, and the street is the bench, and the bench is the lamppost, and the lamppost is the guitar. The thesis carries the detail that makes the laboratory real: the sessions ran from the morning to late at night, unpaid, and the unpaid is the mechanism, because the work was a commitment to a process, not a job, and the commitment cost him problems at home, and the problems are the proof that the laboratory was serious, and the seriousness is the music, and the music is the two albums that came out of it. And the laboratory is also a memory: Felix says Chabuca was for him a living reference for what he could no longer ask his mother, because his mother did not want to remember or never told him more, and his mother at eleven had been the nurse of Chabuca's younger brother while Chabuca was thirteen, and the two women were contemporaries, and Chabuca loved to recreate and return to that time, and the returning is the mechanism, because the old Lima Felix's mother would not remember is the old Lima Chabuca remembered for him, and the remembering is the laboratory, and the laboratory is the guitar. The career that follows is the same mechanism at the level of the trip: the laboratory's work was defended in Madrid at OTI in 1977, then on "300 millones", then in the "Tarimba Negra" recording of 1978, and the defending is the proof that the laboratory was not a rehearsal, it was the work, and the work is the stage. Watch the laboratory: when the image is named, and the guitar answers, and the session runs past midnight, and nobody is paid, and the old Lima comes back through the remembering, and the music is the street and the bench and the lamppost, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** name the image, and let the guitar answer it. The laboratory is the house. Against the chain: Chabuca (#361) also worked in a house, but hers was a salon that held two goddaughters; Casaverde is a laboratory inside that house, which is why her version is a salon and his is a workshop. The Kipus (#364 M7) also had a home base, the Rosita Rios, but theirs was a venue that launched caravans; Casaverde is a home that launched nothing but the music, which is why their version is a base and his is a laboratory.

## M9. The refusal of the label

He refuses the label, and the refusing is the mechanism. At his wake Antoine George reports Felix's own words in his grave voice: "Antoine, yo no soy afroperuano! Soy negro y sobre todo soy peruano", I am not Afro-Peruvian, I am black and above all I am Peruvian, and the sentence is the mechanism, because the label "afroperuano" is a platform, and the platform is a movement, and the movement is the renaissance led by Nicomedes and Victoria Santa Cruz in the 1960s, and Felix says that before that renaissance these genres were not heard, and he resisted identifying with the platform the movement proposed, and the resisting is the identity, and the identity is the guitar. The argument with Nicomedes is the proof, and the proof is told in the blog: Felix would argue with "Don Nico", the "Papa" of the Afro-Peruvian renaissance, and explain to him that the black man "de por aca" had little to do with the blacks "de por alla", that many peoples with their cultures had passed through here, that there was the Chinese migration and this and that, and Nicomedes always tried to find a black origin for everything, and time has shown it was not so, and the argument lasted until SICLA in 1986, the Semana de la Integracion Cultural Latinoamericana, when "Don Nico" called him and said, "Oye hijo, todo lo que hemos conversado estos anos... tenias razon... todo esta aca", and Raul Garcia Zarate was witness that night, and the concession is the mechanism made audible, because the man who built the platform admits the guitarist was right, and the rightness is the guitar. Manuel Acosta Ojeda carries the same refusal in another form: Felix was one of the few black artists who agreed with him that in Peru the only African thing there was, was the embassies, and when asked about "lo afroperuano" he always answered with his accustomed calm, "nada de afroperuano, aqui no hay religiones africanas, el que ha visitado Senegal, Angola, de primera mano, sabe que esto es harina de otro costal", and the calm is the mechanism, because the refusal is not shouted, it is stated, and the stating is the dignity, and the dignity is the man. Watch the refusal: when the label is offered, and the guitarist declines it, and the argument runs for years, and the elder concedes, and the music keeps playing the coast instead of the continent it is supposed to come from, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** refuse the label, and let the guitar say where you are from. The refusal is the identity. Against the chain: Baca (#363) also worked inside the Afro-Peruvian name, but hers was a building of an institution on it, the Instituto Negrocontinuo; Casaverde is a refusal to stand on it, which is why her version is a building and his is a declinature. Nicomedes Santa Cruz, the next candidate in the chain, is the man he argued with, and the argument is the doorway to him, because the chain's next study is the other side of this one.

## M10. The accompanist who is not an accompanist

He accompanies everybody, and the accompanying is not accompanying, and the not-accompanying is the mechanism. The list is long: Chabuca Granda, Susana Baca, Eva Ayllon, Tania Libertad, Olga Milla, Carmina Cannavino, Julie Freundt, Marcela Perez Silva, Patricia Saravia, Carmen Lamarque, Mirtha Guerrero, among others, and the length is the proof that he was the guitarist Lima called when a singer needed a guitar, and the calling is the mechanism, because the singers called him an accompanist, and he was not one, and the not-one is the statement George makes at the wake: "Dicen de el que 'acompan~o' a casi todas la grandes interpretes de la cancion peruana...
