# Iskra Velitchkova: Study Notes

**Site:** Iskra Velitchkova (b. 1988, Bulgaria), generative artist living and working in Madrid, Spain; self-taught; economics degree, then data visualisation, then a bank's mathematics and physics team, then full-time art (quit c. 2020-21, year unpinned); plotter-based practice in ink on paper alongside long-form digital systems (Art Blocks, Feral File, Verse, fxhash, objkt). Her stated program: "Generative art is about humans, not machines." Her central theme is distance. Her core method is recreate-then-break: build a system that makes something recognisable, then deliberately break it until something else emerges.

## The life: the data visualiser who quit the bank

The biography is well documented because she tells it herself, twice at length: a Bright Moments Quarterly interview (Malte Rauch, November 2022) and her own research notebook (iskraovelitchkova.com/research.html, 479 lines), plus a Unit London interview, a HOLO dossier interview, and an objkt interview from January 2026. The study does not fill the gaps those sources leave.

Iskra Velitchkova was born in 1988 in Bulgaria; no city is named in any source found (C1950). She moved with her family to Spain and grew up there -- her identity, in the objkt interview's words, "unfolded between these two cultural geographies", Balkan heritage plus Mediterranean upbringing (C1953: the move rests on that single source). She studied economics at university, choosing between economics and physics -- "understanding the social or natural world" -- and choosing the social one; the institution is never named (C1952). In her third semester a professor who knew her background suggested data visualisation as a way to combine economic analysis and creativity. She co-founded a data-visualisation studio with that professor ("He was very good with the statistics and I was really good with the design"); the studio was "quite successful in Spain" and closed for personal reasons when her partner could not continue. "I owe him a lot."

Then she joined a team of mathematicians and physicists at a large bank in Spain, doing explorative work with data. The turning-point assignment: her team was asked to build a restaurant recommendation app. For a month she rode Madrid on her motorbike with a GoPro, documenting streets, asking what happens to the city when it comes to us compressed into data. "When she left the bank, it was not the data itself that interested her, but the algorithms behind it." She quit to dedicate herself full-time to art. No source pins the year; the interviews (November 2022, January 2023) say "a few years ago", consistent with c. 2020-21 but not stated (C1951). Do not assert a year.

Her own summary of the move, quoted everywhere: "Generative art, you could say, is the same thing as data visualization but you are not dealing with data." She wanted to explore algorithms "without any kind of external constraints". The data-viz years are not a prehistory to the art; they are its first draft. The studio taught her that a system can carry a story, the bank taught her that data is a compression of a lived place, and the motorbike month taught her the question she has kept asking since: what is lost, and what is found, in the distance between a thing and its representation.

## The turn: the year of machines

After quitting she spent a year "entirely focused on exploring where I wanted to go", with the clear vision it would be technology-based but needed "to link to something physical". She bought a pottery wheel without knowing what she would do with it, learned clay and porcelain, then bought a plotter and "fell in love with it and I forgot my original mission!" Her own notebook says it plainer: "I spent the following year at home, surrounded by all kinds of machines, toys, musical instruments and even a pottery wheel. I wanted to give myself the greatest possible freedom and see what direction emerged from it."

The plotter is the turn, and it is also the method. "A composition appears on screen in less than a second, but once you plot it, the course of time changes. You can observe for hours and see the rationale behind the drawing... I create a piece, I plot it, I understand it, and then I go back to the computer with stronger directions." The plotter is her analogue camera now: "You cannot get crazy if you don't master your tools." The year of machines produced a practice with two poles that have never merged: the digital works go fluorescent and saturated (Svetlo's glow, ToSolaris's orange and magenta), the plotter works go black line on ochre paper (Prayers). The same system, two bodies -- the same deliberate non-identity He arrived at from the other side, in watercolor.

Two dated turning points follow. In 2021 she is in -GRAPH, Feral File's plotter exhibition curated by Casey Reas, alongside He, Jovanic, Gachadoat, Merrill, and Hobbs: her CHERTI makes 30 unique physical drawings from one piece of software. In 2022 she sells a piece at Sotheby's Natively Digital 1.3 in New York and uses the proceeds, at the same auction, to buy Vera Molnar's "2% of disorder in co-operation" -- the student buying the teacher, in public, with the sale money. She had met Molnar shortly before Molnar died, just before turning 100: "the beauty of continuing to play at ninety nine". The purchase is the practice's emblem: the machine's disorder, bought back by hand.

## The practice: distance, the second self, selection

Three load-bearing ideas organise everything she makes, all in her own words.

First, distance. "The central theme of my work is distance, an evolving concept in our contemporary world." Distances are no longer purely physical: between a person and a book, a fear, a desire; the distance between a coded bird and a real bird; between representation and reality. Her notebook's epigraph for the whole practice is a question: "How many lines of code are from me to a bird?" The bird series is the distance made visible -- a recognisable thing approached through code, never arrived at.

Second, the second self. "When you create rules and are surprised by your own rules, you initiate a conversation with a parallel version of yourself. It's you, but not quite." Randomness is the dialogue partner: "We guide the machine but then it brings an expanded outcome from our initial instruction. It becomes a dialogue in the form of a loop, where randomness is the guiding force... to play with the largest possible range of randomness. It is there where I found the most interesting happening." The system is not a tool that executes; it is a parallel self that answers back, and the surprise is the point.

Third, selection as the artistic gesture. "The system can generate, but it cannot decide what matters to me... That responsibility remains entirely mine." And the method that follows from it: "I no longer create an image with the intention of producing that particular image. I create the conditions, I observe, and I wait. Then, through that waiting, the image appears." Narrative is mandatory, not decoration: "I absolutely need narrative in my work... meaning is, on some level, constituted by narrative... The first step in each of my projects is concerned with this guiding story." Long-form projects let one story connect many outputs; ToSolaris is a novel told as a system's behaviour over time.

Her proposition about technology, quoted in HOLO: "I don't think we should make technology more human, I believe that we have to push technology forward to understand us better." And the challenge that follows: "I challenge the notion that digital art opposes physical art... The presentation format, often inherent to the work, is merely a medium." "Can technology, in a way, be nature communicating with us?" Prayers is, in her words, "a hypothetical place for that hypothetical dialogue."

The influences she names are lineage, not decoration: Vera Molnar (subtle, elegant, minimal, complex all at once); Chillida's sketches ("the work of his left hand"); Basquiat ("raw, blazing chaos"); Malevich (tattooed on her arm); Alma Mahler, Tarkovsky, Dali, the Beatles, the band Travis (each INK composition anchored by a Travis song), Tanizaki on shadow, and the Bauhaus, suprematists, and Dada as the shared geometric inheritance.

The practice has a rhythm she states plainly: chaotic, no structure, works all the time; no alarm, coffee, coding on her terrace; intense multi-day sprints then decompression; plotter work as the calming counterweight to coding. And a dated correction to that rhythm: she entered 2023 "with a clear goal -- to slow down. After two frenetic years she needed to take a step back and focus on developing her technique and art practice." ESCAPE is the product of that pace change. "2024 started with a clear ambition: to think."

## The evolution: from saturated worlds to a bird made of cloth

The arc has five phases, and the constant is the three pillars of the practice: a rule system, a physical material, and a story. What migrates is the palette and the hardness of the form.

Phase 1, saturated worlds (2021): They are coming iii is red-orange translucent slabs standing on a glowing teal sea -- colour-saturated, architectural, science-fiction world-building. CHERTI, in the same year, is already monochrome and plotter-based. The two poles exist from the start: the colour-saturated digital and the black-line physical.

Phase 2, systems with a subject (2022): the breakout year. Light as a sphere (Svetlo), the sea as a horizon (horizon(te)s, with Lieberman), a city from ellipses only (Uninhabitable), a novel as a sequence of episodes (ToSolaris), a curtain as a threshold (Behind Curtains), a hypothetical dialogue with nature (Prayers). The palette splits cleanly: the digital works go fluorescent, the plotter works go black on ochre.

Phase 3, the slowdown (2023): ESCAPE turns the palette to black and white and the subject to architecture -- map, then Soviet brutalism, then the opera house, the break now stated plainly as the method. ANATOMY turns the bird monochrome and striped: a rabbit's ear becomes a beak. PAL breaks a generative system with a physical magnet on a VHS tape, live, in front of an audience.

Phase 4, colour returns as quotation (2024): Figures is Vasarely in red and blue; A Captive Parrot is flat constructivist colour blocks. The bird studies split into three poles: block, burst, glow. INK begins, the thinking dated to 2024, the work about memory as the container that outlasts both ink and pixels.

Phase 5, the bird as fabric (2026): ISG 01 | Bird / Scarf is the softest bird yet -- hairline threads, no hard edge, the animal dissolving into cloth. The arc of the whole catalogue is from hard geometry that becomes a bird, to a bird that becomes cloth.

## M1 -- Recreate, then break

The method, in her two-step description (objkt interview, January 2026): first recreate something recognisable in code -- a tree, for instance -- and then deliberately break it, pushing the system until something else emerges. Her own words: "Once I have constructed a system, I begin to break it. I undo my own decisions, disturb its logic and move things for no particularly clear reason. I build the game and then dismantle it, simply to see how the pieces find their way back to one another."

ESCAPE (2023, 325 pieces, Tonic/Verse) is the dated proof, and the clearest in the catalogue. The algorithm "began in systems that balance order and chaos. First a distinctive style reminiscent of architectural maps emerged. From this grew the brutalism characteristic of former Soviet cityscapes of the artist's early childhood in Bulgaria. Ultimately stifled by this rigidity and repetition, Velitchkova pushed the algorithm further -- breaking it and in the result discovering the form of an opera house, a structure that had become a symbol of freedom to the artist, the promise of losing oneself in art." Map, then brutalism, then break, then opera house: three states of one system, each the breaking of the last. The inspected Escape #207 shows it: a glowing white scaffold slab like an opera house seen front-on, built of thin lines, brackets, rectangles and translucent grey panels on black, fading into darkness at the edges.

PAL (2022 work, 2023 live performance at ART SG) is the only work where the breaking is done by a physical object in real time: an analogue computer, a video recorder, one VHS tape, a timer, a generative system on a digital screen, and a magnet. The tape played continuously for four days while the artists rewound it to random positions and passed a neodymium magnet around it -- real-life interference, not digitally produced -- then digitised into 100 unique 30-second pieces.

**Takeaway:** build the recognisable thing first, then break it on purpose; the break is the work, not the failure of it. The break must be visible as a sequence -- map, brutalism, opera house -- not as noise.

## M2 -- The emergent recognisable form

The bird was not designed; it arrived as a deviation, and what matters is the gap between the coded bird and a real one. The objkt interview: the bird series came out of an output unexpectedly resembling a bird; what mattered was "the gap: the distance between her coded bird and a real one". Sotheby's, on le bird Noir: "By playing and disturbing recursive methods Iskra builds, breaks and refines its own generative models to explore how sometimes, organic and natural illusions emerge from code by allowing humans to intervene in the process."

ANATOMY of a rabbit but bird (2023, +GRAPH, Feral File) is the mechanism with a date and a story. She started from "emptiness" and "how to do more with less", in line with her monochrome minimalist work of the year. Sketching, a silhouette emerged that reminded her of a rabbit; delighted, she made test drawings from the lines on screen; as she worked, "the rabbit slowly transformed into a bird. An ear became a beak; fur became feathers. Her iconic birds had emerged again." Her summary: "And that's how, through a generative mythology, you could say, my first birds created on a plotter came to life. Thanks to a rabbit." The inspected plotter drawing (#1/30) shows it: a bird in black-and-white stripes, flowing parallel bands curving into wings, a dense dark knot at the centre where the bands tangle.

The three inspected bird studies from the Unit London feature show the same system at three poles: A Captive Parrot (flat colour blocks, a cage the bird does not feel), bird-birth (a cloud of thousands of tiny white hairline strokes, a murmuration frozen, no clear bird shape, pure emergence), golden-bird (a glowing golden-orange oval of fine radiating filaments, an ember egg). The bird is the system's attractor: whatever the parameters, the work keeps drifting back to it.

**Takeaway:** tune the system so recognisable forms arrive uninvited, then keep the gap visible -- the coded bird is interesting precisely because it is not a real bird. Never draw the bird; let it arrive.

## M3 -- The plotter as translator and teacher

"A composition appears on screen in less than a second, but once you plot it, the course of time changes. You can observe for hours and see the rationale behind the drawing... I create a piece, I plot it, I understand it, and then I go back to the computer with stronger directions." The plotter is her analogue camera. The slowness is not a cost; it is the instrument. On screen the image is instant and therefore unreadable; on paper, over hours, the order of the lines becomes legible, and the legibility is what teaches the next version of the code.

CHERTI (-GRAPH, 2021) is the first appearance of the device. Her own words on the show: "The difference between digital and physical allows me to see the process itself. The constraints of the pen, the paper, and many other circumstances that only happen in real life make me more aware of how much we can learn from technology and its capacity to produce complexity." The software drawing in the browser is a small window of the full composition, to show more detail; the physical drawing reveals the whole picture, and both views are displayed together. Window versus whole: the screen shows a detail, the paper shows the system.

un-distance (2022, Verse, 3 items) is the first fully documented digital-plus-physical twin: the same system rendered twice, once on screen in Processing and once by the plotter in pen and ink with a hand-painted background, layering multiple drawings on top of each other -- deliberately non-identical, the same disagreement He builds in watercolor. Each NFT includes a unique plot (42 x 29.7 cm). The +GRAPH page shows the same pairing for ANATOMY: the software image and the plotted drawing diverge (different papers and inks), and Feral File shows both so the viewer can compare.

**Takeaway:** slow the fast thing down until its logic becomes visible. The plotter's hours are the pedagogy; a piece that appears all at once teaches nothing.

## M4 -- Layering, transparency, overlap

Casey Reas on +GRAPH: "Iskra works with minimal geometry through laying and transparency to construct images that lead toward introspection." Forms are made by the accumulation of translucent layers, not by outline. The overlap is the form; the outline is what is left over.

Svetlo (2022) is the mechanism as a sphere: millions of overlapping lines at varying scale, rotation, and line width, the glow bleeding past the sphere's edge into the field. The inspected Svetlo iii is a mint-green glowing sphere in a grainy green-gold haze, concentric rings rippling outward from two small dark dots inside it; Svetlo iv is a huge translucent grey-white sphere like a glass vessel, an hourglass or a tunnel of rings descending to a bright point. Hypothetically Micro / Macro (2021, Feral File Field Guide) is the earliest dated evidence: "a mysterious cluster of glowing semitransparent organic primitives" whose overlaps, not outlines, make the form.

Behind Curtains (2022) is the same mechanism as fabric: vertical translucent curtain panels in warm rust-orange and dusty lavender-grey, fine woven texture, soft vertical folds. Prayers (2022) is the same mechanism as architecture: dense black fine lines in vertical columnar bundles on ochre paper, so dense they read as solid black in places, then opening into airy arcs. The opera house in Escape #207 is the same mechanism as a scaffold: thin lines, brackets, rectangles, translucent grey panels, symmetric-ish around the centre, fading into darkness.

**Takeaway:** never draw an outline when an overlap will do. The image should be built by accumulation, and the density of the accumulation should be the drawing.

## M5 -- Scale, rotation, line width as focus

Svetlo again, but as a camera. Her words in the Unit London interview: "Playing with scales, rotations and line widths, makes the system evolve from (and to) very different moods and emotions." Svetlo i is clearly delineated; ii and iii "play actually with the opposite... this fluorescent glow... blurs the boundaries between the shape and the background." Definition depends on scale and the amount of lines, "almost like the way a camera works. The focus... becomes a creative element." She begins with an initial palette "which gets completely corrupted by the mess of pixels that emerge from the chaos of the system." The force of the light, even static, gives a sense of dynamics, "almost like witnessing a supernova".

Three dials, one system: the same sphere reads as intimate or cosmic depending on scale, rotation, and line width. The focus is not a rendering setting; it is the creative element. The palette is not chosen; it is corrupted by the system, and the corruption is kept.

**Takeaway:** three dials are enough -- scale, rotation, line width -- if the dials are the composition. Let the palette corrupt; do not re-pick it clean.

## M6 -- The sequential system with a base

ToSolaris (2022, Art Blocks, 100 editions, with Bright Moments) is a novel told as a system's behaviour over time, not by illustration. She broke Stanislaw Lem's Solaris (1961) into episodes and sketched scenes with code; the sketches "are not directly representational... they allude to scenes." The goal: "an algorithm capable of expressing the central themes of the narrative in a condensed form. It's a sequential base of rules, a growing form based on a common element: a base (a platform), from which different elements grow and navigate through Solaris." The system has an introduction, conflict, climax, and denouement -- "the whole context of an algorithm, not one image."

The inspected ToSolaris #23 shows the base doing its work: a blazing orange ground, magenta-pink radiating lines fanning from a central vertical red tower standing on a small disc base, the tower striped like a column of light. Her working notes record the system surprising her: the radiated tower produced "little caves emerging from the shape"; she explored shaders (introduced by Marcelo Soria-Rodriguez) for the eroded 60s-70s mood; a Truman Show rain mirage appears occasionally. The novel, for her, is about "the multiple lives we have within a single life... Are we our acts or our thoughts?" The Little Prince series applies the same method to a second literary source: "translating its themes into her own distinctive visual language and colour palette. Encounters with the rose or the snake are reimagined in ways that would be unrecognisable without context" (C2003: date, platform, edition size unconfirmed).

**Takeaway:** give the system a base and a story with acts, then let the episodes grow from the base. The work is the sequence, not any single frame; a single output is a scene, not the piece.

## M7 -- The physical feedback loop

ToSolaris's working notes record the loop that M3 describes in theory: she printed pieces and poured ink over the drawings, the ink following the grooves of the printed lines; the prints inspire how she pours, the pouring changes the tones she codes. Digital and physical correct each other, in both directions, repeatedly. The ink is not a finish applied to a digital image; it is a second system acting on the first, and the first is then re-coded in response.

The same loop runs through the practice at smaller scale: plot, observe, re-code (M3); hand-painted backgrounds under plotter lines in un-distance; the software image and the plotted drawing diverging in ANATOMY and both being shown, so the divergence itself is on display. The physical is never a printout of the digital. It is a collaborator that answers back with grooves, bleed, and paper.

**Takeaway:** let the physical step change the code, not just receive it. A print that feeds back into the next version is a loop; a print that only records the last version is a copy. (Not folded into a prompt this run; the loop is the method behind IV3 and IV6.)

## M8 -- The threshold that hides

Behind Curtains (2022, objkt 1/1s) draws only the veil; the scene beyond is never rendered. The work is the condition of not knowing. The objkt interview: "What the viewer encounters is deceptively simple: a transparent curtain, either still or gently swaying in the breeze. There is no visible contour of a window." The curtain is a threshold between interior and exterior, domestic and natural, accessible and out of reach. The series works like a travel diary: fragments of memory (a sunrise, the sea, poppy fields) tied to moments the artist may have witnessed. Two works imagine the window through the eyes of real people: Marseille of Alma Mahler in stark black and white, "charged with anticipation and tension"; Cadaques of Dali and Gala "unexpectedly still".

The inspected Cadaques piece shows the restraint: a wide panoramic frame, vertical translucent curtain panels in warm rust-orange and dusty lavender-grey, fine woven fabric texture, soft vertical folds, a faint horizontal band low in the frame -- a horizon, or a window sill. Nothing is depicted beyond the fabric itself. The system draws fabric, not a scene; the viewer infers the unseen beyond it.

**Takeaway:** draw the veil and withhold the view. The viewer completes the work by imagining what is behind the curtain, and the imagining is the interaction -- no click required.

## M9 -- The horizon as division

horizon(te)s (2022, with Zach Lieberman, Verse, 400 items on Tezos) is one clean line dividing two fields; everything else is noise on either side of it. The division is the work. The question the series asks: "What are the edges and borders of our vision and what do we see there?" Rather than one collective piece, a series in three modes each: "Me as me", "Me as you", "Me as you as me". Lieberman as himself: block/glitch images on a simple horizon. Lieberman as Velitchkova: layers and simple geometry, rectangles only, "blocks, windows and portals". Velitchkova as Lieberman: minimal work in lines, colour, contrast and shadow "to recreate this feelight of shine... using just lines". Velitchkova as herself: the sea -- "Either a port or a mystic sailing boat, both have some disturbing elements in the scene. Sometimes a mesh -- is it possible to reflect this smell of iron? And sometimes an undefined stone cave -- is it there to protect us, or to attack us?" She went through "more than 5 different systems before getting the right one" for her own mode.

The inspected series cover shows the division at its cleanest: a dense pixel-block field split by a razor-sharp horizontal horizon line at mid-height; above, a golden-yellow sky sparsely flecked with teal, red, and blue; below, a dark teal/red/blue sea, densely speckled, noisier and heavier. (C2001: which artist and which of the six modes produced the cover image is unconfirmed; the block/glitch look matches Lieberman's described "me as me" mode.)

Uninhabitable (2022, fxhash) is the same division built from a single primitive: a generative system built only from ellipses, in four distinct compositions. The inspected Uninhabitable #47: a pale cream ground, mostly empty, a low horizontal band of black translucent rectangles -- a ruined city skyline -- overlapping into darker blocks at their crossings, faint vertical hairlines rising above and falling below the band like rain or threads. One shape, one division, a city.

**Takeaway:** one line can be the whole composition if everything on either side of it disagrees. The horizon is not a background; it is the argument.

## M10 -- Selection as the artistic gesture

"The system can generate, but it cannot decide what matters to me... That responsibility remains entirely mine." Selection "is not an administrative step that comes after the work. It is one of the central artistic gestures." The waiting in M3 is the other half of it: "I create the conditions, I observe, and I wait. Then, through that waiting, the image appears." The artist's job is not to draw the image but to recognise it when it appears, and to say no to the thousand images that do not matter.

This is the load-bearing idea behind the whole practice, and the one that keeps the recreate-then-break method honest: the machine proposes, the human disposes, and the disposing is the art. INK (2024-, in progress) pushes it furthest: the work "had to be free... I wouldn't let it depend on the inevitable decay of ink on paper or the unavoidable obsolescence of pixels. INK would rely on the one thing that truly depends on us: our memory. That would be its foundation. Its container." "I wanted the act of remembering to transcend the act of seeing." Each stroke reflects "fragments of the human experience: figures, letters, dreams." While curating it she was pregnant with her daughter, and "quite suddenly, I saw the body of a woman emerge from the system. A mature woman, carrying the traces of time, of her children, of her skin and of herself." (C1957: the project in that notebook fragment is unnamed; do not attach the pregnancy to INK without verification. C2005: INK's date and release status unconfirmed.)

**Takeaway:** the system generates; the artist selects. A piece that shows everything the system made is a catalogue; a piece that shows the one image that mattered is a work. (Not folded into a prompt this run; the gesture is the discipline behind IV1 and IV2.)

## The aesthetic logic

The aesthetic is the distance made visible. Minimal geometry, layered until it glows or until it goes black; transparency instead of outline; a recognisable thing approached and never quite reached. The palette splits by medium and never reconciles: fluorescent digital (Svetlo's mint and smoke, ToSolaris's orange and magenta, the horizon's gold and teal) against monochrome plotter (black line on ochre, striped birds, scaffold opera houses). The split is the argument -- the same system, two bodies, deliberately non-identical -- and it is the same argument He makes in watercolor, from the other side of the plotter.

The work is quiet where the field is loud. A Bailey line from the Field Guide show: her work "stands out in contrast to all the noise for how quiet, subtle, and meditative it is." The quiet is structural, not decorative: the systems are small (ellipses only; three dials; one horizon line; a base and episodes), the outputs are mostly air (Uninhabitable #47 is a low band of rectangles on a mostly empty cream ground), and the meaning arrives as a gap -- between the coded bird and a real bird, between the curtain and the window, between the map and the opera house. The viewer does the last step. The pieces that ask for interaction earn it by withholding: the curtain that never opens, the horizon that never resolves into a scene, the plotter that takes hours to say what the screen said in a second.

The evolution's direction is toward softness without losing the system: from hard geometry that becomes a bird to a bird that becomes cloth. The 2026 bird is a scarf of hairline threads. The system did not get looser; the form it arrives at got softer. That is the whole practice in one image: the rules stay strict, the thing they make learns to dissolve.
