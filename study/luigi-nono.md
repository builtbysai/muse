## M1. The row as a political choice: the serial row is never neutral material

The first acknowledged work already states the whole method. Variazioni canoniche sulla serie dell'op. 41 di A. Schoenberg (1949-50) is built on the twelve-tone series of Schoenberg's Op. 41 Ode to Napoleon, including the "Ode-to-Napoleon" hexachord, plus a six-element row of rhythmic values, and Nono later called the piece "the consequence of my first studies of enigmatic canons but also an ideological choice", because the Ode was an anti-Hitler work, so the row was chosen for what it already meant, and the row's structure then carries the meaning. Il canto sospeso (1955-56) uses an all-interval twelve-tone row, and the Ricordi/Stenzl summary is that from the earliest works "the techniques of the avant-garde were put at the service of an expressive aim and a direct position on a given issue". The mechanism is: choose the row for what it already means, then let the row's structure carry the meaning, so that the politics is inside the notes before any text is sung. A piece built this way has an abstract surface that is not abstract at all, and the listener who hears only pitches is hearing the politics in code, whether or not the code is ever decoded aloud.

**Takeaway:** the material is the message before the text arrives; a generative piece can carry its meaning in the choice of its seed, not in a label pasted on top.

## M2. Text shattered into syllables, distributed across voices: the sentence exists only as a cloud

In Il canto sospeso the texts are farewell letters written by young resistance fighters condemned to death by the Fascists, and Nono does not set them as lines to be sung. The words are "fractured into syllables exchanged between voices to form floating, diversified sonorities", and the Foundation biography describes the text as "enunciated in its vocalic or phonetic components either successively in the individual parts or simultaneously as a sound bloc or aggregate", and Nono's stated aim was "to musicalize the texts by breaking the language up into syllables, thus making its musical content ready for composition". Stockhausen heard this as withdrawal and concealment, and his 15 July 1957 Darmstadt lecture "Sprache und Musik" attacked the piece on exactly that ground, saying the texts "are not delivered, but rather concealed" in a form so strict "they are hardly comprehensible when performed... Why, then, texts at all?", and Nono replied angrily in his 8 July 1960 lecture "Text-Musik-Gesang", saying "it is complete nonsense to conclude, from the analytic treatment of the sound shape of the text, that the semantic content is cast out". The mechanism is distribution: no single voice carries a sentence, the sentence exists only as a cloud passing between voices, and the phonetic-semantic whole survives precisely because it is broken apart, the way a face survives in a crowd of glances. The Kolner Stadt-Anzeiger, on 26 October 1956, called the 32-year-old "the most powerful of Webern's successors", and Jurg Stenzl calls the piece one of the central masterpieces of the 1950s, and the work is described as an "everlasting warning" and a refutation of Adorno's "to write poetry after Auschwitz is barbaric".

**Takeaway:** break the message into particles and scatter them across agents; the whole is recovered by the listener, not delivered by any one voice.

## M3. The documentary tape: real sounds as material, pulverized and reassembled

From La fabbrica illuminata (1964) on, Nono records the actual acoustic environment of the subject and composes with it. In May 1964 Nono, the writer Giuliano Scabia and the technician Marino Zuccheri recorded at the Italsider steelworks at Cornigliano near Genoa, blast furnace roar, molten iron casting, workers' voices and words, during a strike of 40,000-plus metalworkers, and the four tape tracks went to four loudspeakers at the corners of the room while the soprano sang unamplified, and the opening line is "The factory of death, they call it." A floresta e jovem e cheja de vida (1965-66) takes the Living Theatre, Julian Beck and Judith Malina, running screaming between three studios along a path of microphones in Milan in autumn 1965, and Zuccheri taped them, and their "reading of the dollar", numbers and bank names from a US banknote, screamed and whispered, went into the piece, and Nono and Zuccheri studied South Vietnamese and Cuban speech inflections. Contrappunto dialettico alla mente (1967-68) takes Rialto fish and vegetable market cries in Venice, voices singing, speaking and shouting, pipes of varying thickness and length, filtered and superimposed, and Nono called the texture "phonetic dust". Musica-Manifesto n. 1 (1968-69) takes the June 1968 Venice Biennale riots, about 150 students and workers against 5,000 police who "militarily occupied the city", plus May 1968 Paris slogans read by Edmonda Aldini and the voices of Che Guevara, Ho Chi Minh, Mao and Rudi Dutschke. Castro's recorded speeches are quoted in A floresta, Ein Gespenst geht um in der Welt (1971) and Al gran sole, and in Y entonces comprendio (1969-70) and Fur Paul Dessau (1974) the rhythm of Castro's speeches, "punctuated by vocal outbursts, became musical parameters structuring the work". The processing is mechanical and named: microphone technique, third-octave variable filter, dynamic modulator, variable speed, square-wave oscillators. Nono's own formula is that "the fusion of electronic material and natural material is analyzed and composed so as to make it impossible to distinguish where one begins and the other ends", and the result is a tape in which a factory, a market or a riot is gradually pulverized into particles, "almost like the tesserae of a mosaic", and reassembled as music. La fabbrica is "the first of his works in which the magnetic tape has a central and innovative role", and its live voice "interacts with itself pre-recorded on tape", and a delegation of Italsider workers attended the premiere on Tuesday 15 September 1964 at La Fenice, and a long night of discussion followed, and the workers later invited him to perform it at the factory and questioned his technical choices, not his message.

**Takeaway:** record the real place, then grind it until the source is unrecognizable and the music is the only thing left standing; the document survives as structure, not as quotation.

## M4. Choral phrasing built from orchestral fragmentation: the choir behaves like a fragmented orchestra

Ricordi/Stenzl record that in his choruses Nono developed "an utterly new method of 'choral phrasing' by shaping the fragmentary phrasing of the orchestra onto the vocal ensemble", inaugurating "an utterly new expressive relationship between the texts and the music". The choir does not sing lines; it behaves like a fragmented orchestra. The mechanism is a transfer of phrasing logic across ensembles: take the pointillist, interrupted, interval-leaping phrasing of the serial orchestra and impose it on voices, so that the chorus sounds pointillist and instrumental rather than melodic, and the text is carried by the ensemble the way a melody is carried by an orchestra that never plays it whole. Restagno's observation, quoted in the Red Bull piece, is that even before electronics, Il canto sospeso and Cori di Didone (1958, on Ungaretti) contain "potentialities that would be expressed... later with the help of electroacoustic instruments", because the early choral writing was already reaching for spatially mobile sound-shapes and hovering clusters. The choir was already a loudspeaker array before there were loudspeakers.

**Takeaway:** give one ensemble another ensemble's phrasing; the mismatch is the sound.

## M5. Harmonic blocks and their transformations: the point gives way to the mass

From Canti di vita e d'amore (1962) Nono "expanded his serial structures and worked with 'harmonic blocks' and their transformations", and the instrumental writing "conglomerate[s] the 'punctual' serial style of the early 1950s into groups, clusters of sounds, broad strokes that complimented the use of tape collage". The point gives way to the mass. Canti di vita e d'amore: Sul ponte di Hiroshima is in three movements, and the first is "the criminal madness of our time" on Gunther Anders's Diary from Hiroshima and Nagasaki, orchestral blocks conjuring the 200,000 victims, and the second is a monody for soprano, "Djamila Boupacha", the Algerian resistance fighter tortured by the French army, a "Song of Hope", and the third is a Cesare Pavese love poem, confidence in the future, with bells and metal resounding. The mechanism is the block as unit: dense chordal slabs that shift as units rather than single notes in series, and the transformation of a block, its thickening, thinning, transposition and fragmentation, is the composition, the way the transformation of a row used to be. This is the technical form of the political turn, because a block is a crowd where a point was a person, and the music stops counting individuals and starts moving masses.

**Takeaway:** when the material gets too big for points, move slabs; the slab is the crowd made audible.

## M6. Azione scenica and the theatre of consciousness: history as simultaneous rooms

Intolleranza 1960 (1960-61) and Al gran sole carico d'amore (1972-74, revised 1977) are not operas, and Nono forbade the word "opera" for Intolleranza. They are "stage-actions" built as montage: episodes placed side by side with no linear narrative, no conventional plot or characters, presenting "pivotal moments in the history of Communism and class-struggle side-by-side". The sources are Meyerhold and Mayakovsky 1920s theatre, Piscator's political theatre (Das politische Theater, 1929), and the magic lantern, and the staging of Intolleranza was by Josef Svoboda and Emilio Vedova. Intolleranza tells an emigrant's plight, exploitation, demonstrations, arrest, torture, concentration camp, refuge, abandonment, and its premiere on Thursday 13 April 1961 at La Fenice caused a riot, with neo-Nazis attempting disruption with stink-bombs and leaflets denouncing Nono, and its US premiere in Boston in 1965 was refused a visa for Nono by the US consulate in Trieste on the basis of PCI membership, while the Boston Symphony Orchestra circulated a petition and Svoboda wanted to project "Cuba yes, Yankees no!" and "Down with discrimination". Al gran sole has no dramatic narrative at all, and it is a montage of episodes side by side, the Paris Commune of 1871 with the heroine Louise Michel, the Russian Revolution of 1905, 1960s Chile with Che Guevara and Tania Bunke, the Cuban revolution, Turin workers, producing Nono's "theatre of consciousness", with a libretto by Nono and the director Yuri Lyubimov after Brecht, Tania Bunke, Castro, Che Guevara, Gorky, Gramsci, Lenin, Marx, Louise Michel, Pavese, Rimbaud and others, and the title from Rimbaud's "Les Mains de Jeanne-Marie", and four high solo sopranos in lyrical moments juxtaposed with massive tutti for soloists, chorus, orchestra and tape, and the premiere on Friday 4 April 1975 at Teatro alla Scala, Milan, conducted by Claudio Abbado, directed by Lyubimov, sets by David Borovsky, and Nono later called it a "monster of resources", and the Foundation biography calls it "the climax of Nono's overtly political period" and the apogee of his second period. In Prometeo the stage is stripped away entirely, and "all the 'theatre' or action is in the sound". The mechanism is montage as dramaturgy: history as a set of simultaneous rooms the listener walks between, not a story that walks past the listener.

**Takeaway:** do not tell the story in order; lay its rooms side by side and let the viewer walk between them.

## M7. Space as a compositional parameter: the room is an instrument

Venice's St Mark's Basilica acoustics drew the young Nono, and the mature works make space structural. Four loudspeakers at the corners of the room in La fabbrica illuminata (1964); four loudspeakers around the piano in ... sofferte onde serene ... (1976), two on the piano and two at the stage corners; the Halaphon spatialization program in Das atmende Klarsein (1980-81); the laminated-wooden ark in Prometeo (1984), designed by Renzo Piano and built inside the disused Church of San Lorenzo, with the audience in the centre and orchestra, choir and loudspeakers on three tiers around them, and Abbado conducting remotely via video monitors; the violinist walking between stands in La lontananza nostalgica utopica futura (1988-89). The new Freiburg technologies "allowed the sound to circulate in space, giving this dimension a role no less important than its emission", and space was conceived as an environment "in which spatial and temporal relationships could form part of a total dimension both in acoustic terms (with the multiplication and spatialization of the sound sources) and visually (by eliminating the separation of stage and auditorium)". The mechanism is spatialisation as composition: where a sound comes from, and where it goes, is written into the score the way pitch used to be, and the listener's position in the room changes what the piece is, because the piece is the room.

**Takeaway:** compose the room, not just the sound in it; the listener's seat is a parameter.

## M8. Live electronics: transformation in real time, a performance that can fail

Con Luigi Dallapiccola (1979), for six percussionists and live electronics, is the first work in which Nono "went beyond the mere use of tape, with sounds being transformed in real time", and it is his farewell to the RAI Studio di Fonologia, obsolete by then, and a tribute to a leading light of his youth. At the Freiburg Experimentalstudio Nono worked with delays, a harmonizer, and Hans Peter Haller's Halaphon to transform sung and played sounds as they happen and arrange them in space. The contrast with tape is the whole point: tape is fixed, and live electronics is a performance that can fail, drift, and be re-shaped each night, and the late works proceed by "'attempts' or 'choices'... allowing [the sound] to be enriched by new possibilities thrown up as reactions to technical hitches". A familiar instrument, flute or voice, is audibly stretched, delayed and moved around the room while it plays, and the transformation is the piece, not an effect applied to it.

**Takeaway:** transform the sound while it is being made, in the room, where it can go wrong; the risk is part of the instrument.

## M9. Real-time computer synthesis, the 4i: a computer played by hand, then deliberately retired

For Prometeo the CSC Padova team built the 4i processor, Di Giugno's IRCAM 4C adapted, with "i" for Italy, 64 oscillators, PDP 11/34 control, 16 kHz sampling, for real-time synthesis, and the distinction is explicit: explicitly NOT deferred-time synthesis (MUSIC 5) and NOT signal processing, because the ADC was not ready and Freiburg did the live electronics. Two performance environments were written for it. "Peata" is FM synthesis, 24 voices evoking a choir through beating micro-intervals, played live on six potentiometers by Alvise Vidolin and Sylviane Sapir, communicating by nods, and its principle derives from Richard Teitelbaum's Barcarola, which used the 4i before Prometeo, with unison-oscillator beating where Peata used frequency modulation. "Inter2" is nonlinear synthesis imitating Murano glass bells, and Sapir learned the timbre by playing crystal glasses by ear at Vidolin's house. Granular synthesis in Isola 1 produced continuity, not pointillism, "suono mobile", and a 116.5 Hz (B flat) drone opens over seven octaves into a distant choir to evoke the opening chord of Mahler's First Symphony, a "sonic silence" in Interludio 2. The 4i and the PDP had to sit at least 50 metres from the ark because of ventilation noise, and the PDP was carried by boat to San Lorenzo. The 4i was used ONLY in Venice 1984 and Milan 1985 and then abandoned by mutual decision: heavy, expensive and humidity-sensitive, and "from the third performance and later, only Freiburg technology was used". Nono delivered the definitive score only during rehearsals in September 1984, and Milan was, per Vidolin reporting Nono's opinion, "the better performance ever made". The mechanism is a computer that is played like an instrument, by hand, in the room, and then deliberately retired, because the technology served the listening and not the other way round.

**Takeaway:** build the machine, play it by hand, and be willing to abandon it after two performances; the machine is a means, never the piece.

## M10. Silence as material, the text that is never spoken: the audience listens to what has just sounded

Fragmente-Stille, an Diotima (1979-80), Nono's only string quartet, commissioned by the Beethovenfest, Bonn, and premiered on Monday 2 June 1980 in Bonn-Bad Godesberg by the LaSalle Quartet, is "music on the threshold of silence". The score is interspersed with fragments from Holderlin's poetry addressed to "Diotima", 52 sections, with 52 to 53 Holderlin fragments, sources differ, see C15, which the players are to "sing" silently, never spoken aloud, striving for Holderlin's "delicate harmony of inner life", and dynamics run from pppp to ffff, with frequent fermatas and varied bowing, and about 40 minutes, and a quotation of Ockeghem's chanson "Malheur me bat" appears in the viola like a cantus firmus near the end, "hardly recognizable", and there are also fragments of Beethoven and Verdi. Nono said "First, music has to breathe. Then (secondly) silence is very important to me here. Silence is a lively moment of sound. Silence sometimes has different needs. Sometimes it means to listen with the ears to something different that had been played before in order to find a relationship", and a forty-minute quartet is reported in which "not a single person coughed". The Academia.edu reading is that this is continuous with the early works, because "conveying a text is less important than creating an engaging human context", and the piece is "a turning point not only in Luigi Nono's oeuvre, but also in the history of musical modernism". The mechanism is silence as a structural element with a job to do: long silences in which the audience listens to what has just sounded, and words present only as a pressure on the players' playing, because the players are singing Holderlin inwardly and the inward singing changes the outward sound.

**Takeaway:** put the text in the score where nobody can hear it, and let it change the playing; the silence between sounds is the loudest part.

## M11. Suono mobile, mobile sound: the listener tracks a moving object

Nono's own term for the late works is suono mobile, mobile sound, and his definition is that sound whose "direction and origin... have to move through space in time. The point is not to confound or confuse the listener, but rather to stimulate active participation... the listener should be actively attempting to find his or her way through the proposed labyrinth". Concrete images from the Vidolin diary: winds "from zephyrs, very sweet, very pleasurable, to tornadoes, all on the move", and in Isola 1 the 4i sounds are "like dolphins. They periodically come to the surface and then disappear beneath the ocean's surface. Strings are the ocean". The mechanism is motion as the audible parameter: a sound that is heard approaching, passing and receding, so the listener tracks it like a moving object, and the listening becomes navigation, the listener finding a way through a labyrinth that is made of sound moving in space.

**Takeaway:** make the sound move, and the listener will move with it; navigation is the listening.

## M12. The walking performer and the edge of audibility: the body searches while the sound retreats

The last works make the performer's body move. In La lontananza nostalgica utopica futura the violinist walks slowly between six to ten stands spread across stage and auditorium, sometimes among the audience, and the tapes were made 15 to 19 February 1988 at the Freiburg Experimentalstudio from hours of Gidon Kremer improvisations recorded in early 1988, and Nono selected and processed sounds, so that "the tapes contain 1000 Gidons", and the Berlin premiere on Saturday 3 September 1988 was a version Nono was dissatisfied with and withdrew, and the final version was completed 31 January 1989 in Berlin, see C19. In "Hay que caminar" sonando (1989), the last completed work, two violinists walk among six stands, "literally searching out the music", which reworks material from La lontananza including the scala enigmatica, the enigmatic scale Verdi used in the Ave Maria of the Four Sacred Pieces. Simultaneously the sound retreats toward inaudibility: micro-intervals and "sonorities verging on the inaudible", introduced "to reawaken the ear, the eyes, human thought, intelligence; a maximum of alienated interiority", and Schott notes the late works "often move on the edge of audibility" and follow "the aesthetic principle of constant change", with Venice as the symbol. The mechanism is the coupling of two motions in opposite directions: the body moves more, and the sound moves less, until the performer is visibly searching for the next page while the sound itself is barely there, and "one is made always conscious of the sense of time passing".

**Takeaway:** let the performer walk and the sound whisper; the search is the piece.

## M13. Composition as collaboration, the score as a process: the piece is unfinished until the performers finish it

From La fabbrica and A floresta on, Nono's process "becomes increasingly linked to specific performers, whom he selected for their timbric and expressive characteristics": Carla Henius, Kadigia Bove, Liliana Poli, then Maurizio Pollini, Roberto Fabbriciani, Ciro Scarponi, Giancarlo Schiaffini, Susanne Otto, Stefano Scodanibbio. The tape of ... sofferte onde serene ... was made from Pollini's own playing, recorded over three days at the RAI Studio di Fonologia in December 1975 and sessions through 1976, extended by pedal resonances and Venice bell sounds heard from Nono's home on Giudecca, and the La lontananza tapes were made from Kremer's improvisations. Works were "often left at the stage of instructions, project or outline, to be defined in a complete form merely in the directions given to the performer (whose memory could often constitute, wholly or in part, the text of the work)", and A floresta itself was only reconstructed and published after his death, in 1998. Prometeo's definitive score was delivered only during rehearsals in September 1984, and it is "a sort of 'work in progress'", almost the only Nono work revised after its premiere. The mechanism is the score as a process rather than a finished object: a piece that is visibly unfinished until the performers finish it, differently each time, and the performer's memory and body are part of the text.

**Takeaway:** write the piece as instructions and let the performers finish it; the unfinished score is a feature, not a failure.

## M14. Quotation without collage: the historical fragment dissolved until it is barely recognizable

Nono repudiated neoclassicism early, but "very soon used 'canon' techniques and other experimental forms of early music... in the form of quotations or allusions to other music, since they came from either a 'high' culture or a 'folk' culture. He kept completely clear, however, of all forms of collage". The instances are the proof. The Ockeghem "Malheur me bat" cantus firmus buried in the viola in Fragmente-Stille, "hardly recognizable". The Mahler First Symphony opening chord, 116.5 Hz, B flat, recreated by the 4i and opened over seven octaves into a distant choir in Prometeo's Interludio 2. Verdi's scala enigmatica in "Hay que caminar" sonando and La lontananza. The Franco-Flemish canons studied with Maderna at the Biblioteca Marciana, Machaut, Dunstable, Ockeghem, Josquin, Willaert, the Gabrielis, feeding Variazioni canoniche. The mechanism is quotation as hidden load-bearing structure: a historical fragment is dissolved into the new texture until it is barely recognizable, and it functions as a structure, not a displayed sample, so that a familiar harmony surfaces for a second inside an unfamiliar sound and then sinks again.

**Takeaway:** quote by dissolving, not by displaying; the old music holds the new music up from underneath.

## Evolution

P1. Apprenticeship, 1941-1950. Malipiero from 1941 at the Venice Conservatory, as an external student, Renaissance madrigal, Zarlino, Gaffurio, Vicentino, Schoenberg, Webern, Bartok; law at Padua, enrolled 1942 at his father's insistence, graduated 1947, thesis on exceptio veritatis; Maderna from 1946, free composition lessons, Biblioteca Marciana study of Machaut, Dunstable, Ockeghem, Josquin, Willaert, the Gabrielis, Hindemith's Unterweisung im Tonsatz; Dallapiccola's criticism of an early score, "you still have to study a good deal", sends him back to study "practically from scratch" as Maderna's apprentice; Scherchen from 1948, conducting course in Venice, copyist then author for Ars Viva Verlag. No active part in the war or the Resistance, see C2. First compositions lost or unpublished: La discesa di Cristo agli inferi, 1945, lost; Due liriche greche, 1948-49, unpublished.

P2. Serial Darmstadt, 1950-1959. Variazioni canoniche (1950) to Diario polacco (1958-59). Annual Darmstadt attendance, teacher from 1957. Central figure of the post-war avant-garde alongside Boulez and Stockhausen, the "avant-garde trinity". He coined the term "Darmstadt School" himself in the 1958 lecture "Die Entwicklung der Reihentechnik", likening the group to the Bauhaus. A personal serial language is established in Canti per 13 and Il canto sospeso. The break: the 1 September 1959 lecture "Geschichte und Gegenwart in der Musik von heute", written with his pupil Lachenmann, attacking Cagean chance and integral serialism as a-historical, and a violent argument with Stockhausen the same day over "statistical structures", which Nono called "fascist mass structures", and the 1960 lecture "Text-Musik-Gesang" attacks Stockhausen's reading of Il canto sospeso. The friendship with Stockhausen effectively ends until the 1980s.

P3. Political theatre and tape, 1960-1974. Intolleranza 1960 (1960-61) opens the phase, and Omaggio a Emilio Vedova (1960) opens the electronic work at the RAI Studio di Fonologia with Zuccheri. Works become "broad frescos" for voices, instruments and tape: La fabbrica illuminata (1964), A floresta (1965-66), Contrappunto dialettico (1967-68), Musica-Manifesto n. 1 (1968-69), Ein Gespenst (1971), Como una ola (1971-72), Al gran sole (1972-74) as apogee. Documentary material, speeches, slogans, field recordings, on tape, and "concrete situations". The music leaves the concert hall: discussion concerts in factories, universities, trade unions, first in 1962, audiences up to 5,000, and performances in factories at workers' invitation. Travels: Eastern bloc from 1958, USSR 1963, USA 1965, Latin America 1967-71, Cuba, Chile. PCI Central Committee, March 1975.

P4. Crisis and rethinking, 1975-1979. "Between 1975 and 1979 Nono completed only two works". His father dies in October 1975, his mother in January 1976. Nono in a 1981 interview: "after Al gran sole I felt the need to rethink my way of being a musician and intellectual in today's society and to find new ways of knowledge and imagination. Certain patterns and certain thoughts are antiquated". "Immediately after Al gran sole an unutterable silence descended". The collapse included political convictions, and critics wondered if he had "broken" with his past. The catalyst is the friendship with Massimo Cacciari, known since 1965, which blossoms, and study of Holderlin, Benjamin, the Hebrew mystics. The way out is ... sofferte onde serene ... (1976), which "taxed me to the extreme".

P5. The late listening, first wave, 1976-1980. ... sofferte onde serene ... (1976) is "the first of Nono's works in what became his late style". Con Luigi Dallapiccola (1979) moves beyond tape to real-time transformation. Fragmente-Stille, an Diotima (1979-80): silence, fragments, pianissimi verging on the imperceptible alternating with explosions, the structural value of space. The Foundation biography warns against reading this as an abrupt break: "the germ of all these elements was already present (and indeed exploited) in a number of works going back to the 1950s", silences and near-inaudible sonorities in Polifonica-Monodia-Ritmica, chiaroscuro dynamics in Due espressioni per orchestra (1953), Il canto sospeso, La terra e la compagna, Cori di Didone.

P6. Prometeo and the Caminantes, 1980-1990. Freiburg Experimentalstudio from 1980; four Prometeo "studies", Io, Das atmende Klarsein, Quando stanno morendo, Guai ai gelidi mostri; Prometeo itself (1984, revised 1985) as "the culmination of all the dramaturgical experiments carried out in the wake of Intolleranza 1960, subsumed in a sound world in which sight gradually cedes to pure listening". Then the orchestral visionary works, A Carlo Scarpa (1984), Caminantes... Ayacucho (1986-87), No hay caminos... (1987), and the chamber and live-electronic tributes, A Pierre (1985), Risonanze erranti (1986), Decouvrir la subversion: Omaggio a E. Jabes (1987). The Toledo inscription, seen in 1985, "Caminante, no hay caminos, hay que caminar", a paraphrase of Antonio Machado's "Proverbios y cantares", generates the closing Caminantes trilogy. Berlin: DAAD grant 1986-87, Wissenschaftskolleg 1987-88, Grosser Kunstpreis Berlin, March 1990, death two months later. The late works "cast a fresh light on the early ones", and engagement is now "subjective", author, performer and listener together, rather than declamatory.

## Aesthetic logic

The logic of Nono's work is that listening is a political act, and the politics is never pasted onto the music, it is built into the material, the space and the silence. The row is chosen because it already means something. The text is shattered because a sentence spoken by one voice is a slogan, and a sentence scattered across a chorus is a crowd. The tape is documentary because a factory that is described is an illustration, and a factory that is recorded, ground down and reassembled is a fact that has become music. The theatre is montage because history does not happen in order, it happens all at once, in rooms side by side. The space is composed because a listener who sits in the middle of the sound is inside the event, not in front of it. The silence is structural because a silence after a sound is the listener hearing themselves listen. And the late turn is not a retreat from politics into contemplation, it is the same politics with the volume turned down until only the listening is left, because by 1976 Nono no longer trusted the declamation, and he trusted the ear instead. Boehmer, quoted in Zattra, states the difference from Cage exactly: for Nono what you hear "is not a purpose in itself... but is rather the announcement of the unheard". The heard sound announces something that has not been heard yet, and the listener's job is to wait for it, to track it as it moves, to find their way through the labyrinth. That is why the late works put the performer on foot and the sound on the edge of audibility at the same time: the body searches and the ear strains, and the searching and the straining are the same act. The whole oeuvre is one long education of the ear, from the row that means something, through the factory that sounds like something, to the silence that is something, and the education never ends, because the last work is called "Hay que caminar", you must walk, and there are no paths.

## Network

N1. Bruno Maderna (1920-1973). Teacher, friend, "first great mentor". Met 1946 through Malipiero; free composition lessons; joint study at the Biblioteca Marciana; both disciples of Scherchen from 1948. Maderna conducted Nono's music, including Il canto sospeso, and championed him in Europe. Nono's grief at Maderna's death in 1973 is part of the "harsh wind of death" behind ... sofferte onde serene .... Maderna already stands in the carried list from #401, so he is NOT re-proposed as new.

N2. Hermann Scherchen (1891-1966). Conductor, mentor, "spiritual guide in both cultural and personal matters". Presented Variazioni canoniche at Darmstadt 1950 and conducted the Il canto sospeso premiere in Cologne in 1956. Nono worked as his copyist and then author at Ars Viva Verlag, and stayed with him in Zurich, Rapallo and Gravesano. Scherchen introduced Nono to Darmstadt and to Varese.

N3. Karlheinz Stockhausen (1928-2007). Darmstadt co-leader, the "avant-garde trinity" with Boulez and Nono. Stockhausen's 1957 Darmstadt lecture attacked Il canto sospeso's text-setting, Nono answered angrily in 1960, and the 1959 lecture and the "statistical structures" argument ended the friendship until the 1980s. Nono's 1969 text "Il potere musicale" calls Stockhausen's position imperialist and "technological-reactionary". Stockhausen already stands as an alternate from #402 and #408, so he is NOT re-proposed as new.

N4. Pierre Boulez (1925-2016). Darmstadt co-leader; Nono distanced himself from Boulez and Stockhausen together in 1959. Difficulties kept Nono from working at IRCAM. Reconciliation: A Pierre, dell'azzurro silenzio, inquietum (1985) is a tribute whose dedication "marks an important moment of reconciliation". Boulez already stands as an alternate from #402 and #408, so he is NOT re-proposed as new.

N5. Christian Wolff (b. 1934). The doorway link. In the BOMB interview of April 1997 Wolff, asked about the political radicalization of the early 1970s, says: "We found them for example in one not quite contemporary Italian composer, Luigi Nono, who was a card carrying member of the Italian Communist party. In fact he was a fairly high functionary within the party and at the same time was part of this Darmstadt avant-garde in the '50s. So he was one figure, and then we also found the earlier figure of Hanns Eisler... So those were the father figures if you will, but closer in generation there was Rzewski, Cardew, Yuji Takahashi, Erhard Grosskopf in Berlin, there was a Dutch composer Peter Schat". NOTE: no direct personal contact between Nono and Wolff was found this leg, no meeting, letter or performance, and the link is Wolff's acknowledged allyship and influence, running through Rzewski and Cardew. Wolff was studied as #402, so he is NOT re-proposed.

N6. Hanns Eisler (1898-1962). Paired with Nono by Wolff as the two "father figures" of political music. Nono's Fur Paul Dessau (1974) processes Castro speeches, and Eisler is the Schoenberg-pupil and Brecht-collaborator root of the political song tradition. Eisler was studied as #407, so he is NOT re-proposed.

N7. Helmut Lachenmann (b. 1935). First private pupil: met Nono at Darmstadt 1957, studied with him in Venice 1958-60, staying in his home, and co-wrote the 1959 Darmstadt lecture. "From Nono, he acquired the belief that music should aim to serve a message of social relevance". FUTURE CANDIDATE, top pick for #410.

N8. Maurizio Pollini (1942-2024). Friend from 1966. The piano parts of Como una ola de fuerza y luz (1971-72) and ... sofferte onde serene ... (1976) were "inspired by and written for Pollini", and Pollini premiered ... sofferte onde serene ... and was its only performer for some years. Schott: the late exploration of "subtle sound nuances" was "inspired by the piano technique of his friend, the pianist Maurizio Pollini". FUTURE CANDIDATE.

N9. Claudio Abbado (1933-2014). Friend from 1965. Conducted the premieres of Como una ola, Al gran sole carico d'amore and Prometeo, and conducted the 1992 Berlin Philharmonic Il canto sospeso recording. "My silent friend: remembering Luigi Nono" (Abbado 1999). FUTURE CANDIDATE.

N10. Massimo Cacciari (b. 1944). Venetian philosopher; friend since 1965, friendship "blossomed" in the mid-1970s crisis. Assembled the Prometeo texts from Hesiod, Holderlin, Benjamin and others, and his reflections shaped Das atmende Klarsein, Quando stanno morendo, Guai ai gelidi mostri and Prometeo. Mayor of Venice 1993-2000. FUTURE CANDIDATE, alternate.

N11. Emilio Vedova (1919-2006). Painter; friend from 1942, "maintained more or less constantly up until the composer's death". Designed sets and costumes for Intolleranza 1960 and lighting, "image-signs in movement", for Prometeo. Omaggio a Emilio Vedova (1960) is Nono's first tape piece. FUTURE CANDIDATE, alternate.

N12. Renzo Piano (b. 1937). Architect; designed the laminated-wooden ark, the auditorium as a "giant sound box", built inside San Lorenzo for Prometeo. Nono's note to him, 6 December 1983, is the work's manifesto: "No opera / no director / no set designer / no traditional characters / but / dramaturgy-tragedy with mobile sounds that / read discover / empty fill up space". FUTURE CANDIDATE, alternate.

N13. Luigi Dallapiccola (1904-1975). "One of the most widely respected composers of the time and a crucial point of reference for many young musicians". Nono met him around 1946; Dallapiccola's criticism of an early Nono score sent Nono back to study, and Due liriche greche (1948-49) acknowledges the inspiration of Dallapiccola's Canti di prigionia. Con Luigi Dallapiccola (1979) is the tribute and the first live-electronics work. Dallapiccola already stands in the carried list from #408, so he is NOT re-proposed as new.

N14. Edgard Varese (1883-1965). Met at Darmstadt, "acknowledged as having a considerable influence on his own compositional technique". Nono's account: Varese knew African drum orchestras, "drums played with fingers, fingernails and the palm", and showed him new African drums in New York, and noted that Ionisation was first performed in Havana in 1931. Varese was among the admirers of Variazioni canoniche at its 1950 premiere. Varese already stands as an alternate from #400 and #401, so he is NOT re-proposed as new.

N15. John Cage (1912-1992). The antagonist: Nono's 1959 and 1960 Darmstadt lectures attack Cage's a-historical chance procedures and his followers, and Ricordi notes Nono "harshly criticized the 'a-historical' positions of John Cage and his followers". Boehmer via Zattra: Nono "differs profoundly and until the end from Cage" on listening, because for Nono what you hear "is not a purpose in itself... but is rather the announcement of the unheard". Cage was studied as #398, so he is NOT re-proposed.

N16. John Chowning (b. 1934). Met in Los Angeles during the 1965 US trip; Nono visited CCRMA, met Chowning and Charles Dodge, and "for a moment... contemplated a period of study in Stanford" before choosing Freiburg and Padova instead. Chowning's computer music showed Nono "the possibility of using technology free from academicism". The Stanford road not taken explains the European 4i project.

N17. Richard Teitelbaum (1936-2020). His Barcarola used the 4i processor before Prometeo, and the "Peata" FM environment derives its principle from Barcarola's unison-oscillator beating mechanism, with Peata using frequency modulation where Barcarola used fixed waveform. A concrete technical debt from the MEV and American network to Nono's late work. Teitelbaum was studied as #406, so he is NOT re-proposed.

N18. Victor Jara (1932-1973). Met on the 1967 Santiago trip, met again in September 1972 at a Latin American music festival in Havana, became friends. Nono's L'Unita tribute after Jara's murder by Pinochet's forces: "While a prisoner in the Santiago National Stadium, Victor Jara is said to have started singing. Immediately, his wrists were broken...". Jara is in the carried list, so he is NOT re-proposed.

N19. Fidel Castro (1926-2016). Met in Havana, 1967. Castro's speeches are quoted in A floresta, Ein Gespenst, Al gran sole, and processed electronically in Y entonces comprendio (1969-70) and Fur Paul Dessau (1974). Nono's stance: Cuba as model, and the Padilla affair of 1971 shows the ambiguity, because Nono signed the first protest letter, declined the second, and published an apology and clarification in El Siglo, 9 May 1971.

N20. Gyorgy Kurtag (b. 1926). Honoured by Omaggio a Gyorgy Kurtag (1983-86). FUTURE CANDIDATE.

N21. Gidon Kremer (b. 1947). Met Nono in 1987, via Charlotte Geselbracht, in Venice, then Freiburg in March 1987; recorded hours of improvisations at Freiburg in early 1988 from which the La lontananza tapes were made, and the work's full title carries his name. FUTURE CANDIDATE, alternate.

N22. Salvatore Sciarrino (b. 1947). La lontananza is dedicated to him, and Nono borrowed the word "lontananza" from Sciarrino's All'aure in una lontananza (1977). FUTURE CANDIDATE, alternate.

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. Birth date. Ricordi's bio says Nono "was born in Venice on 24 January 1924". WRONG. 29 January 1924 is given by Wikipedia, Schott, Britannica, Encyclopedia.com and the Fondazione Archivio Luigi Nono biography. Use 29 January, Tuesday.

C2. Resistance membership. Wikipedia says "Nono had been a member of the Italian Resistance during the Second World War", and Encyclopedia.com says "an active participant in the Italian Resistance Movement against the Nazis". The authoritative Fondazione biography says the opposite: "For health reasons he was exempted from military service and did not play an active part either in the war or in the Italian resistance movement. Nonetheless, during these years Nono made a point of frequenting young Venetian Socialists and exponents of the local opposition". The Resistance-membership claim FAILS on the best source. Do not repeat it. He was, however, from an anti-fascist family milieu, and his politics were formed in the Resistance years.

C3. Joining the PCI "under Mussolini". Encyclopedia.com says "he joined the Italian Communist Party while the country was still under the dictatorship of Mussolini". IMPOSSIBLE: Mussolini fell in 1943 and was executed in April 1945, and Nono enrolled in the PCI in 1952. The Encyclopedia.com sentence conflates his anti-fascist youth with his party enrolment.

C4. Law degree year. Schott and Ricordi say he graduated in law in 1946. The Fondazione biography says he "graduated in law in 1947 with a thesis on the juridical concept of exceptio veritatis". Prefer 1947, the archive biography, and the 1946 figure is the common secondary-source error. He enrolled at Padua in 1942.

C5. "Functionary", precision, not a failure. Wolff's "fairly high functionary within the party" is confirmed in substance: Nono was elected to the PCI Central Committee in 1975 and remained a member until his death, and the Fondazione biography pins it to March 1975. Correction: he was an elected Central Committee member, not a salaried party official, and no source read says he was on the party payroll. Also note the nuance: Murphy calls him "an active though somewhat unorthodox member of the PCI", and the Transatlantic article notes Carola Nielinger-Vakil's "cultural ambassador of the Party" framing needs nuance.

C6. Darmstadt participation end date. Ricordi says Nono "took part in the 'Laboratorium der seriellen Musik' of Darmstadt until 1955 (until his Incontri)". The Fondazione biography says he "went back to Darmstadt each year for ten years, from 1957 as a teacher", that is through about 1960. The 1959 lecture was delivered at Darmstadt, so he was certainly there in 1959. Use: participant from 1950, teacher from 1957, rupture 1959-60. Ricordi's "until 1955" is too early.

C7. Meeting Nuria. Wikipedia says Nono met Nuria Schoenberg "at the 1953 world premiere of Moses und Aron in Hamburg". The Fondazione biography says: "In 1954 Schoenberg's Moses und Aron received its first performance in Hamburg, and on this occasion Nono met the daughter of the Austrian maestro, Nuria Schoenberg". The Hamburg concert premiere was Friday 12 March 1954. Use 1954; Wikipedia's 1953 is wrong.

C8. Il canto sospeso date. Some secondary sources date it 1960. WRONG. Composed 1955-56, premiered Wednesday 24 October 1956, Cologne, conducted by Scherchen.

C9. Intolleranza "first performance in Italy". Ricordi claims the Venice premiere was "the very first performance of a Nono work in his home country". Flag: Stenzl's claim, NOT independently verified this leg, and it is surprising given Nono's Italian activity before 1961, and it should not be repeated without a check. What is solid: premiere Thursday 13 April 1961, La Fenice, Venice Biennale; riot with neo-fascist stink-bombs, and Encyclopedia.com adds leaflets, and leaflets and stink-bombs are compatible, both reported, not contradictory.

C10. La fabbrica illuminata date. The Red Bull article calls it "the 1965 piece". WRONG. Composed 1964, premiered Tuesday 15 September 1964 at La Fenice, Venice Biennale, after RAI rejected it for the Prix Italia in Genoa, scheduled 12 September 1964. Voice type: "soprano" in most sources, and La Fenice calls Carla Henius a mezzo-soprano, so wording varies, and the study says "voice (Carla Henius)" to be safe.

C11. A floresta title spelling. "cheja" is a misspelling; correct Portuguese is "cheia". Nono's title perpetuates the error, and the study prints Nono's spelling and notes the error in the same breath. The title phrase is the words of an Angolan guerrilla named Gabriel: "They cannot burn the forest, for it is young and full of life".

C12. Ricorda cosa ti hanno fatto in Auschwitz, date conflict, UNRESOLVED. Wikipedia says 1965, and Encyclopedia.com's works list and the La Folia tape survey say 1966. The Die Ermittlung stage music it grew from is 1965, Frankfurt, Tuesday 19 October 1965. Safest: "1965-66" or "1966 (from the 1965 Ermittlung music)" with the flag.

C13. Al gran sole premiere venue, sources disagree. Wikipedia's main article and Ricordi say Teatro Lirico, Milan. The dedicated Al gran sole article, Naxos, and the Rochester course page say Teatro alla Scala, Friday 4 April 1975, conducted by Abbado, directed by Lyubimov. Prefer La Scala, because the dedicated work article and Naxos agree, and the premiere was a La Scala commission, and La Scala produced the revised version in 1978. Also: Ricordi's bio says the work covers "the situation of the workers in Turin around 1980", which is IMPOSSIBLE for a work premiered in 1975, an error in Ricordi's text, because the Turin material concerns 1950s-60s unrest.

C14. Al gran sole dates. Given as 1972-74, 1972-75, and "1972-1974, revised 1977". Use 1972-74, revised 1977, premiere 4 April 1975. Encyclopedia.com's bare "(1974)" is a simplification.

C15. Fragmente-Stille Holderlin count, sources differ. Wikipedia says "53 quotations". The Quartetto Maurice and HHV note and the Academia.edu abstract say 52 fragments and 52 sections. UNRESOLVED, 52 versus 53. Say "52 sections, with 52-53 Holderlin fragments (sources differ)". Also: one MusicWeb review says the quartet was "commissioned for the 1983 Bonn Beethoven Festival", which is WRONG, because it was commissioned for the Beethovenfest Bonn and premiered Monday 2 June 1980 by the LaSalle Quartet.

C16. Das atmende Klarsein dates. Wikipedia 1981-82, MUSE summary 1980-83, Encyclopedia.com 1980-81. Say 1980-81, with some sources extending to 1983. The Halaphon is Hans Peter Haller's spatialization program at the Freiburg Experimentalstudio.

C17. Omaggio a Gyorgy Kurtag, forces conflict. Encyclopedia.com says trombone and live electronics, 1983. Ricordi's 2024 catalogue says contralto, flute, clarinet, tuba and live electronics, 1983-1986. The work evidently exists in versions; do not state forces without a version and date qualifier.

C18. Prometeo dates and the 4i. Composed 1981-84, or 1981-85; premiere Venice Tuesday 25 September 1984; revised Milan version Wednesday 25 September 1985; further revisions at successive moments. The 4i processor was used ONLY in Venice 1984 and Milan 1985, and "from the third performance and later, only Freiburg technology was used". Do not imply the computer synthesis continued into later productions. Milan was, per Vidolin reporting Nono's opinion, "the better performance ever made".

C19. La lontananza premiere status. The Berlin premiere on Saturday 3 September 1988 was a version Nono was dissatisfied with; he "completely re-wrote the solo part and re-imagined the role of the tapes", and the final version was completed 31 January 1989 in Berlin. Do not call the 1988 premiere the definitive version; the authoritative recording, DG 1992, is of the revised version.

C20. Last work. "Hay que caminar" sonando (1989) is the last completed work, and La lontananza (1988-89) is the penultimate composition. Do not call La lontananza the last work.

C21. Encyclopedia.com marriage claim. "Nono was married to Schoenberg's daughter, Nuria, in 1955; they separated on friendly terms after several years; they had 2 daughters. Nuria settled in her father's last residence in Los Angeles". CONTRADICTED by the record: Nuria Schoenberg Nono lived in Venice, founded the Archivio Luigi Nono there in 1993, and is cited throughout as the archive's driving force. No other source read reports a separation. Treat as a Slonimsky error; do not repeat.

C22. Encyclopedia.com works-list typos and unverified items. "Non consumiano Marx" for Non consumiamo Marx; "Corno una ola" for Como una ola; "Sul ponte di Hiroscima" for Hiroshima; "Voci destroying Muros" (1970, for women's voices and instruments "featuring a machine gun pointed toward the audience"), a garbled title, not verified this leg; "2 piano concertos (1972, 1975)", unverified, because Como una ola 1971-72 is sometimes described as a piano concerto, and no 1975 piano concerto is identified in any other source read. Do not cite the Encyclopedia.com works list without cross-checking.

C23. Il canto sospeso dated 1960 in the NTS and Alchetron mirror, wrong, see C8.

C24. Death date. One Answers.com mirror says Nono "died of a liver [ailment] on May 6, 1990". WRONG. 8 May 1990, Tuesday, Venice, of a serious liver disorder, in the house where he was born. He received the Grosser Kunstpreis Berlin in March 1990, two months before his death.

C25. "Darmstadt School" coinage. Nono himself created the expression in his 1958 lecture "Die Entwicklung der Reihentechnik" to describe the music of himself, Boulez, Maderna, Stockhausen and others. It is not a label later imposed on him. He likened the group's significance to the Bauhaus.

C26. "Founding member of the Darmstadt School" (Wilkins thesis) is loose: the "school" was a retrospective label Nono coined in 1958, not an institution with members. Prefer "central figure; coined the term".

C27. The 1959 rupture, sequence. The lecture "Geschichte und Gegenwart in der Musik von heute" was delivered 1 September 1959 and written in conjunction with his pupil Lachenmann. The violent argument with Stockhausen followed Stockhausen's talk the same day on "statistical structures", which Nono read as "fascist mass structures". Combined with Nono's anger at Stockhausen's 1957 reading of Il canto sospeso, this "effectively ended their friendship until the 1980s".

C28. Caminantes... Ayacucho and Shining Path. The Transatlantic article calls the work "another ode to Latin American revolutionaries, especially Shining Path in Peru". Wikipedia describes it more neutrally as inspired by a region "that experiences extreme poverty and social unrest". Keep the Shining Path association attributed to the Transatlantic article, not stated as Nono's own dedication.
