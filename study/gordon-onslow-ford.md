# Gordon Onslow Ford: Study Notes

**Site:** Gordon Onslow Ford (Wendover, Buckinghamshire, England, Thursday 26 December 1912 - Inverness, California, Sunday 9 November 2003, aged ninety), the British naval officer who resigned his commission to become the youngest surrealist in Breton's Paris, the man who pushed Roberto Matta into painting and into surrealism and was pushed by Matta into automatism, the inventor of coulage, the painter who gave up his trees to become a surrealist and then gave up surrealism to paint the inside of the mind as if it were a sea, the lecturer who stood in front of Baziotes and Gorky and Motherwell in New York in the first months of 1941 and gave them permission to think differently about painting, the exile who carried automatism to Mexico and then to California, the founder of the Dynaton, the man who walked into a redwood grove on Tuesday 5 June 1951 and saw in a flash that the straight line, the circle and the dot were at the root of art, the painter of the inner worlds, of the SEED, of the voyage, of the Live-Line-Beings, the man who painted until three weeks before his death. He was born at Wendover on Thursday 26 December 1912, the son of a family that sent him to the Royal Naval College at Dartmouth, and he painted his first oil, a seascape, at the age of eleven in 1923 while sick with mumps, and he served as a naval officer, stationed in Egypt in 1934 to 1936, painting watercolors of Alexandria and of fellahin gardeners, and he met his patron Richard Combe Abdy, who secured his release from the Navy, and his resignation was accepted in June 1937, and he moved to Paris in the summer of 1937 and met the Chilean architect Roberto Matta, and the two spent the summer of 1938 together in a cottage at Travignon in Brittany, in homage to the birthplace of Yves Tanguy, painting together and refining their automatic processes, and he was invited by Andre Breton to join the surrealist group in 1938, and in 1939 he invented coulage, pouring Ripolin enamel onto canvas lying flat on the floor, and he spent the summer of 1939 at Chemillieu with Breton and Tanguy and Kay Sage and Esteban Frances, on the recommendation of Gertrude Stein and Alice B. Toklas, and he left Paris in September 1939 and was in London in 1940, and Kay Sage invited him to New York, and from January to March 1941 he gave four lectures at the New School for Social Research, "Surrealist Painting: An Adventure into Human Consciousness", at 66 West Twelfth Street, with exhibitions of surrealist paintings hung alongside them, and he met the poet Jacqueline Johnson at the lectures and married her in 1941, and he moved to Mexico in 1941 and bought a mill at Erongaricuaro, a Tarascan village on Lake Patzcuaro, and he lived there six years, visited by Matta and Paalen and Varo and Frances and Mabille and Peret and Moro and Rahon, and he formally broke with the Surrealists in 1943, feeling the movement too occupied with politics and not enough with the marvelous, and he moved to San Francisco in 1947, and in 1949 he bought the decommissioned ferryboat Vallejo at Sausalito with the poet Jean Varda for 500 dollars down and 60 dollars a month, and he formed the Dynaton group with Wolfgang Paalen and Lee Mullican and showed with them at the San Francisco Museum of Art in 1951, and on Tuesday 5 June 1951, walking in the redwood groves, he saw in a dazzling flash that the line, the circle and the dot were at the root of art, and he studied calligraphy with the Zen master Hodo Tobase from 1952 to 1957 and read Ouspensky and Jung and Sri Aurobindo and Zen Buddhism and Vedanta, and he moved to Inverness in 1957, and in 1960 to 1962 he made the monumental black-and-white hyperspace paintings with a paint formulated by the chemist William Parle so that black and white would dry simultaneously, and he published Painting in the Instant in 1964 and Creation in 1978, and his wife Jacqueline died in 1976, and he met the lucid-dream researcher Fariba Bogzaran in 1989 and co-founded the Lucid Art Foundation with her in 1998, and he kept painting the inner sky, the stars, the black holes, the Live-Line-Beings, until three weeks before his death at Inverness on Sunday 9 November 2003, aged ninety, and his last paintings, 2000 to 2003, are an array of circles, lines and dots. His name is spelled "Onslow Ford" without a hyphen in his own usage and in most scholarly sources, against "Onslow-Ford" with a hyphen in dealer sources such as Findlay Galleries and the Whitney Museum, and the study uses "Onslow Ford". Several facts in this paragraph are disputed between sources and are carried as disputes, not settled: he joined the surrealist group in 1938 by Wikipedia and Sotheby's, against 1939 by Carlson Art Projects and Aaron Payne, and the study carries both; his Mexico years are 1941 to 1947 by Wikipedia and Findlay and Sotheby's, against 1941 to 1948 by Carlson and Aaron Payne, and the Sausalito Historical Society says seven years, and the study uses 1941 to 1947 and names the dispute; the Dynaton was formed with Paalen and Mullican by Wikipedia and SFMOMA, against "with Matta, Sybil Moholy-Nagy, and Paalen" by the dealer biographies, and the study carries both, noting the 1951 exhibition is consistently Paalen and Mullican and Onslow Ford; the New School exhibitions were four by Wikipedia and Sotheby's, against six exhibitions and thirty-six paintings by Caterina Caputo from the New School Bulletin, and the study carries both; the lecture title is "An Adventure" singular by Dorfman and Caputo, against "Adventures" plural by Bogzaran, and the study uses the singular and names the dispute; and the death year is 2003 by every narrative source, against "(1912 - 2002)" in the Carlson Art Projects page header, which its own text contradicts, and the study uses 2003 and calls the header a dealer typo.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Wifredo Lam study, and the doorway HOLDS WITH CORRECTIONS as a personal chain, the tenth hold in a row, and the corrections are stated first because one thread in the brief FAILS as phrased and is CORRECTED, not smoothed, and the Lam half of the doorway fails as a documented contact, and the study does not invent a handshake to save it. The brief said Onslow Ford met Matta in Brittany in the summer of 1938, and the Brittany summer holds but the meeting does not, because they met in Paris in the summer of 1937, and Sotheby's says it plainly, "In 1937 he met Gordon Onslow Ford, who had recently resigned his officer's commission in the British Navy", and Bogzaran says it plainly, "In the summer of 1937 Onslow Ford moved to Paris... While there, he met the painter Roberto Matta", and the Brittany summer of 1938 was a year into an already-established friendship and working partnership, not a first meeting, and the correction matters, because a year of working together changes what the summer means, and the summer is the deepening, not the beginning. What the summer was, holds. Bogzaran: "he spent the summer with Matta in Travignon, Brittany in homage to the birthplace of their mutually respected surrealist painter, Yves Tanguy. They experimented with drawings and paintings, read and studied art, science and esoteric philosophy, and engaged in intense dialogues. That summer changed the direction of their lives and work forever." Dorfman: "While they were living together in a cottage in Brittany in the summer of 1938, Onslow Ford helped Matta break that barrier [reluctance to paint in oils], giving him paint tubes and canvas." Carlson and Aaron Payne, from the Guggenheim text: "In the summer of 1938, Onslow Ford and Matta developed an artistic partnership in Brittany, painting together and refining their automatic processes." The second correction is not to merge the Brittany summer with the Swiss Alps chalet trip, because Onslow Ford remembered the Alps trip as spring 1938, and Sotheby's footnote argues it may well be spring 1939, judging by the dates of his transparent mountain paintings and Matta's psychological morphologies, and the two trips are two trips, and the study carries both dates and does not merge them. The third correction reverses the teacher framing, because the influence runs both ways, and the "teacher" label is backwards in one important respect. Onslow Ford did not learn surrealism from Matta and then pass it on, because the Canadian Centre for Architecture says of Matta, "Through a friendship he made at that time with Gordon Onslow Ford... he [Matta] learned about Surrealism", and a Matta obituary says, "Encouraged by an English friend, Gordon Onslow-Ford... he began to make his first drawings in 1937", and another says it was when Onslow Ford "shut him up in a [Breton] cottage and ordered him to paint that he began to produce his first canvases", and Wikipedia agrees Onslow Ford admired Matta's drawings as "the most exciting images" he had seen in Paris and encouraged Matta to shift from architecture to painting, and what Matta gave back was automatism, because Bogzaran says Matta "assisted Onslow Ford to be free and 'automatic,' to experiment and find his new language", and the honest line is mutual ignition, Onslow Ford pushing Matta into painting and into surrealism, Matta pushing Onslow Ford into automatism, and neither was the other's master, and the study does not call either the teacher. The fourth correction is that who introduced whom to Breton is disputed and cannot be carried as a clean chain, because Findlay says Matta introduced Onslow Ford to Breton and Tanguy and Ernst, and Wikipedia says Breton invited Onslow Ford to join the group in 1938, and Sotheby's says Breton invited Matta in 1937, and Christie's says Breton introduced Matta to Surrealism, and these cannot all be literally true in the same order, and the study presents Matta and Onslow Ford as arriving together at Breton's circle in 1937 and 1938, not as a chain with a first link. The fifth correction narrows "taught New York to read Matta", because the lectures were four, from January to March 1941, titled "Surrealist Painting: An Adventure into Human Consciousness", at 66 West Twelfth Street, and Yau, drawing on Sawin's monograph, says the first three lectures were on Giorgio de Chirico, Max Ernst, and Rene Magritte, and the last touched on Paul Delvaux, Victor Brauner, Wolfgang Paalen, Stanley William Hayter, Kurt Seligman, Roberto Matta, Esteban Frances, and Onslow Ford himself, and Dorfman gives a slightly different topic list, de Chirico, Ernst and Miro, Magritte and Tanguy, and the variance is carried, and Matta was one name among many in the final lecture, not the subject of the series, and the lectures' real claim, per Yau, is that they "gave the artists permission to think differently about painting, to reconceive it", ending with an appeal for a "revolution in consciousness", and the Matta-transmission claim rests on the shared automatism and inscape language and on Onslow Ford bringing the paintings over from Europe, not on a Matta lecture that never happened. The sixth correction is the audience, because Baziotes, Busa, Gorky, Hare, Hayter and Motherwell are solid, and Pollock is only "according to some" by Yau, and Dorfman does not list him, and Findlay asserts Pollock and Mark Rothko attended, and Wikipedia says only "said to have influenced artists such as Jackson Pollock, Robert Motherwell, and others", and the study does not present Pollock as a confirmed attendee. The seventh correction is the Lam hop, and it FAILS as a documented contact, because no source among the fifteen read end to end, and no snippet, names Wifredo Lam in connection with Onslow Ford, not as a correspondent, not as a fellow exhibitor in his lifetime, not in any anecdote, and what exists is circumstantial overlap only, because Lam arrived in Paris on 1 May 1938 with a letter to Picasso, and Onslow Ford was in Paris from 1937 to September 1939, and both moved in Breton's circle, but Lam's Paris contacts named in sources are Picasso and Breton, and his surrealist-group membership is dated to 1940, after Onslow Ford had left Paris in September 1939 and after the Chemillieu summer, and Lam's 1940 and 1941 path, the Villa Air-Bel at Marseille, the ship to Martinique with Breton in March 1941, Cuba from 1941 to 1946, never intersects Onslow Ford's, New York in January to March 1941, then Mexico from 1941 to 1947, and the only joint appearance found is posthumous and curatorial, both listed among the artists in the Reina Sofia exhibition "Surrealistas en el exilio y los inicios de la Escuela de Nueva York" in 1999 to 2000, and the honest doorway is therefore Matta as the shared node, because Lam's work was also shaped by Matta, per Christie's, "central to this period in Lam's work is the influence of... Roberto Matta", with Breton's Paris circle of 1938 and 1939 as the shared room, and NOT a Lam-Onslow Ford meeting, and the study says this plainly rather than inventing a handshake. The eighth correction is the name trap, because Gordon Matta-Clark was Matta's son, born in 1943, two years after Onslow Ford left New York for Mexico, and his name appears in none of the Onslow Ford sources read, and the CCA collection guide mentions Onslow Ford only as Matta's friend who taught Matta about surrealism, and there is no Onslow Ford and Matta-Clark contact, and the two Gordons, both tied to Matta, must not be confused, and the study names the trap so the next study does not fall into it. What holds, then, is the parallel exile: Lam and Onslow Ford were both in Breton's Paris in 1938 and 1939, both taught by the same two men, Picasso the introducer and Matta the younger Chilean both of them circled, and both carried automatism out of Paris into exile, Lam to Cuba, Onslow Ford to New York and Mexico, and the doorway is the shared room and the shared hinge, Matta, and the two transplantations, one into the Caribbean plantation and one into the New York School and then into Mexico and California, both routed through Breton, both dated 1937 to 1941, and the study opens on that parallel exile and on Matta as the hinge, and not on a handshake that never happened.

## M1. Coulage (the pour)

The mechanism is that the paint is poured, not brushed, and the pouring is the picture. In 1939, while sharing Victor Brauner's studio in Paris, Onslow Ford could not concentrate with Brauner present, so he began casually pouring paint on top of a canvas, and the casual pouring is the mechanism, because a pour that begins as a distraction becomes a method, and the method is the distraction, and the distraction is the pour. He poured Ripolin enamel, a fast-drying house paint, onto canvas lying flat on the floor, and the colors would not mix but rather run into and around each other, and the not-mixing is the mechanism, because each color keeps its own edge while flowing into the next, and the keeping is the running, and the running is the edge, and the edge is the color. After the enamel began to dry, he would cut into some parts of the dry surface to reveal wet enamel underneath, and the cutting is the mechanism, because the dry skin is opened and the wet color shows through, and the showing-through is the cutting, and the cutting is the skin, and the skin is the wet. Then he articulated the space with drawn lines, fixing a geometric web of white bands over the flow, and the web is the mechanism, because the pour is the chance and the web is the order, and the order is the pour, and the pour is the web, and the web holds the flow the way a net holds water. The watchable result is marbled, atmospheric flow under a crisp geometric web of white bands and rectangles, and the push-and-pull perspective of Without Bounds and Space Web, both 1939, and the push-and-pull is the mechanism, because the web pushes forward and the marbling pulls back, and the pulling is the pushing, and the pushing is the web, and the web is the marbling. Bogzaran likens the discovery to Middle Eastern marbling, and Yau notes that most of the coulages were lost from the Paris studio at the end of the Occupation, and two were shipped to the United States in 1940 and were possibly seen by Baziotes, and the dealer claim is that coulage came years before Pollock's drips, and Yau says Sawin is "being diplomatic about the connection between Ford's pours and Jackson Pollock's drips", and the study does not overclaim the Pollock line, and the pour stands on its own as a mechanism, because the paint is poured and the surface is cut and the web is drawn, and the drawing is the pouring, and the pouring is the cutting, and the cutting is the web. **Takeaway:** pour the paint flat, let the colors run without mixing, cut the dry skin to show the wet color beneath, then fix a white web over the flow, so the pour is the chance and the web is the order. Against the chain: Wifredo Lam (#384 M6) also let the drawing be the painting, and his is charcoal first and thinned oil washed over it so the line stays visible, and Onslow Ford's is enamel poured flat and cut open so the wet shows through, which is why Lam's is a washing and Onslow Ford's is a pouring, and the pouring is the washing made with a house paint.

## M2. Automatism as fast start, slow finish

The mechanism is that the work begins without preconception, in a single fast gesture, and is then worked for months, and the months are the gesture. Sotheby's, on Matta, says a work "was started without preconception, often with a random gesture", and the random gesture is the mechanism, because the hand moves before the mind knows what it is doing, and the moving-before-knowing is the random, and the random is the gesture, and the gesture is the start. Onslow Ford watched Matta do it in the Alps: palette-knife gestures, paint run and splashed, "then would look at it and work on the edges with brushes; it was begun fast and then he would work on it for months", and the looking-and-working is the mechanism, because the fast start makes the field and the slow finish finds the form in it, and the finding is the working, and the working is the looking, and the looking is the months. His own version was the same: "painting spontaneously from an inner vision" after tearing up many drawings and beginning again, and the tearing-up is the mechanism, because the failed start is not kept and patched, it is destroyed and begun again, and the beginning-again is the tearing-up, and the tearing-up is the spontaneity, and the spontaneity is the vision. The watchable result is a fast, gestural underlayer with slowly refined edges and layered translucent washes, and the underlayer is the mechanism, because the speed of the beginning stays visible under the slowness of the finishing, and the staying-visible is the underlayer, and the underlayer is the speed, and the speed is the months. **Takeaway:** start with a random gesture and no preconception, then work the edges for months, so the fast beginning stays visible under the slow finish. Against the chain: Roberto Matta (#382 M1) also let the hand move before the mind, and his is a stain rubbed on at random and recognised into form, and Onslow Ford's is a gesture flung fast and then edged for months, which is why the father's is a recognising and Onslow Ford's is a finishing, and the finishing is the recognising made slow.

## M3. Landscape-to-inscape (unwinding representation)

The mechanism is that a landscape is unwound, mark by mark, until only inner structure remains, and the unwinding is the picture. The core transformation of 1938 and 1939 is that Onslow Ford took a landscape and unwound it, and Bogzaran says it plainly, "Slowly he began to unwind his representational style by introducing new forms and marks in his work. He tore up many drawings and began again and again, finally managing to embrace the technique of automatism", and the tearing-up-and-beginning-again is the mechanism, because each new form replaces a piece of the landscape, and the replacing is the unwinding, and the unwinding is the tearing-up, and the tearing-up is the landscape. Matta's word for the destination was "inscape", and the inscape is the mechanism, because a landscape turned inside out is no longer a place but a state, and the state is the inside-out, and the inside-out is the inscape, and the inscape is the landscape. Matta said of him in a 1999 interview, quoted by Bogzaran, "Gordon had to give up his trees to become a surrealist", and the giving-up is the mechanism, because the trees are the last thing to go, and the going is the giving-up, and the giving-up is the tree, and the tree is the surrealist. The watchable result is trees that walk, in 3 Trees Move Along of 1938, and islands and horizons that dissolve into biomorphic forms and dotted lines of force, and the walking is the mechanism, because a tree that moves is no longer a tree, it is a mark that remembers being a tree, and the remembering is the walking, and the walking is the mark, and the mark is the tree. **Takeaway:** take a landscape and unwind it mark by mark until the trees walk and the horizon dissolves, so the picture stops being a place and becomes a state. Against the chain: Wifredo Lam (#384 M10) also thinned the picture year by year, and his is a crowd thinning until one figure stands on a wash, and Onslow Ford's is a landscape unwinding until only inner structure remains, which is why Lam's is a thinning and Onslow Ford's is an unwinding, and the unwinding is the thinning made of trees.

## M4. Psychological morphology (the inscape as 4D space)

The mechanism is that the picture is a graph of transformations, and the transformations are the picture. Matta's term for the shared project was "psychological morphology", defined in his typescript, translated by Onslow Ford and quoted by Sotheby's, as painting "the graph of transformations resulting from the emission of energies and their absorption in the object... in a geodesical psychological medium", and the graph is the mechanism, because the picture does not show a thing, it shows what happens to a thing, and the happening is the graph, and the graph is the transformation, and the transformation is the picture. Objects in the inscape oscillate "from point volume to moment eternity, from attraction repulsion to past future, from light shadow to matter movement", and the oscillating is the mechanism, because a form is simultaneously solid and transparent, volume and void, and the simultaneously is the oscillating, and the oscillating is the point, and the point is the eternity. Karlstrom says Onslow Ford's and Matta's automatism "had more to do with time and space than with Freud", and the time-and-space is the mechanism, because the unconscious here is not a Freudian cellar but a fourth dimension, and the fourth dimension is the time-and-space, and the time-and-space is the automatism, and the automatism is the geometry. The watchable result is forms that pulse between volume and void, with no fixed viewpoint and a depth that reads as time, and the pulsing is the mechanism, because the eye cannot settle on whether a form is coming forward or going back, and the not-settling is the pulsing, and the pulsing is the depth, and the depth is the time. **Takeaway:** paint the graph of transformations, let each form oscillate between point and volume and moment and eternity, so the depth reads as time. Against the chain: Roberto Matta (#382 M2) also painted an inscape, and his is organs and landscapes in a cosmic field, and Onslow Ford's is the same project from the other side of the partnership, the graph drawn by the man who translated Matta's typescript, which is why the father's is the definition and Onslow Ford's is the translation, and the translation is the definition made into a picture.

## M5. Transparency and superimposition (the "reveal")

The mechanism is that forms are layered, interpenetrating and semi-transparent, and the layering is the picture. Personages are made of lines and colors "separated into the complex background of what he called later 'a reveal'", per Sotheby's on Parliament of Space Makers, and the reveal is the mechanism, because the background is not behind the figure, it is inside it, and the inside is the reveal, and the reveal is the background, and the background is the figure. Yau says it in one line, "paint was colored light", against the New York counterparts for whom "paint was paint", and the colored light is the mechanism, because paint that is light passes through paint, and the passing-through is the colored light, and the colored light is the layer, and the layer is the light. The watchable result is forms passing through each other, in Temptations of the Painter of 1941 and Crime Meets Crime of 1939, and in the 1950 and 1951 works on paper, "semi-transparent abstract forms and varied patterns... within a film-like space, hovering delicately between emergence and dissipation", and the hovering is the mechanism, because the form is never fully there and never fully gone, and the never-fully is the hovering, and the hovering is the emergence, and the emergence is the dissipation. Yau notes the one parallel is celluloid film transparencies overlaying each other, and the parallel matters, because Onslow Ford's friend was Len Lye, the abstract filmmaker of scratched celluloid, and the celluloid is the mechanism, because a painting built like film is a painting that moves without moving, and the moving-without-moving is the celluloid, and the celluloid is the layer, and the layer is the film. **Takeaway:** let the forms pass through each other as colored light, so each layer shows through the next and the picture hovers between emergence and dissipation. Against the chain: Wifredo Lam (#384 M11) also let the ground shine through the figure, and his is a figure left blank so the paper shines through bright white, and Onslow Ford's is a figure made of light passing through layers, which is why Lam's is a leaving and Onslow Ford's is a layering, and the layering is the leaving made of light.

## M6. Line, circle, dot (the basic language)

The mechanism is that the whole picture is built from three marks, and the three marks are the picture. On Tuesday 5 June 1951, walking in the redwood groves, Onslow Ford saw "in a dazzling flash of certainty... the straight line, the circle and the dot were at the root of art", per Dorfman, and the flash is the mechanism, because a lifetime of painting collapses into three marks in a single instant, and the collapsing is the flash, and the flash is the three, and the three are the art. Bogzaran traces the three marks to Egyptian hieroglyphs, to Asian calligraphy, and to the game of Mahjong he played as a child, and the tracing is the mechanism, because the three marks arrive from three directions at once, and the arriving is the tracing, and the tracing is the hieroglyph, and the hieroglyph is the calligraphy, and the calligraphy is the game. The watchable result is that after 1951 the paintings reduce to three marks used at speed, and the three stacked vertically make a glyph symbolising his whole method and "the act of cosmic creation itself", and the stacking is the mechanism, because a line over a circle over a dot is a sentence, and the sentence is the stacking, and the stacking is the creation, and the creation is the glyph. By 1958, per Carlson, "he dotted the canvas with a brush and made loose, calligraphic circles, replacing his earlier grids with fluid composition", and the replacing is the mechanism, because the grid was the old order and the loose circle is the new one, and the new is the replacing, and the replacing is the dot, and the dot is the circle. **Takeaway:** build the picture from three marks only, a line, a circle and a dot, and let them stack into a glyph, so the three are the language and the language is the three. Against the chain: Wifredo Lam (#384 M15) also built his whole language from two curves, and his is the crescent and the breast, the moon and the horn and the hoof, the body and the fruit and the coconut, and Onslow Ford's is three marks, the line and the circle and the dot, which is why Lam's is a two and Onslow Ford's is a three, and the three is the two made into a glyph.

## M7. Painting in the instant (speed of awareness)

The mechanism is that the speed of the mark must match the speed of the thought, and the matching is the picture. The theory comes from his 1964 book Painting in the Instant, per Dorfman: "the speed with which the artist makes marks should reflect the speed of his or her thought-movements in the inner-worlds", and the reflecting is the mechanism, because a fast thought needs a fast mark and a slow thought needs a slow mark, and the needing is the reflecting, and the reflecting is the speed, and the speed is the thought. "Painting in the instant is the direct manifestation of the unknown through the painter as an instrument. The painter disappears in the instant, and reappears in the painting", quoted by Findlay, and the disappearing is the mechanism, because the painter is not the author of the instant, the instant is the author of the painter, and the authoring is the disappearing, and the disappearing is the instrument, and the instrument is the painting. The watchable result is marks made at wildly varying speeds in one picture, slow deliberate lines beside flung dots, and in the 1960 to 1962 black-and-white works, Parle's paint let black and white dry simultaneously so marks could be layered "at slow or extremely fast speed", per Bogzaran, and the layering-at-two-speeds is the mechanism, because the picture holds two times at once, and the two-times is the layering, and the layering is the slow, and the slow is the fast. Blunk Space says it as a law: "time is governed by speed of awareness, and space is manifest in the painting", and the law is the mechanism, because the clock of the picture is the speed of the mind, and the clock is the law, and the law is the speed, and the speed is the space. **Takeaway:** let the speed of each mark match the speed of the thought that made it, so the picture holds two times at once, and time is governed by speed of awareness. Against the chain: Gordon Matta-Clark (#383 M1) also let the speed of the body be the work, and his is a chainsaw drawn through a wall in one continuous cut, and Onslow Ford's is a brush moving at the speed of a thought, which is why the son's is a cutting and Onslow Ford's is a marking, and the marking is the cutting made at the speed of awareness.

## M8. The painting as portal (the voyage and the SEED)

The mechanism is that the picture plane is a portal, not a surface, and the portal is the picture. Dorfman: "An Onslow Ford painting cannot simply be looked at. It needs to be contemplated; in fact, entered. The picture plane is a portal", and the entering is the mechanism, because the viewer does not stand before the picture, the viewer goes into it, and the going-into is the entering, and the entering is the portal, and the portal is the plane. The portal opens onto inner space, "not merely his own private world of imagination but rather accessible by all people", and the accessible-by-all is the mechanism, because the inner world is not his property, it is a place anyone can enter, and the entering is the accessible, and the accessible is the inner, and the inner is the all. A "personnage" voyages from one canvas to the next, in All One's Company of 1993, and the voyaging is the mechanism, because the same small figure travels across paintings the way a sailor travels across seas, and the travelling is the voyaging, and the voyaging is the personnage, and the personnage is the canvas. The origin image is the night watch on a navy deck, fixing the ship's position by sextant and stars, per the Mint catalogue, and the sextant is the mechanism, because a sailor finds where he is by looking at the stars, and the looking is the sextant, and the sextant is the voyage, and the voyage is the painting. The SEED is the destination of the voyage, from Creation of 1978, quoted by Dorfman: "an adventure in painting into the inner-worlds to the place of potential that I call the SEED... From there the adventure begins to evolve towards a new way of seeing", and the SEED is the mechanism, because the voyage does not end at a picture, it ends at a place of potential, and the potential is the SEED, and the SEED is the adventure, and the adventure is the seeing. **Takeaway:** make the picture a portal, let a small personnage voyage across it from canvas to canvas, so the viewer enters rather than looks, and the voyage ends at the SEED. Against the chain: Wifredo Lam (#384 M2) also made the picture a place with no exit, and his is a jungle that is a psyche, a thicket with no horizon, and Onslow Ford's is a portal with a voyage through it, which is why Lam's is a closing and Onslow Ford's is an entering, and the entering is the closing made into a door.

## M9. Spirit guardians and power objects

The mechanism is that the picture is a protected enclosure, arranged around a guarded center, and the guarding is the picture. Before painting he held two small stones in his hands "to receive inspiration", and he placed stones on top of his canvas "to arrange compositions before he painted", per Bogzaran, and the holding is the mechanism, because the stone is held first and the picture follows the stone, and the following is the holding, and the holding is the stone, and the stone is the composition. Up to the 1980s one object was a double-sided Vajra, a Tibetan ritual bolt, and the Vajra is the mechanism, because a ritual object carried into the studio makes the studio a ritual place, and the making is the Vajra, and the Vajra is the studio, and the studio is the ritual. In Mexico he placed Tarascan statues and an Aztec Chac-Mool as guardians in front of his creative places, and "power objects" in the foundations of new buildings, "reminiscent of the objects placed in the ancient Egyptian pyramids", and the placing-in-foundations is the mechanism, because the building stands on the object the way the picture stands on the stone, and the standing is the placing, and the placing is the foundation, and the foundation is the guardian. The watchable result is compositions arranged around a guarded center, paired male and female personages flanked by guardian forms, as in Parliament of Space Makers, and the flanking is the mechanism, because the center is protected on both sides, and the protecting is the flanking, and the flanking is the pair, and the pair is the center. **Takeaway:** place a stone on the canvas before painting, arrange the picture around a guarded center, so the picture is a protected enclosure, not an open field. Against the chain: Wifredo Lam (#384 M8) also cast his picture with named gods, and his is an altar built with the right thing for the right god, and Onslow Ford's is a picture guarded by stones and a Vajra, which is why Lam's is an offering and Onslow Ford's is a guarding, and the guarding is the offering made into a wall.

## M10. Cosmic and star iconography (the inner sky)

The mechanism is that the stars move from the background to the subject, and the moving is the picture. Five-pointed stars, the Seba, Sirius and Septit, the "Lady of Heaven", black holes, star-bursts, oval and circular voids, and the star is the mechanism, because a mark that was once decoration becomes the main event, and the becoming is the star, and the star is the decoration, and the decoration is the subject. "Spaces containing bursts of star-like explosions, oval and circular black holes, and calligraphic lines he called 'Live, Line, Beings'", per Bogzaran, and the burst is the mechanism, because the black ground is not empty, it explodes, and the exploding is the burst, and the burst is the black, and the black is the star. The watchable result is that after 1960 the star marks migrate from background decoration to the main subject, and the late paintings are black and blue depths with planets "brilliantly illuminated by alien suns against the blackness of deep space", in Divine the Void of 1989, per Dorfman, and the illuminating is the mechanism, because the planet is lit by a sun that is not in the picture, and the not-in-the-picture is the illuminating, and the illuminating is the alien, and the alien is the planet. The oval of the late 1930s becomes "clusters of images of a black void" before his death, and the becoming-a-void is the mechanism, because the shape that once held a figure now holds nothing, and the holding-nothing is the becoming, and the becoming is the oval, and the oval is the void. **Takeaway:** let the stars migrate from the background to the subject, let the black ground explode, so the late picture is a depth with planets lit by alien suns. Against the chain: Roberto Matta (#382 M2) also painted a cosmic field, and his is volcanoes and lava-blood in an inscape, and Onslow Ford's is stars and black holes in an inner sky, which is why the father's is a volcano and Onslow Ford's is a star, and the star is the volcano made cold.

## M11. The falcon (the bird's-eye journey)

The mechanism is that the picture is seen from above, by a bird, and the above is the picture. The Future of the Falcon series of 1947, painted while moving from Mexico to California, gives "a bird's-eye view of his inner-world adventures", per Bogzaran, and the bird's-eye is the mechanism, because the picture is not seen eye-level, it is seen from above, and the above is the bird's-eye, and the bird's-eye is the adventure, and the adventure is the picture. The falcon is Horus, the sky god, "the eternal protector" and "the seer, [who] assists the painter towards his new journey", and the seer is the mechanism, because the bird does not only fly, it sees, and the seeing is the seer, and the seer is the falcon, and the falcon is the journey. The watchable result is a bird-like image perched above a central birth-scene, a male figure left, a female figure right, a new possibility in the center, ringed by cosmic hieroglyphic marks, and the perching is the mechanism, because the bird sits above the birth and watches it happen, and the watching is the perching, and the perching is the birth, and the birth is the center. The picture reads top-down, as if seen by a bird, not eye-level, and the top-down is the mechanism, because a journey seen from above is a map, and the map is the top-down, and the top-down is the bird, and the bird is the journey. **Takeaway:** put a falcon above the picture and read the scene top-down, a male figure left, a female figure right, a new possibility in the center, so the bird sees the journey and the journey is the seeing. ## M12. The Dynaton meditation object

The mechanism is that the picture is an object for active meditation, not for looking, and the meditating is the picture. With Paalen and Mullican in 1951, the Dynaton showed pictures that were "objects for that active meditation which does not mean detachment from human purpose, but a state of self-transcending awareness", per Paalen's catalogue essay, quoted through Karlstrom, and the active meditation is the mechanism, because the viewer does not relax before the picture, the viewer works before it, and the working is the meditating, and the meditating is the active, and the active is the picture. "Our images are not meant to shock nor to relax; they are neither objects for mere aesthetic satisfaction nor for visual experimentation", and the neither-nor is the mechanism, because the picture refuses both the shock and the decoration, and the refusing is the neither-nor, and the neither-nor is the image, and the image is the meditation. The watchable result is A Stone That Dreamed of 1950: "a vertical surface grid of broken color creates a screen through which pulsating dots subtly move. It is as though reality were coming into focus from behind a veil of illusion. Viewers, actively meditating, situate themselves in the kinetic space of the painting", per Karlstrom, and the veil is the mechanism, because the dots move behind a screen and the viewer must focus through it, and the focusing-through is the veil, and the veil is the screen, and the screen is the dot. Paalen assigned Onslow Ford the element water: "all it hides and bares, the moon, the neckline of the figurehead and the breath in the shell", and the water is the mechanism, because water hides and bares at once, and the hiding-and-baring is the water, and the water is the moon, and the moon is the breath. **Takeaway:** make the picture a veil with pulsating dots moving behind it, so the viewer must focus through the screen and meditate actively, and reality comes into focus from behind the illusion. Against the chain: Wifredo Lam (#384 M7) also filled the field until there was no empty space, and his is a dense packing where figure and ground trade places, and Onslow Ford's is a veil with dots pulsing behind it, which is why Lam's is a crowding and Onslow Ford's is a veiling, and the veiling is the crowding made into a screen.

## M13. Black-and-white hyperspace (Parle's paint)

The mechanism is that black and white dry at the same time, so marks can be layered at any speed, and the drying-together is the picture. In 1960 to 1962 he made the monumental black-and-white works, hiring the chemist William Parle of the California Ink Company in Berkeley to formulate a paint "so that the black and white would dry simultaneously, allowing him to layer the marks at slow or extremely fast speed", per Bogzaran, and the formulating is the mechanism, because the paint is invented for the picture, not bought for it, and the inventing is the formulating, and the formulating is the drying, and the drying is the layering. The watchable result is dense networks of intersecting black and white lines, dots and circles "that resemble the glowing trails and explosions of light made by subatomic particles and cosmic rays inside a cloud chamber", per Dorfman, and the cloud chamber is the mechanism, because the picture records invisible things passing through, and the recording is the chamber, and the chamber is the trail, and the trail is the light. "Within the explicit chaos of the network of lines... there can also be seen an implicit order", per Bogzaran, and the implicit order is the mechanism, because the chaos is not random, it has a shape inside it, and the shape-inside is the implicit, and the implicit is the chaos, and the chaos is the order. Bogzaran links these works to "states of consciousness akin to deep meditative states or particular states of non-representational lucid dreaming known as hyperspace lucidity", and the linking is the mechanism, because the picture is a record of a state, not a picture of a thing, and the record is the linking, and the linking is the state, and the state is the picture. **Takeaway:** invent a paint that dries black and white together, layer marks at slow and extremely fast speed in one picture, so the network looks like particle trails in a cloud chamber, chaos with an implicit order inside it. Against the chain: Gordon Matta-Clark (#383 M1) also let a line remove instead of add, and his is a chainsaw drawn through a wall, and Onslow Ford's is a network of black and white lines drawn at two speeds, which is why the son's is a cutting and Onslow Ford's is a tracing, and the tracing is the cutting made of light.

## M14. Live-Line-Beings

## M15. The reveal as love story (the journey between inscapes)

The mechanism is that the picture is a meeting, not a scene, and the meeting is the picture. A wide canvas is a journey because it takes time to cross, and the crossing is the mechanism, because the eye walks from left to right the way the painter walked from one inscape to the next, and the walking is the crossing, and the crossing is the eye, and the eye is the journey. **Takeaway:** make the wide canvas a journey from one inscape to the next, a male side and a female side joined by lines across a center that marries them, so the picture is a meeting and the meeting is the love story. ## Evolution

The evolution is the sailor who becomes the surrealist, and then the teacher, and then the exile, and then the meditator, and then the solitary cosmologist, and the becoming has six phases, and each phase keeps the one before it. From 1923 to 1937 he is the sailor who paints the sea, the first oil at the age of eleven in 1923, a seascape painted while sick with mumps, the naval watercolors and logs, the Egypt watercolors of 1934 to 1936, Alexandria and the fellahin gardeners, the sea and the voyage already the metaphors, and the sailing is the painting, because the boy who fixes a ship's position by sextant and stars is already learning to find his place by looking at the sky, and the looking is the sailing, and the sailing is the painting, and the painting is the sea. From 1937 to 1939 he is the Paris surrealist, the break, the resignation accepted in June 1937, the move to Paris in the summer of 1937, the meeting with Matta, the Brittany summer of 1938, the giving up of his trees, automatism learned from Matta, coulage invented in 1939, biomorphic forms and hard-edged polygons and dotted lines of force, a bright, broad palette, the inscape replacing the landscape, the Chemillieu summer of 1939, and the breaking is the giving-up, because he tears up many drawings and begins again and again, and the beginning-again is the breaking, and the breaking is the tree, and the tree is the inscape. From 1940 to 1941 he is the teacher, the astral and anxious wartime pictures, Propaganda and Cycloptomania of 1940, the move to New York, the four lectures at the New School from January to March 1941 and the exhibitions hung alongside them, transparency becoming central in Temptations of the Painter of 1941, the meeting with Jacqueline Johnson at the lectures and the marriage in 1941, and the teaching is the giving-permission, because the lectures "gave the artists permission to think differently about painting, to reconceive it", and the giving is the teaching, and the teaching is the permission, and the permission is the picture. From 1941 to 1947 he is the exile in Mexico, the mill bought at Erongaricuaro on Lake Patzcuaro, cosmic elements and illuminated landscapes with islands and pyramids and fauna and volcanoes, the mid-1940s large pointillist dots and luminous solar systems, personages becoming lines, the "reveal", the falcon series, the formal break with surrealism in 1943, the wide panorama of Parliament of Space Makers dated Thursday 12 October 1944, and the exiling is the revealing, because the picture stops being a scene and becomes a journey from one inscape to the next, and the journey is the exiling, and the exiling is the reveal, and the reveal is the love story. From 1947 to 1957 he is the meditator in San Francisco and on the Vallejo, the move to San Francisco in 1947, the ferryboat bought with Varda in 1949 for 500 dollars down and 60 dollars a month, the Dynaton formed with Paalen and Mullican and shown in 1951, the meditation objects, the water element, the veiled grids and pulsating dots, the line, circle and dot discovered on Tuesday 5 June 1951 walking in the redwood groves, calligraphy with Hodo Tobase from 1952 to 1957, the readings in Ouspensky and Jung and Sri Aurobindo and Zen Buddhism and Vedanta, the grids replaced by fluid composition by 1958, and the meditating is the veiling, because the dots move behind a screen and reality comes into focus from behind a veil of illusion, and the focusing is the meditating, and the meditating is the veil, and the veil is the dot. From 1957 to 2003 he is the solitary cosmologist at Inverness, the move in 1957, the monumental black-and-white hyperspace works of 1960 to 1962 with Parle's paint, the second visit to Tutankhamun's tomb in 1963, Painting in the Instant in 1964, Creation in 1978, the death of Jacqueline in 1976, the meeting with Bogzaran in 1989, the Lucid Art Foundation in 1998, the star and inner-sky motif dominant to the early 1990s and then periodic to 2002, the palette muted because "Colour, in the absence of an exterior source of light, becomes muted", per Creation, the late works black and blue depths with planets lit by alien suns, the Live-Line-Beings, the painting until three weeks before his death at the age of ninety, and the becoming-cosmic is the becoming-inner, because the sea of the first oil becomes the inner sky of the last painting, and the sky is the sea, and the sea is the inner, and the inner is the cosmic. The arc, in one line: from painting the sea, to painting the inside of the mind as if it were a sea, and then the inner becoming cosmic, with the voyage staying the same the whole way through, only the sea changing, from water to mind to sky.

## Aesthetic logic

The aesthetic logic is the pour and the unwinding and the language and the voyage. The pour is the coulage and the fast start and the transparency and the hyperspace, the enamel poured flat and cut open so the wet shows through, the random gesture worked for months until the edges are slow, the forms passing through each other as colored light, the black and white drying together so marks can be layered at any speed, and the pour is the gesture, and the gesture is the light, and the light is the speed. The unwinding is the landscape-to-inscape and the psychological morphology and the meditation object, the trees given up, the landscape unwound mark by mark until only inner structure remains, the graph of transformations oscillating from point to volume and moment to eternity, the veil with pulsating dots moving behind it, and the unwinding is the graph, and the graph is the veil, and the veil is the dot. The language is the line, the circle and the dot, and the speed of awareness, and the Live-Line-Beings, the three marks seen in a flash in the redwoods, the speed of the mark matching the speed of the thought, the three marks fusing at a certain speed into living beings, and the language is the speed, and the speed is the fusing, and the fusing is the being. The voyage is the portal and the guardians and the stars and the falcon and the love story, the picture as a portal to be entered, the stones placed on the canvas before painting, the stars migrating from background to subject, the falcon seeing the journey from above, the wide canvas crossed from one inscape to the next, and the voyage is the portal, and the portal is the guardian, and the guardian is the star, and the star is the falcon, and the falcon is the journey. The four hold together because the pour needs the unwinding to have something to pour, and the unwinding needs the language to have marks to unwind with, and the language needs the voyage to have somewhere to go, and the voyage needs the pour to have a sea to sail on, and the sea is the pour, and the pour is the unwinding, and the unwinding is the language, and the language is the voyage. He said, "The pioneer painter paints primarily in order to see", and the seeing is the mechanism, because the painting is not made to be looked at, it is made to be entered, and the entering is the seeing, and the seeing is the voyage, and the voyage is the picture.
