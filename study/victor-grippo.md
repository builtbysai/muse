## The life: chemist, informalist, maker of batteries

Victor Grippo was born in Junin on 10 May 1936, the elder of two sons of an Italian immigrant father and an Argentine mother of Albanian origin -- the Albanian detail is single-source (English Wikipedia lineage) and is flagged, not settled. He began drawing and painting at twelve with Juan Comuni, and learned the sculptor's trade from an Italian blacksmith in Junin; his parents' manual work mattered to him, and CVAA ties it directly to his later celebration of trades. First shows in Junin in the mid-1950s (a 1953 solo is single-source, CVAA English only; the 1954 collective is the safer date), a lithograph prize at Tandil in 1957 during military service, and in 1958 twenty-five lithographic inks at the Tandil newspaper Nueva Era with his first self-reflective text: "No creo que el arte sea solo expresion" -- I do not believe art is only expression.

In 1955 he moved to La Plata to study Chemistry at UNLP -- two years, a Biology assistantship in 1956, a first scientific essay. In 1959 he attended Hector Cartier's "Vision" courses, the decisive influence, the same room his whole generation shared. In 1961 he took part in Grupo Si, the La Plata informalist collective -- participant, not founder (C9816). His work of that period was still expressive, then shifted mid-decade toward geometric abstraction: first solo at Galeria Lirolay, Buenos Aires, 1966, geometric oils; 1968-70 a luminous kinetic phase, machines wrapped in cloth and grazed by light; 1968 the Cubo with Sirabo and Segura (C9813). In 1970 he linked with CAYC, Jorge Glusberg's Centro de Arte y Comunicacion, and showed Analogia I at Arte de Sistemas I; in late 1971 he was a founding member of the Grupo de los Trece (the ICAA thirteen: Bedel, Benedit, Dujovny, Ginzburg, Grippo, Gonzalez Mir, Marotta, Pazos, Pellegrino, Portillos, Romero, Teich, Glusberg). In 1977 the group won the Sao Paulo Biennial Grand Prix for Signos en ecosistemas artificiales, Grippo showing two seven-metre tables.

He never lived from his work, CVAA says plainly, but from parallel activities -- jewellery design from about 1968 into the 1980s, and teaching. The only documented teaching post is the Escuela de Arte Xul Solar in Junin, from around 1969; when the contract ended he kept coming every Saturday from Buenos Aires to teach the young people. No university professorship was found in any source read; do not describe him as one. He was reserved, disliked being called "maestro", and when introduced as "artista" said he was a sculptor, "that history would say whether he was an artist" (his daughter Paulina, Infobae 2022). He talked and listened all night -- Ricardo Salido recalled talks from 8pm to 9am, "because Grippo loved listening" -- and his house in Buenos Aires was, in Camden's words, "a veritable laboratory". He was selected for Documenta 11 in 2002 and died before it opened; the 2002 Diamond Konex was shared with Luis Felipe Noe, not a solo honour. His credo, from Algunos oficios (1976): "Cuando el hombre construyo su primera herramienta, creo simultaneamente (...) la primera obra de arte" -- when man built his first tool, he simultaneously created the first work of art.

## M1 -- The potato battery: matter into energy, energy into consciousness

Analogia I (1970-71, W04) is forty potatoes in a painted wooden case, each with a copper and a zinc electrode, wired together to a voltmeter. Each potato yields about 0.7 volts; forty together drive the needle. Grippo's own text panel states the analogy: the potato as object is to consciousness as intangible -- the potato, a food native to the Americas and a staple of the poor, stands for Latin American resources and suppressed peoples, and its contained energy was, in the early 1970s, also a metaphor for revolutionary uprisings in the Southern Cone (ICAA). The current is real and measurable, and it fades as the potato rots: Getty's conservators must change the potatoes about every fifteen days or the voltmeter reads nothing, and when the meter reads zero the analogy reads as a lack of conscious energy. Time is literally in the work. The late return, Tiempo, 2nd version (1991, W18), reduces the battery to its minimum: four potatoes running a small digital clock, with a text from Dostoevsky's The Devils. The work performs its idea rather than illustrating it -- a needle that lives, weakens, dies; a clock that runs on food.

**Takeaway:** a humble organic store of energy, made visible by a simple technical means, with time built into the reading. A piece that shows a battery without the decay has missed the mechanism; the meter must be allowed to fall to zero.

## M2 -- Real vs replica: the doubled place setting

Analogia IV (1972, W05) lays two place settings side by side on one wooden table: on white linen, three real potatoes on a ceramic plate with a real metal fork and knife; on black velvet, three potatoes carved in solid transparent acrylic, with knife and fork in transparent acrylic resin. Presentation against representation, natural against artificial, humble against sumptuous. The acrylic potato is the same shape with the life taken out -- it will never rot, never conduct, never feed anyone. The doubling is the argument: which setting is the real one, the one that decays or the one that lasts?

**Takeaway:** the pair must stay visibly different from each other -- one edible, one frozen -- and the comparison must do the thinking. A piece that shows only the replica, or only the real thing, has nothing to compare and no analogy.

## M3 -- The energy race: potato against coal

Sintesis (1972, W06) is the smallest-scale statement of the analogy: one potato and one lump of charcoal, side by side. Two stores of energy -- one living and rotting, one fossil -- set against each other with no winner declared. The potato will sprout or rot; the coal will sit unchanged until it is burned. The race is slow and the terms are unfair in both directions, which is the point: energy is not one thing, and the humblest store is the one that is alive.

**Takeaway:** a slow contest with no declared winner, the two stores kept in the same frame. A piece that crowns one side has turned a comparison into a verdict and lost the mechanism.

## M4 -- The public oven: bread as action, bread as violence

Construccion de un horno popular para hacer pan (1972, W07) built a traditional rural earth oven in a public square, Plaza Roberto Arlt, Buenos Aires, with Jorge Gamarra and A. Rossi, a rural worker who had such an oven at home; bread was baked and given free to passers-by -- artUS says nearly 5,000 people were fed -- and the police destroyed the oven two days later. The action is the work: an oven appears in the city, feeds strangers for free, and is destroyed by the state. The valise answers it five years later: Valijita de panadero (1977, W12), burnt bread treated for preservation, mounted in a painted wooden and acrylic box -- flour plus water plus excessive heat, in CVAA's reading the devastating consequences of an unrestrained, furious application of energy, a symbol of the violence of the military dictatorship at its worst, the suitcase alluding to exile. Bread given away is generosity; bread burnt black in a case is violence. Same material, opposite operations.

**Takeaway:** the oven must actually feed people, and the destruction must actually happen -- the work is the appearance and the disappearance, not the object left behind. A piece that shows an oven nobody eats from has missed the mechanism; a piece that shows burnt bread without the free bread first has only the second half.

## M5 -- The table as laboratory

The table is Grippo's laboratory bench, dining table, workbench and altar in one object, and it is both theme and support -- CIFO: the tables testify "to the confluence and transmutation of experience and memory". In Analogia I the potatoes sit in a wooden case; in Analogia IV the meal is laid out as an experiment; in Todo en marcha (1973, W08) five flasks of coloured salt solutions, five crystallisation jars and rotting potato vapours share the top with five potatoes across the sprouting cycle; in Energia vegetal (1977, W10) a long table holds piles of sprouting potatoes and eight laboratory flasks. Tabla (1978, W13), the first of the Mesas, is a sanded wooden table inscribed in permanent black ink with everything it witnessed: "Sobre esta tabla... se partio el pan, cuando lo hubo..." -- on this board the bread was broken, when there was any -- through homework, tears, books, shared joys, and trades. Mesas de trabajo y reflexion (1978-94, W17) extends it to a dark room of old school desks carrying handwriting under spotlights, shown at documenta 11 after his death. The table accumulates writing and chemistry over time; it is where the experiment happens and where life happens, and Grippo refuses to separate the two.

**Takeaway:** a surface that accumulates -- writing, chemistry, food -- over time, the lab glassware and the dinner sharing one top. A piece with a clean table has nothing to accumulate and no laboratory.

## M6 -- Latent force bursts containment

Vida, muerte, resurreccion (1980, W15, MoMA) is five hollow lead bodies -- sphere, pyramid, cube, cylinder, cone -- filled with beans and water in a glass box, with five empty duplicates standing beside them as witnesses. The beans germinate unseen inside the lead, expand, and burst the containers. Death into life; life under dictatorship -- MoMA's label notes the work "took on a special meaning during Argentina's military dictatorship of 1976-83". The force is invisible until the rupture: slow growth, then the lead splits. The bean colour is disputed in the sources (black per Camden and artdaily, red per MoMA) and the plate count in La comida del artista differs by source too; both are flagged, not resolved (C9819) -- probably different versions, and the mechanism does not depend on the colour.

**Takeaway:** invisible growth, then rupture, with witnesses standing by. A piece that shows the burst without the slow unseen growth has only the effect; a piece that shows growth without the containment has nothing to break.

## M7 -- The plumb line: equilibrium as a silent space

From Opuestos (1981, W16) the plumb line first appears, and with it the Equilibrios series that runs to the end of his life: boxes, plumb lines, balance, weight, gravity, the human face as a mask. The noisy experiment becomes a silent space. The plumb line never lies -- gravity is the one instrument that cannot be faked -- and the work turns on whether the weight balances. After the potatoes and the ovens and the bursting beans, Grippo's last two decades are inward and quieter: the table room darkens, the handwriting accumulates, the line hangs straight. The medium of Opuestos itself is unstated in the sources fetched; do not invent one.

**Takeaway:** a line that never lies, a weight that must balance, in a space gone quiet. A piece that adds noise to the equilibrium has missed the turn; the silence is the mechanism now.

## M8 -- Trades as first art

Algunos oficios (1976, W09) lays out the tools of five trades -- blacksmith, stonemason, bricklayer, carpenter, farmer -- at rest, with a bed of earth watered daily and a wheelbarrow propped against the wall. The tools are specimens, and the earth must be watered daily or the piece dies: maintenance is part of the work. The valijitas extend the same idea in miniature -- small valises honouring the baker, the builder, the critic, Le Corbusier, Kafka. The credo behind it is his own: when man built his first tool, he simultaneously created the first work of art. The trade is not a subject for the art; the trade is the art, and the artist's job is to lay the tools out and keep the earth wet.

**Takeaway:** tools laid out like specimens, with a living element that needs daily care. A piece that shows tools as decoration, without the maintenance, has the props and not the practice.

## The aesthetic logic: transformation is the only verb

Chillida's arc for the whole catalogue runs from a crowd fed for free around the 1972 oven to the silent, non-communicating crowd of Anonimos (1998-2001, W20) -- painted plaster forms under glass, shapeless, expressionless, almost lifeless. The career's single verb is transformation: matter into energy (the potato battery), energy into awareness (the analogy), food into consciousness (bread), death into life (beans bursting lead), art into science and back (his own equation: "art, science and metaphysics should constitute a single search"). The means stay deliberately low-tech and handmade -- "never sophisticated or costly" (artdaily) -- which is itself the political point: energy does not need the First World's apparatus. The early paintings are geometric abstraction in oil; the middle work is a circuit, an oven, a table; the late work is a plumb line and a dark room of desks. What never changes is the refusal to illustrate: the current is real, the bread is really eaten, the beans really burst the lead, the earth really needs watering. The analogy is not a picture of an idea; it is an apparatus that performs it, and then it rots, dries out, or bursts, on schedule. The aesthetic is the schedule -- a work that keeps working after the artist leaves the room, and stops working when its material gives out.
