# Jesus Reyes Ferreira: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #323.

**Site:** Jose de Jesus Benjamin Buenaventura de los Reyes y Ferreira (Guadalajara, 17 October 1880 or 1882 - Mexico City, 5 or 6 August 1977), called Chucho Reyes, self-taught Mexican painter of "papeles embarrados" (smeared papers): tempera and gouache on papel de china, the tissue of fiesta decoration, made on a patio table with homemade aniline colors, blanco de Espana craquele, and late gold dust. He refused the title of painter ("Yo no pinto, embarro"), signed nothing for decades, gave the work away, and ended as the color counselor of Mexican architecture: the Torres de Satelite's chromatic range was his idea, the Capuchinas pink cross his transposition of Golgotha, Barragan's walls his palette taught aloud. Identity anchors: the ephemeral support chosen on purpose; the homemade color; the rooster as kinetic fire; the curtain that always opens on the same ring; the house as total installation; color as architecture.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Luis Barragan study: from the man who borrowed the colors to the man who mixed them. Barragan's foundation holds 194 of Chucho's works, sold or given; the architect who tuned courtyards for serenity called the paper-smeared decorator his teacher of colors. Where Barragan built permanence out of plaster and lava, Chucho made his wager on the opposite material: tissue paper not meant to last, and he made it outlast the argument. This study is about the fountain the triad drank from, the painter who refused the title and ended up painting, by advice, the most photographed concrete in Mexico.

No mechanism invented; every engine below is footed to a viewed frame or an end-to-end read.

## M1. Papel de china as anti-canvas

He chose a paper "not meant to last": the tissue of papel picado and fiesta decoration, and made fragility the whole wager. In practice he laid the crepe paper over a silk base flat on a patio table (EN wiki), never an easel, and drove tempera into it with boiling-water mixes. The paper wrinkles under the paint and becomes visible texture: in the red-and-yellow rooster frame the wrinkled black ground is part of the image; in the blue rooster the creases cross the white ground like another drawing. Pellicer wrote the epitaph for the method: "se diria que el artista, en un gesto de orgullo, escogio para trabajar material tan deleznable. Que pinto asi 'por no dejar'." The UAM essay gives it the Quevedo line: "lo fugitivo permanece."

Against the chain: Barragan (#322 M8) specified materials to age into poetry; Chucho starts at the far end, with a support already dying, and dares the image to survive it. Carrington (#318) painted cosmologies to be timeless; Chucho's wager is that the fugitive stays. Goeritz's gold bruised with age; Chucho bakes the bruise in as the first coat.

**Takeaway:** Choose the support the serious people reject; let its weakness do half the drawing. Watch the sheet: when the wrinkle reads as intention and the fragility as pride, the mechanism is on.

## M2. The kitchen of color

Vegetal pigments and shiny anilines, boiling water, vegetable glue; blanco de Espana (white lead, calcium carbonate) for the craquele and antique effect; gold and silver dust in maturity as the signature ornament. He mixed his own gouaches every morning in the patio (nephew David: "luego de preparar sus colores, pues el hacia sus propias mezclas"). Colors bleed deliberately past contours; the raw paper tone is kept pure somewhere in almost every sheet (Kassner: the original tone of the china paper "always has capital importance"). In the frames: gold strokes over the red and black of the rooster, gold pigment in the angel's hair, unblended lime and chrome-red flats in the horse-dancer.

Against the chain: Goeritz (#321 M6) used gold as spiritual technology on metal; Chucho dusts it on tissue like sugar. Burger (#320) had the press's ink and the deadline; Chucho has the kitchen and the morning. Nobody else in the chain manufactures the paint as the ritual that starts the workday.

**Takeaway:** Mix the color the same morning from ingredients that stain the fingers; let it cross the line. Watch the edge: when the bleed looks deliberate and the raw paper holds its ground, the mechanism is on.

## M3. Fill-to-the-edges composition

"Gallos, caballos y angeles llenan el compacto espacio empujandolo a sus limites": the figure expands until the paper can barely contain it. In the pink-ground rooster the tail vortex touches all four edges; in the red-and-yellow rooster the comb bleeds off the top; the golden beast is pressed into its purple rectangle like dough. La Jornada: the roosters' plumage is "como si el espacio que ocuparan pudiera apenas contenerlos," "una belleza fulgurante que participa de la condicion dinamica del fuego."

Against the chain: Horna (#319) composed for the camera's six feet; Chucho composes for the paper's last millimeter. Barragan's walls also fill the frame, but with stillness; Chucho fills his with agitation. The chain's miniaturists guard the margin; he abolishes it.

**Takeaway:** Let the figure outgrow the sheet; the paper is a container under pressure. Watch the border: when the comb has to leave the paper to finish, the mechanism is on.

## M4. The flaming rooster

His master subject, and never a bird study: spiraling flame-brushwork, elongated neck, gold crest and tail, ground fields of Mexican pink, black, or blue. Five distinct rooster frames in this study repaint the same subject as different states of agitation; one tangles the rooster with a nude figure. The readings layer without canceling: palenque virility, Peter's denial before dawn (the UAM essay's religious-sexual charge), pure kinetic fire.

Against the chain: Carrington's animals were citizens of a cosmology; Chucho's rooster IS the cosmology, one animal carrying the whole metaphysics. Burger drew animals as jokes with knives; Chucho draws one bird as five different fires. Goeritz had no animal at all.

**Takeaway:** Pick one animal and repaint it as weather; the subject is the excuse, the agitation is the portrait. Watch the tail: when the feathers read as flame and the ground as heat, the mechanism is on.

## M5. "Yo no pinto, embarro"

He refused the title of painter, called the works "papeles," neither signed nor dated for decades, and entered art through window displays, wedding halls, church decoration, bullring adornment, and antiques-shop wrapping paper. The market had to come to HIM: customers started asking for the wrapper itself, "ya no como meras envolturas sino como creaciones independientes." "Embarrar" (to smear, to daub) is the core gesture: color smeared with the hand's joy, only "por el placer de hacerlo" (nephew David). The late signed works (pink-ground rooster, angel) against the unsigned unmarked sheet show the arc from amateur mantle to acknowledged master without changing the gesture.

Against the chain: Barragan disowned his functionalist years; Chucho never disowned anything, he just refused to sign it. Burger fought the market in the daily press; Chucho ignored the market until it rearranged itself around his table. The chain's professionals all have a signature strategy; his was to not have one.

**Takeaway:** Do the work where the money is not looking, and let the signature arrive decades late or not at all. Watch the unsigned sheet: when it needs no name to be finished, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The gift economy

He gave works away for decades (the Chagall "papeles" gift is the famous instance); late in life the gesture inverted into signed, gold-dusted, authentication-certified auction lots (Morton, Rivero Lake). The Fundacion Barragan holds 194 works, "sold or given to the architect." The work is frequently forged: a backhanded market verdict. One legend does not survive the sources: the 1967 Misrachi sale-show story appears in no consulted source; what the sources actually say is that wrapper-sales began early in Guadalajara on customer demand, and that he kept the amateur mantle by choice, not by failure.

Against the chain: Burger's deadline was the market in its purest daily form; Chucho's market was the friend's outstretched hand. Goeritz coordinated and got paid in projects; Chucho counseled and got paid in gratitude, then in forgeries. Nobody else in the chain turned giving into a pricing mechanism.

**Takeaway:** Give the work until the giving becomes the legend, then let the market authenticate what it once refused. Watch the gift: when the paper changes hands and neither party mentions money, the mechanism is on.

## M7. Circus as cosmogony

Many works are literally staged: painted curtains, the written legend "va a empezar la funcion," ringmasters, clowns, ecuyeres defying gravity, a strongwoman holding a lion head (chocolate ground), a harlequin with a golden mask and ruff, a clown in acid green with a white ruff, an ecuyere on a green horse. He choreographed shows for the vedette Tortola Valencia. Nandino, quoted in La Jornada, gives the doctrine: the world is "el circo de la vida." Even the sacred enters through the curtain.

Against the chain: Burger (#320 M4) used the caption to contradict the image; Chucho writes the legend ON the curtain and the image obeys it. Carrington staged cosmologies for sitters; Chucho stages them for ticket-holders who never arrive. Horna photographed real rooms; he paints the room the show needs.

**Takeaway:** Put the curtain in the painting and write when the show starts; the legend is a stage direction the image must follow. Watch the curtain: when the painted velvet feels heavier than the act behind it, the mechanism is on.

## M8. The blue body

A recurring figure type: female bodies rendered as flat fields of cobalt and ultramarine with black drawing strokes, faces simplified to near-erasure, set against reserved paper white or red sunburst grounds. The blue standing figure, the blue mermaid with red sunbursts, the curled blue nude with the gold signature: the body becomes a color swatch that happens to be human. UAM: "los rostros desaparecen o exhiben un dolor perdurable."

Against the chain: Goeritz's bodies were folded ribbons with no faces at all; Chucho keeps the body and erases the face, keeping the humanity as a rumor. Barragan's blue was a wall; Chucho's blue is a person. Burger's women were punchlines; these are weather systems.

**Takeaway:** Paint the body as one flat color and let the face dissolve; the figure is a field first. Watch the white ground: when the blue reads as a person and the reserve as air, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The sacred-profane braid

Christ in lace, crowned in gold, a bird perched on his arm; a crucifixion amid floral masses; doll-prostitutes titled with the street cry "Pasa guero"; dead girls painted as angelitos with Christian piety; flowered skulls doing circus contortions; devils as angels and angels as devils ("diablos angelicales, como se quiera"). Kassner via UAM: his plants and animals are NOT innocent; the flowers evoke mortuary crowns, "fleurs du mal"; the black flower vase in the Rivero Lake collection is the proof object.

Against the chain: Carrington braided alchemy and Catholicism as system; Chucho braids them as gossip, on the same sheet, in the same hour. Barragan's pink cross at Capuchinas is Chucho's braid built at architectural scale (M11). Burger's sacred was a target; Chucho's sacred is a neighbor.

**Takeaway:** Put the lace on the Christ and the street cry on the saint; let the piety and the profanity share the pigment. Watch the bird on the arm: when the sacred looks decorated rather than solemn, the mechanism is on.

## M10. The house as total installation

The Milan house: lighted cubes, colored walls, niches for religious objects, the courtyard studio, hundreds of pre-Hispanic, colonial, and folk objects "colgadas, erguidas, acostadas y, sobre todo, revueltas." Westheim: not a museum, not a collection, "una Jauja de la imaginacion artistica." The paintings were decorations OF the house before they were commodities; Soriano learned more there than at school. The sepia frames of Chucho standing in the dark interior under the glass dome show the magician inside his own cabinet.

Against the chain: Barragan's house is a UNESCO instrument tuned for serenity; Chucho's house is a jungle tuned for wonder, and the student learned at the jungle. Horna staged rooms for the camera; Chucho staged rooms for living in, and the camera was lucky to be invited. Goeritz built El Eco as a one-year machine; the Milan house ran for forty.

**Takeaway:** Accumulate until the accumulation is the architecture; every object is a note and the house is the chord. Watch the niche: when the religious object and the toy share a shelf without irony, the mechanism is on.

## M11. Color as architecture

He was the counsel behind the walls: the Torres de Satelite's original chromatic range (1957; the neoplastic color range was his idea, per UAM), the Capuchinas chapel's intense-pink cross (1960-65, "a transposition of Golgotha," rooted in his funerary-decoration practice), Barragan's San Angel projects and characteristic wall palettes (Noelle). He advised Goeritz, Max Cetto, Juan Sordo Madaleno, Ricardo Legorreta. Goeritz's tribute: architects owed him "su sentido infalible para los colores, materiales y texturas" and his plastic ideas and instinct for volumes and spaces. The rosa mexicano Barragan built with is the pink ground of Chucho's rooster frame, carried from paper to concrete.

Against the chain: Goeritz (#321 M2) confronted the visitor with one yellow wall; the palette of that confrontation's whole era passed through Chucho's patio table. Barragan (#322 M1) made color structure; Chucho taught him the notes. The chain's architects built; the decorator told them which color to build in.

**Takeaway:** Advise, don't build; one decisive color suggestion outlives a building. Watch the wall: when the pink reads as inevitable and nobody remembers who chose it, the mechanism is on.

## M12. Artificial antiquity

He deliberately aged his own paintings: the blanco de Espana craquele, the "antique" impression, gold dust. Paramo: "Reyes busca lograr una impresion antigua" via pigments, boiling water, vegetable glue, white lead. The antiquarian's instinct inverted: instead of finding old beauty, manufacture the patina of time on yesterday's paper. The crackled surface is visible on the white ground of the blue rooster frame.

Against the chain: Barragan (#322 M8) specified materials to age in place; Chucho skips the waiting and paints the age in. Burger's newsprint yellowed whether he liked it or not; Chucho's paper arrives pre-yellowed by recipe. Goeritz welcomed the bruise on his gold; Chucho applies the bruise as the first coat.

**Takeaway:** Paint the patina, don't wait for it; the antique effect is a morning's work with white lead. Watch the craquele: when the crack reads as history and the paper is a month old, the mechanism is on.

## The aesthetic logic: fragility as permanence

Chucho chose the material least likely to survive and made the most durable thing in the chain out of it. "Lo fugitivo permanece": the fugitive stays, not because tissue is strong but because the decision was repeated every morning for forty years at a patio table. The doctrine has two words: "embarrar." To smear, to daub, with joy, "por el placer de hacerlo," on paper nobody framed, unsigned, undated, given away. It is the decorator's revenge on the academy: no canvas, no signature, no easel, no title, and the work ends up authenticating auctions, advising Pritzker winners, and teaching Juan Soriano more than school. Permanence, in Chucho's Mexico, is not a property of materials. It is a property of decisions repeated until the world rearranges itself around the table.

## Evolution (from sources; early work not visually verifiable)

1. **Guadalajara formation (1880s-1938):** lithography apprentice at Loreto y Ancira (1894), window dresser at Casa Pellandini, decorator of party halls, churches, and bullrings, antiquarian, watcher of the Judas-figure painters at El Rincon del Diablo. The crepe wrappers originate as antiques-shop wrapping paper; customers demand the paper as the work. Habit: buy two copies of every book, cut one up for designs.
2. **Mexico City courtyard factory (1938-1950s):** the forced move after arrest, beating, and the sign; the Milan house; 85 percent of total output. Group shows: New York 1942, Fogg 1942 (with Picasso), MoMA 1943, London 1945. 1945 Helena Rubinstein salon decorations. 1950 first exhibition at Goeritz's initiative. 1957 Torres color advice.
3. **Color counselor (1950s-60s):** Barragan, Goeritz, Cetto, Sordo Madaleno, Legorreta; the Capuchinas pink cross.
4. **Late recognition (1962-1977):** 1962 Homenaje Nacional at Bellas Artes; 1967 Bellas Artes solo; first trip abroad at 87 (Paris, Rome, Madrid), then the Middle East and New York; 1977 Orozco Medal.
5. **Posthumous (1979-now):** 1979 Bellas Artes homage; 1984 Museo Rufino Tamayo homage; 2002 MAM "La mirada estetica" (built from his own thousands of objects, 180 colonial paintings shown); 2009 Casa Luis Barragan interventions; 2018 Bellas Artes "La fiesta del color." Through-line: the decorator's hand never changed; only the world's address for it did, from wrapper to wall to auction lot.

## Study-method note

The brief carried claims the sources would not corroborate, and this study does not repeat them: the 1967 Misrachi sale show (no source; the 1967 event was the Bellas Artes solo); "never sold a painting in his lifetime" (wrapper-sales began in Guadalajara on customer demand); the Rivera Anahuacalli connection; the Olga Flores Rivas museum; a Tamayo friendship; Montenegro/Dr. Atl/Paalen/Zarraga links; cut-paper papel-picado works (he painted ON the paper of papel picado); Day-of-the-Dead ofrenda pieces; Torres color-maquettes and Goeritz studio photos; portraits of specific circle members; a Tonala house-museum; murals. What survives is stronger than the legend: the gift economy is documented, the architecture counsel is documented, the Chagall and Picasso encounters are documented, and the forty-year patio-table practice is the load-bearing fact.
