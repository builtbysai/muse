# Violeta Parra: Study Notes

**Site:** Violeta del Carmen Parra Sandoval (born in San Carlos on Thursday 4 October 1917, died in her tent in La Reina, Santiago, on Sunday 5 February 1967, aged 49), Chilean folk singer, songwriter, poet, visual artist, ethnographer and collector, the founder of the nueva cancion chilena, the woman who gathered more than three thousand Chilean songs from the countryside and sang them back as her own, the composer of "Gracias a la vida" and "Volver a los 17" and "El gavilan", the singer of the cueca and the tonada and the canto a lo divino with a guitar and a guitarron of twenty-five strings, the embroiderer of arpilleras who showed them in the Louvre in 1964, the first Latin American artist with a solo exhibition there, the painter of oils on wood and the maker of wire sculptures and paper-mache masks, the mother of Isabel and Angel Parra, the sister of the anti-poet Nicanor Parra, the teacher of Victor Jara, the lover of Gilbert Favre, the woman Chabuca Granda sang about after her death, and the founder of a tent in La Reina she called a university of folklore, and the artist whose museum opened in Santiago on 4 October 2015, on what would have been her ninety-eighth birthday, and whose birthday, 4 October, is the Day of Chilean music and musicians. ## M1. The field recording as the work

The collecting is the work, and the work is the mechanism. In 1952, pushed by her brother Nicanor, she abandons the boleros and rancheras and Mexican corridos she has been singing in the bars of the Mapocho district, and she goes out with a gramophone to gather the authentic music of Chile, and the gathering is the career, because she walks the poorest barrios of Santiago and the mining towns and the farms, and she records the songs of the countryside, and the recording is the method, and the method is the gramophone carried into a kitchen, and the kitchen is the studio, and the studio is the people's house, and the house is Barrancas in 1953, and in Barrancas she finds Dona Rosa Lorca, a midwife and healer who knows more than five hundred songs, and Rosa is the teacher, and the teacher is not a professor but a neighbour, and the neighbour is the archive, and the archive is the people, and the people are the book, and her own words are the record, that when she went to collect her first song in Barrancas she learned that Chile is the best book of folklore ever written, and the book is the country, and the country is the work, and the work is the collecting, and the collecting is the song, and the song is the people, and the people are the source, and the source is the mechanism. More than three thousand songs are gathered and summed up in the book "Cantos folkloricos chilenos", with photographs by Sergio Larrain and scores by Gaston Soublette, and the book is the harvest, and the harvest is the collecting, and the collecting is the work. **Takeaway:** carry the gramophone into the kitchen, and the kitchen is the studio, and the people are the archive. Against the chain: Chabuca (#361) also collected a city, but hers was Lima's old houses and streets remembered from above, and Violeta's is the countryside gathered from below with a gramophone, which is why Chabuca's version is a memory and Violeta's is a field recording.

## M2. The abandonment of the old repertoire

The turning is the mechanism, and the turning is a refusal. Until 1952 she sings what the public wants, boleros and corridos and Spanish songs and Peruvian valses, and she sings them well, and she is paid for them, and the payment is the trap, and the trap is the repertoire, and the repertoire is foreign, and the foreign is the habit, and the habit is broken by her brother, and Nicanor tells her to defend the authentic music of Chile against the stereotypes, and the telling is the push, and the push is the turning, and the turning is the abandonment, and she drops the old repertoire whole, and the dropping is the mechanism, because she does not add Chilean songs to the boleros, she replaces them, and the replacement is total, and the total is the birth, and the Fundacion's timeline names 1952 "Nace la verdadera Violeta Parra", the true Violeta Parra is born, and the birth is the abandonment, and the abandonment is the work, and the work is the refusal, and the refusal is the repertoire, and the repertoire is Chilean, and the Chilean is the field, and the field is the country, and the country is the song. **Takeaway:** drop the old repertoire whole, and the dropping is the birth, and the birth is the true voice. Against the chain: Daniela (#368) also turned, but hers was a prestige turn toward the criollo repertoire late in a salsa career, adding heritage to a settled voice, and Violeta's is a turn at the start that throws the settled voice away, which is why Daniela's version is an addition and Violeta's is a birth.

## M3. The decima as autobiography

The ten-line verse is the life, and the life is the mechanism. She writes the "Decimas autobiograficas", her life told in decimas from childhood to the trip to Europe, and the decima is the form, and the form is ten lines of eight syllables, and the ten lines are the container, and the container is the popular poetry of Chile, and the poetry is the people's, and the people's is hers, and she fills the container with her own life, and the filling is the mechanism, because the decima is not a diary, it is a sung form, and the sung form is the memory, and the memory is the verse, and the verse is the life, and the life is the song, and the song is the decima, and the decima is the autobiography, and the autobiography is the work. She invents the centesima beside it, a numbering decima that counts to three hundred, a form that does not exist in folklore, and Nicanor, her brother the poet and mathematician, keeps pushing her on it, and the counting is the invention, and the invention is the decima grown longer, and the longer is the life, and the life is the counting, and the counting is the mechanism, and the mechanism is the verse that holds a whole life in ten-line rooms. In May 2026 the Museo received the ninety-one-page notebook in which she wrote the decimas by hand, both sides of every page, and the notebook is the life, and the life is the handwriting, and the handwriting is the decima. **Takeaway:** tell your life in ten-line rooms, and the room is the verse, and the verse is the life. Against the chain: Daniela (#368 M16) also made her discography a diary, but hers is six album titles restating the self after each rupture, and Violeta's is one continuous verse form counting her life line by line, which is why Daniela's version is a shelf of titles and Violeta's is a single long room.

## M4. The arpillera as painted song

The embroidery is the song, and the song is the mechanism. She begins the arpilleras in earnest in 1959, in bed with a severe hepatitis that keeps her lying down for months, and the bed is the studio, and the studio is the sickness, and the sickness is the turn, and the turn is the needle, and the needle is the new instrument, and she takes the wools at hand and embroiders her daughter's bedspread, and the bedspread is the first canvas, and the canvas is the cloth, and the cloth is burlap, and the burlap is the sack, and the sack is the people's material, and the material is the message, and the message is that art is made from what the earth and the everyday give you, and the giving is the mechanism. She works with no design and no prior drawing and no corrections, and the work appears only when the cloth is stretched, and the stretching is the revelation, and the revelation is the image, and the image is the song, and the song is painted, and the painting is stitched, and the stitching is the singing, and her own words are the record, "Las arpilleras son como canciones que se pintan", the arpilleras are like songs that are painted, and the painted song is the mechanism, and the mechanism is the needle doing what the voice does, and the voice is the thread, and the thread is the colour, and the colour is the wool, and the wool is the song. The complete visual work is twenty-two arpilleras, also called lanigrafias, jute tapestries embroidered with wool, and twenty-six oil paintings and thirteen wire sculptures, made between 1958 and 1965 in Santiago and Buenos Aires and Paris and Geneva, and the count is the Fundacion's, and the count is the work. **Takeaway:** stitch the song, and the needle is the voice, and the cloth is the canvas, and the work appears when you stretch it. Against the chain: Ronaldo (#367) also made his art from the people's materials, but his was a company built from a barrio's hardship into a stage, and Violeta's is a bedspread embroidered in sickness from the wools at hand, which is why his version is a construction and hers is a convalescence.

## M6. The hawk and the hen

The hawk eats the hen, and the eating is the mechanism. "El gavilan" is begun at the end of the 1950s and finished in 1964, and it is twelve minutes and eighteen seconds long, and it was written for a ballet that was never staged, and the never-staging is the first fact, and the first fact is the mechanism, because the ballet exists only as a song, and the song is the ballet, and the ballet is the allegory, and the allegory is a hen and a hawk, and the hawk is the man, and the hen is the woman, and the woman is the one who suffers, and the suffering is the love, and the love destroys almost always and does not always build, and her own words are the record, that the hawk is the man with claws and bad feelings, and also the power, and also capitalism, and the powerful, and the hawk is all three at once, and the three are the mechanism. The music breaks the folk form open, and the breaking is the sound, and the sound is the tritone and the unresolved dissonance and the fierce cueca rhythm and a strumming the scholars call homicidal, and the strumming is the hawk, and the hawk is the guitar, and the guitar is the claws, and the atonality is the pain, and the pain is the point, and the point is that she sings it in a suffering voice, and the suffering voice is the hen, and the hen is the one who says "Gavilan, gavilan, tiene garras, y yo sorda segui monte arriba, gavilan me saco las entran~as", and the entrails are the cost, and the cost is the love, and the love is the hawk, and the hawk is the mechanism. She heard Margot Loyola sing the Mapuche "Machi ul" on Saturday 10 September 1960 at the Teatro Municipal, and the hearing is the influence, and the influence is the Mapuche air in the piece, and the air is the rhythm and the intensity, and the intensity is the hen's cry, and the cry is the song. There is no studio version, only three informal recordings, one from a 1960 radio interview in Concepcion, one recorded by Miguel Letelier in her house in La Reina, one in Paris in 1964, and the informality is the record, and the record is the song, and the song is the hawk. **Takeaway:** let the hawk eat the hen for twelve minutes, and let the dissonance be the pain, and the pain is the love. Against the chain: Caitro (#366) also built a song from a bird, but his was a festejo assembled from grandmothers' stories, and Violeta's is a ballet that was never staged and a love that eats the lover, which is why his version is an assembly and hers is a wound.

## M7. The curse litany

The curse is a list, and the list is the mechanism. "Maldigo del alto cielo" curses the world item by item, and the itemizing is the song, and the song is a catalogue, and the catalogue runs down from the sky, and the sky is the first curse, and the first curse is the star with its reflection, and the reflection is the second, and the second is the blue tiles and the gleam of the stream, and the stream is the third, and the third is the low ground and the stone with its outline and the fire of the oven, and the oven is the fourth, and the fourth is the mountain range, and the range is the fifth, and the fifth is the spring with its gardens in flower, and the flower is the sixth, and the sixth is the autumn and its colour, and the colour is the seventh, and the seventh is the solitary figure of the flag and any emblem and the Venus and the Araucaria and the wide sea and the moon and the landscape, and the landscape is the last, and the last is the curse put in Greek and in Spanish, and the putting is the mechanism, because the curse is total, and the total is the grief, and the grief is the soul in mourning, and the mourning is the reason, and the reason is given inside the song, "porque mi alma esta de luto", because my soul is in mourning, and the mourning is the curse, and the curse is the list, and the list is the world, and the world is cursed one thing at a time, and the one-at-a-time is the mechanism, and the mechanism is the inventory of everything loved and then damned, and the damning is the mirror of "Gracias a la vida", and the mirror is the pair, and the pair is the blessing and the curse, and the blessing thanks the world item by item, and the curse damns it item by item, and the itemizing is the form, and the form is the list. **Takeaway:** curse the world one thing at a time, and the list is the grief, and the grief is the soul in mourning. Against the chain: Chabuca (#361) also listed a world, but hers was a list of Lima's streets and houses remembered with love, and Violeta's is a list of the sky and the sea and the spring damned with grief, which is why Chabuca's version is a remembrance and Violeta's is a curse.

## M8. The thanksgiving that is a farewell

The thanks is the goodbye, and the goodbye is the mechanism. "Gracias a la vida" is composed in La Paz, Bolivia, in 1966, and it opens on a shift between A minor and E major, then goes to G7 and C and back to the Am and E motif, and the shift is the song, and the song is simple, and the simplicity is the mechanism, because the chords are common and the melody is plain, and the plainness is the vessel, and the vessel holds the list, and the list is the thanks, and the thanks runs through the eyes that distinguish black from white and the high sky with its starred depth and the multitudes and the man she loves, and the loving is the last item, and the last item is the man, and the man is Favre, and Favre is the love, and the love is the song, and the song is the thanks, and the thanks is the farewell, and the farewell is the reading, and the reading is the paradox, and the paradox is that the author of a hymn to existence takes her own life a year after writing it, and the year is 1967, and the taking is the suicide, and the suicide is the shadow, and the shadow is the song, and the song is read as a suicide note, and the note is the thanks, and the thanks is the irony, and the irony is that a life full of health and opportunity and worldly experience offers no consolation to grief, and the grief is the song, and the song is the thanks, and the thanks is the farewell, and the farewell is the mechanism. Against the chain: Daniela (#368 M2) also made a return from silence into a shout, but hers is a protest shout after two silent years that opens a new career, and Violeta's is a thanksgiving that closes a life, which is why Daniela's version is a return and Violeta's is a farewell.

## M9. The return to seventeen

## M10. The Mapuche ear

Against the chain: Los Kipus (#364) also carried another people's music, but theirs was a trio carrying the criollo vals of the coast, and Violeta's is a lone folklorist carrying the Mapuche lament of the south, which is why theirs is a repertoire and hers is a listening.

## M11. The tent as university

## Network

- **Victor Jara (1932-1973):** the disciple and heir. Evidence: he meets Violeta in 1957, and she takes him as a disciple, and he visits her house in La Reina and spends whole afternoons with her, and she takes an interest in his guitar style and his way of singing, and she encourages him to go on, and she conceives the idea that he and her son Angel should play together, and the project does not come to pass, but Victor and Angel become great friends, and after her death he becomes one of the leading figures of the nueva cancion chilena. The Fundacion names him among those on whom she exercised a notable influence, with Hector Pavez and Gabriela Pizarro and the groups Cuncumen and Chamal.
- **Nicanor Parra (1914-2018):** the brother and anti-poet. Evidence: he pushes her from 1952 to collect the authentic music of Chile and to abandon the old repertoire; she musicalizes his "Cueca larga de los Meneses"; in 1960 he records "Defensa de Violeta Parra" with her accompanying on guitar in "Tres Palabras"; he keeps pushing her on the centesimas, the counting decimas to three hundred; her last letter is to him, "Yo no me suicido por amor. Lo hago por el orgullo que rebalsa a los mediocres".
- **Isabel Parra (born 1939) and Angel Parra (1943-2017):** the children, collaborators and heirs. Evidence: they sing and tour and record with her from childhood; they form the duo Isabel y Angel Parra in Paris in 1962; they found the Pena de los Parra in 1965; they record "Las ultimas composiciones" with her and Alberto Zapican in 1966; they found the Fundacion Violeta Parra in 1992, Isabel its first president; their son and grandson Angel Parra Orrego donates the unpublished "Arbol de la vida" arpillera and the ninety-one-page decimas notebook to the Museo in May 2026.
- **Gilbert Favre (1936-1998):** the Swiss lover. Evidence: anthropologist and clarinetist and quena player, met on her forty-third birthday, Tuesday 4 October 1960, eighteen years younger; lives with her in Geneva, dividing time between France and Switzerland; co-founder of the Bolivian group Los Jairas; leaves in December 1965 and marries in Bolivia; "Gracias a la vida" is written for him, and "Run Run se fue pa'l norte" is dedicated to him after he leaves; Chabuca Granda's "Cardo o ceniza" poetizes the affair.
- **Margot Loyola:** the folklore peer. Evidence: Violeta replaces her on Radio Agricultura in 1954; Loyola sings the Mapuche "Machi ul" at a recital on Saturday 10 September 1960 at the Teatro Municipal de Santiago, and Lucy Oporto Valencia argues the hearing shaped the interpretation of "El gavilan".
- **Atahualpa Yupanqui (1908-1992):** the pan-American peer. Evidence: the English Wikipedia "Nueva cancion" names Parra and Yupanqui as the two transitional figures whose mastery of folk music and leftist involvement joined folk and politics into nueva cancion; the Pena de los Parra welcomed him among its non-Chilean musicians; Yupanqui and Angel Parra record "El ultimo recital" in Zurich on 8 February 1992, including "Volver a los diecisiete" and "Homenaje a Violeta". Limit carried: no direct Violeta to Yupanqui meeting is evidenced in sources read; the link is movement-peer through the Pena and through Angel.
- **Mercedes Sosa:** the popularizer. Evidence: her 1971 version of "Gracias a la vida", on the album "Homenaje a Violeta Parra", spreads the song through the Spanish-speaking world; she records "La carta" with Joaquin Sabina on "Cantora, un viaje intimo" in 2009.
- **Chabuca Granda (1920-1983):** the mourner. Evidence: after Parra's death she writes the Violeta cycle, "Cardo o ceniza" and "No lloraba, sonreia" and "Si fuera cierto", about the affair with Favre; Caitro Soto plays the cajon on "Cardo o ceniza".
- **Pablo Neruda:** the poet friend. Evidence: met during the collecting years; writes "Elegia para Cantar" for her; on her death he says, "De cantar a lo humano y a lo divino, voluntariosa hiciste tu silencio, sin otra enfermedad que la tristeza". Date conflict flagged: the Fundacion places the elegy in 1953, the English Wikipedia in 1970.
- **The makers around her:** Sergio Larrain, photographs for "Cantos folkloricos chilenos"; Gaston Soublette, scores for the same book and transcriber of fifty cueca manuscripts for her Concepcion museum; Miguel Letelier, who records "El gavilan" in her La Reina house around 1960; Yvonne Brunhammer, curator of the 1964 Louvre show; Don Isaias Angulo, the tenant farmer who teaches her the guitarron in 1953; Dona Rosa Lorca of Barrancas, the midwife and singer who teaches her more than five hundred songs; Alberto Zapican, the Uruguayan singer who plays on "Las ultimas composiciones" and receives "El Albertio".
- **The chain names she touches by repertoire and by mourning:** Eva Ayllon and Susana Baca, who both sing her songs in Peru; Los Folkloristas and Tehua of Mexico, who sing her in the same folk lineage; Milton Nascimento, who sings "Volver a los 17"; Leon Gieco and Victor Heredia and Charly Garcia and Fito Paez, who carry her in Argentina; Joan Baez and U2, who carry "Gracias a la vida" in English.
