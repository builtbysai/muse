## Evolution: three phases, early vs recent

PHASE 1, THE ARCHIVE MAN, 1934-1955. The Italian Problem in European Diplomacy 1847-49, 1934, Vienna research on Italian unification, a first book in contemporary history when the profession thought the Victorian era too recent for serious study; The Habsburg Monarchy, 1941, Pribram's favourable Habsburgs giving way to Namier's unfavourable ones; The Course of German History, 1945, written to defend Britain's war and justify the Russian alliance, National Socialism as the inevitable product of all German history, the Sonderweg stated at full strength; The Struggle for Mastery in Europe, 1954, the diplomatic narrative at full stretch, his masterpiece; Bismarck, 1955, unification more by accident than by design, the accident entering as a thesis. The driver is the archive, the Austrian, French and British files laid out on one table, the young man slipping into libraries where no one would recognise him, the history still written as if the documents could speak for themselves.

PHASE 2, THE SYNTHESIS AND THE CONTROVERSY, 1954-1965. The Trouble Makers, 1957, the Ford Lectures of 1955, dissent as heroism, his favourite book, Fox and Cobden and Bright and Hobson as the exceptions who were right; The Origins of the Second World War, 1961, the reversal of the received war-guilt story, written in six weeks by his own claim, before most of the archives were open, the accident theory stated as a general doctrine, the Nuremberg Thesis refused, the normal leader proposed, and the profession answering with the fiercest controversy of his life, Trevor-Roper in Encounter in July 1961 and on television the same year; English History 1914-1945, 1965, the turn to English social and cultural history, written as a deliberate parody of Olympian judgement, the poor always right and the rich always wrong, selling more in its first year than all previous Oxford History of England volumes combined. In the same years the freelance economy takes over, income overtaking salary by 1950 and trebling it by 1958, television from 1957, the Regius chair going to Trevor-Roper in 1957, the lectureship not renewed in 1964. The driver is from the archive to the argument, from the document to the epigram, the quarrel sought out on purpose, the paradox loved for its own sake.

PHASE 3, THE POPULAR HISTORIAN AS INSTITUTION, 1966-1985. ## Aesthetic logic

Taylor's work is beautiful in the way a short, hard sentence is beautiful, and that is the point. Butterfield's mechanism is the sentence snapping shut and holding the whole argument in it; Taylor's is the sentence landing like a verdict and the scene freezing on it. His aesthetic is the lecture hall, the epigram, the paradox, the reversal, the timetable, the dead hand, the one-day slip, and the beauty, where there is any, is in the phrase that lodges in the memory because it is strange and true at once: "Mobilization means war", "a dictator who did not dictate", "Most men could do better while shaving", "the saviour of his country". He writes for the general reader and the crowd, he refuses the seminar's jargon, he stands at 8:30 in the morning with empty hands in front of a full room, and the empty hands are the style: a Lancashire radical in a bow tie, a two-seater sports car outside and a library card inside, the showman and the hidden worker taking turns. His colours are the colours of Magdalen stone and railway black, lecture-hall amber, the ink black of a docket, the red of a bow tie, the grey of a timetable, the white of a television studio. His motion is the motion of the reversal, the slip, the lock, the widening dock, the shrinking monument, the splitting figure: two men swapping places, a plan slipping one day late, a timetable locking the next train, a dock widening until the room is in it, a monument shrinking on approach, one figure becoming two. The piece that follows him should never be solemn or monumental; it should be quick, paradoxical, and performed, and its one quiet moment is the hand that writes "obligation" over "right" while the audience watches, the question "What happened next?" hanging at the end of every scene.

**Takeaway:** land the one line and let the scene freeze on it, and the piece should ask what happened next and answer with a swerve.
