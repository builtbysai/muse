# Le Jeune Renard: Study Notes

**Site:** Le Jeune Renard is the working name of Sean Zellmer, a shader-focused creative coder. Art Blocks identifies its "Contrast Agent" collaborator as "Creative Technologist -- Sean Zellmer" and links him directly to instagram.com/lejeunerenard; the same name appears on the GitHub profile `lejeunerenard`, whose display name reads "Sean Zellmer" and whose self-description is "Freelance Creative Coder & Web Developer". GitHub lists his location as Glen Ellyn, IL, corroborated by an acnatoo.org petition page listing "Sean Zellmer | Glen Ellyn, IL". Birth date and birthplace not established in any source read. Living and active as of late 2026 (a TikTok video dated Sunday 17 August 2025; a 2025 CodePen series reusing his GLSL debugger). [C36616]

**Relevance:** Zellmer's work asks what a picture is when it is made of nothing but binary states: every mark is either on or off, long or short, a dot present or absent, and the image is the sum of those decisions changing on a fixed loop. The evidence is Hans's own saves -- three pieces from his "Generative Art" collection, each captioned with a pair of Braille characters: "Flipping ⠄⠢", "Binary Flow ⠇⡵", "Pegs ⠈⠻" -- a body of work in which the caption itself is a binary pattern and the picture is a field of two-state marks. [C36617]

**Doorway:** The 450th doorway. Matejka ends on the question of what a predicting system is allowed to change -- the picture, the interface, or nothing at all. Zellmer answers the third branch by removing the question's premise: nothing in his systems predicts anything, there is no interface to adapt, and the picture changes anyway, on its own fixed loop, whether or not anyone is watching. The open question carried forward is: when a system has no model of the viewer at all, what is left for the picture to do -- and can a loop that only ever flips its own bits still surprise? Leg 3 judged this a break in the chain rather than a continuation; the study records it as the reset the question invites, not as a forced link. [C36618]

**Depth:** Sixteen works inspected -- 11 viewed as images, 3 as text/metadata only, 3 as existing-but-unrendered WebGL pages. The three saved works were read as poster frames from the local files and their Instagram post pages were fetched (dates, likes, hashtags, and the pages' own motion summaries returned). Two 2017 dailies viewed as images from the artist's own site; the Contrast Agent still (#10) viewed; the Lush commission description read in the artist's own words; three early photographs (2010–2012) viewed. Read end to end: the Art Blocks Contrast Agent page, the "Glitch Interview" (Notion, interviewee unnamed on the page -- attribution caveated below), ilithya's daily-practice essay, the artist's GitHub profile, and the TikTok profile as crawled. Not read or watched: no video was watched by any leg; no Instagram post grid was seen; no source code of the artist's was read; ljr.io returned no content; x.com was blocked for the fetch tool; the Vans collaboration named in the Art Blocks bio was not found described anywhere. ## A life in code: photography to the daily shader

Zellmer started with a camera, not a compiler. The early photography on his own site -- aerial wetlands shot on a Nikon D40X (Exif 12 February 2010), a staged self-portrait pouring a bottle over his own head (Exif 30 October 2011), a near-featureless colour-field gradient (Exif 2 September 2012) -- is representational work, years before the daily shader practice, and it matters because the turn away from it is total: nothing in the later work depicts anything at all. [C36620]

The turn comes in 2017. That year his site lists four WebVR/WebGL dailies -- Crystal, Glitch Oil, Heavenly Body, Lava King, Mask -- and the Lush Creative Showcase commission, an interactive identity piece that appeared on screens throughout Lush's event and "responded to users posting on Instagram or Twitter with the hashtag #creativeshowcase, becoming more energetic with more social media activity", driven by "a webgl feedback simulation where the previous frame is fed back into the generation of the next frame", with randomized initial parameters "so that it is virtually impossible for the interactive to repeat itself". The same year is the start of the daily practice his TikTok bio still claims: "Making generative art everyday since 2017", 2,365 videos as crawled on 27 November 2026. [C36621]

The background, as told in the "Glitch Interview" -- with the caveat that the page does not name its interviewee, and the attribution to Zellmer rests on matching details (the Visual Debugger project, joining SFPC through Zach Lieberman, a math degree) rather than a name on the page -- runs: a high-school Web Design course; a math degree in college, with web dev and design filled in from free resources (bento.io, hackdesign.org); a college bandmate showing him Patatap (Jono Brandel's interactive visual sound kit), which "completely encompassed everything I wanted to do as a creative person" and led him through JavaScript, animation, and SVG to Kandinsky and visual music; joining the School for Poetic Computation through Zach Lieberman; early projects the Visual Debugger (with Nabil Hassein) and Randomized Form. He works, in his own words, "largely with geometry and a basic level understanding of calculus", and moves "pretty slowly on the new technology beat" -- looking back into art and design history rather than forward into bleeding-edge technology. His model for technology serving expression is J Dilla on the MPC3000, and his line on authorship is: "Technology isn't the boss of the digital artist any more than the camera is the boss of the photographer." [C36622]

His tools are GLSL fragment shaders -- the Art Blocks bio's phrase is "focusing on shaders" -- and he is also a toolmaker: his "GLSL Debugger in JS" (a Three.js-based shader-error viewer) appears verbatim as a header comment in other artists' CodePen pens, including a 2025 series by levoxtrip, and his pinned GitHub repository is raymarching-experiments. No p5.js, Processing, or TouchDesigner use is documented in any source read. Shader artist ilithya, writing about building an almost-daily practice, names him among five daily posters she followed when she started in 2019 -- Matt DesLauriers, Saskia Freeke, Sean Zellmer, Dave Whyte, Zach Lieberman -- which independently corroborates the daily-practice reputation. [C36623]

**Takeaway:** The practice is the piece. A daily shader is a discipline -- one system a day, finished, looped, posted -- and the catalogue is the artwork as much as any single loop. Build from the discipline, not from one frame: one rule, one day, one loop that returns exactly to its start. [C36624]

## M1 -- The binary flip ring ("Flipping", 2019)

Subject: a ring of triangular spikes around a dark centre, on a flat purple field. The post (https://www.instagram.com/p/B5PryO6HIxr/) was published Sunday 24 November 2019, 146 likes, 2 comments, hashtags including #webgl, #glsl, #shader, #generative, #realtime, #loop, #everyday, #mathart, #creativecoding. [C36625]

Visual principle: radial symmetry in position, broken by extent. The still frame shows eight pale-lavender triangles radiating from a dark centre circle, uneven in length -- the bottom spike much longer than the rest, one lower-right triangle much shorter -- so the ring reads as symmetric in position but asymmetric in extent; the asymmetry is the subject, and the symmetry is what makes it legible. [C36626]

The motion is known only from the post page's own summary, not from observation: a pale-lavender octagon on the purple field splits into eight triangles that drift outward, rotate slightly, and reassemble into the octagon, then the colours invert (lavender field, purple octagon) and the fracture repeats -- a seamless loop. [C36627]

Computational mechanism (FORMALISATION, not the artist's stated method): N identical elements placed at angles θᵢ = 2πi/N around a centre; each element carries a binary state sᵢ(t) ∈ {short, long}; a flip wave travels around the ring, sᵢ(t) = square(ωt − θᵢ), so each spike flips long-to-short as the wavefront passes it; the still frame is one instant of that wave, which is why the lengths are uneven. Parameters: N, the two lengths, wave speed ω, wave shape (square vs. eased). [C36628]

**Takeaway:** A star that breathes by discrete snaps rather than smooth pulsing -- the binary change against a symmetric form is the whole drama, and the wave makes time visible as position around the circle. [C36629]

## M2 -- The dash-flow cube ("Binary Flow", 2022)

Subject: an isometric cube drawn with nothing but short dashes. The reel (https://www.instagram.com/reel/CbZfUOrjjE9/) was published Tuesday 22 March 2022, 510 likes, 6 comments, music "supercodex 09" by Ryoji Ikeda, hashtags including #3d, #lineart, #pattern, #webgl, #glsl, #shader, #generative, #minimalism. [C36630]

Visual principle: volume implied purely by oriented marks. The still frame shows a black field holding the cube: the top face and the left face are filled with dashes running along each face's direction, varying in length and brightness, while the right face is left blank black -- so the volume is implied only by the marks on two faces, and the dashes' direction tells the viewer which face they belong to. No page summary of the motion was returned, so nothing about its movement is asserted here beyond the title and the single frame. [C36631]

Computational mechanism (FORMALISATION): a 3D cube projected isometrically; each face carries a grid of dash cells; each dash has a phase p = (u + t·v) mod 1 along its face axis, drawn only while p is inside a short window, with length and brightness from a second field (sine or noise over the face); the flow runs along the three face directions simultaneously, so the solid looks like it is streaming through itself. Parameters: dash density, flow speed per face, window length, projection angle. [C36632]

**Takeaway:** A solid that is also a flow -- solidity from motion, not surface -- and the cube is the simplest possible volume, so all attention lands on the mark-making. [C36633]

## M3 -- The perturbed peg grid ("Pegs", 2022)

Subject: a regular grid of rings with a wave passing through it. The reel (https://www.instagram.com/reel/CjU8KfVjZwJ/) was published Wednesday 5 October 2022, 1,289 likes, 14 comments, music "Emerald Rush" by Jon Hopkins, hashtags including #grid, #loop, #mesmerizing, #soothing, #webgl, #glsl, #shader, #generative. [C36634]

Visual principle: regularity makes the disturbance legible -- the wave is visible only because the grid is perfect; the dent is the picture. The still frame shows a black field holding a tight, even grid of thin white-outlined circles, roughly ten columns by about nineteen rows in the visible frame; rings near the centre and lower-middle are smaller and dimmer than the rest, and the grid appears to bend there: a disturbance is visible mid-passage through an otherwise perfect grid. [C36635]

The motion is known from the post page's summary, not from observation: waves of distortion travel vertically and horizontally, rows of circles compressing, stretching, and rippling "as if the surface were flexible". In the comments the creator replies to a question about each piece's objective that there is no formal objective -- the works are mostly experiments with the sole goal of creating math-based visuals. [C36636]

Computational mechanism (FORMALISATION): grid points (i, j); a travelling scalar field w(i, j, t) = sin(k·d − ωt), where d is distance from a moving source (or a plane-wave coordinate); each ring's radius r = r₀·(1 − a·w) and brightness b = b₀·(1 − c·w), with optional displacement of the grid point along the wave normal; the still frame shows the wave mid-passage, rings shrunk and dimmed near the front. Parameters: grid spacing, wave speed, wavelength, amplitude, source path. [C36637]

**Takeaway:** A wave you see as a dent in order rather than as a drawn line -- the grid is the instrument and the wave is the player. [C36638]

## M4 -- The orbit-trap fractal ("Contrast Agent", 2023)

Subject: nested, self-similar vascular-like structure, animated as an infinite loop. Contrast Agent, with Tim Richardson, is an Art Blocks 500 release in the Presents category: a fixed edition of 14 unique animated artworks, released Friday 10 March 2023. Its concept: CT-scan contrast agents as the metaphor; fractal geometry built from cylindrical orbit traps with a nested, self-similar structure "much like the vascular system of the body"; each work an infinite visual loop, "an animated 'liquid physiology'". [C36639]

The one still viewed -- Contrast Agent #10 (Art Blocks media-proxy image, 640×815) -- shows a vertically symmetric, hourglass-like mass of intertwined ribbon strands on a flat neutral-gray ground: pastel lavender, pale green, and off-white, semi-translucent, fanning wide at top and bottom and pinched at a narrow central waist, with fine striations along the ribbons. Editions #7 and #12 were seen as text pages only, no image. [C36640]

Computational mechanism (AS STATED in the Art Blocks text, not a formalisation): fractal geometry "constructed with cylindrical orbit traps that possess an inherent self-similarity", the symmetric structure "embodied as an infinite visual loop", each artwork "bound to this temporal cycle". In executable terms this is the standard orbit-trap construction: iterate a complex map, measure each orbit point's distance to a trap shape (here a cylinder), colour by trap distance and iteration count, and cycle a parameter over a fixed period so the animation returns exactly to its start. [C36641]

**Takeaway:** A body rendered as pure recursion -- interior physiology made visible by a rule that repeats itself at every scale -- and the loop-exactness is the discipline: frame N must be identical to frame 0, or it is not the piece. [C36642]

## M5 -- The noise-displaced volume ("Crystal", "Glitch Oil", 2017)

Subject: a solid form whose surface is turbulence. "Crystal 7/365" (artist's own site, image viewed): a faceted, crystalline solid on a black ground -- a tilted, roughly octagonal mass in metallic silver-gray, its surface covered in fine recursive scale-like detail, like a mineral grown out of smaller copies of itself, the lower part stepping down in flat faceted ledges. "Glitch Oil 8/365" (image viewed): an irregular, roughly circular blob on black, electric blue and pale yellow-green marbled forms churning together with black gaps -- like an oil slick frozen mid-churn, with no hard edge anywhere. [C36643]

Three more 2017 dailies exist on the same site -- Heavenly Body, Lava King, Mask -- but their pages are live WebGL apps whose visuals are generated at runtime, so no still of any of the three was inspected. [C36644]

Computational mechanism (FORMALISATION): a signed-distance field of a base primitive (sphere, faceted crystal) displaced by 3D fractal Brownian-motion noise; rays marched through the field (raymarching), shaded by the surface normal with a palette mapped from the noise value; time advances the noise's third coordinate, so facets melt and reform while the silhouette persists. Parameters: base shape, noise octaves and scale, displacement amplitude, palette bands. [C36645]

**Takeaway:** The cheapest sublime in shader art -- one noise function turns any primitive into something organic -- and that cheapness is the risk: a noise-displaced blob reads as generic unless some other rule (the loop, the silhouette, the palette discipline) does the distinguishing. [C36646]

## M6 -- The caption as a bitmap

Subject: the caption itself -- two Braille cells and a bar. Every one of Hans's three saves carries the same caption form: a two-word title followed by two Braille characters and a trailing "|": "Flipping ⠄⠢|", "Binary Flow ⠇⡵|", "Pegs ⠈⠻|". A 2025 TikTok post continues the pattern: "Plethora ⠌⡛|" (Sunday 17 August 2025, 20-second video, metadata only -- no frame was accessible, so nothing about its visuals is asserted). [C36647]

The dot positions, computed from the Unicode values (bit 0 = dot 1 … bit 7 = dot 8): "Flipping" ⠄ dots [3], ⠢ dots [2, 6]; "Binary Flow" ⠇ dots [1, 2, 3], ⡵ dots [1, 3, 5, 6, 7]; "Pegs" ⠈ dots [4], ⠻ dots [1, 2, 4, 5, 6]. In English Braille ⠢ (dots 2, 6) is the "en" contraction, ⠻ (dots 1, 2, 4, 5, 6) is the "er" contraction, and ⠇ (dots 1, 2, 3) is the letter "l" -- but the three pairs do not spell anything verifiable in any Braille code checked, and ⡵ uses dot 7, which belongs to 8-dot Braille. No decoding is asserted. The durable observation is the form: a Braille cell is a 2×3 binary dot matrix, and the caption pairs two such matrices with a cursor-like bar -- the caption itself is a tiny binary pattern, a visual rhyme with the binary logic of the work. [C36648]

Computational mechanism (FORMALISATION / INTERPRETATION, not the artist's stated practice): treat the two cells as a 12-bit seed -- each dot is one bit; the seed drives the piece's parameters (grid size, flip pattern, wave phase); the trailing "|" is a cursor, marking the pattern as live input rather than a label. Parameters: which dots are on, and the mapping from bits to parameters. [C36649]

**Takeaway:** The piece's control panel hidden in plain sight in its own title -- the viewer who decodes the caption holds the seed -- but only if the seed-to-picture mapping is made visible; otherwise the dots are a gimmick, not a control. [C36650]

## The aesthetic logic

Zellmer's logic is a discipline of subtraction. One system per piece, one ground (flat purple, or black), one mark repeated (triangle, dash, ring), one motion (a wave, a flow, a flip), looped so the end is the beginning. The palette is either a single flat colour against a single ground or neon-on-black; there is no shading, no texture for its own sake, no second subject. The two saves that look most different -- the purple star and the black dash-cube -- are the same logic with different parameters: a field, a grid of marks, a rule that changes the marks over time. [C36651]

What makes the work worth stealing from is not any single image but the ratio of rule to result: the rule is always small enough to state in one sentence, and the result is always larger than the rule -- the flip wave makes a star breathe, the dash flow makes a cube out of nothing, the peg wave makes a dent you can feel. The failure mode is the mirror image: when the rule is generic (noise over a primitive, M5), the result is generic too, and only the loop-exactness or the silhouette saves it. [C36652]

**Takeaway:** Steal the ratio, not the look. One sentence of rule, one field of marks, one loop that returns exactly -- and let the surprise come from the rule running, not from decoration added to it. [C36653]

## Rejections

Six candidates were considered and rejected, so the prompts below are not a quota filled by lowering the bar. (1) A literal "Flipping" clone (purple field, lavender triangles, same composition): a copy, not a new piece -- LJR1 is the computational interpretation of the same idea and supersedes it. (2) A "daily shader" piece that cycles through Zellmer-like loops on a schedule: a feed, not a mechanism -- the schedule is a premise, and no rule distinguishes one day's loop from the next. (3) A piece that spells words in Braille: the decoding is not established, and spelling is a premise, not a system. (4) A literal "Pegs" clone (same grid, same wave, no second source): a copy -- LJR3 adds the pointer as a second wave source, which is the mechanism. (5) A literal "Binary Flow" clone (same cube, same dashes): a copy -- LJR2 is the interpretation with rotation as the earned interaction. (6) A noise-only crystal with no loop discipline and no silhouette rule: generic turbulence, already the weakest prompt (LJR6) and rejected outright if the build leg finds it reading as such. [C36655]
