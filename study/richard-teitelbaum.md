## M1. The brainwave-to-control-voltage chain: the brain steers the synth

First use: In Tune of 1967. The distinction matters and is the one Teitelbaum owns against Alvin Lucier: Lucier sonified alpha by direct audification into percussion in Music for Solo Performer, premiered Wednesday 5 May 1965 at Brandeis with the physicist Edmond Dewan, and Teitelbaum was the first to run alpha through a synthesizer as control voltage, and the first to use a synthesizer to sonify his alpha rhythm, per Lutters and Koehler 2016. **Takeaway:** wire the brain to the synth and let thought steer the knobs, and the performer thinks the music. ## Aesthetic logic

The body is the score, and the machine listens, and the room answers, and that is the logic that runs through all fourteen mechanisms, and the piece is not an object but a circuit you enter, and the brainwave is the circuit's current, and the eye-blink is the circuit's switch, and the radio is the circuit's wire, and the sequencer is the circuit's clock, and the ensemble is the circuit's ground, and the audience is the circuit's load, and the piano is the circuit's memory, and the golem is the circuit's will, and the sampler is the circuit's past, and the threshold is the circuit's edge, and the notation is the circuit's alphabet, and the touch is the circuit's breath, and the telephone is the circuit's length, and the drift is the circuit's rest. The early MEV years are the same logic in its loudest form, a room so wired that anyone in it is an input, and the biofeedback years are the logic turned inward, one body as the whole circuit, and the Japan years are the logic turned outward, other traditions as other circuits, and the digital piano years are the logic turned back on itself, a machine that listens to what was just played, and the opera years are the logic at its largest, a creature built out of letters that develops a will, and the music is not about him, it is about what happens between the signal and the machine, and between the player and the room, and between the thought and the sound, and when the situation changed, the music changed with it, all the way down to the notes, but the body never left the circuit, and the circuit never left the body.

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. MOOG ARRIVAL YEAR IS DISPUTED. Curran, February 1981, first-person: Teitelbaum "showed up in Rome in 1966 with one of the first models of the Moog". Holmes / Bob Moog Foundation, 2016, citing Teitelbaum personal communication 4 March 2016: "In the Fall of 1967, Teitelbaum made the down payment on a used Moog Synthesizer and returned to Europe with this equipment". Both cannot be the first arrival unless the 1966 sighting was a different machine or a loaner. The study must not state a single year flat; give both, with the 1967 date as Teitelbaum's own later account and 1966 as Curran's memory.

C2. "FIRST MOOG IN EUROPE" IS VERIFIED BUT MUST BE NARROWED TO MOOG. Paolo Ketoff's Synket was already in Rome in 1964 and Curran knew it before the Moog; Curran names it in the same paragraph as the Moog's arrival. The claim is "first Moog", not "first synthesizer in Europe".

C3. DEATH DATE IS APRIL 9, 2020, NOT APRIL 16, 2020. The AllMusic biography says "Teitelbaum died on April 16, 2020, following a stroke", wrong. NPR of 9 April 2020, RA of 10 April 2020 and the Keyser funeral home obituary all give Thursday, April 9, 2020, HealthAlliance Hospital, Kingston, NY, major stroke. Weekday verified with date -d: Thursday 2020-04-09.

C4. BIRTH DATE IS MAY 19, 1939, NOT MARCH 19, 1939. Giftpals and Prabook say March 19, 1939, wrong. Wikipedia, NPR, the funeral home and the official bio agree on May 19, 1939, New York City. Weekday verified with date -d: Friday 1939-05-19.

C5. MEV WAS FOUNDED IN 1966, NOT 1965. The All About Jazz Evocation liner notes say Teitelbaum was "prominent in electro-acoustic improvisation from the co-founding Musica Elettronica Viva in 1965", wrong. Consensus across DRAM, the official bio, the Moog Foundation, NPR and Bard: spring 1966 crypt concert, named MEV, first billed MEV evening March 1967, Avanguardia Musicale 2.

C6. "CO-FOUNDED WITH CURRAN AND RZEWSKI" IS SHORTHAND. The founding group also included Allan Bryant, Ivan Vandor, Carol Plantamura and Jon Phetteplace, per DRAM. Les presses du reel gives the fuller form: "co-founder, with Allan Bryant, Alvin Curran and Frederic Rzewski". The trio form is fine for a doorway line but the study should name Bryant at least once.

C7. IN TUNE DATE: FIRST PERFORMED LATE 1967, NOT 1968. Wikipedia, citing History of Brainwave Music, says "first performed with Barbara Mayfield in late 1967"; DRAM says first performed by MEV in 1967 at St Paul's American Church. Some secondary sources say 1968, the Moog Foundation photo caption dates the wired Mayfield photo 1968, and a UC thesis says "In Tune (1968)". The 1968 date appears to come from the version-1 schematic of 1968 and the 1968 photo, not the premiere. Use 1967, note the 1968 variant.

C8. TIME ZONES: RECORDED 1976, RELEASED 1977. Wikipedia's Teitelbaum discography lists "Time Zones (Freedom, 1976)", that is the recording year. The album page gives: released 1977, recorded 10 June and 16 September 1976. Both recording dates verified as Thursdays with date -d.

C9. BLENDS: COMPOSED 1977, ALBUM RELEASED 2002. Do not date the piece 2002. The Wire top-ten listing is for the 2002 New Albion CD, NA118cd, which pairs Blends of 1977 with Kyotaku/Denshi of 1995.

C10. GOLEM: PREMIERED 1989, JEWISH MUSEUM, NEW YORK, WORK SPANS 1989-1994, TZADIK ALBUM 1994. Do not date the opera 1994 only, and do not call 1989 the completion date. Satellite pieces: Golem Sketches, Golem I, Golem Studies, Golemics, per the AllMusic bio.

C11. SOUNDPATHS DATES CONFLICT AND ARE UNRESOLVED. Fromm Music Foundation: commission 2004, completed 2009. Official bio: "received a commission from the Fromm Music Foundation and was premiered in fall, 2005", Da Capo Chamber Players, New York. A 2005 premiere and a 2009 completion cannot both describe the same version. The study gives the commission, 2004, and premiere, fall 2005, from the composer's own bio and flags the Fromm 2009 date as unexplained.

C12. NO WORK TITLED "PIANO CONCERTO" WAS FOUND IN ANY SOURCE READ. What exists: "Concertino for Piano and Orchestra", for Hiroko Sakurazawa and the Woodstock Chamber Orchestra, per NPR, "Concerto Grosso", 1985, and "Concerto Grosso No. 2", 1988. The brief's phrase "Piano Concerto" must not be used as a title.

C13. BARD APPOINTMENT: JOINED THE FACULTY IN 1988, DIRECTOR OF THE ELECTRONIC MUSIC STUDIO, RETIRED END OF 2019 AFTER 30-PLUS YEARS. Not a 1970s appointment.

C14. WORLD BAND: FOUNDED 1970, WHILE STUDYING ETHNOMUSICOLOGY AT WESLEYAN. Members from India, Japan, Korea, the Middle East and North America.

C15. TWO DIFFERENT SHAKUHACHI TEACHERS, DO NOT CONFLATE: Kodo Araki V at Wesleyan in 1970-71, and Katsuya Yokoyama in Japan on the 1976-77 Fulbright. Gagaku hichiriki was studied with Masataro Togi, chief court musician of the Imperial Household.

C16. BIOFEEDBACK AND THE ARTS PUBLICATION YEAR VARIES BY CITATION: Rosenboom (ed.), Aesthetic Research Centre of Canada, Vancouver, cited as 1976 by the Moog Foundation and DRAM, "MIT Press, 1976, pp. 35-56", as 1975 in the BCMI lit review, and as 1974 in the eContact bibliography. Teitelbaum's chapter is consistently "In Tune: Some Early Experiments in Biofeedback Music", pp. 35-56. NPR cites the paper as "In Tune: Some Early Experiments in Biofeedback Music (1966-74)", which is the paper's own date range, not the book's year. Cite the chapter as 1976 and do not assert a book year beyond it.

C17. ASPARAGUS FILM YEAR: les presses du reel and Black Truffle say "cult 1978 animation"; Wikipedia says Teitelbaum "provided the score for the 1979 animated short film Asparagus". The soundtrack material was recorded 1975-76 at York University. Use 1978 for the film with the 1979 variant noted.

C18. THE MOOG WAS BOUGHT USED. Holmes: "made the down payment on a used Moog Synthesizer", fall 1967. Do not write that Moog gave him a new instrument or that he imported a new one.

C19. TEITELBAUM DID NOT INVENT BRAINWAVE MUSIC. Alvin Lucier's Music for Solo Performer premiered Wednesday 5 May 1965 at Brandeis, with physicist Edmond Dewan, and is the first brainwave piece by consensus. Teitelbaum's firsts, precisely: first to use a synthesizer to sonify his alpha rhythm, and first to use brainwaves as control voltages for a Moog. "Pioneer of brain-wave music" is fair; "inventor" is not.

C20. "PERFORMED OVER 200 CONCERTS ON THE INSTRUMENT", AllMusic bio, IS AN UNVERIFIED COUNT. The Moog Foundation, the more careful source, says "MEV performed dozens of concerts over a three-year period". Do not repeat 200 without attribution to AllMusic.

C21. Z'VI: SPELL WITH A STRAIGHT APOSTROPHE, Z'vi, SUBJECT SABBATAI Z'VI, ALSO SPELLED SABBATAI SEVI / SHABBETAI TZVI, 1626-1676, THE SELF-PROCLAIMED MESSIAH WHO CONVERTED TO ISLAM IN 1666. The official bio's phrase "17th Century Jewish-Moslem Messiah figure" is the safe form. Do not call him simply a Jewish messiah without the conversion.

C22. DRAM NOTES LINE 42 MISSPELLS THE NAME "RICHARD TEITLELBAUM", MISSING E. Source typo, not a variant spelling.

C23. TWO DIFFERENT NUMBERED MEV SYMPHONIES, DO NOT CONFLATE: Symphony No. 106, 2017 album and 50th-anniversary tour, National Sawdust and Big Ears, and Symphony No. 107 - The Bard, recorded live at Bard in 2012, edited by Matt Sargent October 2022, released posthumously 2023, Bandcamp best contemporary classical 2023.

C24. NO GUARDIAN OBITUARY WAS FOUND. Searches for a Guardian obituary returned no Guardian piece, only Wikipedia, the funeral home, NPR mirrors and RA. The brief's parenthetical "(NYT, The Guardian, etc.)" does not establish that either paper ran an obituary; the NYT quote in the Bard release, "the most creative improvising synthesizer soloist in contemporary music", is a critical quote, not an obituary. Cite NPR, RA and the funeral home instead.

C25. THE INTERVIEW PDF CARRIES NO DATE IN THE FILE. Internal evidence places it c. 1975-76: Threshold Music described as current, York University period, "two years ago we lived north of Toronto", Tai Chi piece "last year", shakuhachi piece "last year", Moog owned "almost ten years", so after c. 1976 if acquired 1966-67, a Canada tour planned "in the spring". Do not cite a precise interview date.

C26. PIANO PLUS, 2013: NPR IS THE ONLY SOURCE READ THAT NAMES IT, "a 2013 album titled Piano Plus, featuring Rzewski, Ursula Oppens and Aki Takahashi as well as himself". No label, catalogue number or track list was found in sources read. Treat as an album credit; verify the label before citing details.

C27. NO CALARTS OR YORK APPOINTMENT WAS FOUND. Bard is the teaching post, 1988-2019. York University, Toronto is where the Asparagus material was recorded, 1975-76, and where Rosenboom ran the Laboratory of Experimental Aesthetics; it is not a Teitelbaum post. No CalArts connection appears in any source read. Do not claim either.

C28. GOLEM SATELLITE TITLES, Golem Sketches, Golem I, Golem Studies, Golemics, ARE LISTED IN THE ALLMUSIC BIO AS PART OF THE GOLEM SERIES; NO SOURCE READ DATES OR DESCRIBES THEM INDIVIDUALLY. Name them only as satellites of the opera.

C29. ORGAN MUSIC VENUE CONFLICT, UNRESOLVED: NPR says the Milan performance took place "in an auditorium in Milan with speakers surrounding the audience"; the Moog Foundation says Organ Music, 1968, was "performed at the Le Petit Pharmaceutical Co. in Turin, Italy". These may be two different performances of the same piece. The study says "Milan" for the surround-sound performance NPR describes and does not assert Turin as the premiere.

C30. THE (1966-74) IN NPR'S CITATION OF THE IN TUNE PAPER IS THE PAPER'S OWN COVERAGE RANGE, "Some Early Experiments in Biofeedback Music (1966-74)", NOT A PUBLICATION DATE. The chapter was published 1976. Do not write that the paper was written in 1966-74 as a publication fact without the qualifier.

C31. JAPAN FULBRIGHT YEAR VARIES SLIGHTLY: Wikipedia says "In 1976 and 1977, another Fulbright fellowship allowed Teitelbaum to travel to Japan"; the official bio says "In 1977 he spent a year in Tokyo". Give as 1976-77.

C32. "HIUCHI-ISHI", DENON JAZZ, 1978, APPEARS IN WIKIPEDIA'S DISCOGRAPHY WITH NO DESCRIPTION IN ANY SOURCE READ. Do not describe its contents, forces or mechanism. It is plausibly a shakuhachi record; that is a guess, not a fact.

C33. "SPACECRAFT" WITH A CAPITAL C VS "SPACECRAFT" WITH A LOWERCASE C: SOURCES USE BOTH. DRAM and the Moog Foundation use SpaceCraft, capital C, in the piece title and in the MEV 40 track list; Brouse, Discover and the interview-era texts use Spacecraft. This study uses SpaceCraft and notes the variant once.

C34. "THE MEMBER WHO MADE MEV ELECTRONIC", DOORWAY CLAUSE 4, IS CORRECTED IN THE DOORWAY TEST ABOVE: Bryant's homemade synth and the amplified junk predate the Moog, and improvisation was MEV's program, not a lack. See D3-D4.
