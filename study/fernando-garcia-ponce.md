# Fernando Garcia Ponce: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-04. ig-artist-study #328.

**Site:** Fernando Garcia Ponce (Merida, Yucatan, 25 August 1933 - Mexico City, 11 July 1987, age 53, heart attack in his workshop), Mexican painter of the Generacion de la Ruptura, younger brother of the writer and critic Juan Garcia Ponce. Architect by training (UNAM 1952-56, quit one year short of finishing; some 50 buildings 1961-1969 in Merida and Mexico City) who became the Ruptura's strictest geometric hand without ever writing a program or a manifesto. First solo April 20, 1959 at the Galeria de Arte Mexicano: 26 cubist-analytic still lifes. Abstract turning point "No. 1" in 1960. First prize at the Salon de Artistas Jovenes (Salon ESSO), February 1965, shown at the Museo de Arte Moderno, Lilia Carrillo second with "Seradis" (1963); the award ceremony ended in a physical confrontation, the verdict defended by Jose Luis Cuevas, Arnaldo Coen, and his brother Juan, who was nearly lynched and had white wine thrown in his face. Married Denise Brosseau (previously married to Alejandro Jodorowsky) on 27 April 1972; son Esteban Garcia Brosseau. Posthumous: the Museo de Arte Contemporaneo Ateneo de Yucatan (MACAY) inaugurated 1994 carrying his name; the MAM retrospective "Un impulso constructivo" (2012, 45 works, cur. Daniel Garza Usabiaga); "La no-pintura" catalog (2003); "Un impulso creativo" at Felguerez's Zacatecas museum (2015). Identity anchors: the attraction of the void; geometry drawn and then un-drawn; the torn fragment as the unit of construction; the crossing-out as a mark; 3 or 4 colors, dirtied by black; the architect who changed his client from the city to the canvas.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Lilia Carrillo study: from the Ruptura's only woman to the painter who took the ESSO from her. He beat her 1963 "Seradis" at the Salon ESSO in February 1965, jury including his own brother Juan and Rufino Tamayo, and the scandal was precisely that the first and second prizes went to two non-figurative painters. The doorway corrections travel with the hop: the official name was the Salon de Artistas Jovenes (ESSO was the sponsor, with the OEA and INBA); the year is 1965, not 1964 and not 1966; he died at 53, not 54; his first exhibition was 20 April 1959, not 1956; and there was never a "concrete-painting program" - the constructive label is a 2012 curatorial frame, the real engine is Juan's phrase "la atraccion del vacio". #327 ended with the human kept inside the field and white dissolution as destination; #328 is the painter who composed the emptiness first, tore his sources instead of cutting them, and crossed out Siqueiros himself.

**Depth:** 16 sources read end to end (Juan Garcia Ponce's full essay from Nueve pintores mexicanos via the official site download; Valeria Vandder's full "La no-pintura" 2003 catalog text; the MPBA exhibition text for "Lilia Carrillo. ## M1. La atraccion del vacio

Juan Garcia Ponce: the painter is dominated by "la atraccion del vacio y la tendencia natural del artista a luchar contra el"; color and form are "puros instrumentos al servicio del impulso hacia la creacion"; the finished works are "espacios vivos... iluminada interiormente" by a captured spirit. The viewings prove the void as a compositional element, not a ground: the Artsy collage pins a dense central cluster of torn fragments into a vast black margin; carousel slide 9 centers a tiny collage in a big plywood field; slide 10 hangs a narrow column on a gray field with drips. He composes the emptiness first, then populates it. Against the chain: Carrillo (#327 M12) let the white dissolve the forms; Garcia Ponce defends the void, keeps it empty, and the fragment is its tenant. **Takeaway:** Draw the emptiness as the figure; the fragment is the guest. Watch the margin: when the empty field is doing the composition's work, the mechanism is on.

## M2. Cuadriculas difuminadas

MACAY 2012: "ordenamiento geometrico, a la manera de cuadriculas difuminadas"; Juan Acha: the planes "se desgeometrizan y se ligan por textura y por color", linked to action painting and European/US informalism. Carousel slide 2 shows it literal: strict horizontal bands (deep blue / gray-white / black) with rough bleeding edges and white drips at the boundaries; the grid is drawn, then un-drawn by the hand. Slide 4 hardens one band to black while the cream and sage beside it stay soft. Against the chain: Rojo (#326 M5) used the geometry set as a language and kept it crisp; Garcia Ponce draws the rule and then breaks it with the wrist. **Takeaway:** Geometry as intention, informalism as execution. Watch the edge: when a band bleeds exactly where it should be straight, the mechanism is on.

## M3. The diagonal against the right angle

Mariana Frenk-Westheim: "no es un geometrista ortodoxo y nunca lo fue... imposible imaginarse... capaz de limitarse al angulo recto"; Salvador Elizondo: he takes up "la inopinada diagonal, tradicionalmente el patrimonio geometrico de la pintura figurativa... las filosas aristas se atenuan". Viewed: slide 8's taped strip running lower-left to upper-right across the red ground, carrying printed text columns; the '78 serigraph's black diagonal wedge cutting the vermilion field. MACAY 2016 describes his late works as "estructuradas por octogonales atravesadas por elementos de la diagonal y fisuras agresivas". Against the chain: Rojo's Ts were vertical-and-horizontal in perfect balance (#326 M1); Garcia Ponce's signature move is the escape from perpendicularity. **Takeaway:** Give the composition one licensed diagonal and let it break everything. Watch the angle: when exactly one line refuses the right angle, the mechanism is on.

## M4. Restricted palette, dirtied color

Armando Torres Michua: "no existen muchos colores, 3 o 4 por lo regular, mas la variedad de tintes, de tonos, el ensuciado del color (por medio del negro)". Every viewed work obeys: ultramarine, black, white, red, ochre, sage recur in rotation; slide 3's red ground carries white flecks of dirt; the early "Grafismo sobre ocre" is ochre/teal/black. The official-site quotations page records his own line: "Elegi la abstraccion porque considero que el color y la forma son suficientes para hacer pintura." Against the chain: Carrillo's palette swung between white and black to catch a red (#327 M6); Garcia Ponce's red is one of four workers, and black is the water everything is washed in. **Takeaway:** Opulence from few colors; dirt is a color. Watch the palette: when three colors plus dirt do the whole painting, the mechanism is on.

## M5. Collage as compositional strategy

MACAY 2012: "El collage opera en estos cuadros, tambien, como estrategia de composicion o como un principio articulador. En estas obras de los setenta, donde desaparece la gestualidad asociada con el informalismo, Garcia Ponce empieza a ensamblar sus cuadros." The fragment replaces the brushstroke as the unit of construction. Viewed: slide 6's torn strips ARE the composition, a jagged horizontal band of pink, blue, red, white, black, ochre over a dark ground; slide 4's pasted printed column ("JUGOS"-like embossing) structures the right side. Against the chain: Carrillo disintegrated the fragment until it nearly disappeared (#327 M2); Garcia Ponce assembles with it, leaves it visible, and the assembly IS the painting. **Takeaway:** Build the painting out of pre-made matter; the fragment is a brushstroke that already has a past. Watch the construction: when the pieces are assembled rather than painted, the mechanism is on.

## M6. Tear, don't cut

MACAY 2012: fragments "cortados de una forma poco meticulosa o minuciosa, pareciera como si hubieran sido arrancados de las paginas de las publicaciones"; Vandder: "orillas rasgadas". Viewed: slide 6's torn strip edges, slide 7's gray plane with the torn bottom edge, the Artsy collage's torn newspaper. Precision lives in placement, not in cutting; the torn edge is itself a drawn line. Against the chain: Rojo's cut-and-paste began at age four with scissors and glue (#326 M10); Garcia Ponce's scissors stopped at the edge, and his hand does the rest. **Takeaway:** The torn edge is a line drawn by physics. Watch the edge: when it is rough and exactly right, the mechanism is on.

## M7. Paint over the collage

MACAY 2012: "La aplicacion de la pintura sobre los collages tambien es atrevida y generalmente aparece como manchas, brochazos y goteos." The two media trade jobs: paper is drawn, paint is pasted. Viewed: slide 8's aggressive black slashes laid across the pasted papers; slide 10's gray drips running down the field; the Artsy collage's paint worked over and around the fragments. Against the chain: Carrillo buried fabric under paint and let the weave hold the picture (#327 M11); Garcia Ponce leaves the paper visible and hits it with the brush. **Takeaway:** Glue the paper, then attack it with paint. Watch the hierarchy: when the paint is over the paper but the paper still wins, the mechanism is on.

## M8. La tacha

Carlos Garcia Ponce (MACAY 2013): in the 80s Fernando pasted a reproduced Siqueiros painting and "le ponia una tacha", pasted the Museo de Antropologia building and blurred it; the official archive holds a work titled "Tache" (/obra/91). Viewed: "Tache" itself, a tall thick black vertical mass dominating an ochre ground with pasted printed columns at its right; slide 8's black slashes crossing the diagonal strip; slide 10's big blue-gray X laid over a printed photo at the bottom. Negation as a mark: his own private Ruptura against reproduction itself. Against the chain: Rojo's negation was systematic, each T negating the previous (#326 M2); Garcia Ponce's tacha is personal and violent, a hand crossing out what it pasted. **Takeaway:** Cross out what you put down; the refusal is part of the work. Watch the mark: when a black X reads as the painting's subject, the mechanism is on.

## M9. Octogonals crossed by diagonals and aggressive fissures

MACAY 2016: "composiciones claramente estructuradas por octogonales atravesadas por elementos de la diagonal y fisuras agresivas". The '78 serigraph shows the mode: vermilion-red field with a large circular arc in a second red, a white vertical band at left, a wide black band with a torn edge, a gray folded plane with a sharp black triangular cut - even the most geometric works are built to be broken by the diagonal. Against the chain: spite's shaders break grids computationally; Garcia Ponce breaks them with the wrist and the tear. **Takeaway:** Build the most ordered thing in the room, then let the diagonal attack it. Watch the geometry: when it looks engineered to fail, the mechanism is on.

## M10. Studio life as subject

Vandder: unlike Schwitters' street trash, "uso la vida de su estudio como tema principal, haciendo de sus collages sucesos cotidianos"; the materials were "todo lo que en su estar cotidiano le era familiar". Viewed: slide 6's Italian magazine ad and machine photo (the Paris/Barcelona desk, 1976-78, when unfamiliar paint tubes forced the collage turn); slide 9's plywood and small collage (the workshop wall). The collage is a diary of the desk, not a window on the street. Against the chain: Rojo's memory was material too, but his was the civil-war childhood painted in 2019 (#326 M13); Garcia Ponce's diary is yesterday's desk. **Takeaway:** Collage the room you are standing in. Watch the source: when the paper is studio trash, the mechanism is on.

## M11. Typography as matter

Vandder: from the Italian futurists he took printed and handwritten words, letters, meaningless numbers for "un toque de cartel publicitario". Viewed: slide 4's embossed printed column; slide 6's Italian ad text ("alta tecnologia costruttiva..."); slide 8's text columns on the diagonal strip; "Tache"'s newspaper columns. Words used as matter, not message: legibility optional, texture mandatory. Against the chain: Rojo's Escrituras built a whole secret alphabet that refuses to resolve (#326 M6); Garcia Ponce's letters stay worldly, they advertise, they label juice. **Takeaway:** Set the type as if it meant nothing; let the grain mean everything. Watch the text: when you can read it but shouldn't, the mechanism is on.

## M12. Placement precision

Carlos Garcia Ponce (MACAY 2013): in Barcelona, Carlos accidentally moved one piece of an unfinished collage; Fernando "se quedo parado unos cinco minutos hasta que la alineo como estaba anteriormente". Vandder: "Fernando corta, elige, cambia y pega y, en ocasiones, arranca y vuelve a comenzar. Para el, la obra es el resultado de un dialogo con el medio." Improvisation with a memory of place: the hand remembers where the fragment belongs. Against the chain: Carrillo's meditation-then-swiftness fired the gesture once (#327 M8); Garcia Ponce rehearses the placement until it is inevitable, then commits. **Takeaway:** Improvise, but remember every position. Watch the process: when a fragment is replaced to the millimeter, the mechanism is on.

## M13. The frame is collaged too

Viewed: slide 3's masking-tape-colored border holding the "image"; slide 9's bright blue painted border on plywood (a window frame on wood); slide 7's corrugated-cardboard column as a frame element at the painting's edge; Teresa del Conde's materials list includes "carton corrugado". The support performs; the boundary between image and mount is one of his working materials. Against the chain: Rojo's book designs were images held by pages (#326 M8); Garcia Ponce makes the page itself part of the picture. **Takeaway:** Let the mount do pictorial work. Watch the border: when the frame is glued on, the mechanism is on.

## M14. Classic order; feeling cedes to reason

Juan Garcia Ponce: "el unico sentimiento que se expresa es el de la razon, el de la forma dominada y sometida a una estricta voluntad creadora"; "negando las apariencias... lo que esta pintura nos entrega es otra pura apariencia"; "el artista se hace a un lado, desaparece del cuadro". Viewed: "Domus", the white panel as a house containing the black loops, blue and red strips inside, the gesture living as a tenant within strict walls; slide 2's banded geometry. Emotion under house arrest. Against the chain: Rojo's paintings also held secrets, but his were alphabets and rain (#326 M6, M7); Garcia Ponce's secret is the absence of the artist. **Takeaway:** Submit the feeling to the form; the strictest canvas holds the deepest. Watch the artist: when he is gone from the picture, the mechanism is on.

## M15. Saturacion atmosferica

Vandder: "En los ochenta dio una nueva direccion a sus collages. La yuxtaposicion especial y la cuadricula cubista se abandonaron en favor de una nueva estructura... 'saturacion atmosferica', de elementos de su vida cotidiana: alfombras, linoleums, cartones, catalogos, sin dejar un espacio sin trabajar"; pasted elements reached "aun mayor libertad de colocacion". Viewed: the Artsy work's dense central cluster saturating the middle of the void, linoleum-pattern paper beside checkerboard beside dark arch photo. Late work fills the void he once courted; the void loses, the desk wins. Against the chain: Carrillo's late work dissolved toward white (#327 M12); Garcia Ponce's late work saturates toward clutter. **Takeaway:** Court the void for twenty years, then fill it. Watch the density: when no space is left unworked, the mechanism is on.

## M16. Architectural thinking

Museo Amparo: he approached "el lienzo en blanco como un espacio que adquiere sentido a traves de la forma"; the 2012 retrospective showed perspectives with Alain Lipkes where "superficies transparentes se relacionan con planos y volumenes solidos... pintados de distintos colores". Viewed: the Hotel Maya Hilton rendering (modernist glass tower on pilotis, the architect period, mid-1950s); "Domus", the panel as house; slide 9, the blue border as window. He never stopped being an architect; he changed the client from the city to the canvas. Against the chain: Barragan built the city with planes of color; Garcia Ponce built the painting like a building. **Takeaway:** Treat the blank canvas as an empty lot. Watch the plan: when the composition reads as a floor plan, the mechanism is on.

## The engine in one paragraph

The void is the client and the diagonal is the contractor. He draws the geometry a building would need - the grid, the right angles, the plan - then hires the tear and the diagonal to break it: blurred grids (M2), the torn fragment (M6), paint laid over collage (M7), the crossing-out (M8). The fragments come from his own desk, never the street (M10); they are torn, not cut, and their placement is remembered to the millimeter (M12); the words in them are texture (M11); the frame itself is glued on (M13). Color is restricted to three or four workers dirtied by black (M4) because, as he said, "color and form are sufficient to make painting." The architect's training never leaves: the canvas is a space made sense by form (M16), the panel is a house (M14), the void is composed first (M1), and in the 80s the void finally loses to the saturating desk (M15). Feeling cedes to reason, the artist steps aside, and what remains is a rigorous, torn, crossed-out space that glows from inside.

## Evolution

1944 the family settles in Mexico City (age 11); 1951 "Autorretrato" as pastime, a young man with a brush jar (viewed); 1952 UNAM architecture plus Enrique Climent's workshop ("no ensenaba, solo pintaba", recognized as his maestro); 1956 geometry increases, Europe with Alain Lipkes, decides to quit architecture one year short; 1957 painting only. 20 April 1959: first solo, Galeria de Arte Mexicano, 26 oil still lifes, cubist-analytic in the Juan Gris line (viewed "Grafismo sobre ocre"). 1960 "No. 1": the abstract turning point; collages begin; honorable mention, Salon de la Plastica Mexicana. 1961 the Galeria Juan Martin nucleus concentrates (Carrillo, Felguerez, Rojo, Gironella, von Gunten, Coen, Corzas, Gabriel Ramirez). 1963 first fully abstract show, 14 works, Galeria Juan Martin. 1964 Casa del Lago retrospective; MAM inaugural contemporary show. February 1965: Salon de Artistas Jovenes (Salon ESSO) first prize; Carrillo second; jury includes his brother and Tamayo; the verdict defended amid a riot; IV Bienal de Jovenes, Paris; appears in Jodorowsky's "Tajimara" (from Juan's story) with Felguerez and Maka Strauss. 1966 "Mexico: The New Generation" (Portland, Nebraska, Texas); Confrontacion 66. 1968 Nueve pintores mexicanos (ERA): Fernando one of the nine with Carrillo, Felguerez, Corzas, Gabriel Ramirez, von Gunten, Coen, Gironella, Rojo, Hector Garcia portraits; presentation at Galeria Juan Martin photographed with Fernando and Lilia among twelve others; Salon Independiente I. 1969-70 Salon Independiente II and III; Osaka 70: mural for the Mexican pavilion (with Carrillo, von Gunten, Icaza, Nissen, Felguerez, Vlady, Coen, Aceves Navarro, Corzas, Peyri). 27 April 1972 marries Denise Brosseau; son Esteban. 1973 Palacio de Bellas Artes retrospective 1962-1972. 1974 the "voluntad de sintetizar el frio ordenamiento geometrico" (MACAY). 1976-78 Paris and Barcelona with Denise and Esteban: Poligrafa and Vallirana workshops, 10 serigraphs and 12 lithographs; unfamiliar paint tubes force the collage turn (Vandder). 1978 MAM show of 40 works from 1977-78. 1980s: newspaper collage, "saturacion atmosferica", the tacha works. 11 July 1987 dies in his workshop, heart attack, 53. Posthumous: MACAY 1994; "Vida, formas y muerte de pintor" 1997; "La atraccion por poblar el vacio" 2002; "La no-pintura" 2003; "Un impulso constructivo" MAM 2012; Cabanas 2013; "Un impulso creativo" Zacatecas 2015; San Luis Potosi 2016 (99 pieces); Museo Arocena "El mundo grafico" 2016-17.

## Load-bearing corrections (11)

1. ESSO YEAR. 1964 (Artsper, Yucatan Magazine) and 1966 (the family-site PDF) are wrong. Correct: February 1965 - MPBA exhibition text, El Universal MAM history, Montero Fayad 2022, Fuente's book. The family-site error travels with the study as a warning: even the artist's own archive misfiles the prize.
2. AGE AT DEATH. 54 (Artsper, Yucatan Magazine, MACAY-derived text) is wrong. Born 25 Aug 1933, died 11 Jul 1987: 53.
3. FIRST EXHIBITION. 1956 (lot-art citing Borras) is wrong. Correct: 20 April 1959, Galeria de Arte Mexicano.
4. ALAIN LIPKES. cultura.gob.mx prints "Alian Lipkes"; he was Alain Lipkes, Frenchman of Ukrainian origin, architecture partner.
5. WIFE'S DEATH. The MACAY text derived from the official site says Denise died "a year after" the 1978 MAM show (i.e. 1979), "fallece tragicamente"; es.wiki/Denise Brosseau says 2 April 1986, Montreal. UNRESOLVED. Needs the Borras monograph or the 2012 catalog. Both dates are recorded here; neither is picked.
6. JUAN MARTIN NUCLEUS. The seed list (Carrillo, Rojo, Cuevas) was wrong. Per Emerich (1961): Carrillo, Felguerez, Garcia Ponce, Gironella, Rojo, von Gunten, Coen, Corzas, Gabriel Ramirez. Cuevas is NOT in it; his gallery path was separate. Arocena's shorter version: Carrillo, Felguerez, Rojo, Garcia Ponce.
7. NO CONCRETE-PAINTING PROGRAM. The seed's "concrete-painting affinities" and "system-like serial mode" refine to: no manifesto, no program. His own line: "Elegi la abstraccion porque considero que el color y la forma son suficientes para hacer pintura." "Constructive" is Garza Usabiaga's 2012 curatorial frame. Acha's desgeometrization is the accurate technical descriptor. His seriality is numbered compositions and graphic portfolios, not a declared system.
8. WEDDING DATE. Married Denise Brosseau 27 April 1972, not 1976; the 1976 trip was the Paris/Barcelona sojourn with wife and son.
9. ARTSDOT'S "COMPOSICION DE TRES CIRCULOS". Confabulated; not independently verified. Do not cite.
10. "TMFXY SOBRE ROJO, 1982" (Artsy crawl). Garbled extraction; the image was viewed but its title is unreliable. Cite the image, not the title.
11. IIFILOLOGICAS "patrocinado por Mobil Oil... cerca del ano de 1964". Approximate and wrong: ESSO Mexicana plus OEA plus INBA, February 1965 (Fuente, El Universal).
