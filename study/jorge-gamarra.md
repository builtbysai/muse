## The life: the self-taught stonemason of the sphere

Jorge Gamarra was born in Buenos Aires in 1939, into a family of makers: his grandfather was a cabinetmaker in Tigre, his father made his own furniture. His father blocked Fine Arts, so he went to industrial school instead -- trades, drawing, hand work -- which he credits as decisive: the hand learned before the eye. At nineteen he was a model-maker in Mendoza. He has worked in the same studio since 1981, a converted butcher's shop, and he makes a maquette of every work before committing to the full piece. No death record was found in any source read; treat him as living.

The career falls into five phases, and Herrera stresses the continuity: the same programme, written in 1973, runs through all of them. (1) Wood, first forms, 1965 to about 1970: De los petalos (W01, 1965), a 25 cm wooden sphere, the only sculpture in his own house, the seed of fifty years. (2) Acrylic and optical-kinetic, early 1970s to 1976: spheres and lenses (W03), abandoned around 1976 because the material refused; the Horno (W02, 1972) as the social exception in the middle of it. (3) Hard dark woods, late 1970s to 1990s: lapacho, caoba, guayacan, quebracho; Premio Palanza 1981; Gran Premio de Honor, Salon Nacional, 1996. (4) Line in space and monumental geometry, 1990s to 2000s: Arco, Torsion, Piedra Sinuosa (W09-W11); stone from about 2000. (5) Tools, fossils, stone, 2000s to the present: Fosiles, Herramientas, Plomada, Compas exterior, public monuments. Annual drives through the Atacama to Iquique feed the late stone imagery.

The 1973 credo, his own words: "Simple forms, organic or similar to primitive human realisations. Surface treatment always balanced with form. Controlled sensuality. Forms tending to the perfect sphere. Forms remaining from the perfect sphere. Opposition -- equilibrium: technological material / forms derived from the living." The masters he names: Brancusi above all -- "always Brancusi", an "eliminator of details" -- then Paparella, Iommi, Girola, Badii, Heredia, Kosice. On Grippo: "Lo de Victor Grippo me parecia buenisimo, fuimos muy amigos... pasabamos mucho tiempo hablando de los oficios que se han perdido... Y hablabamos muchas horas de las herramientas" -- the tool theme was shared ground between them. Critic Ricardo Martin-Crosa: his minimal forms are "obstinately quiet, silent, and, by this condition, enriching experiences of contemplation." Herrera: each work "is installed as founder of a genealogy, of a conceptual series" -- one idea, exhausted in variants.

## M1 -- The sphere that remains

Forms tending to the perfect sphere, and forms remaining from it -- the 1973 credo, and the one idea the whole career keeps returning to. W01 De los petalos (1965) is a 25 cm wooden ball, the seed: every later form is a subtraction, a pressure, or a remainder of that ball. W03 Esferas (1973-2012) carries the sphere into acrylic, lenses that gather and throw light. W18 Circulos Opuestos (about 2015) grows it to monumental stone circles in blue Labrador granite. The sphere is not a shape he uses; it is a gene he keeps expressing, and the work is what survives the pressure applied to it.

**Takeaway:** one perfect sphere as a gene; every later form a subtraction, a pressure, or a remainder. A piece that shows a sphere without showing what was taken away from it has the shape and not the mechanism.

## M2 -- Action on matter: making the rigid behave soft

"Hacer decir al material lo que parecia imposible a si mismo" -- make the material say what seemed impossible to itself. W04 Impronta (1980) and W08 Impronta, second version (1996, quebracho and steel, 200 by 30 by 25 cm): a pressed prism, "a different back appears" -- the wood takes an imprint as if it were wax. W05 Inclusion (1984) sets one material inside another. W07 Desgarramiento (1995, guayacan, 120 by 40 by 15 cm) tears what should not tear. Cilindro Mineral, undated and the highest-priority gap in this study, is a granite column bent as if by hand. W09-W11 carry the same action into stone. The paradox is made visible, slowly: the material does what its nature forbids, and the viewer watches it happen.

**Takeaway:** a material that should not bend, bending -- the paradox made visible, slowly. A piece that bends soft clay has missed the point; the material must resist, and lose.

## M3 -- Optical-kinetic acrylic: light as material

In the early 1970s the sphere became a lens. W03 Esferas (1973-2012) are acrylic spheres and lenses in which the sculpture is what the light does through them -- gathered, inverted, thrown across the room. The work is not the object; it is the light the object moves. And the phase ended around 1976 because the material refused: acrylic would not do what he asked of it, so he left it. A phase that ends in refusal is part of the mechanism, not a failure of it -- the material gets a vote, and sometimes it votes no.

**Takeaway:** the sculpture is what the light does through it, and the work ends when the material refuses. A piece with a glowing lens that never changes has the glow and not the negotiation.

## M4 -- The fossilised tool: homo faber monumentalised

The late work's great subject is the tool, grown to monument size and buried as a fossil. The seed is a river pebble found in 1970, enclosed in a fitted wooden niche "like a jewel" -- a found thing given a setting, the first fossil. From it come the Fosiles series (Fosil II, Tronzador, Compas), W16 Sin titulo (serie Herramientas, 2013), W13 Plomada (2012, steel, wood and slate, 30 by 30 by 11 cm) and W20 Pendulo (about 2019, second version 2025, guayacan and steel), where the plumb-line marks the exact centre, and W14 Compas exterior (2012, basalt and found object). W19 El Cincel (public series, Martinez, 2017) is the thesis at full scale: a bronze chisel about ten metres tall, 3,000 kg, four placements. The tool is not depicted; it is invented as an imprint, a fossil of work that never happened, and then set up in public as if it had always been there.

**Takeaway:** tools grown to monument size and buried as fossils. A piece that shows a big hammer has the scale and not the idea; the tool must read as something dug up, not something built.

## M5 -- The line in space

From the 1990s on, the line is the main theme -- the OSDE retrospective's nucleus is literally titled La linea en el espacio. Ondulaciones and Ascension draw the line in stone; W09 Arco (2002, granite, 180 cm), W10 Torsion (2003, grey Mara granite, 140 by 130 by 40 cm) and W11 Piedra Sinuosa (2003, black Zulu granite) bend it; W15 Cinta (2013, public, UNSAM) and W18 Circulos Opuestos carry it to public scale. The line never closes. It is drawn by a stone, not on one -- the material is the drawing, and the drawing is the material's own weight and curve.

**Takeaway:** a single line, drawn by a stone, that never closes. A piece whose line loops back on itself has made a shape; the mechanism needs the open end.

## M6 -- The useful sculpture: form-function confluence

Herrera's phrase for the Horno is "the perfect confluence between form and function", and Gamarra calls the late pieces "esculturas para usar" -- sculptures to use. W02, the Horno (1972), is the proof: an oven that bakes and feeds, judged by whether it works, not how it looks -- and it worked for one day before the Municipality shut the show down. W12 Cedro y Canamo (2004, cedar and hemp, 104 by 48 by 48 cm) carries the same test into the studio: a sculpture that does something. Usefulness is not a compromise of the art; it is the art's hardest test, because a sculpture that fails at its use fails outright.

**Takeaway:** a sculpture judged by whether it works, not how it looks. A piece that looks like an oven but bakes nothing is a picture of an oven; the mechanism needs the use, and the risk of failing at it.

## M7 -- The monumental public gesture

The intimate sphere grown to monument: what changes when the hand-made form becomes civic. W15 Cinta (2013, UNSAM), W18 Circulos Opuestos (about 2015), W19 El Cincel (Martinez, 2017, four placements), W17 Haz (2014, MAT), Simiente and Homenaje al productor. The maquette discipline matters here -- every work begins as a small model in the butcher's-shop studio, and the monument is the model grown, not redesigned. Scale is the only thing that changes; the form stays the one the hand made. That is why the monuments read as quiet rather than loud: they are small works, enlarged, and they keep the small work's silence in the square.

**Takeaway:** one small hand-made form scaled to ten metres in a public square -- what changes when the intimate becomes civic. A piece that designs big from the start has the size and not the growth; the monument must still carry the maquette's hand.

## M8 -- One idea, exhausted in variants

Herrera: Gamarra "agota una idea experimentando con ella en sutiles variantes y distintos materiales" -- he exhausts an idea by experimenting with it in subtle variants and different materials. Two Improntas sixteen years apart (W04, 1980; W08, 1996). Esferas reworked across four decades (W03, 1973-2012). Pendulo re-made (W20, about 2019 and 2025). The series, not the object, is the work: each variant is a question asked again in a new material, and the idea is finished only when no material is left to ask it in.

**Takeaway:** one form re-made in every material until the idea is exhausted. A piece that shows one variant has an object; the mechanism needs the series, and the sense that the next variant is already coming.

## The aesthetic logic: pressure, patience, and the hand that stays quiet

Martin-Crosa's "obstinately quiet, silent" is the key to the whole career, and it is the opposite of Grippo's. Grippo's works argue: a battery, an oven, a table, a plumb line that accuses. Gamarra's works do not argue; they endure. The career's single verb is pressure: pressure on the sphere until something remains (M1), pressure on wood and stone until they behave soft (M2), pressure on a tool until it becomes a fossil (M4), pressure on one idea until it is exhausted (M8). The means stay deliberately low-tech and handmade -- a maquette, a chisel, a river pebble in a niche -- and the artist's own biography explains why: the industrial school, the model-maker's bench at nineteen, the grandfather's cabinetmaking, the butcher's-shop studio since 1981. Craft is not a subject for the art; craft is the art's method, and the method is slow. The early work is wood and light; the middle work is dark wood and the line; the late work is stone and tools. What never changes is the refusal to illustrate: the stone really is bent, the lens really does throw light, the oven really did feed people, the chisel really does stand ten metres tall in Martinez. The aesthetic is the pressure -- a work that keeps holding its form against its own material, quietly, for decades.
