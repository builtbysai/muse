## Evolution: five phases, early vs recent

PHASE 1, APPRENTICESHIP, 1899-1906. England in the Age of Wycliffe, 1899, from the 1898 Trinity fellowship dissertation, then England under the Stuarts, 1904, then The Poetry and Philosophy of George Meredith, 1906. A medieval subject, then the seventeenth century, then literary criticism. The tools are already literary: narrative, character, landscape. The first book is critical of Roman Catholicism in favour of Wycliffe, and the Stuarts opens on country gentlemen hunting and fowling, and the Meredith study feeds the trilogy's poetic register.

PHASE 2, THE ITALIAN HEROIC PHASE, 1907-1911. The trilogy: Garibaldi's Defence of the Roman Republic, 1907, Garibaldi and the Thousand, 1909, Garibaldi and the Making of Italy, 1911. Reputation made; Garibaldi as Carlylean hero, poet, patriot and man of action; the partnership of Garibaldi, Cavour and Mazzini. The high-water mark of Whig-liberal confidence, and the phase Mack Smith later overturned. The books were translated into Italian in 1909, 1910 and 1913, and Trevelyan walked Garibaldi's marches on foot before writing them.

PHASE 3, THE ENGLISH TURN AND THE WAR, 1913-1926. The Life of John Bright, 1913, the first full English political biography after the trilogy; three years commanding a British Red Cross ambulance unit on the Italian front, 1915-1918, and Scenes from Italy's War, 1919; Lord Grey of the Reform Bill, 1920, and the James Tait Black Memorial Prize; British History in the Nineteenth Century, 1922; Manin and the Venetian Revolution of 1848, 1923, a return to the Risorgimento; History of England, 1926, the one-volume national story with its five-item meaning list. The elegiac turn, M15: the war makes him more appreciative of conservatism and less insistent that progress was inevitable.

## Aesthetic logic

Trevelyan's work is beautiful in the way a well-told story is beautiful, and that is the point. Salvemini's mechanism is the document refusing to close; Croce's is the lyric closing cleanly; Trevelyan's is the story opening outward and carrying the reader with it. His aesthetic is the march, the landscape, the portrait, the verse epigraph, and the beauty, where there is any, is in the sentence that makes a campaign feel walked and a century feel named. He writes for the general reader, he avows his bias, he walks the ground, and the walking is the style: a route followed on foot does more work than a chapter of theory, because the slope pins the story to a day when men actually tired. His colours are the colours of the English countryside and the Italian sun: green fields, white stone, the red of a Garibaldian shirt, the brown of a walked track. His motion is the motion of a column on the march and a river under the surface: the retreat backward, the progress upward, the river onward, each at its own pace. The piece that follows him should never be dry or footnoted; it should be walked, paraded, and quoted, and its one quiet moment is the older man correcting his younger self without ceremony, the disavowal that makes the whole heroic account honest.

**Takeaway:** walk it, parade it, quote it, and the piece should let the older man correct the younger without ceremony.
