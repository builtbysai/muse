# Sebastian: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-05. ig-artist-study #338.

**Site:** Enrique Carbajal Gonzalez, known as Sebastian (Santa Rosalia de Camargo, Chihuahua, 16 November 1947), Mexican sculptor. The most internationally present contemporary Mexican sculptor, author of more than 200 monumental urban works in steel and concrete in Mexico and abroad, and of the small transformable cubes those monuments are rehearsed in. Disciple of Mathias Goeritz; the youngest hand in two collective works, GUCADIGOSE in Villahermosa in 1975-1976 and the Espacio Escultorico at UNAM in 1979. Full-time researcher at UNAM since 1971; Premio Nacional de Ciencias y Artes 2015. Founder of the Fundacion Cultural Sebastian (1997) and of the Museo Sebastian in Chihuahua (1995). Identity anchors: the fold that becomes the form; the transformable cube; the gate as a civic verb; the letter as a monument; the nested arch; the animal reduced to folded plate; the monument that works; color as the material's voice; the scale jump; the collective as corridor; the city as the gallery and the car as the viewer; the polemic as part of the work; the living material; the hand that teaches.

**Doorway:** Hop from the geles-cabrera study: from Cabrera's transitable Villahermosa monument, built by five hands along a Tabasco road, to Sebastian, the youngest of those five hands, who spent the next fifty years building walkable geometry at city scale. #337 closed with a sculptor who built a monument you walk through and then lost it, so that only the photograph carries the walk. #338 opens with the man who was standing beside her on that road, the youngest of the five, and who never lost a monument because he never stopped building them: more than two hundred, in medians and roundabouts and highway nodes, each one a gate, a letter, an arch, or an animal folded out of flat steel and painted one loud color. Where Cabrera reduced the body until one curve carried it, Sebastian reduces the city until one fold carries it. The doorway is the five-hand road itself: Cabrera walked it once and it was destroyed; Sebastian walked it and kept walking, from Villahermosa to the Espacio Escultorico to every city that would give him a median.

## M1. The fold that becomes the form

The governing operation. A flat plane folded once makes a volume, and the fold is the drawing, the structure, and the image at once. He quotes Klee as his own rule: "En el salto del plano al espacio, el impacto hace brotar el volumen, la tercera dimension." The desplegables of his second solo show were paper carton folded by hand; the Caballito viewed first-hand is the same fold at 28 m in steel plate, a horse's head made of five or six bent planes, the mane a broad curved band, the muzzle a faceted wedge, the light breaking on each plane as if the steel remembered being paper. Colotl, viewed first-hand, is one continuous bent blue plate that is a scorpion because the plate bends where the scorpion bends. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M1) reduced the body until one curve carried it; Sebastian reduces the city until one fold carries it, and the fold survives the jump from paper to steel without changing its logic. Goeritz (#321) built emblems to be read against the sky; Sebastian folds the emblem out of the sky's own material, plate steel, and lets the fold do the drawing. **Takeaway:** Fold the plane once and let the fold be the form; the scale changes, the operation does not. Watch the fold: when a horse, a scorpion, and a gate are all one bent plate each, and the crease is doing the work of the contour, the mechanism is on.

## M2. The transformable cube

The laboratory where the monument is rehearsed, at hand scale. Leonardo4, Durero4, and Brancusi4, 1972, injected polyurethane, 20 x 20 x 20 cm, in editions of 500: cubes that the viewer's hands refold into new sculptures, again and again. The 4 is the fourth dimension; the cube is a mathematical model turned into sculpture, and the participation is the point. Sainz calls them "autenticos ejercicios sinfin", endless exercises, and the most valuable of his formal researches, derived from the Roseton Victoria of 1967. He still demonstrates them live at a table, in Austin in 2017 and at UNISON in 2025, his hands folding small bright cubes into sculptures "como por arte de magia" while the audience watches. The small cube and the 60 m warrior obey the same rule: the form is not fixed, it is a system of recompositions. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M4) laid one body down in sequence until it was a step; Sebastian lays one cube down in the hand until it is a monument, and the sequence is folded, not laid out. Escobedo (#335 M3) repeated one frame and let one parameter drift until a snake emerged; Sebastian repeats one cube and lets the hand drift until a city emerges. **Takeaway:** Build the monument first as something a hand can refold; if it cannot change in the hand, it will not change in the city. Watch the hands: when the artist folds the work live at a table and the audience gasps at a cube becoming a sculpture, the mechanism is on.

## M3. The gate as a civic verb

The Puerta series: a city gets a gate, and the gate becomes the city's signature. La Puerta del Sol, Chihuahua, 1998, 35 m tall and 16 m wide, takes the T shape of the doors of Paquime, the ancient ruins nearby. La Puerta de Chihuahua, viewed first-hand, is orange-red, 43 m, 500 tons, and stacks three architectural orders into one frame: the mestizaje of Chihuahua, the caserio of the Paquime pueblos, and the Spanish missions. Gran Puerta a Mexico in Matamoros marks a border; Puerta de Torreon, Puerta de Monterrey, Puerta Saturnina in Aguascalientes, Puerta del Camino Real in Colima mark the rest. The gate does not enclose anything; it announces. A gate is the one architectural form that is pure threshold, and Sebastian has built it in a dozen cities because a threshold is the cheapest way to give a city a symbol. Against the chain: Escobedo (#335) built gates you pass through as a refusal of the monument; Sebastian builds gates you drive through as the monument itself, and the gate is the work, not the refusal. Goeritz (#321) conceived the Ruta de la Amistad as a corridor experienced from a car; Sebastian builds the corridor one gate at a time, and each gate is a complete sentence. **Takeaway:** Give the city a threshold, not a statue; the gate is the signature. Watch the threshold: when a city's postcards and its road signs both carry the same frame, and the frame contains nothing but the road, the mechanism is on.

## M4. The letter as a monument

La X, Plaza de la Mexicanidad, Ciudad Juarez, 2013: one letter, 62 to 64 m tall, 800 tons, red acrylic enamel. The X is the crossing and mixing of native and Spanish cultures, and it is also a building with a hub: the construction photograph viewed first-hand shows the cylindrical hub half open, lattice visible inside one side, a solid red disc on the other; the completed photograph shows the same hub closed, a red cylinder on one side and an eye-shaped glass window on the other. The upper-left arm is a staircase of stepped blocks, so the letter is built, not drawn. A letter is the most compressed symbol available: everybody can read it at highway speed, nobody needs a plaque, and it works in every language the border speaks. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M2) stamped one letter, the T, across masks and torsos and stencils; Sebastian stamps one letter, the X, at 64 m, and the letter is large enough to contain a building. Gurria (#336 M7) reduced the figure to silhouette plus one incision; Sebastian reduces the city to a letter plus one hub, and the hub is where the building hides. **Takeaway:** Build the letter large enough to walk into; the symbol is the structure. Watch the letter: when one letter carries a whole city's name, its history, and a window, and the stepped arm shows it was assembled piece by piece, the mechanism is on.

## M5. The nested arch, repeated until it is a tunnel

Arcos del Milenio, Guadalajara, approved 1999: four yellow arches (of six planned), 52 m tall, each larger than the last, spanning the confluence of Lazaro Cardenas and Mariano Otero. The street photograph viewed first-hand shows traffic passing under the arches; the close-up shows nested yellow bands, a tunnel of curves, light and shadow striping the inner faces. Puerta de Chihuahua, viewed first-hand, works the same way in the other direction: three nested arches on the left leg that dissolve on the right into a diagonal staircase of offset blocks stepping down. Repetition with growth turns a frame into a passage: the driver does not look at the sculpture, the driver passes through it, and the sequence of arches paces the road the way a colonnade paces a plaza. The work is unfinished by design, four of six arches, and the incompleteness is part of the mechanism: the tunnel is still growing. Against the chain: Goeritz (#321) built the Ruta as a corridor of separate sculptures; Sebastian builds the corridor out of one sculpture repeated, and the repetition is the corridor. Cabrera (#337 M9) built a monument with no front view, only a path; Sebastian builds a monument that is all path, four arches in a row, and the front view is the driver's windshield. **Takeaway:** Repeat the arch with growth until the frame becomes a passage; the road completes the work. Watch the tunnel: when the arches grow as you drive and the light stripes the inner faces like a colonnade, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The animal reduced to folded plate

The horse, the scorpion, the crane, the reclining figure: each reduced until folded plate is enough. Caballito: a horse reduced to a head, the head reduced to folded planes, the planes painted yellow, 28 m, 85 tons. Colotl: a scorpion reduced to a blue arch you could walk under, 6 by 6 by 10 m, in the scrub at UNAM. Tsuru: a crane reduced to one elegant abstraction, 20 m, Kadoma, 1996, called the first sculptural haiku, a poem in one image. Chac Mol: the reclining pre-Hispanic figure reduced to repeated versions in bronze, steel, and recinto stone from 1988 onward, the same body re-cut the way Cabrera re-cut one body in eleven materials. The animal survives as geometry: the mane is a curve, the sting is a fold, the crane is a single inclined plane. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M7) migrated one body across eleven materials and let each change its behavior; Sebastian migrates one animal across folded plate and lets the fold change its species, horse to scorpion to crane, and the migration is the career. Gurria (#336 M9) built one shape with two readings, butterfly or skull; Sebastian builds one fold with many readings, horse or crane or gate, and the reading changes with the angle of the road. **Takeaway:** Reduce the animal until the fold is the animal; the species is the angle. Watch the reduction: when a horse is a head, a scorpion is an arch, and a crane is a haiku, and each still reads as the animal, the mechanism is on.

## M7. The monument that works

The sculpture earns its scale by doing a job. Caballito is a vent: the hollow yellow head is the chimney of lumbrera 26 of the deep drainage system, built to dissipate the system's decomposed vapors; Sainz calls it functional art of the first magnitude. Guerrero Chimalli's shield arm is an observation platform, 40 m long, reached by 250 steps and an elevator, holding 30 people; its plinth is a museum. La X's eye is a window. The work is not decorated infrastructure; it is infrastructure that decided to be sculpture, and the decision is visible: the vent is shaped like a horse because the city needed a horse and a vent in the same place, and Sebastian gave it both in one object. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M12) promoted the classroom material to sculpture because access is the work; Sebastian promotes the drain vent to sculpture because the city is the work, and the promotion is the same operation at the other end of the scale. Escobedo (#335 M8) engineered failure, boats designed to sink; Sebastian engineers function, vents designed to breathe, and the breathing is the work. **Takeaway:** Give the monument a job and let the job shape the form; the vent is the horse. Watch the job: when the sculpture is also the chimney, the lookout, and the museum, and removing the job would remove the form, the mechanism is on.

## M8. The color is the material's voice

Enamel acrylic in primary, pre-Columbian colors: yellow Caballito, red Chimalli and La X, orange Puerta de Chihuahua, blue Colotl. The color is not decoration on the steel; it is how the steel is read at car speed and against the sky. The photographs prove it: the yellow head breaks the blue sky into planes, the red warrior breaks the grey sprawl of Chimalhuacan into a single figure, the blue scorpion breaks the green scrub into a fold. The silver phase proves the point by subtraction: "Sebastian hablando en plata", 80 small and medium pieces in silver at the Museo Macay, the same forms unpainted, and the forms hold, which is how you know the color was a voice and not a crutch. The palette is deliberate and narrow: one color per monument, so the monument is the color. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M3) let the stone stay a stone and kept its veining loud; Sebastian lets the steel stay steel and paints it one loud color, and the color is the veining. Gurria (#336 M10) re-cut one motif in three materials and let each misbehave differently; Sebastian re-cuts one fold in one color per city and lets the city misbehave around it. **Takeaway:** Paint the monument one color and let the color be the reading; the city supplies the rest of the palette. Watch the color: when the yellow head, the red warrior, and the blue scorpion are each one color against a whole city, and the color is what you remember, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The scale jump

The same idea at 20 cm and at 60 m: the transformable cube becomes the gate, the maquette becomes the city symbol. The 1970 manifesto states the limit case: a sculpture so large the globe could barely contain it, only parts peeking out at certain points. Every monument since is a rehearsal of that unbuildable one. The La X maquette, 90.5 by 45.5 by 45 cm, shown in El Paso in 2016, is the 64 m original at hand scale; the Brancusi 4 cube, 20.5 cm, edition 211 of 500, is the gate at pocket scale. The jump is not a change of idea, it is a change of address: the same fold, the same cube, the same letter, delivered to a different scale, and the engineering follows the idea instead of preceding it. Against the chain: Gurria (#336 M8) declared the maquette finished because the monument is the maquette grown; Sebastian declares the cube finished because the monument is the cube grown, and the growth is the whole career. Cabrera (#337 M10) declared the house finished because the house is the museum grown; Sebastian declares the globe finished because the globe is the sculpture barely contained, and the containment is the joke the career keeps telling. **Takeaway:** Design the idea at hand scale and let the city scale it; the fold does not change, the address does. Watch the jump: when the same cube is a toy, a maquette, and a 64 m letter, and the idea is identical at all three sizes, the mechanism is on.

## M10. The collective as corridor

Twice the youngest hand in a shared work, and twice the corridor, not the single piece, is the work. GUCADIGOSE, Villahermosa, Tabasco, 1975-1976: Gurria, Cabrera, Juan Luis Diaz, Goeritz, and Sebastian, five plazas along a road, Goeritz the agglutinating figure, friendship, in Sebastian's words, extraordinarily well carried, as the working relation. The Espacio Escultorico, UNAM, inaugurated 23 April 1979: six artists, Federico Silva's idea, a ring of 64 triangular prisms around a lava sea from the Xitle eruption, outer diameter 120 m, inner 92.78 m, and Sebastian's Colotl in the Paseo beside Silva's Ocho Conejo, Escobedo's Coatl, Goeritz's Corona del Pedregal, Hersua's Ave Dos, and Felguerez's Variante de la Llave de Kepler. The panorama viewed first-hand shows the Paseo as it works: separate sculptures in scrub, no single front, the walk is the work. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M14) proved the same thesis from inside the corridor, as one of its five hands, and her hand is the one that made the corridor walkable; Sebastian proves it from the youngest end, and his hand is the one that made the corridor geometric. Goeritz (#321) conceived the corridor; Sebastian walked it twice, and the second walk made him. **Takeaway:** Build the ensemble from different hands; the corridor breaks if one is removed. Watch the break: when six sculptures in scrub add up to one ring and one walk, and no piece imitates another, the mechanism is on.

## M11. The city as the gallery, the car as the viewer

The monuments sit in medians, roundabouts, and highway nodes and must read in one glance from a moving car: one color, one letter, one arch, one animal. The traffic is the audience, and the work is timed to the speed of the road. The photographs keep proving it: the Caballito shot from a car window on Reforma, the Arcos shot from the highway with cars passing under, the Guerrero Chimalli shot from the avenue with a bus lane in the foreground, the La X shot with a white SUV for scale. He says it himself: he was born in a state with a large, wide landscape and makes large sculptures, but not from a pharaonic complex; urban culture needs dimension to value its surroundings. The gallery has no walls, no tickets, no opening hours; it has rush hour. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M10) built a museum in a garden because the institution would not house her; Sebastian built a museum in the median because the institution could not hold him, and the median charges nothing either. Escobedo (#335 M9) declared the maquette finished because 90 percent of her proposals stayed proposals; Sebastian's proposals became medians, and the medians outlasted the proposals. **Takeaway:** Exhibit in the median and time the work to the speed of the road; the car is the viewer. Watch the road: when the sculpture reads in one glance at 60 km/h and the glance is the whole visit, the mechanism is on.

## M12. The polemic as part of the work

The cost is always argued, and the argument is part of the work's life in the city. The 2007 Ruta Escultorica del Acero y el Cemento: he said he would take no fee, then charged the state over 17 million pesos for materials and workmen's labor. Guerrero Chimalli: about 30 million pesos in a municipality where half the population lives in some degree of poverty, and the public compared the warrior to Mazinger Z, Ultraman, Godzilla, and Transformers; he answered by comparing the reception to the Eiffel Tower's. In January 2026 the base of La Puerta de Chihuahua was repainted with a Mata Ortiz-style design without his consent, and he answered that authors' rights are sacred, like human rights. The polemic does not damage the work; it is how the work stays in the city, argued over, memed, repainted, defended. A monument nobody argues about is a monument nobody sees. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M11) made the photograph carry the monument; Sebastian makes the argument carry the monument, and the argument needs no photograph. Goeritz (#321) built emotional architecture as a sequence of spaces; Sebastian builds emotional architecture as a sequence of arguments, and the argument is the space the city actually inhabits. **Takeaway:** Let the city argue about the price; the argument is the work staying visible. Watch the argument: when the memes, the cost, and the repainting are all still about the same red warrior, and the warrior is still standing, the mechanism is on.

## M13. The living material

Esculturas cultivadas, shown at the Museo Tamayo: structures built by him and submerged in the sea for months, while marine organisms built their own habitat on them by electrodeposition; the artist builds the armature, the sea finishes the sculpture. The same logic extends off the planet: the Nanoesculturas plan, with the CIC of the Instituto Politecnico Nacional, to launch ten nanometric sculptures into space with a message, art for locations beyond Earth, a plan as of 2017, not a flown work. The material is not finished when he finishes it; it is finished when something else, the sea, the orbit, grows on it. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M8) suspended the form so gravity threatened it; Sebastian submerges the form so the sea completes it, and the completion is the work. Goeritz (#321) built with whatever the site gave him; Sebastian builds with whatever the sea gives him, and the sea gives him a habitat. **Takeaway:** Build the armature and let something else finish it; the growth is the sculpture. Watch the growth: when the structure comes out of the sea carrying a habitat it did not have going in, and the habitat is the work, the mechanism is on.

## M14. The hand that teaches

Five children's books on geometry; a full-time UNAM researcher since 1971, the first such appointment for an artist in any university, by his own account; demonstrations at a table where his hands fold small bright cubes into sculptures while the audience watches. "Enrique Carbajal invento a Sebastian, es parte de su creatividad y de su creacion." He calls the language "sebastino", and the pedagogy is the practice: the fold is teachable, the cube is handlable, and the monument is the lesson grown large. The teaching is not separate from the sculpture; it is the sculpture at its smallest scale, the scale where a child can hold it. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M12) chose cheap material because the student demands access; Sebastian chooses the fold because the student demands a handle, and the handle is the work. Gurria (#336 M1) kept time with a hammer; Sebastian keeps time with a child's hand folding a cube, and the beat is the lesson. **Takeaway:** Teach the fold at hand scale and the city will learn it at monumental scale; the lesson is the work. Watch the table: when a child folds the cube and the cube becomes a sculpture in their hands, and the monument outside is the same fold, the mechanism is on.

## Aesthetic logic

The career is one long demonstration that geometry is a civic language, and that a city can be addressed in it the way a person can be addressed in words. The logic runs: fold the plane once and let the fold be the form (M1); rehearse the monument as a cube the hand can refold (M2); give the city a gate, a threshold, not a statue (M3); build the letter large enough to walk into (M4); repeat the arch with growth until the frame becomes a passage (M5); reduce the animal until the fold is the animal (M6); give the monument a job and let the job shape the form (M7); paint it one color and let the color be the reading (M8); design at hand scale and let the city scale it (M9); share authorship until the corridor itself is the work (M10); exhibit in the median and time the work to the speed of the road (M11); let the city argue about the price, because the argument keeps the work visible (M12); build the armature and let the sea finish it (M13); and teach the fold at hand scale, because the lesson is the work (M14). The through-line is his own 1970 manifesto, the sculpture so large the globe could barely contain it: every monument since is a piece of that unbuildable sculpture, peeking out of the globe at a different point, a yellow head on Reforma, a red warrior in Chimalhuacan, a blue scorpion at UNAM, a red X in Juarez. That is why the same fold can be a toy, a maquette, and a city symbol: nothing is decorated, everything is reduced to the operation, and the operation is always something a hand can fold, a car can drive through, or a city can argue about.
