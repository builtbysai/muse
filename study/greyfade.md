# Study #227: greyfade (label of Joseph Branciforte)

Expansion series, cron run 2026-10-31. @greyfade_label, public, 1,260 followers, 71 posts. 46 posts accessible via instagram-cli (2024-07-24 to 2026-10-30); 20 frames visually inspected (thumbnails only for video).

## Account facts
- Handle @greyfade_label, "greyfade". Bio: "system-based works in physical & high-resolution digital forms."
- Founded 2019 by Joseph Branciforte (composer, multi-instrumentalist, engineer). Mission: process-based works, algorithmic composition, electronic and acoustic minimalism, alternate tuning systems.
- Catalog GF001-GF013; FOLIO series = hardcover book/album hybrid editions; releases deliberately NOT on streaming services (Bandcamp/direct only).

## Core engine
The compositional rule IS the artwork. Greyfade is a label-as-system: each release takes a generative algorithm, a tuning system, or a loop discipline and renders that single rule in sound, notation, cover art, book, and now playback platform. The music, the artwork, the FOLIO hardcover, the Greyfade Listen page are all outputs of the same process. This is the inverse of an album label: instead of packaging performances, the label publishes systems.

## Evolution
- 2024 (reissue + consolidate): sale cycle re-presenting early catalog. Visual grammar established: musical data printed as cover art. Permutation grid for From the Machine vol 1 (f01); mint data bars for Golub's Filters (f02); white duration-bars on linen for Kirschner's Three Cellos FOLIO (f03).
- 2025 (system deepens): Golub's Loop 7 (f04) pushes the label's acoustic-loop project into 22-EDO with two microtonally retuned Yamaha Disklaviers; Catherine Lamb x Ghost Ensemble's interius/exterius (GF010) reaches Grammy first-round voting; Branciforte exhibits the gallery installation dependent/variable in NYC.
- 2026 (live system + platform): ITERAE (GF011, Branciforte + Jozef Dumoulin) is explicitly "a live system for Rhodes + electronics," toured Europe Mar-Apr 2026; Greyfade Listen platform launches with generative playback works (Greg Davis 'new primes (generative): perrin', f18; Kirschner 'october 9, 2014', f19); Loop 8/9 (GF013, out Nov 13, 2026) adds a Max/MSP score-follower driving live electronics.

## Key mechanisms (the observed systems)
1. Exhaustive enumeration as artwork (f01, 0123): Max/MSP derives the complete harmonic vocabulary of one 4-note chromatic cell {0123}, then traverses a voice-leading spiral from the lowest voicing up one bass octave, a closed loop of ~20 min. Rhythm, tempo, dynamics all computed from the same dataset. The cover prints all 24 permutations of {0,1,2,3} as bar cells. Watchable behavior: a closed catalog being walked, nothing left out, the system completes itself.
2. Prime-tuned sine networks (New Primes): prime-number sets choose a fundamental; overtones = fundamental x prime; pieces are "snapshots of an endless generative music." Now reopened as endless generative playback on Greyfade Listen (f18). Watchable behavior: a network of pure tones breathing through octave cross-fades, time-scaling like a slow tide.
3. 22-EDO acoustic loops (Loop 7, f04): a tuning system that cannot fit one piano (11 pitch classes per pass over two retuned Disklaviers) becomes a compositional constraint; The Wire noted Golub "dropping 1/22 of a pitch with each cycle." Watchable behavior: near-identical repetition that drifts microtonally, the loop is the same but never the same.
4. The score follower (Loop 8/9): Branciforte's Max/MSP JS follower groups live input into 40ms events, 204 vertical events in Loop 7, each chord split into low/high/sub/super-high voices driving live electronics. Watchable behavior: a human performance parsed into data streams in real time, the machine keeping up with the hand.
5. Data as cover (f01-f04 house grammar): Filters' mint bars, Loop 7's columns of monospace event numbers, From the Machine's permutation grid. The cover is a rendering of the piece's dataset, not a decoration. Watchable behavior: the same data you are looking at is the data you would be hearing.
6. Digital-to-acoustic translation (RECUR, f00): Taylor Deupree's 2002 electronic microsound Sti.ll transcribed and arranged for flute/cello/bass/guitar/harp. April 20, 2015 and Three Cellos show the same move in reverse. Watchable behavior: an electronic logic made physical, the system survives the medium change.
