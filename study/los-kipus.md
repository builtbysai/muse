# Los Kipus: Study Notes

**Site:** Los Kipus (also spelled Los Quipus), Peruvian musica criolla trio founded in August 1959 in Lima by Francisco "Paco" Maceda (first guitar), Genaro Ganoza Torres (second guitar) and Carmen Montoro (first voice): two acoustic guitars, a lead female voice and Peruvian percussion, the first trio mixto of Peru, still performing 67 years later under Paco Maceda Jr., most recently at the Teatro Segura in Lima on Saturday 31 October 2026. The name is the Quechua khipu, the Inca knotted cords that recorded history, and the trio behaves like its name: the catalogue is a record kept by re-knotting, voice after voice, while the guitars stay the same. ## M1. The mixed trio as the invention

The invention is the format itself, not any one song, and the format is the mechanism. In August 1959 Paco Maceda proposes to Genaro Ganoza that they find a first female voice, Carmen Montoro is chosen, and the trio is founded as a deliberate novelty in the Lima scene, "creo un trio mixto como novedad en el medio de la farandula", the first trio mixto of Peru: two northern guitars plus one woman's voice. The mechanism is the mixture as the novelty: two men and one woman, two guitars and one voice, and the audience had not seen that shape before, and the shape is the draw, because the voice stands out against the guitars the way a single colour stands out against two greys, and the guitars hold the voice up the way two hands hold one thing. The first recording is "Por que sigues llorando" by Rafael Otero, on Iempsa, and it has little welcome in Lima, and the trio's fame does not come from the first record, it comes from the festival prizes in M13, which is the proof that the format was the bet and the songs were the payoff, and the bet was right. The career that follows is the same mechanism at every scale: the trio is still a trio mixto in 2026, Paco Maceda Jr. on first guitar with a female lead voice, Mayra Guzman at the 2020 online concert, and the format has outlived both founders, because the format was never about the three people, it was about the shape, and the shape is still the draw. Watch the invention: when two guitars and one woman's voice take the stage, and the voice is the only woman and the guitars are the only men, and the shape is the thing the audience came to see, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** invent the shape, and let the shape carry the songs. The mixture is the novelty.
