## The life: painter, then the break, then the question

Herve Fischer was born in 1941, in Paris according to Fischer himself and most biographies; the Pompidou press text says Bourg-la-Reine. He graduated from the Ecole Normale Superieure (rue d'Ulm) in 1964, defended a master's thesis on Spinoza's political philosophy under Raymond Aron, devoted his main research to the sociology of colour, and later took a doctorate in sociology at UQAM on the sociological theory of colour. He taught at ENSAD 1969-1980 and sociology of culture and communication at the Sorbonne 1971-1987 (master lecturer from 1981). (C18208, leg 1 C18001, C18002; synthesis C18151)

As a young painter he worked with Support/Surface, making handprint paintings on hand-towel cloth -- a way of deconstructing painting from inside its own materials, the handprint replacing brushwork, the domestic cloth replacing the canvas. Then came the break, under the title "Hygiene de l'art": campaigns to eradicate art of its traditional mores and media, inviting artists to send him works, tearing them up, and displaying the shreds in small plastic bags. When he stopped painting is contradicted and must not be collapsed: the UQO homage says he stops painting in the early-1970s sociological-art impetus; Fischer himself says "I personally chose to cease all traditional artistic practice in 1983"; the Pompidou text puts the end of these practices in the mid-1980s. These may describe different things -- easel painting, all traditional practice, the sociological-art phase -- but no single year may be printed. (C18209, leg 1 C18003, C18004; synthesis C18152)

Sociological art was coined in 1971; in 1971 he wrote the Manifesto of Sociological Art. The Collective itself was officially declared on 10 October 1974 with the first manifesto in Le Monde, signed Fischer, Forest and Thenot, after Michel Journiac convened founding meetings at his Ile Saint-Louis apartment in summer 1974. The Collective published four manifestos (1974-1977), ran the Ecole Sociologique Interrogative from Fischer's apartment (May 1976), staged the two-week Perpignan intervention (July 1976) and the Venice Biennale French Pavilion project (July 1976, at Pierre Restany's invitation), and ended in 1980 when Forest and Thenot published an ad selling the school and a tract declaring the Collective over. Its stated principles were critique, communication, intervention and pedagogy. (C18210, leg 1 C18005, C18006, C18007; synthesis C18153)

Fischer was a special guest at Venice 1976 and Sao Paulo 1981, and a participant in Documenta 7 (1982). He moved to Quebec in the 1980s -- sources give 1980 and 1987, and his Sorbonne post ran to 1987, so no single move year is printed; from 1985 he co-founded La Cite des arts et des nouvelles technologies de Montreal (with Ginette Major), co-founded Infographie Canada (1986), started the Telescience festival (1990) and the Multimedia International Market (1993), and founded the International Federation of Multimedia Associations (1997). (C18211, leg 1 C18008, C18009; synthesis C18154)

The digital turn came in the 1980s: "Le Chant des Etoiles", a 12-minute computer-animated film (1987), won first prize in the National Computer Graphics Association competition (1988). From 1999 he painted again as a "painter of the digital age" -- binary languages, barcodes, financial icons, in a mode the Pompidou text calls "fauvisme digital" -- with solo shows in Latin American national museums (Buenos Aires 2003, Montevideo 2004, Santiago 2006, Neuquen and Havana 2009) and a Ceret retrospective in 2010. He held the Daniel Langlois Chair at Concordia 2000-2002 (Hexagram consortium), and since 2006 has directed the International Digital Observatory at UQAM. From 2011 he has made tweet art: small digital icons posted on Twitter in place of 140 characters. He founded the International Society of Mythanalysis in 2014. The Centre Pompidou gave him a retrospective, "Herve Fischer et l'art sociologique", 15 June-11 September 2017. His latest book is Mythanalyse de la couleur (Gallimard, 2023). (C18212, leg 1 C18010, C18011, C18012, C18013, C18014, C18016; synthesis C18155)


## The works: three phases, one question

The catalogue runs W01-W20, 1971-2011 and beyond. One grade A exists (W20, Fischer's own statement read end to end); the grades below are B where a good secondary description was read end to end, C where only a bibliographic record exists. The evolution falls into three phases, and the phases are the argument: the medium changes every decade and the question does not. (C18213, leg 2 C18050-C18079; synthesis C18164, C18165)

**W01, "Essuie-mains" (hand-towel paintings), early 1970s (c. 1971-1972)** -- B. Paintings on rolls of hand-towel cloth, his handprints pressed onto the fabric, made in his Support/Surface period. The mechanism is deconstruction by literal use: the painting support is a domestic, utilitarian cloth, and the handprint replaces brushwork, so painting is taken apart from inside its own materials. (C18214, leg 2 C18050)

**W02, "Hygiene de l'art" (Hygiene of Art) campaigns, c. 1972-1974** -- B. A grouped set of campaigns aimed at eradicating art of its traditional mores and media. The "hygiene" metaphor is the mechanism: art is treated as something to be cleaned, disinfected and prophylactically protected from its own traditions, the market and the museum system. The campaign frame -- public-health language, prophylactic slogans -- is itself the artwork, not a set of objects. (C18215, leg 2 C18050)

**W03, "La dechirure des oeuvres d'art" (The Tearing-Up of Works of Art), c. 1973-1974** -- B. Fischer invited other artists to send him their works, then tore the works up and displayed the shreds in small plastic bags. The mechanism is a three-step demotion: solicitation (artists volunteer their work), destruction (tearing by hand), and display as bagged waste, turning the unique art object into packaged refuse. (C18216, leg 2 C18050)

**W04, "Dix questions sur l'art sociologique" (Ten Questions on Sociological Art), fall 1973** -- B. Critic Francois Pluchart initiated a debate between Michel Journiac, Gina Pane, Fischer and Thenot, published as "Ten Questions on Sociological Art" in artTitudes (the magazine Pluchart founded in 1971). The mechanism is interrogation as art practice: the artwork is a structured public questioning of what sociological art is, conducted in print before the movement had a manifesto or a collective. (C18217, leg 2 C18051)

**W05, "Art et communication marginale. Tampons d'artistes" (Art and Marginal Communication: Artists' Rubber Stamps), 1974 (book, Balland, Paris)** -- B. An exploration of the rubber stamp as an artistic medium: Fischer collected and analysed artists' rubber stamps, treating a cheap, bureaucratic, reproducible marking tool as a vehicle for marginal communication outside the gallery and market circuits. The project put him in touch with Argentine artists including Carlos Ginzburg, Juan Carlos Romero, Edgardo Antonio Vigo and Horacio Zabala. Note: the Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery holds a folio of the same title dated 1976, so the book (1974) and the folio object (1976) appear to be distinct editions of the same project -- the 1974/1976 discrepancy is carried, not resolved. (C18218, leg 2 C18052, C18073)

**W06, "Higiene del Museo" / "Higiene de las obras de arte" (Hygiene of the Museum / Hygiene of Works of Art), 1974 (text); video screened 1975 at CAYC, Buenos Aires** -- B. In a text for the CAYC newsletter, Fischer sets out a socio-pedagogical practice for the museum's way of showing art, and discusses the video "Higiene de las obras de arte" to be screened there. The video uses video's own technical possibilities -- disruptions, smudges, crossings out -- to demythologize artworks, which the art system reduces to simple consumer objects. A year after the text, the CAYC invited him to present a solo exhibition in Buenos Aires. (C18219, leg 2 C18053)

**W07, First Manifesto of Sociological Art, published in Le Monde, 10 October 1974** -- B. The official proclamation of the Sociological Art Collective, signed by Herve Fischer, Fred Forest and Jean-Paul Thenot. Published in France's most widely read newspaper rather than an art magazine, the manifesto claimed that the practice of art is rooted in the fundamental links connecting art, society and life, and that sociological art relies on scientific methods, approaching its art actions as a privileged sphere of research devoted to exploring social theories. The newspaper page is the work's support; mass distribution is its mechanism. (C18220, leg 2 C18054)

**W08, "Pharmacie Fischer & Co." ("Fischer & Cia Pharmacy"), 1975, streets of Sao Paulo** -- B. A pharmacy set up and operated on the streets of Sao Paulo. Fischer dispensed "pills" -- small printed, medicinal-looking units carrying artistic and critical content -- treating art as a remedy and the artist as a pharmacist. The street pharmacy literalises the "Hygiene of Art" metaphor: art as medicine, distributed to passers-by rather than sold to collectors. Documents, photographs and video testimonies of the action were shown in the 2012 MAC USP exhibition. (C18221, leg 2 C18055)

**W09, Ecole Sociologique Interrogative (Interrogative Sociological School), founded March-May 1976, in Fischer's apartment in Paris** -- B. The Collective founded a school located in Fischer's own apartment. The mechanism is institutional: instead of making objects, the artists founded a teaching structure whose curriculum was interrogation -- questioning art, society and their relations. Fischer continued working with the school after the Collective dissolved. In 1980 Forest and Thenot published an ad in Art Press "selling" the school and a tract declaring the end of the Collective -- the school's sale notice was itself the closing gesture. (C18222, leg 2 C18056)

**W10, "Study and Animation of Perpignan", July 1976, Perpignan (two weeks)** -- B. The Collective's two-week intervention in Perpignan began with a study of the conditions and needs of the town's neighbourhoods by a thirty-person interdisciplinary team, then led to dozens of direct interventions: pop-up exhibitions, screenings, interviews, parties, and a multi-neighbourhood photograph swap. The stated aim was to bring together neighbourhoods that, though geographically close, remained distant at the level of social communication. The group set out four principles -- critique, communication, intervention, pedagogy -- as the method of sociological art. (C18223, leg 2 C18057)

**W11, "Theorie de l'art sociologique" (Theory of Sociological Art), 1976 (Casterman, Paris; some listings give 1977)** -- C. Fischer's founding theoretical text for sociological art, the book-length statement of the theory behind the actions of 1971-1976. It is the work in which the practice becomes a doctrine: art as sociological research, intervention and pedagogy. No chapter-level description was found in the sources read; the entry rests on the bibliographic record. (C18224, leg 2 C18058)

**W12, "L'Histoire de l'art est terminee" (The History of Art Is Over), 1981 (Balland, Paris)** -- C. A book whose title is its thesis -- that art history, in the traditional sense of a succession of styles and masters, has ended. It marks Fischer's turn from building sociological art to declaring the old art system finished, a verdict that frames his later move toward new media. No chapter-level description was found in the sources read. (C18225, leg 2 C18059)

**W13, "Citoyens-sculpteurs" (Citizen-Sculptors), 1981 (Segedo)** -- C. A book arguing, from its title, that citizens themselves are sculptors -- that shaping society is a sculptural act open to everyone, not a professional specialism. It extends the Perpignan logic (W10) into a civic claim: the citizen as maker of the social form. No chapter-level description was found in the sources read. (C18226, leg 2 C18060)

**W14, "L'Oiseau-chat" (The Bird-Cat), 1983 (La Presse, Montreal)** -- C. A novel-enquiry (roman-enquete) on Quebec identity, written after Fischer's move to Quebec (he came to Montreal in 1980 per the 2012 Sao Paulo text -- against the UQO 1987 date; the move year is unresolved). The mechanism is the enquiry form: fiction used as a sociological instrument, with the fourth part ("Mythanalyse et societe") carrying the theoretical load. It is Fischer's first major work addressed to his adopted home, applying mythanalysis -- the analysis of social myths -- to a national identity. (C18227, leg 2 C18061; leg 1 C18009)

**W15, "La Calle, A donde llega?" (The Street, Where Does It Lead?), 1984 (Arte y Ediciones, Mexico)** -- C. A book on the street as a social and artistic space, published in Mexico. It carries forward the street-level practice of the Sao Paulo pharmacy (W08) and the Perpignan intervention (W10) into writing: the street as the place where art, society and everyday life meet. No chapter-level description was found in the sources read. (C18228, leg 2 C18062)

**W16, Marco Polo electronic novel (Franco-Canadian participation), 1985** -- B. Fischer organised the Franco-Canadian participation in the Marco Polo electronic novel project, which involved writers from Africa, Europe and Canada and was sponsored by Umberto Eco and Italo Calvino. The mechanism is networked collective writing: a novel produced across continents through electronic communication, with Fischer as the organiser and curator of the Franco-Canadian leg rather than as a single author. (C18229, leg 2 C18063)

**W17, "Le Chant des Etoiles" (The Song of the Stars), 1987 (film); first prize 1988** -- B. A twelve-minute computer animation, Fischer's first major work in the digital medium. It won first prize in the international computer animation competition held by the National Computer Graphics Association (USA) in 1988. The film marks the shift from sociological action in physical public space to image-making with computation, while keeping the cosmic/mythic subject matter that runs through his later theory (mythanalysis, the digital shock). The film itself was not viewed in any leg. (C18230, leg 2 C18064)

**W18, Digital painting ("painter of the digital age"), from 1999 onward** -- B. From 1999 Fischer worked again as a painter, now of the digital age -- making paintings with and through the computer. The work was shown in a run of solo exhibitions in Latin American national museums: Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Buenos Aires (2003); Museo Nacional de Artes Visuales, Montevideo (2004); Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes de Chile, Santiago (2006); Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Neuquen (2009); Centro Wifredo Lam, Havana, X Biennale de La Havane (2009); and a retrospective at the Musee d'Art Moderne de Ceret (2010-11). The mechanism is a return to the easel through the screen: painting reclaimed after twenty years of anti-painting actions. (C18231, leg 2 C18065)

**W19, "Mythanalyse du futur" (Mythanalysis of the Future), 2000** -- B. An open-ended interactive book of about 300-400 pages, published directly on the Internet (hervefischer.net). It applies mythanalysis to the future. The mechanism is the open text: readers navigate a branching, unfinished book online, so the work's form (hyperlinked, expandable, never closed) enacts its argument that the future is an open myth to be written collectively. (C18232, leg 2 C18066)

**W20, Tweet art / Tuitart, from 2011 onward** -- A. Fischer creates small meaningful images and posts them on Twitter in place of the 140 characters allowed, exploring philosophical and ethical questioning about art, politics and main social issues. In his own words, tweet art is "an actualization on the web of the former postal art and the New York Correspondence School of Ray Johnson, of rubberstamp art, wall's graffiti and posters, street's imaginary signalizations, pills of the Fischer Pharmacy, tags, tattoos, etc." Its icons are drawn from the digital age itself: barcodes, binary codes, the four-letter (ACGT) codes of DNA, financial diagrams. He calls it "a new step in sociological art, with its interrogative aesthetics" and a way to link fine arts and digital arts again, working "with painting and computing simultaneously" toward what he calls "digital fine arts". Grade A because Fischer's own statement was read end to end. (C18233, leg 2 C18067)

Beyond the 20: "From Art Hygiene to Society Hygiene" (2012), a new work prepared and performed at MAC USP, Sao Paulo, confronting the 1970s "art hygiene" concerns with his present ones -- the hygiene metaphor extended from art to society itself; later books Market Art (2016), L'Age hyperhumaniste (2019), Les couleurs de l'Occident (2019), Mythanalyse de la couleur (2023); and the retrospective "Herve Fischer et l'art sociologique", Centre Pompidou, Paris, June-September 2017, organised in three parts: early 1970s to mid-1980s; late 1990s to the present; digital practices and new habits. Named in the sources but not in the 20: the "Bureau d'identite utopique" (Utopian Identity Bureau), issuing utopian identity cards -- a named action with no dated work page found. (C18234, leg 2 C18069, C18070, C18071; leg 1 C18003)


## The mechanisms

**M1 -- Hygiene as art.** Art treated as something to be cleaned and disinfected of its own traditions, market and museum system, with public-health language as the artwork itself. W02 (the campaigns, where the slogan is the work), W06 (video smudges and crossings-out demythologising museum artworks). Watchable behaviour: a museum wall being scrubbed by its own medium -- the video glitch as disinfectant. **Takeaway:** Fischer does not attack art's content; he treats art itself as the patient, and the treatment -- cleaning, tearing, bagging -- is the piece. (C18235, synthesis C18156)

**M2 -- Demotion of the art object.** Solicitation, destruction by hand, and display as bagged waste, turning the unique object into packaged refuse; painting deconstructed from inside its own materials. W01 (handprints on domestic cloth instead of brushwork), W03 (artists volunteer their work, Fischer tears it up, the shreds go into small plastic bags). Watchable behaviour: a work of art arriving by post, being torn, and ending up in a bag on a shelf. **Takeaway:** the demotion only works because the artists volunteer -- the art world hands Fischer the knife and watches him use it. (C18236, synthesis C18157)

**M3 -- Interrogation as practice.** The artwork is a structured public question -- a published debate before the movement had a manifesto, a school whose curriculum is questioning, a tweet icon that asks rather than states. W04 (ten questions debated in print), W09 (a school in his apartment whose subject is questioning), W20 (the tweet icon as a question, not a statement). Watchable behaviour: a question printed, debated, taught -- and forty years later shrunk to a single icon that still asks. **Takeaway:** Fischer never answers the question; he builds the room, the school, or the icon in which the public has to answer it. (C18237, synthesis C18158)

**M4 -- Cheap reproducible media as marginal communication.** The rubber stamp, the street sign, the pill, the icon -- bureaucratic and throwaway marking tools used to communicate outside gallery and market circuits, collapsing into the tweet icon, which Fischer himself calls rubber stamp, pharmacy pill, street sign and graffiti tag at once. W05 (the stamp book, and the Argentine mail-art contacts it made), W20 (barcodes, binary codes, DNA letters as the icon's raw material). Watchable behaviour: a stamp hitting paper, a pill changing hands on a street, an icon appearing in a feed -- each one cheap, each one reproducible, each one outside the gallery's price list. **Takeaway:** the cheapest mark is the freest one: a stamp costs nothing, travels anywhere, and belongs to no collection. (C18238, synthesis C18159)

**M5 -- The mass newspaper as support.** The manifesto printed in Le Monde rather than an art magazine, so mass distribution is the mechanism; the theory book then turns the practice into doctrine. W07 (the newspaper page is the work's support -- buy the paper and you hold the artwork), W11 (the book in which the practice becomes a doctrine). Watchable behaviour: a movement announced where the news is, not where the art is. **Takeaway:** Fischer publishes the manifesto in the paper his opponents read at breakfast; the distribution is the declaration. (C18239, synthesis C18160)

**M6 -- Art as remedy on the street.** The artist as pharmacist dispensing printed "pills" to passers-by, and the street as the place where art, society and everyday life meet -- the Hygiene metaphor literalised as medicine. W08 (the Sao Paulo pharmacy, pills handed to strangers), W15 (the street as the book's subject, carrying the pharmacy forward into writing). Watchable behaviour: a man in a white coat on a pavement, handing out small printed doses to people who did not ask for art. **Takeaway:** the pill is the smallest possible artwork with a dosage -- one per person, taken on the street, no prescription and no price. (C18240, synthesis C18161)

**M7 -- Institution-as-work.** Instead of making objects, found a school in your own apartment, take over a whole town for two weeks with a thirty-person team, or organise a novel written across continents -- the structure (school, intervention, network) is the work, with critique, communication, intervention and pedagogy as its four stated principles. W09 (the school in the apartment), W10 (Perpignan: study the neighbourhoods first, then intervene), W16 (the Marco Polo novel, organised across Africa, Europe and Canada). Watchable behaviour: a town's neighbourhoods suddenly talking to each other; a novel arriving from three continents at once. **Takeaway:** Fischer's largest works have no object at all -- the object is the institution, and the institution is a machine for making strangers meet. (C18241, synthesis C18162)

**M8 -- The digital return and mythanalysis.** After twenty years of anti-painting actions, painting returns through the screen -- computer animation, digital painting, an open-ended interactive web book, tweet art -- while mythanalysis (developed with Gilbert Durand) reads the myths governing collective ideologies, from colour to the future to the Net. W17 (the prize-winning computer film), W18 (painting reclaimed through the computer), W19 (the unfinished web book), W20 (the tweet icon). Watchable behaviour: a painting made of barcodes and binary, a book that never closes, an icon that asks a question in a feed. **Takeaway:** the man who tore up paintings spent his sixties painting again -- but the paint is now code, and the canvas is the network. (C18242, synthesis C18163)

## Aesthetic logic: the question as artwork

Fischer's aesthetic logic is that art is not an object to look at but a question to answer, and the question is only real if it is asked where the public already is -- in the newspaper, on the street, in the feed. The early work destroys the object so the question can get out: tear up the painting, bag the shreds, scrub the museum wall with video static. The middle work builds the room where the question can be asked at scale: a manifesto in Le Monde, a pharmacy on a Sao Paulo pavement, a school in his own apartment, a whole town taken over for two weeks by a thirty-person team. The late work shrinks the question to its smallest possible carrier -- a digital icon the size of a stamp, posted where 140 characters used to go -- and the icon turns out to contain the whole career: Fischer himself lists what it actualises, postal art and Ray Johnson, rubber-stamp art, graffiti, street signs, the pharmacy pills, tags, tattoos.

That is why the work survives every change of carrier. A question works in a magazine debate, in a newspaper manifesto, in a school curriculum, in a pill, in a novel written across continents, in a barcode painting, in a tweet. The channel changes; the question does not. And the question is always the same shape: what is art for, who does it belong to, and what happens when the public answers instead of the museum?

The logic has a second edge the prompts must keep: Fischer is a theorist who does the theory with his hands. He does not write about hygiene and then paint pictures of it; he tears up real works and bags the shreds. He does not write about the street; he opens a pharmacy on it. The theory book (W11) comes after the actions, not before them -- doctrine as the record of a practice, not its blueprint. The one place the order reverses is mythanalysis: there the reading of myths comes first and the late paintings are its illustrations, barcodes and financial icons as the myths of the present. **Takeaway:** build the question, not the picture; put it where the public already looks, in the cheapest carrier that will hold it, and let the answer arrive by post, by pill, or by reply. (C18246, leg 2 C18067, C18069; synthesis C18164)
