# Dario T. Pie: Study Notes

**Site:** Dario Jose Pie Arnaiz (Guadalajara, Jalisco, born Monday 19 February 1962), Mexican actor and voice actor, the capocomico of Mexican political cabaret and the man behind La Ro~na, the corpse-parody of Maria Felix who has been his one character for more than thirty years. He trained at the Foro EON under Sergio Bustamante, where he coincided with Regina Orozco and Astrid Hadad, then at the CEA de Televisa, then in cabaret workshops with Jesusa Rodriguez, and he worked twelve straight years as a stage, film and telenovela actor before the mask arrived in 1993. He was nominated for the Ariel for Best Supporting Actor in 2017 for La cuarta compania (2016), and did not win: the award went to Hoze Alberto Melendez for Almacenados. He is the voice of Edna Moda in the Mexican Spanish dub of Los Increibles (2004) and Los Increibles 2 (2018). There is no English Wikipedia article on him. A note on spelling that runs through this whole file: the character is La Ro~na, with the tilde over the n, ro~na meaning scab, filth, grime. This file is pure ASCII, so the tilde is written as "~" after the o, "La Ro~na", and the same convention drops every other Spanish accent. The spelling "La Rona", plain n, is wrong, and it is the error this study exists partly to correct: it appears in the #350, #351 and #352 study-log candidate lists and in the #351 Astrid Hadad study text, and in an enpareja headline and a masradio image filename. Every primary source read in this leg spells it Ro~na, and "La Rona" is also the real nickname of a different person, Pilar Claveria, who has nothing to do with Pie.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Regina Orozco study: Orozco's fellow founder of Mexican political cabaret, the man who told the teenage theatre student "Tu eres una chava como muy sexy", you are a very sexy girl, and who shared her Foro EON classroom, her Santitos cast (1998/1999) and her founders list. The brief that sent this study called his character "La Rona", and the correction matters more here than a spelling fix usually does, because the name is the mechanism: "La Do~na", Maria Felix's nickname, dragged through the dirt letter by letter until the Do~na is a Ro~na, a scab. Orozco's line grows because the same voice keeps coming back in a different song-form; Pie's line grows because the same dead woman keeps coming back in a different title, and the man underneath never changes his act, only the room changes, from the bar El Habito to cable television to the Teatro Helenico to the Teatro de la Ciudad Esperanza Iris. He is the closest network link out of the Orozco study and the best doorway, because the 1993 birth of La Ro~na at El Habito is the moment the five founders became a genre, and because his mechanism is the interview, the living guest brought on stage to be undone by a corpse, which is what the whole chain has been circling since Rodriguez: the room, the guest, the joke that lands because the dead woman is allowed to say it.

## M1. The makeup mask as caricature

Pie's camp works through "la mascara del maquillaje", the makeup mask, and the Spanish Wikipedia article on Mexican political cabaret names the product: his are "human cartoons", painted portraits, retratos caricaturales like Mexican cartoneria, that capture the satirized person's essential form, and beauty belongs only to art. The mechanism is the face painted on, not made up: the two photographs viewed first-hand in this leg show it in one frame each. The stage shot, dtp-01, is whiteface with drawn-on arched brows, false-lash fans, pink cheek circles and an overdrawn red mouth, a black curly wig, a purple/blue ruffled gown with a jeweled necklace and rings, laughing into a microphone against black. The Sale el Sol still, dtp-03, is the same face in a gold puff-sleeve gown on a morning-show couch at 8:48 AM, the mask unchanged by the daylight and the red door behind it. The mask does not try to be Maria Felix; it tries to be a drawing of Maria Felix, and the drawing is funnier and crueller than the likeness, because a drawing keeps only the essential lines and throws the rest away. Watch the face: when the brows are drawn higher than any human brow and the mouth is drawn wider than any human mouth, and the room still reads "La Do~na" before it reads "a man in drag", the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** paint the person, do not impersonate her. The caricature is the likeness. Against the chain: Hadad (#351 M1, M2) also wore the country on her body and changed costume as the unit of the show, but her costumes were icons, cactus, pyramid, tequila bottle; Pie's mask is a single face, repainted nightly, which is why his version travels into television studios and morning shows where a costume change would need a dressing room and a face needs only a mirror.

## M2. The corpse-diva

La Ro~na is a parody of Maria Felix "con aspecto de cadaver", with the aspect of a corpse. The dead diva returns to tour Mexican history and ventilate it, history mixed with the character's fiction, and the joke is resurrection as a talk-show host: death gives her the license to say what the living cannot. The fan account calls the origin a "parodia clown" of La Do~na, and the name does the work the makeup only starts: "ro~na" is Spanish for scab, filth, grime, so "La Do~na" dragged through the dirt, letter by letter, keeps the grandeur, the gowns, the jewels, the armchair, and removes the cleanliness. The etymology reading is this study's own, from the Spanish word itself plus the parody relation; no source read states it in so many words, and it is flagged as interpretation, not quotation. Maria Felix died in 2002, nine years after La Ro~na was born, so the character outlived her model by more than two decades and kept performing her after the model was gone, which is the point: the corpse is not a tribute, it is a license. A living impersonator must flatter; a dead woman can insult. Watch the license: when the diva says the thing about a living politician that no living commentator would say on air, and the room laughs because a corpse said it, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the dead woman say it. Death is the permission slip. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M8) also staged the opera canon as a casebook of jealousy, casting the symptom not the voice, but her villains were characters in arias; Pie's villain is a real dead woman, which is why his version is a talk show and hers is a diagnosis.

## M3. The spontaneous celebrity interview

On Desde Gayola, La Ro~na interviewed real show-business artists, and the interviews were "casi siempre espontaneas", almost always spontaneous. Spontaneity is the engine; the script is the guest. The peak moment is named by Bernardo Esquinca in Letras Libres: La Ro~na interviewed the pop group OV7 and "transformo una promocion de rutina en un festin de albures y carcajadas (incluso contagio al anodino grupo pop con algo de su chispa)", turned a routine promotion into a feast of double-entendres and laughter, even infecting the anodyne pop group with some of her spark. The albur, the double-entendre wordplay, is the interview weapon: the guest arrives with a rehearsed answer and leaves having said something dirty without meaning to, and the audience's laughter is the score. The show itself was the frame that made this possible: Tierra Adentro calls Desde Gayola "un teatro de carpa jotesco televisado", a televised queer carpa theatre, and an open mic for the LGBTTTIQ+ community, cutre and kitsch, low budget, censored, badly paid, and the travestis in it, Esquinca writes, "dificilmente hubieran encontrado apoyo en otro canal", would hardly have found support on another channel. Watch the turn: when the guest stops promoting and starts defending, and the corpse is asking the questions, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** bring the guest, lose the script. The albur is the interview. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M2) also let the room build the show, but her room was the audience in seat twelve picking songs; Pie's room is the guest in the chair, which is why his version is a duel and hers is a jukebox.

## M4. The didactic-rasposo cabaret

Pie's own line, quoted in the Spanish Wikipedia article on Mexican political cabaret: "Mi cabaret es sinceramente didactico, sinceramente divertido y sinceramente rasposo." My cabaret is sincerely didactic, sincerely funny and sincerely rough, rasposo, abrasive, grating. From 2006, the same source says, his interest turned toward mixing critique and analysis with possible solutions. The cabaret does not only mock; it prescribes. This is the curative turn, satire with a remedy attached, and it is what separates his late shows from his early ones: the 2021 run La Ro~na Hasta el fondo! is about economy and current politics with the consigna "decir las cosas como son", say things as they are, and the 2018 Christmas show Yo, la Ro~na hands the audience a pinata for its accumulated rage, which is a prescription in the form of a party game. The roughness is the delivery system for the lesson, because a polite lecture about the economy empties the room and a corpse in a red gown shouting it fills the Esperanza Iris. Watch the prescription: when the joke ends and the solution starts, and the room is still laughing, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** mock it, then fix it. The remedy rides the insult. Against the chain: Rodriguez (#349 M9, M10) also carried the argument in a song and took the stage to the street, but her prescription was the march and the Senate; Pie's prescription is the pinata and the punchline, which is why his version fits a Thursday night at the theatre and hers needed a plaza.

## M5. One character, endlessly re-titled vehicles

La Ro~na va a merendar, La Ro~na Illuminati, La Ro~na en la muerte chiquita, La Ro~na del dinero, La ro~na se pega (2006, single-sourced), La Ro~na Enamorhada, La Ro~na es la Diva, La Ro~na Hasta el fondo!, Yo, la Ro~na, La Ro~na en la Ma...tria! (the title printed truncated with an ellipsis, most likely "Matria", a pun on "patria", but that reading is this study's, not verified). The same diva, a new vehicle each time. The title is the joke and the topic; the character is the constant. A franchise of one. The titles are the whole marketing plan: each one tells the room what tonight's subject is, merendar, the Illuminati, the little death, money, love, the diva herself, the bottom of things, the self, the motherland, and the audience buys the title the way it would buy a newspaper headline. "Enamorhada" keeps its own spelling, a pun on "enamorada" with an h, and must not be corrected to Enamorada; the misspelling is the joke. Watch the title: when the show's name is already the punchline before the curtain rises, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** retitle, do not reinvent. The new name is the new show. Against the chain: Hadad (#351 M2) also made the costume change the unit of the show, up to 14 per night, but her unit was the dress; Pie's unit is the title, which is why his version needs no wardrobe, only a poster.

## M6. The rage pinata

Yo, la Ro~na, Teatro El Galeon Abraham Oceransky, Centro Cultural del Bosque, Thursday 13 to Sunday 16 December 2018, written, directed and acted by Pie, piano by Nahum Carmona, closing the III Ciclo de Cabaret. The Cartelera de Teatro notice frames it as "espectaculo de cabaret decembrino para iniciar las posadas al ritmo del antiguo ponches-ponches", a December cabaret show to start the posadas to the rhythm of the old ponches-ponches, and then gives the instruction that is the mechanism: the audience is told to bring "la mayor cantidad de rabia acumulada posible, eso contribuira a un mejor desempeno durante la pinata", the greatest possible amount of accumulated rage, which will contribute to a better performance during the pinata. The audience's own anger is the prop; the pinata is the release valve. Christmas, the posadas, the season of breaking things blindfolded, is the frame that lets a room of strangers admit they are furious, and the diva is the mistress of ceremonies for the admission. Watch the prop: when the room arrives carrying its own material, and the show's job is only to give it somewhere to break, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the room bring the prop. The rage is the material. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M2, M9) also made the audience the co-author, but her audience supplied requests and heckles; Pie's audience supplies its anger, which is why his version is a ritual and hers is a game.

## M7. The piano as the only set

Nahum Carmona at the live piano; one man, one piano, one diva. That is the whole set, in the UABC tour (La Ro~na en la Ma...tria!, Mexicali Friday 26 August, Tijuana Saturday 27 August, Tecate Sunday 28 August, year unresolved) and in Yo, la Ro~na (2018) alike. The fan account adds the rest of the kit: a big red armchair, "gran sillon rojo al estilo de la primera mitad del siglo pasado", a great red armchair in the style of the first half of the last century, and a mega-screen behind showing texts and images. No scenery beyond the diva's furniture and her evidence. The armchair is the throne and the screen is the dossier: she sits like La Do~na and shows the room the newspaper clipping, the photograph, the headline, while the piano plays underneath. The mechanism is scarcity as authority: a show with no set cannot be accused of hiding behind one, and a diva with only a chair and a piano must hold the room with her face and her tongue, which is exactly what the mask and the albur are for. Watch the furniture: when the chair is the only thing on stage that is not a person, and the screen is the only thing that is not a voice, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** one chair, one piano, one diva. If the set does more than the face, cut the set. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M12) also hid a whole life inside one small object, a chocolate egg with a pocket watch, but her object was a time machine; Pie's object is a chair, which is why his version is a throne and hers is a clock.

## M8. The maker before the mask

Before La Ro~na existed, Pie built Hadad's breakout: direction and costume creation for La Occisa, the show that launched Astrid Hadad to fame. He also collaborated with Antonio Serrano Arguelles on A destiempo (1986), postmodern. The designer-director precedes the performer; the mask was made by someone who already knew how to build a diva from the outside. This is the mechanism the biography hides in plain sight: the twelve straight years before 1993 were not a delay, they were the apprenticeship, and the costume work for Hadad was the rehearsal for his own face. He knew what a diva needed to wear before he needed to wear it himself, and he knew what a show needed to hold together before he had to hold one together alone. The street-interview photograph viewed first-hand in this leg, dtp-04, is the maker without the mask: a grey-haired man in a dark suit and striped tie, being interviewed on the street by a Las Estrellas reporter with a purple microphone, pink wall and crew behind, no whiteface, no wig, no gown, the man who builds the diva standing next to the diva he built. Watch the credit: when the program lists him as director and costume designer before it lists him as performer, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** build the diva before you become her. The mask is made by a maker. Against the chain: Hadad (#351 M1) wore the scenery she designed herself, but her making and her wearing were the same career; Pie made someone else's diva first, which is why his version is a craftsman's mask and hers is a couturier's wardrobe.

## M9. The voice that changes bodies

Edna Moda in Los Increibles (2004) and Los Increibles 2 (2018): a man voicing the tiny tyrant designer, the same authoritarian-diva voice, now in a cartoon body. The Mexican dub was directed by Ricardo Tejedo, and Los Increibles was the fourth Pixar film to include "startalent" celebrity voices, Consuelo Duval, Omar Chaparro, Pie among them. The first trailer voice was Joana Brito, replaced by Pie; some viewers confused the voice with Omar Chaparro's; the Increibles 2 trailer voice was Ramon Bazet, not Pie, and Bazet also voiced Edna in the Disney Infinity and LEGO games, while Favio Posca voiced her in the Argentine dub of the first film. Pie also voiced Edna in "Disney sobre hielo" (2009) and Huevo Brujo in Otra pelicula de huevos y un pollo (2009), and Edna again in the short Tia Edna (2018, Blu-ray extra of Los Increibles 2; the Accio table's 2020 is wrong). The diva survives as pure voice: the same imperious, diminutive, terrifying designer, now small enough for children who will never see La Ro~na, and the joke is that the voice does not change at all when the body does. La Ro~na is a corpse in a gown; Edna is a four-foot fashion tyrant in glasses; the voice is the same woman, and the audience knows it is the same woman even though the body is a drawing. Watch the body: when the voice arrives before the face, and the room knows who is speaking before it sees her, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** keep the voice, change the body. The diva is the sound, not the size. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M16) also kept one instrument across every room, but her instrument was a soprano voice that changed rooms; Pie's instrument is a character voice that changes bodies, which is why his version is a dubbing booth and hers is a concert hall.

## M10. The bar-to-TV-to-cultural-theatre climb

El Habito (1993) to Desde Gayola (2002-2005) to six Teatro Helenico seasons to Teatro de la Ciudad Esperanza Iris (2021). The institutional ladder of Mexican cabaret, climbed by one character without changing her act: the room changes, she does not. The bar was El Habito, Jesusa Rodriguez's room, where La Ro~na was born alongside Pilar Boliver's La Veros in La Ro~na y La Veros. The television was Telehit, where Horacio Villalobos brought her from the cabaret room to the country, premiering February 2002 as a sketch inside Valvula de Escape, about 800 episodes, six seasons, Telehit 2002 to Thursday 3 August 2006, when the cast resigned over "abusos y maltratos", and then 52MX from Monday 4 August 2008 to the final show Thursday 31 January 2013, which Pie did not follow, having stopped appearing in 2005, with Daniel Vives "Ego" taking over the Maria Felix parody slot. The cultural theatre was the Teatro Helenico, six seasons claimed, only La Ro~na Enamorhada (April 2010) independently dated, and the grand stage was the Esperanza Iris, Thursdays, December 2021, La Ro~na Hasta el fondo! at 75% capacity under COVID protocols, a two-hour monologue with live piano, a mega-screen, a big red armchair, and guest stars. The arc is the opposite of a star's: he never left the mask behind, he carried it up the ladder with him, room by room, until the room was the city's grand stage. Watch the room: when the venue gets bigger and the act gets smaller, one chair, one piano, one diva, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** climb the ladder, keep the act. The room changes; she does not. Against the chain: Vasconcelos (#350) also climbed from the bar to the institution, but his climb was the school, the students who took El Habito and renamed it El Vicio; Pie's climb is the character herself, which is why his version is a single mask on a bigger stage and Vasconcelos's is a classroom that outlived its teacher.

## M11. The living archive of Mexican divas

Parodies of Daniela Romo and Agustin Lara as well as Maria Felix, and La Ro~na re-enacts Felix's film characters in "parodia clown" and sings Lara's "Aventurera". One face, re-costumed per icon: the same mask archives a whole golden age. The archive is the show's real subject: La Ro~na is not one diva, she is the shelf the divas sit on, and each night she takes a different one down, puts it on, and talks about the present in its voice. "Aventurera" is the mechanism in miniature: Lara wrote it for his ex-wife's world, Felix lived in that world, and La Ro~na sings it in a red gown at the Esperanza Iris, so the song carries three lives at once, the songwriter, the diva, the corpse. The poster thumbnail viewed first-hand in this leg, dtp-09, is the archive as an image: La Ro~na seated in a voluminous red gown against black, the Yo, la Ro~na poster, a different costume from the purple/blue gown of the stage shot, the same face underneath. Watch the shelf: when the gown changes and the face does not, and the room knows which diva has been taken down tonight, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** wear the archive, one diva at a time. The mask is the shelf. Against the chain: Hadad (#351 M16) also put a life on stage as objects, a wardrobe of 150 costumes, but her archive was her own life; Pie's archive is other women's lives, which is why his version is a museum and hers is a memoir.

## M12. The alter ego that lives only on stage

"La Ro~na es un alter ego que cobra vida propia y solo se materializa en el escenario", La Ro~na is an alter ego that takes on a life of its own and only materializes on stage, and Pie calls her a "vividor", a secondary being in his own life. The source is the enpareja profile of February 2025, low quality, and the claim is carried with that caveat, but the La Jornada interview title that Wikipedia cites, "La Ro~na ha ayudado a definirme: Dario T. Pie", La Ro~na has helped define me, points the same way, and that interview itself could not be found in this leg, via search or Wayback. The mechanism is the split: the man and the diva are two beings, and the diva only exists where the audience can see her. Off stage there is the grey-haired man in the suit from dtp-04; on stage there is the corpse in the gown from dtp-01, and the two do not meet, because the meeting would break the spell. The alter ego is not a secret identity; it is a second life with its own career, its own titles, its own rooms, and the man is its manager. Watch the split: when the interview is with the man and the show is with the diva, and neither one apologizes for the other, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let her live only where she is seen. The stage is her address. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M4, M12) also named herself, La Megabizcocho, and hid a clock in a chocolate egg, but her second self was a name she answered to in life; Pie's second self is a being who only exists on stage, which is why his version is a haunting and hers is a nickname.

## M13. The guest-star relay

La Ro~na's shows bring real stars on stage as guests: Jose Maria Yazpik, La Tesorito (Alejandra Bogue), Yuri, Fey, OV7, Cecilia Gabriela, Francis. The diva hosts the living, on stage exactly as on TV (M3): the interview format follows her from the studio to the theatre. The guest is the material and the diva is the frame: the star arrives with a career, a hit, a scandal, and La Ro~na asks the question no journalist would ask, in the voice of a dead woman who has nothing to lose, and the star answers as a person, not as a promotion. The relay runs both ways: the guest lends the diva their living fame, and the diva lends the guest her dead license, and the audience gets to watch a real person be undone by a painted one. The guest list is carried from the fan blog with that caveat, and the OV7 interview on television is the evidenced peak of the same device. Watch the relay: when the guest stops performing and starts answering, and the diva is the one holding the microphone, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** host the living, interview the dead's way. The guest is the material. Against the chain: Orozco (#352 M2) also brought the room into the show, but her guests were the audience members who shouted requests; Pie's guests are the stars who sit in the chair, which is why his version is a talk show and hers is a jukebox.

## M14. The audience as chorus

The show is a monologue, but "desde el inicio, ocasionalmente Dario T. Pie interactua con el publico, el cual colabora con sus respuestas sencillas y constantes risotadas", from the start, occasionally Pie interacts with the public, which collaborates with its simple answers and constant laughter. The crowd's answers and laughter are part of the text, not a reaction to it. Same device as M6, the rage pinata: the audience supplies the material. The monologue is not a speech; it is a conversation in which one side has all the lines and the other side has all the laughs, and the laughs are timed like lines, because the diva waits for them, answers them, and builds the next joke on top of them. A room that only watches is a bad room for this show; a room that answers, even with a single word, is the co-author. Watch the chorus: when the laughter comes before the punchline, because the room already knows what the diva is going to say, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** write the laughs into the script. The room is the second voice. Against the chain: Rodriguez (#349 M5) also made a weekly newspaper out of bodies, but her chorus was a company; Pie's chorus is the strangers in the seats, which is why his version needs no rehearsal and hers needed a troupe.

## M15. The clown parody as degradation

La Ro~na began as a "parodia clown" of La Do~na. The clown is the degradation: the grandeur is kept, the gowns, the jewels, the armchair, the throne, and the cleanliness is removed. The name does the work the makeup only starts, "La Do~na" dragged through the dirt letter by letter until the Do~na is a Ro~na, and the clown is the permission to do it in public, because a clown is allowed to be filthy and a diva is not, and La Ro~na is both at once. The parody is not affectionate: a tribute would keep the diva clean, and this one dirties her on purpose, because the dirt is the political point, that the golden age was never clean, that the diva's grandeur was always built on something rotten, and that the audience knows it and laughs because someone finally said it. The "parodia clown" label is the fan blog's, and the etymology reading is this study's own, flagged as interpretation in M2, but the mechanism itself is evidenced by the face in dtp-01: the whiteface is a clown's whiteface, the pink cheek circles are a clown's circles, the overdrawn mouth is a clown's mouth, and the gown is a diva's gown, and the two costumes are worn at the same time. Watch the dirt: when the gown is couture and the face is a clown's, and the room laughs at both at once, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** keep the gown, dirty the face. The clown is the criticism. Against the chain: Vasconcelos (#350 M1, M2) also recycled and degraded, turning the news into emergency theatre, but his degradation was of the present; Pie's degradation is of the golden age, which is why his version is a clown and Vasconcelos's is a headline.

## M16. The long apprenticeship

1981 to 1993 is twelve years of straight acting before the mask: children's theatre, comparsa in musical theatre, three nightclub years under Fernando Lujan with Lupe Vazquez, Martha Ofelia Galindo, Fernando Ciangherotti, Irlanda Mora and Gilberto Perez Gallardo, telenovelas from 1989 (Luz y sombra, Balada por un amor, Alcanzar una estrella, En carne propia, Las secretas intenciones, Angeles sin paraiso, Valentina, Marea brava 1999), Fedra (1987, Hector Mendoza, starring Ofelia Medina, where Televisa casting chief Martha Oliver is said to have discovered him, a claim that carries a citation-needed in the source), Vestir al desnudo (1988), Misantropias (1990). La Ro~na arrives when he is 31, built on a trained actor's full toolkit, not on a beginner's dare. The straight-theatre spine stays visible all his life: El sexo opuesto (1994), Histeria (2005), El misterio de Irma Vep (2006, Charles Ludlam's two-hander), 12 hombres en pugna (2014), La ultima sesion de Freud (2015-2016). He never stopped being the straight actor; he added the mask to the toolkit, and the mask works because the toolkit is underneath it. The voice work is the same story in miniature: the Edna Moda voice is a trained actor's voice, not a funny voice, and that is why it survives the change of body. Watch the spine: when the diva does a scene that only a trained actor could do, a pause, a turn, a silence, and the clown face does not get in the way, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** earn the mask with twelve years first. The dare is the last step, not the first. Against the chain: every study in this chain has a version of the apprenticeship, Rodriguez's CUT years, Vasconcelos's revista rescue, Hadad's bar years, Orozco's Conservatorio, but Pie's is the longest and the straightest, which is why his mask is the most theatrical: it was built by a man who had already played Fedra.

## Evolution

1981-1992, apprenticeship: stage debut at 19 in 1981, Los malvados planes de Microbion and Cabaret, then Don Juan Tenorio (1982), Concierto para guillotina y 40 cabezas (1982), La nueva arca de Noe (1983). Training at Foro EON with Orozco and Hadad beside him, then CEA Televisa, then Jesusa Rodriguez's cabaret workshops, with other teachers listed as Rafael Esteban, Morris Savariego, Hugo Arguelles, Phil de Kanter, Juan Jose Gurrola, Hector Mendoza and Flora Dantus. Children's theatre, musical-theatre comparsa, three years in nightclubs directed by Fernando Lujan. First film Bandidos (1991). Telenovelas from 1989. 1993-2001, the mask is born: La Ro~na is born 1993 at El Habito, alongside Pilar Boliver's La Veros, in La Ro~na y La Veros, at the same moment the five university-trained founders, Rodriguez, Hadad, Pie, Vasconcelos, Orozco, turn cabaret into political satire. In parallel he keeps working telenovelas through Marea brava (1999) and film through Santitos (premiered 1998, production year 1999), where he plays Cesar alongside Regina Orozco and Alberto Estrella. Behind the mask he is also the maker: he had already built Hadad's breakout, La Occisa, direction and costumes, before his own character existed. 2002-2005, national television: Horacio Villalobos brings La Ro~na from the cabaret room to Telehit, premiering February 2002 as a sketch inside Valvula de Escape, about 800 episodes, six seasons, and the OV7 interview becomes the signature moment. He plays her from the start until 2004, returns in 2005, then stops appearing in 2005; Daniel Vives "Ego" takes over the Maria Felix parody slot, and Pie does not follow the show to 52MX (2008-2013). 2004-2010, the second mass audience: Edna Moda in Los Increibles (2004), the same authoritarian-diva voice in a cartoon for children who will never see La Ro~na. From 2006 his interest turns toward mixing critique and analysis with possible solutions, the didactic cabaret becomes curative. El misterio de Irma Vep on stage (2006), Casi divas (2008), Huevo Brujo (2009), April 2010 La Ro~na Enamorhada at Teatro Helenico. 2010-2021, the institutional diva: six Helenico seasons, film in parallel, Ella es Ramona (2015), La cuarta compania (2016) and the Ariel nomination (2017), stage 12 hombres en pugna (2014) and La ultima sesion de Freud (2015-2016), television La casa de las flores season 2 (2019) as Dr. Meneses, dubbing Edna again in Los Increibles 2 (2018), and the December 2018 Yo, la Ro~na closing the III Ciclo de Cabaret at El Galeon with a rage pinata. The 2021 Hasta el fondo! run is the same character, 28 years on, now doing economy and current politics to a 75%-capacity room under COVID protocols. 2021-2025, the living monument: no new character, no reinvention. The February 2025 enpareja piece is a "where is he now" profile that still identifies him wholly with La Ro~na. The character has outlived the show that made her famous by twenty years and her model, Maria Felix died 2002, by longer. The arc is the opposite of a star's: he never left the mask behind, he carried it up the ladder with him, room by room, until the room was the Esperanza Iris.

## Aesthetic logic

Pie's aesthetic is the dead diva in the red armchair as the whole country: the gown is couture, the face is a clown's, the chair is a throne, the piano is the only set, and the guest in the chair is the news. The materials are a painted face, a title, a piano, a red armchair, a mega-screen with the evidence, a living guest, and a double meaning, and the scarcity is the argument, because a show with no set cannot hide, a diva with only a chair must hold the room with her tongue, and a corpse can say what the living cannot. The palette is the whiteface against black, the pink cheek circles, the overdrawn red mouth, the purple/blue ruffled gown of the stage shot and the voluminous red gown of the Yo, la Ro~na poster, the gold puff sleeves of the morning-show couch, the jeweled necklace, the red armchair, the black background. The show is built from three moves that never change: the guest sits, the corpse asks, the room answers. Where Hadad's logic was the whole country worn on one body, and Orozco's was the whole country sung by one voice, Pie's is the whole country interviewed by one dead woman, and the difference is the guest: Hadad needs the costume to carry the country because she performs it alone, Orozco needs only the voice because the voice itself does the carrying, Pie needs a living person in the chair, because the interview only works when someone real is there to be undone.
