## M1. The cut as drawing

The mechanism is a line made by removing material instead of adding it, and the removing is the drawing. A chainsaw, a handsaw, a blow torch, a chisel is drawn through a wall, a floor, a roof, and the line it leaves is the drawing, and the leaving is the line, and the line is the cut, and the cut is the drawing. In Splitting the line is a single vertical slice through the whole house, plus the four corners removed from the second floor, and the slicing is the drawing, because the eye follows the cut the way it follows a drawn line, from one surface to the next, and the following is the slicing, and the slicing is the line, and the line is the house. In Conical Intersect the line is a cone rising at 45 degrees through two houses, and the rising is the drawing, because the cone connects rooms that were never meant to connect, and the connecting is the rising, and the rising is the cone, and the cone is the line. In Office Baroque the line is two overlapping circles climbing five floors, and the climbing is the drawing, because the circles play their way up and down through the layers like a musical score, and the playing is the climbing, and the climbing is the circle, and the circle is the line. The drawing is also literal, because his cut-drawings from about 1973 on use the building cuts as models, cutting and overlaying paper the way he cut walls, and his notebooks are full of arrows showing energy and movement, and the cutting of paper is the mechanism, because the father's automatic line becomes the son's sawn line, and the becoming is the cutting, and the cutting is the arrow, and the arrow is the line. **Takeaway:** let the line remove instead of add, let the eye follow the cut from surface to surface, let the drawing be three-dimensional, so a line that removes is still a line, and it draws faster than any pencil because it draws in three dimensions at once. Against the chain: Roberto Matta (#382 M1) also let the hand move before the mind, and his is a stain rubbed on at random and recognised into form, and Gordon's is a saw drawn through a wall and recognised into a line, which is why the father's is a starting and the son's is a cutting, and the cutting is the starting made solid.

## M2. The cut as revelation of structure

The mechanism is that a building hides its own construction behind plaster and paint, and the cut strips the skin and shows the bones, and the stripping is the revealing. Frieze says he cut and disembowelled and dismembered buildings and homes, exposing their heart and soul, and the exposing is the mechanism, because the inside of a house is normally hidden, and the hiding is the plaster, and the plaster is the skin, and the skin is the lie, and the cut is the truth. Ocula says the films and photographs show the light flooding in through the penetrated planes to reveal the normally hidden floor and roof and wall framing structures, and the flooding is the mechanism, because the light does the revealing after the saw stops, and the revealing is the flooding, and the flooding is the light, and the light is the structure. At FOOD he made "sandwiches" from the walls, layer by layer, and the sandwiching is the mechanism, because a wall cut open shows its layers the way a sandwich shows its filling, and the showing is the sandwiching, and the sandwiching is the wall, and the wall is the section. In Santiago in 1971 he cut from a men's bathroom in a basement up through the building to its roof, and mirrors placed at different angles within the hole carried sunlight to the very bottom, and the carrying is the mechanism, because the whole section of the building was visible at once, and the visibility is the carrying, and the carrying is the mirror, and the mirror is the section. **Takeaway:** let the removal expose the assembly, let the viewer see how the thing was put together by seeing it taken apart, let the cut be an X-ray that you walk through, so the bones are the work and the skin was only the cover. Against the chain: Quilapayun (#377 M2) also let a director stage the mechanism and then let the mechanism run, and theirs is a theatre that opens when the curtain rises, and Gordon's is a house that opens when the saw enters, which is why theirs is a staging and this is a stripping.

## M3. Subtraction as construction (unbuilding)

The mechanism is that the work is made by taking away, and the taking away IS the building, and the being is the taking. He called it "unbuilding" and "anarchitecture", and the naming is the mechanism, because the name says the building is undone and the undoing is the building, and the undoing is the naming, and the naming is the work, and the work is the absence. A house minus a slice is not a ruined house, it is a new object with a new identity, and the newness is the mechanism, because the house still stands and still is a house, but it no longer closes, and the not-closing is the newness, and the newness is the slice, and the slice is the house. Frieze on Splitting says a house cut in two has its identity disintegrated, and the cosmologic centre of the family unit, and the locus in which identity is shaped and through which one interfaces with the world, was severed, and the severing is the mechanism, because the cut goes through the centre of the family and not through its edge, and the going-through is the severing, and the severing is the centre, and the centre is the house. The two halves of the Splitting house tilt apart by a few degrees, held by jacks, and the gap between them is the work, and the gap is the mechanism, because the gap is a triangular section of light running through the whole house, and the running is the gap, and the gap is the light, and the light is the work. Arterritory's formulation is worth keeping: before the ability to deconstruct comes the need to build something, and the building-first is the mechanism, because the viewer must hold the whole house in mind before the removal can be an event, and the holding is the building, and the building is the removal, and the removal is the event. **Takeaway:** build the thing completely first, in the viewer's mind, then remove one piece and let the removal be the event, so the taking away is the building and the building is the taking away. Against the chain: Roberto Matta (#382 M7) also dismantled a closed form to prove it has more than three dimensions, and his is a cube unfolding its faces one by one, and Gordon's is a house tilting its halves apart by a few degrees, which is why the father's is an opening and the son's is a severing, and the severing is the opening made with a saw.

## M4. The void as the work

The mechanism is that the empty space left by the cut is the sculpture, not the material removed, and the emptiness is the sculpture. The Anarchitecture group's programme was voids and gaps and leftover spaces, and Matta-Clark said in 1974, "Architecture is environment, too. When you're living in a city, the whole fabric is architectural in some sense. We were thinking more about metaphoric voids, gaps, leftover spaces, places that were not developed", and the thinking is the mechanism, because the subject is the place that was not developed, and the not-developed is the thinking, and the thinking is the void, and the void is the subject. The companion coinage "Non-ument" treats presence as absence, and the treating is the mechanism, because the monument is replaced by the leftover, and the replacing is the treating, and the treating is the absence, and the absence is the monument. In Fake Estates the void is a plot of land too small to stand on, owned and unoccupiable, fifteen parcels bought for 25 to 75 dollars each, fourteen in Queens and one in Staten Island, some narrower than a person's shoulders, and the owning is the mechanism, because he bought the spaces that would not be seen and certainly not occupied, and the buying is the owning, and the owning is the void, and the void is the deed. In Day's End the void is a crescent of sky cut into a tin wall and a hole in the floor through which the river shows, and the showing is the mechanism, because the river surges into view where the floor used to be, and the surging is the showing, and the showing is the hole, and the hole is the river. In Office Baroque the void is a rowboat-shaped hole running through five floors that you walk around and look through, and the walking-around is the mechanism, because the void has a shape and a size and a direction, and you can stand in it, and the standing is the walking-around, and the walking-around is the shape, and the shape is the void. **Takeaway:** make the absence measurable, give the hole a geometry, let the viewer occupy the missing piece, so the void is the work and the work is the void. Against the chain: Isabel Parra (#380 M5) also opened a trunk and set the dead woman's texts, and hers is a finding that becomes a song, and Gordon's is a hole that becomes a room, which is why hers is a setting and this is an occupying.

## M5. The building as found object

The mechanism is that the city is full of buildings already condemned, abandoned, or slated for demolition, and the artist does not build a sculpture, he finds one, and the finding is the making. Frieze says choosing existing, often neglected, structures would become the starting point of his intended reclamation, and the choosing is the mechanism, because the work begins with a search, not a sketch, and the search is the choosing, and the choosing is the building, and the building is the found. Every major cut was made in a building about to die: the Englewood house the Solomons were going to demolish, the Niagara Falls house on Love Canal, the Les Halles townhouses awaiting the Pompidou, the Pier 52 shed, the Antwerp office, the Chicago townhouse, and the dying is the mechanism, because the cut is timed to the building's death, and the timing is the dying, and the dying is the cut, and the cut is the deadline. The building's own history, its decay, its neighbourhood, its demolition date, are part of the piece, and the part-of is the mechanism, because the work dies with the building, and the dying-with is the part-of, and the part-of is the history, and the history is the piece. The cut is also a comment on who owns the city and who gets thrown out of it, and the commenting is the mechanism, because the buildings he cut were the ones the city had already thrown away, and the throwing-away is the commenting, and the commenting is the ownership, and the ownership is the city. **Takeaway:** let the site choose the work, let the demolition date be the deadline, let the building's previous life stay visible in the cut, so the finding is the making and the making is the finding. Against the chain: Joan Jara (#381 M2) also dissolved a stage into a street, and hers is a room replaced by a neighbourhood house, and Gordon's is a house replaced by its own demolition, which is why hers is a leaving and this is a finding.

## M6. Light as material

The mechanism is that the cut is positioned so that the sun does the work after the saw stops, and the positioning is the material. In Santiago in 1971 mirrors inside the cut carried sunlight from the roof to a basement bathroom, and the carrying is the mechanism, because the higher light besieged the depths of the underground, and the besieging is the carrying, and the carrying is the mirror, and the mirror is the light. In Splitting the vertical slice lets a line of light pass through the inside of the house, and the passing is the mechanism, because the light runs where the saw ran, and the running is the passing, and the passing is the slice, and the slice is the light. In Day's End the cuts were calibrated to the sun's daily trajectory, with a west-facing oculus admitting the setting sun and the river surface acting as a reflective agent, and the calibrating is the mechanism, because the work is different at noon and at sunset, and the difference is the calibrating, and the calibrating is the sun, and the sun is the work. In Conical Intersect the cone gives a periscopic view of the city from inside, and the periscoping is the mechanism, because the cut frames the street the way a lens frames a scene, and the framing is the periscoping, and the periscoping is the cone, and the cone is the view. The light moves, and the moving is the mechanism, because the film and the photographs record the light moving across the cut edges through the day, and the recording is the moving, and the moving is the light, and the light is the day. **Takeaway:** cut for the sun, not for the wall, let the light be the part of the work that moves, so the building becomes a sundial and a camera at once, and the sundial is the camera and the camera is the sundial. Against the chain: Roberto Matta (#382 M14) also let light through grit, and his is clay in the oil and burlap with construction debris, and Gordon's is a hole cut so the sun can enter, which is why the father's is a dirtying and the son's is an admitting.

## M7. The photograph and the film as the only surviving form

The mechanism is that the building is demolished, the cut lasts weeks or months, and what remains is the documentation, which he then treats as an independent artwork, and the treating is the surviving. ## M13. The city as material

The mechanism is that the whole urban fabric is the medium, not the single building, and the whole-fabric is the medium. Matta-Clark said in 1974, "Architecture is environment, too. When you're living in a city, the whole fabric is architectural in some sense", and the saying is the mechanism, because the city is the material and the material is the city, and the city is the saying, and the saying is the fabric. The work follows the city's own processes: urban renewal, demolition, abandonment, gentrification, decay, and the following is the mechanism, because the cut is timed to the city's wound, and the timing is the following, and the following is the wound, and the wound is the city. Bronx Floors is fieldwork, walking peripheral neighbourhoods and cutting dead buildings, and the walking is the mechanism, because the work begins with a search through the city's edge, and the search is the walking, and the walking is the floor, and the floor is the neighbourhood. Window Blow-Out answers an architecture exhibition with photographs of broken public-housing windows, and the answering is the mechanism, because the vandalism is the criticism, and the criticism is the answering, and the answering is the window, and the window is the exhibit. Ocula calls him an energetic daredevil guerrilla activist fighting against gentrification and property developer greed and corporate waste, and the fighting is the mechanism, because the cut points at the ownership, not just at the wall, and the pointing is the fighting, and the fighting is the ownership, and the ownership is the cut. **Takeaway:** let the work be sited by the city's own wounds, let the cut point at the ownership, not just at the wall, so the city is the material and the material is the city. ## M14. The drawing as score

The mechanism is that the drawing is not a plan of the cut, it is the cut in another medium, and it can also be the instruction for it, and the being is the instructing. His notebooks combine Surrealist automatic drawing with choreography, and arrows illustrate energy or movement, often blending light washes with an overlay of whirling arrows, and the blending is the mechanism, because the tree drawings dissolve a physical structure into kinetic energy and then into a multitude of arrows, and the dissolving is the blending, and the blending is the arrow, and the arrow is the energy. From about 1973 he made cut-out and overlay drawings using his building cuts as a model, and the modelling is the mechanism, because the paper is cut the way the wall is cut, and the cutting is the modelling, and the modelling is the paper, and the paper is the wall. Office Baroque is the full form: "I wanted to work out an almost musical score in which a fixed set of elements played their way up and down through the layers", and the disposition of spaces determined how the formal elements transformed from uninterrupted circular silences to shrapnel-like bits and pieces as they collided with partitions, and the transforming is the mechanism, because the drawing shows the cut as a sequence in time, a score the eye plays from floor to floor, and the building then performs it, and the performing is the transforming, and the transforming is the score, and the score is the building. Sky Hooks in 1978 is the score without a building at all, and the without-a-building is the mechanism, because the cut leaves the building and becomes a drawing of something that hangs in the air, and the hanging is the without-a-building, and the without-a-building is the drawing, and the drawing is the air. **Takeaway:** draw the cut as a sequence, let the arrows show the movement, let the building perform the drawing, so the score is the cut and the cut is the score. Against the chain: Roberto Matta (#382 M4) also studied a flat shape turning inside out, and his is a cube opening with a group reading Ouspensky, and Gordon's is a circle climbing five floors with a score, which is why the father's is a turning and the son's is a playing.

## M15. Grief as structure

The mechanism is that after Batan's death on Monday 14 June 1976, the work is organized by a halving, and the organizing is the halving. Frieze says in a sense the death of his twin halved him, and these lower-world and upper-world works were a way to work through his loss, and the halving is the mechanism, because the twin is gone and the work is split in two, and the splitting is the halving, and the halving is the loss, and the loss is the work. Jacob's Ladder in 1977 rises heavenward on white netting at Documenta 6, and Descending Steps for Batan in 1977 digs a hole in a Paris gallery basement, and the rising-and-digging is the mechanism, because one goes up and one goes down, and the pair is the structure, and the pair is the rising-and-digging, and the rising-and-digging is the twin, and the twin is the pair. The same halving is already in Splitting in 1974, made two years before the death, a house cut in two, its identity disintegrated, and Frieze reads it as the severing of the family unit as his own had been in childhood, and the already-there is the mechanism, because the cut knew the loss before the loss happened, and the knowing is the already-there, and the already-there is the house, and the house is the twin. LA Weekly makes the connection explicit: Splitting takes on a particular poignancy when you know that Matta-Clark's twin brother, Batan, would commit suicide two years later, and the poignancy is the mechanism, because the viewer reads the cut backwards through the death, and the reading-backwards is the poignancy, and the poignancy is the house, and the house is the death. The late works are paired opposites, up and down, and the cuts are circles, closed and complete, rather than the open slashes of the middle period, and the closing is the mechanism, because the circle closes what the splitting opened, and the closing is the circle, and the circle is the grief, and the grief is the structure. **Takeaway:** let a loss organize the work as a pair, one piece rising and one descending, let the geometry close around the absence, so the grief is the structure and the structure is the grief. Against the chain: Isabel Parra (#380 M5) also carried a dead woman's texts into the present, and hers is a trunk opened and a song set, and Gordon's is a twin halved and a ladder raised, which is why hers is a setting and this is a pairing.

## Evolution

The evolution is the student who becomes the cook, and then the cutter, and then the mourner who draws structures that hang in the air, and the becoming has three phases, and each phase keeps the one before it. From 1968 to 1971 he is the student, the cook, the recycler, Cornell and then Ithaca after graduation, Rope Bridge in 1968 and Deflation in 1969 as temporary site pieces, the February 1969 Earth Art show at Cornell as the hinge, where he assisted Dennis Oppenheim in cutting a swath across a frozen lake for Beebe Lake Ice Cut and met Robert Smithson, and the hinge is the discovering, because he discovered that the common goal of these artists was to move art outside the galleries and beyond traditional materials, and the new art was as extreme as Surrealism, yet it borrowed formal elements from architecture and engineering, and the discovering is the hinge, and the hinge is the lake. In New York from mid-1969 the work is alchemy and recycling: Photo-Fry, frying photographs in oil, Agar, growing mould in tin sheets, Museum, hanging the rot on vines at the Bykert Gallery, Garbage Wall, compressing detritus into shelter, and the alchemy is the cooking, because the materials are food and mould and waste and photographs, and the operations are heating and fermenting and recycling and growing, and the cooking is the alchemy, and the alchemy is the wall. FOOD in 1971 is the social form of the same operations, cooking as performance, dining as event, and the social is the cooking, and the cooking is the event, and the event is the room. From 1971 to 1975 he is the cutter, and the first building interventions are in 1971, Sauna in his own loft and the Santiago museum wall cutting, and then the fieldwork years, Bronx Floors in 1972 and 1973, Walls Paper in 1972, Open House in 1972, Graffiti Truck in 1973, Clockshower in 1973, Intraform and A W-Hole House in Italy in 1973, Fake Estates in 1973 and 1974, the Anarchitecture show in March 1974, Splitting in 1974, Bingo in 1974, Conical Intersect and Day's End in 1975, and the cutting is the growing, because the scale grows with each work, a sauna, a floor, a house, two houses, a pier shed, and the tools grow too, from a handsaw in a loft to chainsaws and blow torches and weightbearing jacks, and the legality thins, because Splitting is invited, the Solomons' house slated for demolition, and Conical Intersect is Biennale-sponsored but the site is closed by officials the moment it is finished, and Day's End is outright illegal, a warrant follows, and the thinning is the growing, and the growing is the warrant, and the warrant is the cut. The documentation hardens into a system, every cut filmed and photographed, and the photographs then cut and collaged into spatial analogues of the site, and the hardening is the system, and the system is the film, and the film is the cut. From 1976 to 1978 he is the mourner, the geometer, the man turning outward, and Batan dies on Monday 14 June 1976, and the work after that date is different in two ways, because first there are the memorial works, Jacob's Ladder in 1977 climbing toward the sky on white netting at Documenta 6, and Descending Steps for Batan in 1977 digging a hole in a Paris gallery basement, up and down, heaven and earth, a halved twin working through a halving, and second the cuts become preplanned and schematic rather than found, because Office Baroque in 1977 and Circus in 1978 both impose a fixed geometric motif, overlapping circles, onto a building, where Splitting had let the house dictate the cut, and Frieze is explicit, the late works were not classic Matta-Clark, as Splitting had been, their design was preplanned and imposed into a space, but they did relate more closely to his drawings, and the imposing is the mourning, and the mourning is the circle, and the circle is the pair. At the same time the work turns outward to the city and the community, Window Blow-Out in 1976 as a criticism of architects, Sous-Sols de Paris in 1977 mapping the underground, the Environmental Resource Center for Loisaida in 1977 planning a neighbourhood rehabilitation with local youth, and Sky Hooks in 1978 drawing constructions suspended in space, past the building altogether, and the turning-outward is the mourning, and the mourning is the city, and the city is the air. He marries Jane Crawford in 1978, and she works with him on Jacob's Ladder and Office Baroque and Caribbean Orange, and he dies of pancreatic cancer on Sunday 27 August 1978, aged thirty-five, two months after his thirty-fifth birthday, a little more than two years after his twin's death, and Frieze says he reconciled with his father as he was dying, and the reconciling is the ending, and the ending is the father, and the father is the son. The arc, in one line: from cooking photographs to cutting houses to drawing structures that hang in the air, with the cut itself moving from something found in a loft to something imposed on a city to something remembered after a death.

## Aesthetic logic

The aesthetic logic is the cut, the void, the meal and the city. The cut is the line and the revelation and the subtraction, the saw drawn through a wall the way a pencil is drawn through paper, the skin stripped to show the bones, the taking away that is the building, and the cut is the line, and the line is the revelation, and the revelation is the subtraction. The void is the work and the found object and the Non-ument, the empty space with a shape and a size and a direction, the plot too small to stand on, the hole in the floor where the river shows, the leftover the city threw away, and the void is the work, and the work is the leftover, and the leftover is the shape. The meal is the social space and the body in the structure, the restaurant as a stage, the kitchen as the performance, the feeding as the event, the body hanging from a clock tower and washing its face, the body performing in a rope pod at the top of a tree, and the meal is the stage, and the stage is the body, and the body is the feeding. The city is the material and the drawing as score, the whole urban fabric as the medium, the cut timed to the demolition date, the score the building performs, the underground mapped, the neighbourhood given back to its youth, and the city is the material, and the material is the score, and the score is the neighbourhood. The four hold together because the cut needs the void to be a work, and the void needs the meal to be a place, and the meal needs the city to be a scene, and the city needs the cut to be seen, and the seen is the void, and the void is the meal, and the meal is the city, and the city is the cut. He is described as an anarchitect, and the description is the mechanism, because he unbuilt what the architects built, and the unbuilding is the architecture, and the architecture is the unbuilding, and the unbuilding is the art.
