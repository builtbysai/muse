# Hersua: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-05. ig-artist-study #340.

**Site:** Manuel Hernandez Suarez, known as Hersua (Ciudad Obregon, Sonora, 20 June 1940), Mexican sculptor. The name is a contraction of HERnandez SUarez, used since the 1960s; Spanish sources write it Hersua with an accent, this study writes Hersua. A painter turned environment-builder turned monumental sculptor, author of the winning maquette for the Espacio Escultorico at UNAM (1979), of Ave Dos in its Paseo de las Esculturas, of a fifty-year run of doors and arches from a 35 cm bronze to a 120 m ring in a lava field, and Premio Nacional de Artes y Literatura 2020. Identity anchors: the walkable work; the door that never closes; the binary union; the fold; color as light and weight; the turning module; the grid of discs; the word as material; the body in the work; the circle without a center; the pyramid of today; the maquette as the real work; rough material, honest matter; the performance that steals itself; the labyrinth that returns you; silence and solitude as the material.

**Doorway:** Hop from the federico-silva study: from Silva's serpents laid low in the lava field to Hersua, the fifth hand of the Espacio Escultorico, whose geometry also learned to live outdoors. #339 closed with the man who proposed the ring and then laid his serpents flat in the scrub beside it. #340 opens with the man whose maquette was chosen for the ring itself, the one the rector approved unchanged, and who spent the next forty years building the same idea at every scale the ring taught him: a door you walk through, a circle with nothing in the middle, a silence you stand inside. Where Silva made geometry move, sound, and shine, Hersua makes geometry stand still and wait for you to walk into it. The doorway is the lava field itself: Silva invited six hands to it, and Hersua's hand is the one that drew the circle.

## M1. The walkable work

The governing operation. The work is an environment the viewer's body moves through, and the viewer is a co-creator; his own phrase is "la escultura transitable", the transitable sculpture. Ave Dos, viewed first-hand in three photographs, is two massive interlocked angular slabs, 7 by 14 by 8 m, painted ferroconcrete, and the work is the triangular passage between them: from outside it is one hot orange mass against black lava, from inside it is a red room you stand in, and the catalog says it plainly, "the exterior is like reality, it changes a lot when we go through it as if it were several works... when penetrating it we feel it welcoming with its red color that refers us to our interior." The Espacio Escultorico, viewed first-hand from inside the ring, is the same operation at 120 m: a ring of leaning grey prisms receding along a curve, a red-brown path, a black lava field with green plants at the center, and the walk is the work, because the ring has no front and no single viewpoint. The Ambientes maquette of 1967, viewed first-hand, is the same operation at 24 by 60 by 35 cm: a circular platform with thin rods topped by gold, white, black, and brown discs at different heights, like a forest of coins, a field you walk among. Puerta de Manu, viewed first-hand, is two tall slabs in a paved plaza and the gap between them is the door. Against the chain: Silva (#339 M5) made the viewer complete the geometry by walking a stairwell until three walls became one image; Hersua makes the viewer complete the sculpture by walking through it until one mass becomes several works, and the completion is bodily, not visual. Sebastian (#338 M10) built the corridor out of different hands; Hersua builds the corridor out of one body walking, and the body is the corridor's second author. **Takeaway:** Build the work as a place to walk through, not a thing to look at; the passage is the sculpture. Watch the passage: when the outside and the inside of one mass are two different works, and walking through it changes the color and the framing, the mechanism is on.

## M2. The door that never closes

One form, two legs and an opening, derived from the Mayan Arch, repeated at every scale and in every material across fifty years. The small maquettes, viewed first-hand, show it at tabletop scale: Puertas del Sol, orange layered curved slats forming an arch with a black interior, like a tiled roof bent into a doorway; Arco Hersua, 2020, a small arch of two leaning legs that do NOT meet at the top, dark hammered metal with a wrinkled organic surface and greenish-silver patina, an open gap at the crown. Gwendolyn, viewed first-hand, is the same arch grown to 6.8 by 6.7 by 3.2 m in iron covered with copper, two linked pointed arches, a folded ribbon forming two doorways, standing in dry yellow grass on a hillside in Japan. Ovi, viewed first-hand, is two large interlocked copper-clad angular arches on green grass in the Museo de Arte Moderno garden, warm copper and rose-gold, walkable underneath. Hened, viewed first-hand, is the same arch carved in quartz stone, 7,000 kg, two massive uneven legs with a pointed top, standing on flat rock among green trees in Australia, its inner face painted deep red. Puerta de Manu, viewed first-hand, is two tall separate slabs whose gap is the door, and the artist's text is the doctrine: the two parts symbolize the human being as duality, "we are the opposites united at the top and that is why it is open, so that the viewer, being in the middle, is the union of the two parts and turns them into a unit - heaven and earth"; "La Puerta de Manu never closes, it will always be open because life always awaits us." Against the chain: Sebastian (#338 M3) built the gate as a civic verb, a threshold that announces a city; Hersua builds the gate as a human verb, a threshold that announces a person, and the gate never closes because the person is always arriving. Silva (#339 M12) built the altar and the gate as a triangular void that is the point; Hersua builds the gate as a void that is the door, and the void is always open. **Takeaway:** Lean two legs toward each other and do not let them meet; the gap at the crown is the work, and it stays open. Watch the gap: when the same two leaning legs appear in bronze, copper, stone, and painted concrete, at 35 cm and at 7 m, and the crown is open every time, the mechanism is on.

## M3. The binary union

A work made of two halves that only become one unit when the viewer stands between them. The Obra binaria and Obra bipartita series, 2010, 2015, 2018, shrinks the two-part door to tabletop scale in ferroconcrete, copper, and painted carbon steel: the catalog photographs show two red textured angular slabs leaning and interlocked on black ground, the same engine, now a private object. Puerta de Manu states it as doctrine, quoted above: who left and who came, yesterday and today, the viewer in the middle is the union. The Rotonda de la Puerta de Manu, viewed first-hand from a drone, is the binary union grown into a place: a paved circular plaza, the two Manu slabs small at the center, four curved segments around them, two long pink stone benches with grass banks and two shorter ones, forming a broken circle with four gaps at the diagonals, four modules including seats inside, a total diameter of 17.62 m, donated with the Puerta to FES Aragon. The catalog calls the Rotonda a derivation of the Espacio Escultorico, "the derivation itself of the modern pyramid." Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M4) laid one body down in sequence until it was a step; Hersua lays two parts down in a gap until they are a unit, and the sequence is replaced by the visitor. Silva (#339 M5) let the moving spectator compose the triptych; Hersua lets the standing spectator compose the pair, and the spectator stands still. **Takeaway:** Make the work in two parts and leave the gap for the viewer; the viewer in the middle is the third part that closes the circuit. Watch the middle: when two slabs lean apart and the space between them is the work, and standing in it makes the two halves one, the mechanism is on.

## M4. The fold

A flat sheet, copper, steel, bronze, or aluminum, folded until it becomes a self-supporting loop or arch; the fold is the structure, no frame needed. The Simbolo de las Ciencias y las Humanidades, 1979, viewed first-hand in two photographs, is a large angular copper-clad folded ribbon and loop indoors against a tall grid window wall, copper-brown with glowing orange interior faces, sharp zigzag folds, an interlocked loop, 7 by 5 by 3.5 m of concrete with copper sheets. Ovi and Gwendolyn are folded ribbon arches. Serpiente, 2018, viewed first-hand, is a small green-patinated bronze, folded angular planes forming a coiled and looped knot, rough verdigris surface, dark interior gaps. Ritmo cosmico, 2020, viewed first-hand, is two dark blue-grey metallic folded forms, a tall angular slab with a folded top and a smaller folded pyramid. The Museo Federico Silva show photograph, viewed first-hand, is a gallery of small folded-plane sculptures on plinths, red, green, yellow, white angular folded forms, a tall pale green and white folded pyramid in the foreground. Against the chain: Sebastian (#338 M1) folded the plane once and let the fold be the form, and the fold survived the jump from paper to steel; Hersua folds the ribbon until it loops back on itself, and the loop is the form, which is the fold's second move. Cabrera (#337 M2) cut one T into stone and let the incision carry the body; Hersua folds one ribbon into a loop and lets the crease carry the space. **Takeaway:** Fold the sheet until it holds itself up; the crease is the structure and the inside face is the second color. Watch the crease: when a ribbon turns back on itself and the inside catches the light differently from the outside, and no frame is holding it up, the mechanism is on.

## M5. Color as light and weight

Color is not decoration; it lightens or gives weight to the form, and it works with the sun. Hersua's own formulation, quoted in the catalog: color sits "between matter and form." Acha's gloss, quoted by Hersua at length: "los colores dan ligereza o pesantez", colors lighten or give weight. Ave Dos is the proof, viewed first-hand: the exterior yellow-orange "makes a comparsa with the sun", the interior red "refers us to our interior", and the later Commons photographs show the weathered orange with white patches, the paint aging in the open air like a second material. Puerta de Manu, viewed first-hand, is deep red and magenta with bright orange-yellow folded ridges, the hot color doing the work of the form. Portal en rojo, 2018, viewed first-hand, is all red: two tall leaning slabs forming an inverted V, a doorway with small curled flaps, and the color IS the form. Obra binaria, 2010, viewed first-hand, is red textured angular slabs on black ground. Against the chain: Sebastian (#338 M8) painted the monument one color and let the color be the reading, the city supplying the rest of the palette; Hersua paints the monument two colors, one for the outside and one for the inside, and the reading changes when you cross the threshold, which is the color doing the work of the door. Silva (#339 M2) let the sun switch the prism on; Hersua lets the sun switch the color on, and the color is the switch. **Takeaway:** Give the outside one hot color and the inside another; the color is the weight, the light, and the door. Watch the threshold: when the outside is sun-yellow and the inside is blood-red, and the same slab is both colors at once, the mechanism is on.

## M6. The turning module

A movement, a body turning, sampled into a series of separate slabs, each one a frozen instant; the viewer walking past them replays the turn. Frida, 1989, viewed first-hand in the Commons photograph from Baconao, Cuba, is a row of tall faceted slabs in white, blue, and red standing on rocky ground against green forested hills: a white slab left, a big blue leaning slab at center, red and white pointed slabs right, each leaning a little further than the last, the sequence reading like a filmstrip of a turn. The catalog's own text is the mechanism stated: Frida is "a series of modular instants of that movement that Cuban women perform by nature... spaces caused by that turning in the trunk of existence." From one angle the slabs line up into a single wall; from another they fan apart into separate instants. The catalog's Fig 7 photograph, which matches this Commons image almost exactly, is almost certainly Frida mis-captioned as the Ambiente circular, and the study does not describe Frida from the catalog's Fig 6 photograph, a magenta cylinder indoors, which is almost certainly the Ambiente circular mis-captioned as Frida; the swap is carried as a high-confidence inference from the image match, not a proven caption, because the one independent witness, the Voces Archivo Hersua Frida photograph, could not be downloaded. Against the chain: Silva (#339 M7) geometrized the figure and kept it; Hersua samples the figure's movement and keeps only the instants, and the figure is gone, only the turn remains. Cabrera (#337 M4) laid one body down in sequence until it was a step; Hersua stands one turn up in sequence until it is a row of slabs, and the sequence is vertical, not horizontal. **Takeaway:** Sample a turn into slabs and let the walk replay it; the movement is the material. Watch the row: when a line of leaning slabs reads as one wall from the front and as a sequence of instants from the side, and the lean grows along the row, the mechanism is on.

## M7. The grid of discs

One identical unit, a disc, repeated on every surface of a cube or a field; the repetition is the work; the viewer's movement changes what the unit looks like, face-on a circle, edge-on a bar. Ambiente Psicomodular, 1970, viewed first-hand, is a white cubic room, 3 by 3 by 3 m, shown at the third Salon Independiente, based on a Mayan fretwork, a greca: rows of white discs mounted on the walls, the floor, and the ceiling in a grid, face-on large circles on the side walls, edge-on thin bars on the back wall, the whole room a single repeated unit. The Ambientes maquette of 1967, viewed first-hand, is the same unit in a field: discs on thin rods at different heights, gold, white, black, and brown, a forest of coins the viewer walks among. The catalog's gloss is that the greca is "the prehispanic idea of the infinite." Against the chain: Escobedo (#335 M3) repeated one frame and let one parameter drift until a snake emerged; Hersua repeats one disc and lets the viewer's angle drift until a circle becomes a bar, and the drift is the work. Silva (#339 M6) used straight lines only; Hersua uses circles only, and the circle is the straight line's opposite, a unit with no direction. **Takeaway:** Repeat one identical unit on every surface and let the walk change what it is; the unit is the infinite, the angle is the reading. Watch the unit: when a grid of discs is circles from one side and bars from the other, and the room is nothing but the repetition, the mechanism is on.

## M8. The word as material

Handwritten words on paper slips, pinned in a grid; the reader recombines them; the poem is a sculpture you read. Primer Poema Geografico, 1969, with Julio Cesar Chara and Sebastian, viewed first-hand in the catalog's black-and-white photograph, is two young men beside a white pedestal and a large dark board with a 3 by 5 grid of handwritten paper slips, legible words among them: "somos", "el principio", "de donde", "no se", "enciende", "en un", "Mira:", "la entrada", "de tu clitoris pequeno", "enamorado", "de la A y de la U", letters "ARTE O..." across the top. It is called the first geographical poem in Latin America, presented at ENAP, and the catalog's account is that it was "so natural its production resulted without anything missing or extra." The three were "los inseparables" in 1969. The grid is the structure and the words are the material, and the reader is the one who finishes the poem by reading across it. Against the chain: Gurria (#336) kept time with a hammer and let the signal be the work; Hersua keeps time with a word and lets the reading be the work, and the word is the lightest material he ever used. Sebastian (#338 M4) built the letter large enough to walk into; Hersua writes the word small enough to read, and the reading is the walk. **Takeaway:** Pin words in a grid and let the reader recombine them; the poem is a sculpture and the reading is the making. Watch the grid: when a board of small paper slips is the whole work, and the words are legible and the order is the reader's, the mechanism is on.

## M9. The body in the work

The sculpture is something a body stands inside, carries, or wears; the body completes it. Sucesos Plasticos, 1975, viewed first-hand in the catalog photograph, is the young artist himself in a brown turtleneck, standing inside a tall stack of interlinked wire rings outdoors on grass, arms raised holding the top ring: the body is inside the sculpture and the sculpture is around the body. The catalog text describes a different moment of the same performance, naked children in the streets carrying crowns, crowns made by rolling them on the university steps, later set on fire in a park and carried off by local children, "their innocence was the one who stole the art"; the photograph may be from a different moment of the same performance, or from the 1980 Sucesos Plasticos at the Espacio Escultorico, and it is not captioned here as the children carrying crowns. The work only exists while a body is in it, and the 1980 performance with children at the center of the Espacio Escultorico is the same engine at the scale of the ring. Against the chain: Silva (#339 M15) gave the work away as a gift and let the dialogue keep it alive; Hersua gives the work away as a body and lets the body keep it alive, and the gift is worn, not kept. Escobedo (#335) engineered failure, boats designed to sink; Hersua engineers wearing, rings designed to be stood in, and the standing is the work. **Takeaway:** Make the sculpture something a body can stand inside or carry; the body is the last material. Watch the body: when a person stands inside a stack of rings and the rings are the sculpture only while the person is in them, the mechanism is on.

## M10. The circle without a center

A circle of 64 identical modules around an empty lava field; the center is NOT marked, it is nature, a void; each visitor who stands in it becomes the center. The Espacio Escultorico, inaugurated 23 April 1979, is the hinge of his career: five maquettes were presented at the meeting called by Jorge Carpizo, Coordinador de Humanidades, the first was chosen, and rector Guillermo Soberon approved it unchanged; the built work follows Hersua's project, and the authorship is collective, six artists working by unanimity, so the attribution to any single member is futile and both facts are carried. The ring is 120 m in outer diameter, about 92.78 m of inner lava, a platform 13.61 m wide, 64 prefabricated concrete prisms, rectangular base 9 by 3 m, 4 m high, in groups of 16 per quarter with wider gaps at the four cardinal points; the diameter is carried as about 142 m in one source, Juan Acha via Spanish Wikipedia, which may include the outer landscape or be an error; the prism count is carried as 34 in the DGCS UNAM 2023 piece and Latamarte, and 73 on the Mexico City government site, against the 64 of the majority. Hersua's own numerology is 6 plus 4 equals 10 equals 1: "the one is the result of the ten." The catalog's own essay says the design came from the sensation of standing at Monte Alban. The center was settled by Leonel Pereznieto Castro, Carpizo's successor: the center is nature itself, cleared of plants, snakes, and scorpions. Luis Cardoza y Aragon, in the support group, insisted the prisms stay solid and silent. Against the chain: Goeritz (#321) built the Ruta de la Amistad as a corridor experienced from a car; Hersua builds the corridor as a ring experienced on foot, and the ring has no front, only a center that is empty. Silva (#339) proposed the project and Hersua's maquette won it, which is the one clean fact inside a collective authorship. **Takeaway:** Build a ring of identical modules around an empty center and let the visitor be the center; the void is the work and the ring is its frame. Watch the center: when a ring of leaning prisms surrounds rough black lava with plants, and standing in the middle makes the ring a horizon, the mechanism is on.

## M11. The pyramid of today

The Espacio Escultorico is a pyramid for today, not built of stone on a hill but of a ring around a lava field. Acha called it "la piramide de hoy", the pyramid of today, and "the modern pyramid"; the catalog calls the Rotonda "the derivation itself of the modern pyramid." The design came from the sensation of standing at Monte Alban, and the near-identical precedent is the circular pyramid of Cuicuilco, a low mound with a circular plan, which the Firenze Architettura essay names directly. A low triangular module repeated around a circle reads as a mound from outside and a crater from inside: the prisms are 4 m high against a 120 m diameter, so the ring is a slope cut into 64 pieces, and the lava field at the center is the crater floor. Against the chain: Silva (#339 M11) laid his serpent as a line in the lava field and let the field be the ground; Hersua raises the field's edge into a ring and lets the field be the center, and the pyramid is the ring. Cabrera (#337 M10) built a museum in a garden because the institution would not house her; Hersua builds a pyramid in a lava field because the institution could not hold the pyramid, and the lava field is the museum. **Takeaway:** Cut a pyramid's slope into identical modules and lay them in a ring; the mound is the pyramid and the crater is its heart. Watch the mound: when a low ring reads as a pyramid from outside and a crater from inside, and the center is raw lava, the mechanism is on.

## M12. The maquette as the real work

The work is decided at maquette scale; the big version is a scaling-up of a model that already works. Five maquettes were made for the Espacio Escultorico; the first was chosen unchanged, and Hersua keeps the model in his studio. Maqueta Puerta de Manu, 2008, painted bronze, 36 by 34 by 15 cm, precedes the full-size 2009 work, painted ferroconcrete, 3 by 3 by 1.4 m, by one year, and the full-size work repeats the maquette's two slabs and its gap. Ritmo cosmico, 2020, painted aluminum, 331 by 39 by 15 cm, has its maquette, 31 by 39 by 15 cm, the same year, the same folded forms at a tenth of the size. The small maquettes Puertas del Sol and Arco Hersua, viewed first-hand, are the monumental arches Gwendolyn, Ovi, and Hened at tabletop scale, the same two leaning legs, the same open crown. Against the chain: Gurria (#336 M8) declared the maquette finished because the monument is the maquette grown; Hersua declares the maquette finished because the ring is the maquette grown, and the ring was chosen at maquette scale by a rector who never asked for a change. Sebastian (#338 M9) designed the idea at hand scale and let the city scale it; Hersua designs the ring at hand scale and lets the university scale it, and the scaling is the whole career. **Takeaway:** Decide the work as a maquette and let the site scale it; if it does not work in the hand, it will not work in the lava field. Watch the model: when a small model already has the whole idea, the fold, the gap, the two parts, and the monumental work repeats it at ten times the size, the mechanism is on.

## M13. Rough material, honest matter

The material is left rough, quartz stone carved on site, hammered bronze, textured ferroconcrete, so the hand and the place stay visible. Hened, viewed first-hand in two photographs, is rough dark stone, quartz-grain stones, 7,000 kg, carved at Wondabyne in Australia and then relocated to Mt Penang Parklands at Kariong: a rough stone arch, two massive uneven legs with a pointed top, standing on flat rock among green trees, the close-up showing the inner face painted deep red and the rough chisel-marked pale grey-green stone. Arco Hersua, viewed first-hand, is dark hammered metal with a wrinkled, organic surface and greenish-silver patina. Puerta de Manu, viewed first-hand, is heavily textured painted ferroconcrete, deep red and magenta with bright orange-yellow folded ridges, the texture visible in the photograph like a skin. Against the chain: Cabrera (#337 M3) let the stone stay a stone and kept its veining loud; Hersua lets the stone stay a stone and paints its inner face red, and the paint is the only smooth thing on it. Silva (#339 M9) used polychrome stone and let the flat colors be the figure; Hersua uses rough stone and lets the chisel marks be the figure, and the color is a single red face inside the roughness. **Takeaway:** Leave the material rough and let the hand stay visible; the chisel mark is the signature. Watch the surface: when a stone arch carries its chisel marks and a bronze arch carries its hammer wrinkles, and the roughness is the same at every scale, the mechanism is on.

## M14. The performance that steals itself

The work is an action that ends with the audience taking it away. Sucesos Plasticos, presented at the fourth Festival Internacional Cervantino in Guanajuato in 1976, is the text: naked children in the streets carrying crowns, crowns made by rolling them on the university steps, crowns later set on fire in a park and carried off by local children, "their innocence was the one who stole the art." The work is gone and only the story remains, and the story is the work's afterlife. The same engine runs in the 1980 Sucesos Plasticos at the Espacio Escultorico, a performance with children at the center of the ring. Against the chain: Silva (#339 M15) gave the work away as a gift and let the dialogue keep it alive; Hersua gives the work away as a fire and lets the theft keep it alive, and the theft is the gift. Escobedo (#335) engineered failure, boats designed to sink; Hersua engineers disappearance, crowns designed to be stolen, and the disappearance is the work. **Takeaway:** Make the work so it can be taken away, and let the taking be the ending; the story is what survives. Watch the theft: when children carry the work off and set it on fire, and the work is gone and the story is told for fifty years, the mechanism is on.

## M15. The labyrinth that returns you

A maze whose end is its own entrance; the visitor gets confused and turns back; the work is a loop, not a path. Arte De-Para, Proposiciones, 1973, Palacio de Bellas Artes, Mexico City, was a solo show of three Ambientes Urbanos with public participation, and it began with a labyrinth whose end met a copy of the entrance: "people got confused and went back." The controversy was the point: "other artists said this is not sculpture." No photograph of the labyrinth was found in the sources read, and none is described here; the mechanism is carried from the artist's own account in the Voces interview and the catalog, not from an image. Against the chain: Goeritz (#321) built emotional architecture as a sequence of spaces; Hersua builds emotional architecture as a sequence that returns to its own start, and the return is the emotion. Silva (#339 M10) stacked the glyph until the tower read like writing; Hersua loops the path until the maze reads like a circle, and the circle is the reading. **Takeaway:** End the labyrinth at its own entrance and let the visitor turn back; the loop is the work, not the way through. Watch the return: when the door you arrive at is the door you entered by, and you turn around and walk out the way you came, the mechanism is on.

## M16. Silence and solitude as the material

The sculpture is a place of solitude and silence; the empty lava field, the red interior, the seat in the Rotonda are all invitations to sit alone and look. Hersua's own words, to the Gaceta UNAM in October 2024, standing in the Espacio Escultorico at 84: "Cuando vengan de preferencia haganlo solos", when you come, preferably come alone. And: "The Espacio Escultorico is a site of solitude, of silence, and there is nothing more creative than that." The Rotonda de la Puerta de Manu, viewed first-hand from a drone, is four modules including seats inside, with vegetation slopes: "a game of forms to introduce ourselves into the work... sitting is meditative." The ring's center is cleared of plants, snakes, and scorpions, and Cardoza y Aragon insisted the prisms stay solid and silent, so the silence is designed, not accidental. Against the chain: Silva (#339 M3) suspended bodies in space and let the silence hold them; Hersua clears the center and lets the silence hold the visitor, and the visitor is the suspended body. Cabrera (#337 M11) made the photograph carry the monument; Hersua makes the silence carry the monument, and the silence needs no photograph. **Takeaway:** Build the work as a place to be alone in; the empty center is the invitation and the seat is the second half of the sculpture. Watch the solitude: when a ring has nothing in the middle, a bench sits inside a broken circle, and a red passage is walked through alone, the mechanism is on.

## Aesthetic logic

The career is one long demonstration that geometry is a place, and that a place can be made by standing a door in it, a ring around it, or a silence inside it. The logic runs: build the work as a place to walk through (M1); lean two legs toward each other and do not let them meet (M2); make the work in two parts and leave the gap for the viewer (M3); fold the sheet until it holds itself up (M4); give the outside one hot color and the inside another (M5); sample a turn into slabs and let the walk replay it (M6); repeat one identical unit on every surface and let the walk change what it is (M7); pin words in a grid and let the reader recombine them (M8); make the sculpture something a body can stand inside or carry (M9); build a ring of identical modules around an empty center and let the visitor be the center (M10); cut a pyramid's slope into identical modules and lay them in a ring (M11); decide the work as a maquette and let the site scale it (M12); leave the material rough and let the hand stay visible (M13); make the work so it can be taken away, and let the taking be the ending (M14); end the labyrinth at its own entrance and let the visitor turn back (M15); and build the work as a place to be alone in (M16). The through-line is his own phrase, "la escultura transitable": the sculpture you walk through. That is why the same door can be a 35 cm bronze, a 7 m arch, and a 120 m ring: nothing is decorated, everything is reduced to the operation, and the operation is always something a body can walk through, stand inside, or sit in alone. He rejects the Ruptura label in his own essay, calling it the later erroneously called rupture generation, and places himself in the neovanguardias, the geometrismo mexicano, the nueva escultura sesentera; the label does not matter, the operation does. The color sits between matter and form, the silence sits between the prisms, and the viewer sits between the two parts, and each of those betweens is the work.
