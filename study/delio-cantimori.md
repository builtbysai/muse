The watchable behaviour is a figure that is invisible until the dossier opens; the portrait assembles itself from stamps, seals and marginal notes, and dissolves when the file closes.

**Takeaway:** open the dossier, and the piece should let the portrait assemble from the erasure itself.

## Aesthetic logic

Cantimori's aesthetic logic is that the idea must be seen incarnated, and the incarnation is not decoration, it is the mechanism, because a historian who keeps historiography clean from the passions of politics must show his ideas walking, choosing, paying, and the walking is the life, and the life is the archive, and the archive is the exile, and the exile is the road, and the road is the tolerance, and the tolerance is the legacy. The logic runs in three moves. First, the idea incarnated: an idea is not a definition but a person walking, choosing, paying, see M11, and the word without a figure evaporates, and the figure is the heretic, and the heretic is the exile, and the exile is the minority that survives by moving, see M2, and the moving is the pattern, and the pattern is the subject, and the subject is the piece, and the piece must be an idea before it is a picture, because the picture without the incarnation is the abstraction, see M11, and the abstraction evaporates, and the evaporating is the error. Second, the minority and its legacy: the heretics' European legacy is tolerance, the aconfessionality of the state, and unprejudiced rationalism founded on the individual ethical conscience, and the legacy is the tolerance, and the tolerance is the Nicodemite double life, see M4, and the double life is the humble religion nobody catalogued, see M13, and the humble religion is the corners, and the corners are the lights, and the lights are the piece, and the piece must let the minority survive by moving and the double life leave no trace, because the doctrine that catalogues everything is the inquisitor, see M1, and the inquisitor is the error. Third, the intimate master and the voice: influence travels by conversation, letters and seminars, never by a school, see M8, and the letters are the real book, see M20, and the real book outlives the monographs, and the outliving is the correspondence, and the correspondence is the key, see M22, and the key is a door, not a doctrine, and the door is the Warburg, and the Warburg is the salamanders, see M23, and the salamanders pass through fire without burning, and the passing through is the detachment, and the detachment is the clarity, and the clarity is the voice from beyond the tomb, see M24, and the voice refuses to fade, and the refusing is the logic, and the logic is that the pieces that follow from it must show the incarnated idea and the moving minority and the intimate thread, and must report the fascist years of 1926 to the late 1930s plainly, with the self-interrogation, see M16, and never as decoration, because the pitiless self-analysis is the method, and the method is the point.

## Load-bearing corrections

C0. D2 citation variant. The Fondazione page cites Cantimori's preface to Mussolini il rivoluzionario as "Torino 1966, pp. XVI-XVII", while Treccani, Miccoli, cites "Torino 1965, pp. XIII e seg." and the Fondazione's own narrative dates the volume's publication to 1965. The preface's page numbers differ between printings. ADOPT: first edition, Torino, Einaudi, 1965, pp. XIII ff.

C1. Brief date for Umanesimo e religione nel Rinascimento. The brief says "(1980, posthumous)". First edition is Torino, Einaudi, 1975, VIII plus 309 pages; 1980 is a later edition. Posthumous either way, Cantimori died 1966. ADOPT: 1975.

C2. Brief "Eretici italiani del Cinquecento (1939, reworked 1967)". The 1967 second edition was a REPRINT, never revised: Cochrane and Tedeschi, Journal of Modern History XXXIX, 1967: "His classic work, Eretici italiani del Cinquecento (Florence, 1939) was reprinted in 1967 but never revised." Do NOT write "reworked".

C3. it.wikipedia's date range "Studi di storia, Einaudi, 1959-1969" is misleading. First edition Torino 1959, xx plus 867 pages; 2nd edition 1965; 3rd edition; the 1976 reprint is in 3 volumes. "1959-1969" is a catalogue span, not the publication history. ADOPT: 1959, first edition.

C4. it.wikipedia lists under Curatele: "Giacobini italiani, Collezione Scrittori d'Italia, Bari, Laterza, 1936." WRONG. Volume I is 1956, volume II, with De Felice, is 1964. The 1936 date is an error in it.wikipedia; all other sources, Scrittori d'Italia list, Museo Revoltella, OpenEdition, Google Books, NDL catalogue, agree on 1956-1964. ADOPT: 1956 and 1964.

C5. Schmitt edition date. it.wikipedia says Cantimori published the Schmitt writings "Nel 1936... titolandoli 'Principi politici del nazionalsocialismo'." WRONG. The volume's title page, archive.org scan, is dated 1935-XIII; Treccani says "i Principii politici del Nazionalsocialismo di Carl Schmitt, del 1935"; Engramma agrees, "L'anno prima [del 1937] aveva pubblicato per Sansoni una serie di scritti di Carl Schmitt". ADOPT: 1935.

C6. D3 nuance, Manifesto of 101. Cantimori did NOT sign the Manifesto of 101. The signatories named in every list found are Colletti, Cafagna, Sirugo, the drafters, De Felice, Caracciolo, Muscetta, Sapegno, Asor Rosa, Siciliano, Crisafulli, Maccanico, Petri, Spriano, Vespignani, Socrate and others; Cantimori appears in none. His exit was a silent non-renewal at the end of 1956. "Left with De Felice in 1956" holds as same-year, same-wave, Fondazione: De Felice left "con altri intellettuali, fra i quali Cantimori", not as co-signatories. ADOPT: same year, same wave; no shared signature.

C7. D2 wording. The brief's "great talent as explorer and researcher" is a correct short rendering of "grande talento di esploratore e di ricercatore", but that phrase only OPENS a much longer sentence, quoted in full under D2 above. Do NOT present the short phrase as the whole quotation.

C8. es.wikipedia gives Cantimori's death as "13 de septiembre de 1958". WRONG. Died 13 September 1966; every other source, it, de, ru Wikipedias, both Treccani entries, Fondazione, agrees on 1966. Do NOT use es.wikipedia for dates.

C9. D3 cause. Hungary was the occasion, not the sole cause. Treccani, Miccoli: he left "all'indomani dei fatti di Ungheria, ma soprattutto... per gravi dissensi sulla linea culturale del partito", above all over grave dissent on the party's cultural line. The 1955-56 polemics, Epiloghi storiografici, Pro e contra, precede the exit. ADOPT: occasion Hungary, cause the cultural line.

C10. Machiavelli, Guicciardini, Galileo placement. The Machiavelli and Guicciardini essays are in Storia della letteratura italiana, volume IV, Milano, 1966, pages 7-86 and 87-148; the Galileo essay was left UNFINISHED and published posthumously in Saggi su G. Galilei, edited by Garin, 1967, reprinted in Storici e storia, 1971, pages 657-674. The three are the first traces of the late, interrupted project for a history of Italian religious life. Do NOT call the Galileo essay finished.

C11. Conversando di storia is not a general essay collection. It is specifically the letters Cantimori wrote to the director of the Genoese review Itinerari between 1960 and 1964, Treccani, it.wikipedia. ADOPT: letters, 1960-1964.

C12. Translation attribution. The Communist Manifesto translation, Einaudi, 1948, still in print, reprinted 11 times 1962-1994, was Emma Cantimori's ALONE, signed by her only; Delio's role was the shared "laboratorio artigiano" and the 1945 Pisa lectures on the Manifesto. Do NOT attribute the Manifesto translation to Delio. The Capital book I translation, 1951-52, WAS joint.
