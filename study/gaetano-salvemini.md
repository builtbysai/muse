## M1. The document as indictment

Take the regime's own papers and publish them so the facts convict, and the publishing is the mechanism, because a stack of seized investigation files is carried across a border in a suitcase, and the same pages reappear on a London library shelf, stamped and numbered, and then in a New York bookshop, and each republication is another courtroom, and the suitcase is the smuggling, and the stamping is the evidence, and the evidence is the indictment, and the indictment is the book, and the book is the piece, and the piece must show the suitcase crossing the border and the stamped pages becoming a book in a shop window. The watchable behaviour is a stack of papers carried across a border in a suitcase, reappearing stamped and numbered on a library shelf, then as a book in a shop window.

**Takeaway:** carry the papers across the border, and the piece should let the stamped pages convict.

## M2. The exile letter as network

Use private letters to plan, recruit and place oneself, the letter as a job application, a programme and a lifeline at once, and the writing is the mechanism, because a man with one foot on the ground and one in the air writes to a lawyer across the ocean, "I foresee that I will soon be forced to leave Italy... I live by the day", and the letter crosses the Atlantic, and an invitation comes back, and a lecture tour follows, and the writing is the planning, and the planning is the network, and the network is the survival, and the survival is the letter, and the letter is the piece, and the piece must show the letter crossing the ocean and the invitation coming back. The watchable behaviour is a letter written by a man with one foot on the ground and one in the air, crossing an ocean, and an invitation coming back.

**Takeaway:** write the letter across the ocean, and the piece should let the invitation come back.

## M3. The lives as method

Periodise your own life by relocations and rebuild from zero each time, publicly, and the counting is the mechanism, because a man writes to a friend, "I am starting life over for the third time: I began it at 17 when I arrived in Florence; I rebuilt it at 35 after losing everything in Messina; I am starting it again at 52 for the third time", and ten years later the count is four, and after the return it is five, and each number is a life, and each life is a city, and the city is the rebuilding, and the rebuilding is the counting, and the counting is the method, and the method is the piece, and the piece must show the lives counted one by one, each with its city, and the count growing as the man lives. The watchable behaviour is a man counting his lives to a friend, three in 1925, four in 1935, five after the return, each life with its city.

**Takeaway:** count the lives as they are lived, and the piece should let the number grow.

## M4. The weekly as school

A journal is not a megaphone but a classroom that trains a political class over decades, and the training is the mechanism, because a diary page says the way out is to find twenty men who will devote twenty years to political culture, above all through a very well-made weekly, and the weekly appears every Saturday, and its readers become its writers, and its writers become deputies and teachers, and the appearing is the teaching, and the teaching is the training, and the training is the weekly, and the weekly is the school, and the school is the piece, and the piece must show the weekly appearing Saturday after Saturday and its readers turning into its writers. The watchable behaviour is a weekly appearing every Saturday, its readers slowly turning into its writers, its writers into deputies and teachers.

**Takeaway:** run the weekly for twenty years, and the piece should let the readers become the writers.

## M5. Concretismo: probity, not impartiality

Start from facts, mistrust general theories, and admit your passions while guarding against them, and the admitting is the mechanism, because a preface says "Noi non possiamo essere imparziali. Possiamo essere soltanto intellettualmente onesti... L'imparzialita e un sogno, la probita e un dovere", we cannot be impartial, we can only be intellectually honest, impartiality is a dream, probity is a duty, and the passions sit on the table in plain sight, labelled, while the facts are weighed one by one, and the labelling is the honesty, and the honesty is the probity, and the probity is the method, and the method is the piece, and the piece must show the passions labelled on the table and the facts weighed one by one. The watchable behaviour is passions laid on a table and labelled, while facts are weighed one by one beside them.

**Takeaway:** label the passions on the table, and the piece should weigh the facts one by one.

## M6. The school as civic laboratory

The public school is where a critical conscience is made or broken, so school reform IS democratic politics, and the making is the mechanism, because a classroom of boys is taught to work by individual effort and intellectual autonomy, and the same classroom, taught the other way, is taught to obey and to pass examinations, and the two classrooms stand side by side, and in one the pupils argue, and in the other they repeat, and the arguing is the conscience, and the repeating is the obedience, and the obedience is the breaking, and the breaking is the politics, and the politics is the school, and the school is the piece, and the piece must show the two classrooms side by side, one arguing, one repeating. The watchable behaviour is two classrooms side by side, one arguing, one repeating.

**Takeaway:** build the arguing classroom, and the piece should set the repeating one beside it.

## M7. The meridionalist reversal

The South is not Italy's backward margin but the key to the national question; link northern workers to southern peasants, and the linking is the mechanism, because a map of Italy hangs on a wall with the South shaded as a problem, and a hand turns the map upside down, and the shaded part is now the centre, and the railways, the taxes, the votes all run through it, and the turning is the reversal, and the reversal is the diagnosis, and the diagnosis is the programme, and the programme is the piece, and the piece must show the map turned upside down and the shaded part becoming the centre. The watchable behaviour is a map of Italy turned upside down, the shaded South becoming the centre, the railways and taxes and votes running through it.

**Takeaway:** turn the map upside down, and the piece should run everything through the South.

## M8. Name the go-between

Expose a power system by naming its intermediaries, with documents, not by denouncing abstractions, and the naming is the mechanism, because a diagram of a prime minister's power shows only a crown at the top and a crowd at the bottom, and then names appear in the middle, landlord, fixer, ballot-box stuffer, each with a document pinned to his name, and the middle fills with faces, and the crown shrinks, and the filling is the exposure, and the exposure is the naming, and the naming is the indictment, and the indictment is the piece, and the piece must show the empty middle filling with named faces, each with a document pinned to it. The watchable behaviour is a power diagram whose empty middle fills with named faces, each with a document pinned to it, while the crown at the top shrinks.

**Takeaway:** name the faces in the middle, and the piece should pin a document to each one.

## M9. The anti-myth history

Take a national myth, the Risorgimento, Mazzini, the "mutilated victory", fascism-as-saviour, and dismantle it with sources, and the dismantling is the mechanism, because a heroic statue stands on a plinth with a plaque full of fine words, and a historian arrives with a folder of letters and ledgers, and the plaque's words come off one by one and are replaced by the folder's sentences, and the statue stays standing but means something else, and the replacing is the dismantling, and the dismantling is the history, and the history is the piece, and the piece must show the plaque's words coming off and the folder's sentences going on. The watchable behaviour is a heroic statue whose plaque words come off one by one and are replaced by sentences from a folder of letters and ledgers.

**Takeaway:** take the fine words off the plaque, and the piece should put the folder's sentences on.

## M10. The exile as observatory

Distance is a method; the outsider sees what the insider cannot, and the seeing is the mechanism, because a man writes from Paris that he would feel more like an exile in Italy, and that in London he is at home, in the homeland of his heart, free among free men, a man among men, and the same Italian street is shown twice, once from inside, crowded and loud, and once from across the Channel, small and legible, and the shrinking is the clarity, and the clarity is the distance, and the distance is the method, and the method is the piece, and the piece must show the street twice, crowded from inside and legible from across the water. The watchable behaviour is the same Italian street shown twice, crowded and loud from inside, small and legible from across the Channel.

**Takeaway:** step across the water, and the piece should show the street small and legible.

## M13. The remittance weapon

Turn the emigrants' savings into pressure on the regime, and the turning is the mechanism, because a stream of small envelopes crosses the Atlantic every month, each one a worker's savings sent home, and one by one the envelopes stop, the stream dries up, and a ledger in Rome shows the shortfall growing, and the drying is the weapon, and the weapon is the refusal, and the refusal is the emigrant, and the emigrant is the piece, and the piece must show the envelopes crossing the ocean and then stopping, and the ledger in Rome showing the shortfall. The watchable behaviour is a stream of envelopes crossing an ocean and then stopping one by one, a ledger showing the shortfall growing.

## M14. The trilogy as escalating indictment

Return to the same enemy in three books, each wider and cooler than the last, and the returning is the mechanism, because the first book is hot and fast, a dictatorship denounced in the year of its making, and the second book is slower, a decade of diplomacy laid out document by document, and the third book is the coldest, the economics of oppression counted in wages and bread, and the three books stand on a shelf in a row, rising like steps, and the rising is the escalation, and the escalation is the indictment, and the indictment is the trilogy, and the trilogy is the piece, and the piece must show the three books standing in a row, rising like steps, each cooler than the last. The watchable behaviour is three books standing in a row like steps, the first hot and fast, the second slower, the third coldest, counting wages and bread.

**Takeaway:** build the three steps, and the piece should climb them from hot to cold.

## M15. Democracy as method

Redefine democracy as a set of procedures and guarantees, not a doctrine or a mood, and the redefining is the mechanism, because a word, democracy, is shown as a mood, a crowd cheering, and then the crowd dissolves and what is left is a table with a ballot box, a court, a free press and a school, each one a small machine that has to work, and the dissolving is the redefinition, and the redefinition is the method, and the method is the democracy, and the democracy is the piece, and the piece must show the cheering crowd dissolving into the ballot box, the court, the press and the school. The watchable behaviour is a cheering crowd dissolving into a ballot box, a court, a free press and a school, each a small machine that has to work.

**Takeaway:** dissolve the crowd into machines, and the piece should keep each machine working.

## M16. The historian as public witness

The scholar's duty is to testify, in public, with evidence, and the testifying is the mechanism, because a historian stands up in a hall and reads from a list, names and dates, and the list is handed to the police, to the navy, to a committee of Congress, and the handing over is the testimony, and the testimony is the duty, and the duty is the historian, and the historian is the piece, and the piece must show the list being read aloud and then handed over, copy by copy, to the authorities. ## M17. The refusal to reconcile

Break with every party and ally rather than compromise a principle; accept isolation as the price, and the breaking is the mechanism, because a man stands at a table where parties sit, and one by one he pushes his chair back, the socialists, the liberals, the exiles' own movement, and each time he walks out alone, and the room empties around him, and the emptying is the price, and the price is the principle, and the principle is the refusal, and the refusal is the piece, and the piece must show the chairs pushed back one by one and the man walking out alone each time. ## M18. The third force

## Evolution: five lives, early vs recent

PHASE 1, APPRENTICESHIP, 1890-1901. Florence and the provinces: a scholarship in 1890, the Istituto di Studi Superiori under Pasquale Villari, who called him "perhaps the best pupil of our Institute" in 1894, masters Cesare Paoli and Alberto Del Vecchio, contacts Carlo Placci and the Berensons, fellow students Ernesta Bittanti, Cesare Battisti and the Mondolfo brothers, the laurea thesis of 1894 and the perfezionamento thesis of 1895, the first book from the second. Teaching at Palermo from October 1895, Faenza 1896-98, Lodi 1898-1900, the Liceo Galilei in Florence 1900. More Loria than Marx in the method; Cattaneo discovered in the Lodi years. A first concorso failed in 1899; the Messina chair of modern history in 1901, aged 28.

PHASE 2, THE SOUTHERNER IN PUBLIC, 1901-1919. Marriage to Maria Minervini, Thursday 21 October 1897; five children, Filippo, Leonida, Corrado, Ugo, Elena. The 1901 prolusione, the French Revolution and Mazzini books of 1905, the teachers' federation with Giuseppe Kirner, the 1905 Royal Commission and the 1908 school book with Alfredo Galletti. Then the rupture: the Messina earthquake, Monday 28 December 1908, wife, five children and sister dead before his eyes under a window architrave, and his own words, "I am a miserable wretch, without home or hearth, who has seen the happiness of eleven years destroyed in two minutes." The Pisa chair 1910-11, the Florence chair December 1916. The Giolitti war of 1910, the PSI break over Libya in 1911, L'Unita, the 1913 Puglia electoral defeat and the 1914 provincial success, democratic interventionism, a volunteer discharged for illness in December 1915, marriage to Fernande Dauriac in 1916, the Adriatic compromise of 1918, deputy 1919-1921 on an ex-combatants list, as an independent radical.

PHASE 3, THE TURN AND THE FLIGHT, 1919-1925. Disappointment with parliament; the 1922 Cattaneo anthology; the 1922-23 diary; the first England trip in the autumn of 1922, learning English, "I am increasingly feeling like a foreigner in Italy. I have wasted thirty years of my life in politics"; the King's College lectures of autumn 1923, arriving as an illegal immigrant, his passport denied; the December 1923 letter to Gino Speranza planning exile work. Fascism first underestimated, "a disgusting brigandish and carnivalesque tragedy" that would not last; after the Matteotti murder, Tuesday 10 June 1924, the turn: the Circolo di cultura, Non Mollare from January 1925, arrest Monday 8 June 1925, Regina Coeli then the Murate, trial Monday 13 July 1925, release on bail, amnesty Saturday 25 July 1925, flight in early August 1925, accompanied at first by his pupil Federico Chabod, resignation letter Thursday 5 November 1925, accepted Wednesday 25 November 1925, then destitution, loss of citizenship in 1926 and seizure of property.

PHASE 4, EXILE, 1925-1949. Paris and London 1925-1934, living by the pen and lecturing; the Matteotti papers to the LSE; the 1927 US tour; L'opera degli emigrati; the trilogy; the Concentrazione antifascista of 1927; Giustizia e Liberta from November 1929; the Shaw polemic of 1927; the 1935 Paris Congress of Writers, against Stalinist communism; the Harvard years from Spring Term 1934, "the best of his life", the 15 March 1935 Widener letter; the democracy essays; Historian and Scientist; the Mazzini Society of 1939, founded in Northampton, Massachusetts, Ascoli's takeover within six months and Salvemini's withdrawal; US citizenship 1940, renounced 1954; the FBI relationship 1940-1944; What to Do with Italy?; the 9 August 1944 letter on Giustizia e Liberta. The exile's self-image moves from the "wandering Jew of antifascism", his own phrase, to the "medieval monk" of Tagliacozzo's biography, a caricature the 2023 scholarship dismantles, the "operational base in the eye of the storm", Killinger 2002.

PHASE 5, RETURN, 1949-1957. A brief trip in the summer and autumn of 1947, the outsider who saw the PCI/DC polarisation clearly; the definitive return in the summer of 1949, aged 75, turning 76 that September; the Florence chair for two years; "Come stavamo dicendo l'ultima volta..."; Prelude to World War II, 1953; the Feltrinelli Prize and the Oxford honorary degree, both June 1955; the polemic against Croce and the old Italy; the third-force journalism in Il Mondo and Il Ponte; the move to Sorrento for health reasons; death Friday 6 September 1957. Vivarelli's verdict, quoted by DBI: Salvemini was, in the end, "uno sconfitto", a defeated man. The message outlived the politics.

## Aesthetic logic

Salvemini's work is not beautiful in the way Croce's is, and that is the point. Croce's mechanism is the lyric closing cleanly; Salvemini's is the document refusing to close. His aesthetic is the file, the list, the ledger, the dated page, and the beauty, where there is any, is in the completeness of the file and the bluntness of the sentence. He writes short sentences, names names, and dates everything, and the dating is the style: a diary entry of 27 November 1922 does more work than a chapter of theory, because the date pins the thought to a day when it could still be acted on. His colours are the colours of paper: ink, newsprint, the brown of a seized archive, the grey of a London library. His motion is the motion of a man packing and unpacking: Florence, Messina, Pisa, Florence, Paris, London, New York, Cambridge, Florence again, and each move is a new life counted in public. The piece that follows him should never be lush or lyrical; it should be filed, stamped, dated, and counted, and its one lyrical moment is the sentence that resumes after twenty-four years, the chalk continuing where it stopped.

**Takeaway:** file it, stamp it, date it, and the piece should let one sentence resume after twenty-four years.
