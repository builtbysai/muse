# Theodora Laird (feeo): Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-01. ig-artist-study #257.

**Site:** @_feeo_ (7,524 followers, public, no verified badge; bio lists mgmt and EU/UK bookings). Theodora Laird is a London vocalist, producer, and multidisciplinary artist; feeo is her solo project. First heard as the voice on Loraine James's 'For You And I' (Hyperdub, 2019), she writes alone, performs with her partner Caius Williams's live energy beside her, and co-produces the GRAIN residency. Her debut LP 'Goodness' arrived on AD 93 in October 2025.

feeo is the project where song, story, and body are one working material. She hears whole songs in her head and then deliberately abandons them; she writes lyrics from prose about cliff faces; she builds tracks out of opposite poles held at once, dark against light, sub frequencies you feel against top detail you hear. Her visual world says the same thing: a small wet figure against a vast beach, a gold body against black, a hot-pink wordmark stamped over monochrome. The system is always the same: let opposites hold each other, then watch what happens in the middle.

**Doorway:** Hop from the Caius Williams study (#256): his weekly playing partner, his life partner, his Crosspiece duo partner, and the co-producer of the GRAIN residency. Where the Williams study is about a bassist who makes the body of the instrument the music, the Laird study is about a vocalist who makes the body the image and the story the score. They are two halves of one working household: he brings the live energy, she brings the songs, and neither process crosses into the other's. Her own account of it, from the Struma+Iodine interview read in full: "I can hear everything" in her head when she writes; he is "not so involved in the recording, writing side of it."

## M1. The heard-then-abandoned song

Her own account of the composing mechanism (Struma+Iodine, read in full): she hears the full song in her head before anything exists, "I can hear everything," and writes the vocal part from that hearing. Then she abandons the imagined arrangement on purpose. The imagined arrangement is her "white whale": every time she chases it, "it always sounds bad." Overcooking kills the song. So the process is a two-stroke engine: total inner hearing, then deliberate erasure of the plan. What survives is the vocal line and the accidents around it. Songwriting, she says, "feels almost like an accident when you write a good song"; refining is "taking more stuff away"; she wants to be "a bit more ambiguous." The visible corollary: the 'run over' EP (2024) campaign posts show the campaign apparatus, announce shoot, singles, MVs, but the mechanism underneath is subtraction. The pieces that come from this mechanism should behave like it: a full form is promised, then withheld, and what remains is truer.

**Takeaway:** hear the whole song, then refuse it; the piece is what survives the abandonment.

## M2. Poetry-first writing

'The Mountain' (2025, AD 93) began as prose, not lyrics: she describes looking at "a hill in the south of France... just looking at the cliff face, just writing about it," and the song came out of the paragraph. She keeps a working distinction between poetry and lyrics: poetry can sit still and be complete, but lyrics have to land with melody, phrasing, and tone, and "you want things to land properly which can completely dictate the arrangement." The pipeline is prose first, then the lyric is a translation that bends the music around it. The PRS Women Make Music grant (2022) confirms the structure as doctrine: the funded project was planned as two four-track EPs plus "a body of written poetry and prose," the writing as a formal limb of the release, not liner-note garnish. In 2025 she printed a triptych of stories as a hand-bound book through zone6press (f10: open chapbook reading "Scene 1 Preface/Prayer," Narrator/Chorus prose). The writing practice has a printed object, a publisher, and a layout; it is as much the work as the records.

**Takeaway:** write the scene before the song; let the paragraph bend the arrangement.

## M3. Oppositional forces, with a frequency map

She names her compositional engine directly: songs work "within opposite poles, dark/light, self-empowered/fragile, forceful/vulnerable." The press copy for 'Goodness' carries her formulation: "an exploration of simultaneous yet opposing states of being... Each song is an adumbration; a partial sketch of one aspect of the LP, each finding its complete meaning when read in the context of the whole." Then she gives this metaphysics an engineering specification. In production she works with frequency contrast as the sonic illustration of the opposites: "you feel sub frequencies, and you hear the detail on the top." She is specific about kick drums and how they behave on sound systems, into gabber and free tekno and minimal dance arrangements, cites Burial's separation of top texture from low rumble as the influence, and dislikes the warehouse-reverb Berlin techno kick. The mechanism is exact and portable: pick two poles, put one in the bass you feel and one in the treble you hear, and let the opposition be the composition. Her singles demonstrate the range the engine covers: 'The Mountain' ("tale of solitude"), 'The Hammer Strikes The Bell' ("Delphic fever dream," Clash), the 'run over' tracks with titles like 'the world weeps her cancer conceived in sweet england'.

**Takeaway:** one pole in the body, one in the ear; the contrast is the arrangement.

## M4. The elemental body in vast space

Her press and campaign imagery runs a consistent visual system across 18 inspected frames: a small human body against a large natural element, usually in black and white. f06, the 'run over' cover: a wet dark figure reclining on a pebble beach while surf spray explodes around her. f17, the Edition Festival flyer: her body floating in sparkling water among rocks, hot-pink 'feeO' stamped over it. f08, 'Oil spill gull': an extreme close-up of a wet profile, oily sheen on skin, on white. f14, the CTM 2026 flyer: her floating in negative-processed black and white with star specks, red band with the stylised 'feeO' wordmark. The lineage is in the moodboard posts: f01, a scan of gold-painted Butoh dancers with motion blur, posted as "visual vocabulary." The body is never decorative; it is the sensor the landscape presses against. Her vocal training is the sonic version of the same discipline: classical singing from age 12, musical theatre (she played the villain in Les Miserables), folk song, and a father, actor Trevor Laird, who coached her breath control using Anita Baker records. Open throat, controlled breath, a body that can hold against scale. 'Days Pt. 1' opens the album with her father's narration (corpus/web): "Awful things happen every day... pianos fall on the heads of infinite strangers, in infinite cities, in infinite parallel universes." The voice is a family instrument, and the scale is always larger than the figure.

**Takeaway:** make the figure small and the element huge; the body is the microphone for the landscape.

## M5. Story as structure, printed

The Lateworks bio (read substantially): her practice "revolves around a principle of immersive storytelling, and a focus on the tension between micro and macro narratives," with "an interest in situating narrative within freely improvised music." The storytelling is not a theme laid over songs; it is the operating system. Evidence in the corpus: the zone6press hand-bound chapbook (f10) with its Scene 1 Preface/Prayer and Narrator/Chorus structure; the 'run over' campaign narrated in serialized singles across four months; 'The Mountain' personified in reviews as feminine, "heavy, hot, and hungry"; 'Requium' with "From my ribs she'll grow black roses"; 'The Last Great Storm' as an interlude of static "like a disconnected telephone line." Songs have scenes, narrators, and stage directions. She studied computing and fine art at Goldsmiths (code, critical theory, postmodernism; Mark Fisher hauntology namedropped in the interview), and she scored a dance piece, 'life is beautiful,' for choreographers Violet Savage and Elisabeth Mulenga, premiered at EartH in July 2026, her stated problem being "how am I gonna play to this room with this big system." Narrative is how she aims sound at rooms.

**Takeaway:** give every piece a narrator, a scene, and a room; story is the targeting system.

## M6. The hyper-real solo self against the live collective

She changed the project name from Theo to feeo "because it felt too obvious": feeo is "hyper-real me." The name marks the solo process as a deliberate construction, and she guards the boundary. Her account of the division of labor (Struma+Iodine): the writing and recording is solitary, the white-whale arrangement is hers to abandon, and Caius is "not so involved in the recording, writing side of it"; what he brings is live: "an immense amount of energy," and the show is "different when solo, without him." Two separate channels, two separate credit lines: the feeo records are authored alone; Crosspiece (the duo with Caius, Cherche Encore album, 30 May 2024, Arts Council-funded collaborative release) is where improvisation is genuinely shared. The cover of Crosspiece's release post (f07) is blown-out flash of two young men in a cluttered bedroom, overexposed DIY; the feeo covers are elemental and composed. Do not present her studio process as collaborative; the collaboration happens on stage, in the duo, and in the GRAIN room, never in the song's conception.

**Takeaway:** the song is authored alone and performed together; keep the two rooms separate.

## M7. Blown exposure and the pink wordmark

The late-era visual system (2025-2026) is a recognizable machine: monochrome imagery, pushed exposure until detail burns out, and a hot-pink stylised 'feeO' wordmark laid over it. f14 (CTM 2026) and f17 (Edition Festival) both run it; f16 shows the exposure side pushed to abstraction, a huge projected "Love" with a tiny colour portrait of her in the corner. Earlier frames run the same exposure logic without the wordmark: f07's blown-out bedroom flash, f11's motion-blurred figure on blown-out white, f05's ember-plumed smoke (the only full-colour frame, 'tnteen' MV cover). The aesthetic logic is the M3 oppositional engine rendered optically: push the light until it eats the image, then stamp one saturated mark on what remains. Ciara Reddy is the principal visual collaborator across this system ('run over' announce shoot, 'tnteen' art department, 'The Mountain' and 'The Hammer Strikes The Bell' cinematography, 'Goodness' press pictures); the DIY snapshots and the polished press portraits share one logic.

**Takeaway:** burn the image with light, then sign the burn with one saturated mark.

- **2019: the first hearing.** Vocalist on Loraine James's 'For You And I' (Hyperdub); track 'Sensual' (corpus-confirmed 2026-02-25: "7 years ago that we worked together on 'Sensual'"). The origin story the later career keeps circling: the ICA London show with James in February 2026 is posted as a full-circle moment.
- **2020-2021: the invisible EP.** Early singles (2020, web only) and the debut EP 'feels like we're getting older doesn't it' (2021). No corpus representation; the visual system is not yet formed in anything inspected.
- **2022: the writing plan.** PRS Women Make Music grant for a "third EP," structured as two four-track EPs plus a body of written poetry and prose. The poetry-first doctrine is formalized as funding.
- **2023: the image arrives.** Corpus floor (March). Moody black-and-white promo ('New things', f00), the 'Sacred pixels' moodboard carousel (Butoh gold bodies, 'On a Silver Globe' stills), the first 'Ah, Hunger!' support wave (Pitchfork, FADER, Clash, NYT; the EP independently released June 2023). The small-body-vs-big-nature register is set.
- **2024: the campaign machine.** The 'run over' EP campaign (January to March: 'it was then that i', 'tnteen' with the Mateo Cavallaro-directed video, and others, out 21 March); the Crosspiece album on Cherche Encore (30 May); NTS mixes; the state51 factory session (November, f09: hooded figure singing into a mic in an industrial room). She is now producing the full apparatus: singles, videos, duo record, mixes, sessions.
- **2025: the album.** 'The Mountain' single (AD 93, July; she directs and edits the video, Ciara Reddy shoots). The debut LP 'Goodness' (AD 93, 10 October; 'The Hammer Strikes The Bell' as second single; 'Days Pt. 1' with her father's narration; 'Win!' the centrepiece). The zone6press story triptych in July makes the prose practice a printed object. The visual system hardens into the hot-pink 'feeO' wordmark over blown monochrome.
- **2026: the room player.** International touring with Caius (Lisbon, Madrid, Barcelona, Birmingham, Cafe OTO), 'Running & Jumping feat. feeo' with lol K on 33-33, the 'life is beautiful' dance score at EartH, CTM 2026, Unsafe+Sounds, Unsound. The practice is now aimed at big systems in big rooms, the GRAIN co-production continuing in parallel.

## Aesthetic logic

Everything in feeo's practice runs on held opposites. A whole song is heard and then abandoned. A paragraph becomes a lyric. The bass is felt and the treble is heard, and the contrast between them is the arrangement. A small wet body faces a huge sea. The light is pushed until the image burns, and one pink mark is stamped on the burn. The songs are written alone and played together, and the name of the project means the hyper-real version of the person. The through-line is not a sound or a look; it is the discipline of letting two poles hold each other and refusing to resolve them. What stays unresolved stays alive: that is what 'Goodness' means as a title, and what the white whale means as a method. The piece is what survives the abandonment.
