## The life: therapist first, artist by inquiry

Jean-Paul Thenot was born in 1943, in France. No birthplace was found anywhere, and the exact day (13 November, French Wikipedia only) is not printed as verified. He studied psychology at the Sorbonne -- the French Wikipedia says he followed the courses of Jean-Francois Lyotard there, single-source -- was initiated into analysis for some years, and worked in an institution receiving children and adolescents with troubles of identity and communication. He is described as a doctor in clinical psychology and a practising psychotherapist; the Fnac publisher biography independently corroborates "docteur en psychologie clinique et psychotherapeute", which strengthens the doctorate claim, though the 1987 Paris VII thesis ("L'Image de soi dans le processus psychotherapique: une utilisation de la video") rests on the French Wikipedia alone. The safest formulation, from the English Wikipedia and Lomholt, is that he was trained as a psychotherapist, a profession he continued while producing art. The French Wikipedia says he was supported by Francois Pluchart and Pierre Restany and noticed by Anne Tronche and Jean-Marc Poinsot -- single-source. (C18508, leg 1 C18300-C18304, C18316; synthesis C18450, C18451)

Around 1969 he began making artworks, starting with "Interventions in the Street": scaled sculptures of everyday objects -- skipping ropes, mousetraps, skirt hangers -- shown at normal size up to a hugely enlarged scale, placed in series in the street. Thenot asked passers-by what they thought of these diversions, what the objects evoked for them, and what sensations they produced: an interest in the resonance art provokes in people. The object list and the passer-by questioning rest on the French Wikipedia alone; the core fact (c. 1969, scaled everyday objects in the street) is corroborated. The French Wikipedia also lists "Interventions dans la rue, 1969" under Chartres. (C18509, leg 1 C18305; synthesis C18455)

Around 1970 he moved away from producing objects into largely textual and conceptual works, notably the "Constats d'existence": typed pages describing dozens of contemporary artists' actions without naming them, mailed to art specialists and also to an unconcerned public of 300 people from various socio-professional categories, chosen at random from number tables. Addressed to anonymous recipients, the messages, isolated from all context, remained partly incomprehensible, acquiring a genuinely interrogative force as enigmas. The first Constat is dated June 1970 (published by Francois Pluchart in Combat no. 8083, July 1970); no. 2 followed in May 1971, no. 3 in September 1971. The June 1970 dating and the 300-recipient detail rest on the French Wikipedia alone. (C18510, leg 1 C18306; synthesis C18455)

In 1972 he began conducting interactive surveys about art and perception. In response to the state-sponsored "Expo 72" ("Douze ans de l'art contemporain en France"), he carried out a large survey inviting respondents to select the most representative French artists, then declared the person whose submitted list was closest to the averaged final list the honorary curator of a public exhibition opening that May. The survey apparatus is corroborated by the publication list: "Reglement du sondage-concours sur 'La creation artistique en France', et bulletin-reponse", Artitudes no. 5, March 1972, and "Resultats du sondage-concours...", Artitudes nos. 8/9, July-September 1972. (C18511, leg 1 C18307; synthesis C18456)

The three artists met at openings and events in the early 1970s, part of a global trend toward more conceptual, less skill-based, intentionally anti-commercial art, in a France changed by May-June 1968. Each brought an expertise: Forest's new media, Fischer's theory, Thenot's inquiry-based research. In the summer of 1974 Michel Journiac organised meetings at his apartment on Ile Saint-Louis, Paris, to discuss an artist movement engaged with critical and sociological realities; the impulse to form the Collective emerged from them. On 10 October 1974 the Sociological Art Collective was officially declared with the publication in Le Monde of its first manifesto, signed by Herve Fischer, Fred Forest and Jean-Paul Thenot. The Collective published texts including manifestos, organised projects including four group exhibitions and large-scale urban interventions, and founded the Ecole Sociologique Interrogative. Key moments: December 1974, "Art against Ideology", Galerie Rencontres, Paris; January 1975, "Art and its socio-economic structures", Galerie Germain, Paris; March 1975, "Problems and Methods of Sociological Art", Galerie Matthias Fels, Paris; April 1975, International Cultural Centrum, Antwerp; May 1975, Manifesto No. 2; June 1975, Musee Galliera, Paris; July 1976, the two-week "Study and Animation of Perpignan"; July 1976, French Pavilion, Venice Biennale (37th Biennale); June 1977, Documenta 6, Kassel. The Ecole Sociologique Interrogative, founded May 1976 in Fischer's apartment, hosted practitioners and intellectuals including Paul Virilio, Abraham Moles, Vilem Flusser, Pierre Restany, Edgar Morin, Rene Lourau, Henri Lefebvre and Rene Thom. (C18512, leg 1 C18308, C18309; synthesis C18452)

The end of the Collective is contradicted and must not be collapsed. The English Wikipedia says that in 1980 Forest and Thenot published an ad in Art Press selling the Ecole Sociologique Interrogative and wrote a tract declaring the end of the Collective; the Fred Forest Archives agree with 1980 and add a sharper account: on 7 February 1980 Forest alone published a manifesto in Le Monde (no. 10894) under his sole signature, "Art sociologique (Acte II)", scuttling the Collective, an act Thenot closely associated himself with, after Fischer allegedly sought to present himself as the group's leader; Forest then persuaded Catherine Millet to publish in Art Press a page in which the Collective, under the signatures of Thenot and Forest, announced its own scuttling. Against this, the French Wikipedia says the movement "implose et se dissout en 1981", and its publication list dates "Ecole Sociologique Interrogative a vendre" (Forest-Thenot) in Art Press to January 1981 and the "Manifeste d'autodissolution du Collectif" (Forest-Thenot) to Intervention no. 12, Quebec, June 1981. Ruth Erickson's dissertation abstract frames the movement as declining "by the election of the Socialist Francois Mitterrand in 1981", consistent with a 1981 end but dating no specific dissolution act. The 1980 Le Monde act and the 1981 Art Press / Intervention documents may be distinct steps in a drawn-out ending, but no source read reconciles them; report the range 1980-1981. The Forest Archives account is written from Forest's point of view and is partisan about Fischer; treat its motives as Forest's version, not as established fact. (C18513, leg 1 C18310; synthesis C18477)

From 1983 to 1988 Thenot developed a therapeutic practice as an artistic practice, moving communication beyond the concept of art and shifting aesthetics into a form of ethics (French Wikipedia, single source). He had introduced video into the therapeutic relationship and into groups, notably with Janine Manant at the village of Botmeur, using its possibilities of visualisation and awakening -- though the Botmeur video is also listed as "La vie quotidienne a Botmeur, video, 1976", which predates the 1983-1988 window, so the chronology of the Botmeur video strand is unclear and the two dates must not be merged. In 1989, for the first time in France, he fixed in a book a theoretical framework for psychotherapies using video: "Videotherapie, l'image qui fait renaitre", Paris, Greco, 1989 (the "first time in France" claim rests on the French Wikipedia alone; the book itself is corroborated by the English Wikipedia bibliography and the Fnac biography). (C18514, leg 1 C18311; synthesis C18459, C18460)

From 1989 he took a particular interest in invisible and energetic phenomena, science and systems of global perception, leading toward awareness of vibrational regulation of places and inhabitants, including geobiology and so-called paranormal psychic phenomena (French Wikipedia, single source). His 2004 book "Les Sorciers face a la science" is presented as using laboratory experiments and scientific explanations to address phenomena such as clairvoyance and telepathy. "Esthetique du Tao", a reflection initiated between 2005 and 2007: showing the invisible, pronouncing the inaudible, feeling the insensible; awakening the individual to his own sensations. "Signatures" (2008-2009): installations around the artist's signature and its meanings -- the artist as fetish, the signature as logo and market value -- proposing artists' signatures in three dimensions, in solid materials such as wood, and later "Ghosts of Art" installations staging signatures on fire. "Cabinet de voyance: l'attente de l'inattendu" (2010-2011): work on the act of clairvoyance, the viewer becoming the viewed, with the assistance of Judith Kazmierzak, using the Yi King (Book of Changes) as support. "Topographie de l'invisible: la vie cachee des musees": work on the invisible topography of places since the early 1990s, resumed from 2010 with a theory, corroborated (per the French Wikipedia) by American laboratories, about quality of life according to environment. (C18515, leg 1 C18313; synthesis C18460, C18461)

No permanent institutional affiliation (museum post, university chair) was found for Thenot. His institutional anchors are the Sorbonne (student, psychology), the Ecole Sociologique Interrogative (co-founder, Paris, from May 1976), the Sociological Art Collective itself, and the village of Botmeur (video/therapy work with Janine Manant). Do not present him as holding a museum or university post. His international exhibition footprint, as listed in the French Wikipedia (single-source list, reproduced only in summary): 7th Biennale de Paris, 1971; Galerie Yellow Now, Liege, 1971; Galerie Yvon Lambert, Paris, 1972; Galerie Germain / Galerie Matthias Fels / Musee Galliera, Paris, 1974-1975; CAYC, Buenos Aires, 1975 and 1977; Museu de Arte Contemporanea, Sao Paulo, 1975 and 1977; ICC Antwerp, 1975; 37th Venice Biennale, 1976 (representing France, per the Fnac biography); 2nd Biennale of Sydney, 1976; Documenta 6, Kassel, 1977; National Gallery of Canada, Ottawa, 1977; and, much later, Galerie Flux, Liege, 2010 -- the English Wikipedia describes this 2010 show as an "Intervention d'art sociologique" including a video interview with the artist. The latest dated activity traced is January 2013 (Flux News no. 60); the latest dated publication is the 2012 "Petit traite d'existence". (C18516, leg 1 C18312-C18314; synthesis C18461)


## The works: four phases, one question

The catalogue runs W01-W20, 1969-2012. Two grades A exist (W09, W14); the grades below are B where a good secondary description was read end to end, C where only a bibliographic record exists. The evolution falls into four phases, and the phases are the argument: the object disappears, the question stays. (C18517, leg 2 C18350-C18379; synthesis C18462-C18466)

**W01, "Interventions dans la rue" (Interventions in the Street), 1969-1970** -- B. Scaled everyday objects -- skipping ropes, mousetraps, skirt hangers -- shown at normal size up to a hugely enlarged scale, placed in series in the street; Thenot asked passers-by what they thought of these diversions, what the objects evoked, and what sensations they produced. The mechanism is scale plus series plus the street question: the object is a prompt, the passer-by's answer is the work. (C18518, leg 2 C18350)

**W02, "Constat d'existence no. 1", postal mailing, June 1970** -- B. Typed pages describing contemporary artists' actions WITHOUT naming them, mailed to specialists and to 300 random strangers chosen from number tables. The mechanism is description without naming plus a random double audience: addressed to anonymous recipients, the messages, isolated from all context, remained partly incomprehensible, acquiring a genuinely interrogative force as enigmas. (C18519, leg 2 C18351)

**W03, "Constat no. 2" (May 1971) / "Constat no. 3" (September 1971)** -- C. Bibliographic record only; no content description was found in the sources read. The mailings continue the Constat form; nothing more can be said about them. (C18520, leg 2 C18352)

**W04, "Concours des progressions", 7th Biennale de Paris, 1971** -- C. A competition applying the "progression" process; what progressed is not stated anywhere read. The entry rests on the bibliographic record. (C18521, leg 2 C18353)

**W05, Sondage-concours sur "La creation artistique en France", 1972 (Expo 72 response)** -- B. A survey inviting respondents to select the most representative French artists, then averaging the lists and declaring the person whose submitted list was closest to the averaged final list the honorary curator of a public exhibition opening that May. The mechanism is vote, average, crown the closest: the public's taste is measured, and the measurement itself confers the curatorship. (C18522, leg 2 C18354)

**W06, "Cent lectures de Marcel Duchamp", intervention 1974 / book 1978 / reissue 2006** -- B. One hundred non-specialists read Duchamp; the gap between work and title is measured. The mechanism is one hundred naive readings: the same canonical work given to a hundred untrained eyes, so the distance between what the work is and what its title says becomes visible as a distribution, not an opinion. (C18523, leg 2 C18355)

**W07, "Dix questions sur l'art corporel et l'art sociologique" (Ten Questions on Body Art and Sociological Art), debate, fall 1973** -- B. Critic Francois Pluchart initiated a debate between Michel Journiac, Gina Pane, Fischer and Thenot, published as "Ten Questions on Sociological Art" in artTitudes (the magazine Pluchart founded in 1971). The mechanism is interrogation as art practice: the artwork is a structured public questioning of what sociological art is, conducted in print before the movement had a manifesto or a collective. (C18524, leg 2 C18356)

**W08, First Manifesto of Sociological Art, published in Le Monde, 10 October 1974** -- B. The official proclamation of the Sociological Art Collective, signed by Herve Fischer, Fred Forest and Jean-Paul Thenot. Published in France's most widely read newspaper rather than an art magazine, the manifesto claimed that the practice of art is rooted in the fundamental links connecting art, society and life, and that sociological art relies on scientific methods. The newspaper page is the work's support; mass distribution is its mechanism. The manifesto text itself was not read (paywalled/archive). (C18525, leg 2 C18357)

**W09, Manifeste 2 de l'art sociologique, postal, May 1975** -- A. The second manifesto, mailed by post rather than printed in a newspaper. Its full text was read end to end (391.org): "questioning and disruption, not management." Grade A because the primary text was read. (C18526, leg 2 C18358)

**W10, 37th Venice Biennale, French Pavilion, 1976; Manifeste 3, May 1976** -- B. The Collective in the national pavilion; Manifesto No. 3 published the same month. No Thenot piece was individually evidenced in the sources read. (C18527, leg 2 C18359)

**W11, "Etude et animation de Perpignan", July-September 1976, Perpignan (two weeks)** -- B. The Collective's two-week intervention in Perpignan began with a study of the conditions and needs of the town's neighbourhoods by a thirty-person interdisciplinary team, then led to dozens of direct interventions: pop-up exhibitions, screenings, interviews, parties, and a multi-neighbourhood photograph swap. The stated aim was to bring together neighbourhoods that, though geographically close, remained distant at the level of social communication. The group set out four principles -- critique, communication, intervention, pedagogy -- as the method of sociological art. The July vs September dating conflict stands (English Wikipedia says July; Forest Archives says September). (C18528, leg 2 C18360)

**W12, Ecole Sociologique Interrogative (Interrogative Sociological School), founded May 1976; sold January 1981; self-dissolution June 1981** -- B. The Collective founded a school located in Fischer's own apartment. The mechanism is institutional: instead of making objects, the artists founded a teaching structure whose curriculum was interrogation. In 1980-1981 Forest and Thenot sold the school by newspaper ad and published a self-dissolution manifesto -- the school's sale notice was itself the closing gesture, and the Collective applied its method to itself. (C18529, leg 2 C18361)

**W13, "Nous sommes tous des ecrivains" (We Are All Writers), Nice Book Festival, 1978 (with Jean-Pierre Giovanelli)** -- B. Visitors become co-authors and co-owners of a book made on the spot -- written, produced and dedicated at the festival. The mechanism is authorship distributed to whoever walks in. (C18530, leg 2 C18362)

**W14, "Hommage aux artistes inconnus" (Homage to Unknown Artists), Lund, 27 March-21 April 1981** -- A. An open call for tributes to unknown artists, in any medium; the gallery kitchen becomes an office; the display grows as submissions arrive; every participant is certified. The invitation text was read end to end (Galerie S:t Petri). The mechanism is the open call plus the accumulating display plus a certificate for all: the prize inverted, everyone wins. Grade A because the primary invitation text was read. (C18531, leg 2 C18363)

**W15, "La vie quotidienne a Botmeur" (Daily Life at Botmeur), video, 1976; "Le psycho-video-drame", 1981** -- B. Video with Janine Manant in a village; the camera as a "speaking mirror" in village groups. The footage was not watched in any leg. The mechanism is the video mirror: show a person their own image, and the double and the exchange of gazes do the work. (C18532, leg 2 C18364)

**W16, "Videotherapie, l'image qui fait renaitre" (Videotherapy: The Image That Makes You Reborn), book, Greco, 1989** -- B. The first French theoretical framework for psychotherapies using video. A BOOK, not a film: video is the tool inside the therapy. The mechanism is rebirth by self-image: the patient sees himself on video, and recognition -- the delayed self -- is the therapeutic event. (C18533, leg 2 C18365)

**W17, "Les sorciers face a la science" (Sorcerers Face Science), book, 2004; "Esthetique du Tao" (Tao Aesthetics), 2005-2007** -- B. Paranormal phenomena argued from lab experiments; Tao aesthetic: show the invisible, pronounce the inaudible, feel the insensible. The mechanism is attunement replacing the survey: instead of asking people what they think, the work asks what they feel in a place. (C18534, leg 2 C18366)

**W18, "Signatures", 2008-2009; "Ghosts of Art" (burning signatures)** -- B. Signatures detached, enlarged into 3D wood, then set on fire; the concept appears as the thing disappears. The mechanism is detach, enlarge, burn: isolate the signature -- the smallest unit of art-world value, the artist as fetish, the signature as logo and market value -- make it a solid object, then destroy it, so the name is freed from the market. (C18535, leg 2 C18367)

**W19, "Cabinet de voyance: l'attente de l'inattendu" (Fortune-Telling Cabinet: Waiting for the Unexpected), 2010-2011** -- B. A one-to-one fortune-telling consultation via the Yi King (Book of Changes); the sitter's life laid out like a painting; payment symbolic only. The mechanism is the sitting as exhibition: one person, one consultation, the life read as three panels -- past, present, future. (C18536, leg 2 C18368)

**W20, "Topographie de l'invisible" (Topography of the Invisible), from 2010; "Petit traite d'existence" (Little Treatise on Existence), 2012** -- B. Museum topography sensed bare-handed -- zones of ease and unease felt in a room, not measured by instruments; the 2012 book catalogues 1,000 actions as playable synopses, authors recoverable only from the end index. The mechanism is sense the room bare-handed and score the act: a museum floor plan with felt zones, and a thousand one-line action scores anyone may perform. (C18537, leg 2 C18369)

Beyond the 20: "Le point de disparition de l'art" (Flux News no. 49, April 2009); "Le Temps ready made" (Flux News no. 50, 2009); "Le musee des signatures" (Flux News no. 51, 2010); "La part invisible des lieux. ## The mechanisms

**M1 -- Scale + series + street question.** Oversize a banal object, place copies in series in the street, ask passers-by what it evokes. W01 (skipping ropes, mousetraps, skirt hangers at escalating scales, passers-by questioned on the spot). Watchable behaviour: one everyday object at three escalating scales in a street scene, with three passer-by answers appearing as captions; the object is a prompt, the answers are the work. **Takeaway:** Thenot never asks the object what it means; he asks the street, and the street's answer is the piece. (C18539, synthesis C18468)

**M2 -- Describe without naming + random double audience.** Describe an act, withhold the author, mail it to experts and to 300 random strangers; the incomprehension IS the question. W02 (typed pages describing artists' actions without naming them, mailed June 1970). Watchable behaviour: an action shown with its author label blanked; the screen splits into an expert reading and a random stranger's reading side by side, and the two readings diverge. **Takeaway:** remove the name and the art world goes blind in one way, the stranger goes blind in another -- and the gap between the two blindnesses is the artwork. (C18540, synthesis C18468)

**M3 -- Vote, average, crown the closest.** Collect lists, compute the mean list, award curatorship to the nearest list. W05 (the 1972 Expo 72 survey: vote for representative artists, average the lists, crown the closest list as honorary curator). Watchable behaviour: the viewer picks five artists, a live average list assembles, the viewer whose list sits nearest the average is crowned honorary curator. **Takeaway:** the strongest generative seed in this study, because the protocol is fully specified -- vote, average, crown -- and the machine can run it for real on the watcher's own input. (C18541, synthesis C18469)

**M4 -- One hundred naive readings.** Give the same canonical work to 100 non-specialists; measure the gap between work and title. W06 (100 non-specialists read Duchamp, 1974; book 1978, reissue 2006). Watchable behaviour: a Duchamp-like readymade with its title hidden, then 100 one-line readings cascade in and the gap between work and title opens visibly. **Takeaway:** a hundred wrong readings are not noise; plotted together they are a portrait of the work's distance from its own name. (C18542, synthesis C18469)

**M5 -- Study first, then animate.** Diagnose a place with a team, then stage exchanges between its separated parts. W11 (Perpignan, 1976: a thirty-person team studies the neighbourhoods first, then a photograph swap between them). Watchable behaviour: a map of two nearby but disconnected neighbourhoods; photographs cross between them and the exchange itself is the work. **Takeaway:** Thenot never decorates a town; he diagnoses it first, and the diagnosis decides what the intervention is. (C18543, synthesis C18470)

**M6 -- Open call + accumulating display + certificate for all.** Anyone may propose a tribute in any medium; the display grows as submissions arrive; every participant is certified. W14 (Lund, 1981: open call for tributes to unknown artists; the gallery kitchen becomes an office; every participant certified). Watchable behaviour: an open call for tributes to unknown makers; each new submission is added to a growing wall and a certificate is issued per submission -- the prize inverted, everyone wins. **Takeaway:** the certificate is the point: in a world that certifies only the famous, certifying everyone is the critique. (C18544, synthesis C18470)

**M7 -- Video mirror / rebirth.** Show a person their own image on video; the double and the exchange of gazes do the work. W15 (the Botmeur video, 1976; "Le psycho-video-drame", 1981) and W16 (the 1989 book: the first French theoretical framework for psychotherapies using video). Watchable behaviour: the viewer is filmed and played back delayed; the delayed self-image is the piece; the double appears and the rebirth is the moment of recognition. **Takeaway:** the survey asked people about art; the video mirror shows people themselves -- the same method, pointed inward, and the answer is no longer an opinion but a recognition. (C18545, synthesis C18471)

**M8 -- Detach, enlarge, burn.** Isolate the signature -- the smallest unit of art-world value -- make it three-dimensional wood, set it on fire; the concept appears as the thing disappears. W18 (signatures in 3D wood, 2008-2009; "Ghosts of Art" burnings). ## Aesthetic logic: the question as artwork

Thenot's aesthetic is an aesthetic of the question, not of the object. Every work removes something -- the author's name (W02), the official canon (W05), the title (W06), the object itself (W12's sale, W18's burning) -- and what remains is a public responding. The visual language that follows is deliberately thin: typed pages, reply slips, averaged lists, certificates, delayed video, smoke. A generative piece in his manner should therefore never render a beautiful object and stop; it should render a protocol -- a question, a sample, a vote, a reading -- and let the watcher's response complete it.

The therapeutic turn is not a break but the same method pointed inward: the survey asked people about art; videotherapy shows people themselves. The late invisible phase is the same method pointed at sensation: the survey asked what people think; the Tao aesthetic asks what people feel. The arc ends where it began -- with attention to what a passer-by feels -- but the object is gone entirely.

That is why the work survives every change of carrier. A question works on a street, in a letter, in a survey, in a debate, in a manifesto, in a school curriculum, in a video mirror, in a fortune-telling cabinet, in a museum sensed bare-handed. The channel changes; the question does not. And the question is always the same shape: what does this evoke in you, and who gets to decide what art is? **Takeaway:** build the protocol, not the picture; put the question where the public already is, in the cheapest carrier that will hold it, and let the answer arrive by post, by vote, or by recognition. (C18551, synthesis C18474, C18475)
