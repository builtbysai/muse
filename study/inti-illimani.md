## M1. The naming at the guitar-maker's house

The name is given in a house, and the giving is the mechanism. In August 1967 the classical guitarist Eulogio Davalos hears the students play, and the hearing is the mechanism, because he invites them especially to his house, and the inviting is the naming, and the naming is the group, and the group is the mechanism. In his house they pass to be called Inti-Illimani, and the passing is the mechanism, because the name is a combination of a Quechua word and an Aymara word meaning "Sol del Illimani", the sun of the Illimani, the mountain that stands imposing in the Bolivian Andes and is seen from La Paz, and the meaning is the landscape, and the landscape is the name, and the name is the mechanism. By some versions Davalos himself baptised them, and by others his sister Gloria proposed baptising the group as "hijos del Illimani", children of the Illimani, and the two versions are carried as a dispute, not settled, and the carrying is the honesty, and the honesty is the mechanism. Coulon tells it with the detail that makes it true: Davalos was then the most noted guitarist in Chile, and it happened that his father's birthday, his father being of Bolivian nationality, coincided with the National Day of Bolivia, Sunday 6 August, and seeing that the students were obsessed with Andean instruments, Davalos invited them and baptised them as Inti Illimani, and Max Berru was already part of the group, and the baptising is the mechanism, because the name arrives through a Bolivian birthday, and the birthday is the mountain, and the mountain is the sun, and the sun is the name, and the name is the mechanism. They did not consider a Mapuche word, says Coulon, and the not-considering is the mechanism too, because what was clear was the decision that the name be a native voice, and the decision is the rebellion, and the rebellion is the name, and the name is the mechanism, because to use ponchos, to take a name of indigenous etymology, to play those instruments, were for them gestures absolutely clear of love and rebellion, love toward that music and that culture which revealed itself to them overwhelmingly, and rebellion because it was a rupture with a musical and cultural environment that olympically ignored the enormous richness that the massive indigenous presence meant in their Latin American societies, and the rupture is the name, and the name is the poncho, and the poncho is the instrument, and the instrument is the mechanism. **Takeaway:** let the name be given in a house on a Bolivian birthday, and let the name be a native voice, and let the wearing and the playing be the rebellion, and the rebellion is the name. Against the chain: Yupanqui (#371) also took a name, but his is a boy in a schoolroom taking two kings' names, Atahualpa and Yupanqui, and letting the Buenos Aires press call him the Peruvian, an invention of the name, and Inti's is a name given by another man in his house on a birthday, which is why his is a taking and theirs is a giving.

## M2. The students who became a band

The university canteen is the rehearsal room, and the rehearsal is the mechanism. In the heart of the Nueva Cancion Chilena, which took force and prominence in Chile between the second half of the 1960s and the start of the 1970s, and around the festive atmosphere of folk pen~as at the Universidad Tecnica del Estado, a group of students joins to form a sextet, and the joining is the mechanism, because Oscar Guzman, Pedro Yanez and Horacio Duran had already performed in various pen~as as the Trio de la calera EAO, and Ciro Retamal, Luis Cifuentes and Jorge Coulon took part in the Pen~a of the UTE, and the trio and the pen~a are the beginning, and the beginning is the mechanism. The group begins to rehearse with three guitars, charango, quena and bombo, and the rehearsing is the mechanism, because three guitars and three Andean instruments are already the whole sound, and the sound is the rehearsal, and the rehearsal is the group, and the group is the mechanism. Within days Max Berru and Luis Espinoza join and Cifuentes leaves, and the joining and the leaving are the mechanism too, because the group is still finding its shape, and the shape is the sextet, and the sextet is the mechanism. In October 1967 Horacio Salinas joins, a young student of only fifteen years by the article's count, sixteen by his birth date, coming from the musical group of the Ballet folklorico Pucara, and the joining is the mechanism, because the boy who joins will be the director of the group for most of its existence, and the boy is the director, and the director is the mechanism. In February 1968 Pedro Yanez leaves to finish his studies and is replaced by Ernesto Perez de Arce, a clarinettist of great jazz training, and the replacing is the mechanism, because the quintet that records the first five discs is now set, and the setting is the sound, and the sound is the group, and the group is the mechanism. The history says the group began underground, in a dark canteen of the Arts and Trades School of the Technical University, and the underground is the mechanism, because the name means sun of the mountain and the history begins in a dark canteen, and the dark is the sun, and the sun is the canteen, and the canteen is the mechanism. **Takeaway:** rehearse in the university canteen with three guitars and three Andean instruments, and let the boy from the folk ballet become the director, and let the dark canteen hold the sun. Against the chain: Jara (#370 M1) also staged the song, but his is a theatre director who stages thirteen productions before he sings, a staging learned in a school, and Inti's is students who rehearse in a canteen until the rehearsal becomes the band, which is why his is a staging and theirs is a joining.

## M3. The Andean census

## M4. The programme set to music

## M6. The anthem that closes the concert

Victor Jara, the teacher and the backed voice: "Canto libre" (1970) with Patricio Castillo and Inti-Illimani, "El derecho de vivir en paz" (1971) with Quilapayun and Inti-Illimani collaborating, the tribute album "Inti-Illimani interpreta a Victor Jara" (1999/2000), Joan Jara at the Paris meeting of November 1973, Jara killed Sunday 16 September 1973 in the Estadio Chile. Violeta Parra, the root: her decimas are "Canto para una semilla" (1972) and "La exiliada del sur" (1971), and the discovery with her of Andean music is where the idea began. Patricio Manns, the duo: "Inti Manns", seven discs 1979-2003, the 1971 album accompaniment, the two 1980s albums, the 2014 tribute album, the 1979 fetch by Jose Seves. Horacio Salinas, the director and main composer: joined October 1967, director 1968-2001, more than eighty pieces, born Sunday 8 July 1951 in Lautaro, author of "La cancion en el sombrero" (2013), his son Camilo Salinas plays piano in the Historico group. Jose Seves, the voice: joined 1971, left 1998, rejoined 2000-2001, left September 2001, fetched Manns from Paris to Rome in 1979, co-composer of the bulk of the group's own repertoire. Jorge Coulon, the founder and first voice: tiple, siku, dulcimer, political leader of the group, author of "En las cuerdas del tiempo" (2025) with Federico Bonadonna. Marcelo Coulon, the brother: three months replacing Jorge in 1970, permanent from 1978, central figure in the split by Salinas's account. Max Berru, the founder: voice, guitar, bombo leguero, 1942-2018, the ranchera and corrido singer, retired 1997. Quilapayun, the twin group: Jara's artistic direction 1966-1969, co-authors with Ortega of "El pueblo unido", the joint Paris pact of November 1973, the "Inti+Quila" concerts of 2004-2006. Sergio Ortega (1938-2003), the hymn composer: "Venceremos" and "El pueblo unido", co-director of "Canto al programa", cultural ambassador with Jara, his funeral sung by Eduardo Carrasco, Jose Seves, Max Berru and Horacio Duran. Luis Advis, the conservatory composer: "Cantata Santa Maria de Iquique" (Quilapayun, 1970), "Canto al programa" (1970), "Canto para una semilla" (1972), "Pueblo Herido" with Jorge Coulon. Isabel Parra, the voice of the seed cantata: sang "Canto para una semilla" (1972) and its Italian and French versions, Violeta's daughter. Eulogio Davalos, the namer: heard them in August 1967 and baptised them Inti-Illimani in his house. Ernesto Perez de Arce, the clarinettist: replaced Yanez in February 1968, jazz training, quena and voice. Jose Miguel Camus, the early member: joined December 1972, replaced by Marcelo Coulon in 1978. Homero Altamirano, the first voice: joined 1968, sang on "Voz para el camino", "Por la CUT" and "Si somos americanos", left before the first LP recorded in Chile. Pedro Yanez, the founder: left February 1968 to finish his studies, later wrote the history of the group. Oscar Guzman, Ciro Retamal, Luis Cifuentes and Luis Espinoza, the first formation, in the disputed founder lists. Manuel Merin~o, the renewal: from Entrama, musical director after Salinas, 2001-2022. Daniel Cantillana, Christian Gonzalez, Juan Flores and Efren Viera, the renewal generation. Jorge Ball, Renato Freyggang and Pedro Villagra, the exile and return members. Fernando Julio, Camilo Salinas and Danilo Donoso, the Historico members. Giancarlo Pajetta and Ignazio Delogu, the Italian helpers: Pajetta who said it would be long, Delogu who guaranteed the credit. Volodia Teitelboim, Gustavo Becerra and Juan Carvajal, the first-months companions. Eduardo Carrasco, who led the Paris decision. Rene Largo Farias, whose radio programme and pen~a "Chile rie y canta" held Manns, Urbina, Illapu and Inti. Los Folkloristas of Mexico, the brotherhood since Ruben Ortiz's visit in 1971. John Williams and Paco Pen~a, the classical partners. Arja Saijonmaa and Holly Near, the exile collaborators. Diego el Cigala and Eva Ayllon, the later partners. Atahualpa Yupanqui, Salinas's model, whose "Huajra" the group recorded twice. Mercedes Sosa, movement kin, carried without a documented Inti collaboration in this leg.
