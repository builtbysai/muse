## M1. The tune as political material: the song does the arguing

The People United takes Ortega's Chilean anthem as its theme, and the North American Ballads take spirituals, labour songs and protest songs, and A Long Time Man takes a Texas chain-gang song, and Bring Them Home! of 2004 takes the seventeenth-century Irish anti-war song Siuil A Run, and the tune is the argument, and the composer does not have to argue it because the audience already knows what the song means. The Ortega song was composed on a night of May or June 1973, three months before Pinochet's coup of Tuesday 11 September 1973, and Rzewski composed the variations in September-October 1975, and the song is the whole piece's political content, carried in a melody anyone in the hall could hum. **Takeaway:** borrow a song the room already knows, and the politics arrives before the first note. Against the chain: Cardew also took the tune as political material, M10, and his late piano pieces take Thalmann's song and workers' songs as themes, and Wolff also took tunes, M9, but his tunes are folk fragments used as material to be broken, and Rzewski's tune is a whole anthem kept whole, and the difference is between a tune used as a brick and a tune used as a flag.

## M2. Variation as accumulation: 36 variations in six groups of six

The People United is a 36-bar theme, then 36 variations in six groups of six, and each group runs six stages, simple events, rhythms, melodies, counterpoints, harmonies, combinations, and every sixth variation summarises the five before it, and the theme modulates up a fifth with each variation, covering all twelve minor keys, and after the last variation the pianist may improvise a cadenza before the theme returns, and form is a machine for generating difference out of sameness. A typical performance spans about an hour, and the sources disagree on the minute count, 45 minutes, 50 minutes, roughly 60 minutes, an hour long, and the study says about an hour, see C6. **Takeaway:** build a machine that makes difference out of sameness, and let it run until the theme comes back changed. Against the chain: Wolff also built forms that generate difference, M2, but his forms are proportions the player cannot see, and Cardew's Treatise is 193 pages with no machine at all, and Rzewski's machine is visible on the page, six groups of six, and the audience can count it as it runs.

## M3. The additive process: add notes, then take them away

Les Moutons de Panurge of 1968 gives everyone the same 65-note tune, and they play first notes 1, then 1-2, then 1-2-3, until the whole melody is heard, then notes are subtracted from the beginning, holding the last C natural until everyone finishes, and Coming Together does the same to a text, first sentence, then first two sentences, then first three, then sentences drop from the beginning until only the last remains, and accumulation then erosion, the same machine in melody and in words. **Takeaway:** add one thing at a time until the whole is present, then take it away one thing at a time, and the shape is the piece. Against the chain: Cardew also built by accumulation, M11, but his accumulation is duration, seven hours in one room, and Wolff's accumulation is cue by cue, and Rzewski's accumulation is arithmetic, one note at a time, and the audience can hear the counting.

## M4. Spoken text over a driving figure: the letter read aloud, a few words at a time

Coming Together sets a letter by Sam Melville, written Sunday 16 May 1971 from Attica prison, and the speaker declaims the letter a few words at a time over a driving pentatonic bass line of steady 16th notes, and the bass is the only fully notated part and the other parts are derived from it by rule, and the accompaniment is 392 bars of continuous single-line 16ths in a G minor blues scale of seven pitches, tethered to low G, always climbing and falling back, and the text is declaimed, not sung, to ensure its comprehensibility and emotional impact. Melville was killed in the retaking of Attica on Monday 13 September 1971, and 43 people died in total, including 33 prisoners, see C16. **Takeaway:** put the words on top of a figure that never stops, and the figure carries the words further than silence would. Against the chain: Cardew also set text over music, M9, but his text scores are sentences that become events, and Wolff's texts are instructions, and Rzewski's text is a dead man's letter read as news, and the difference is between a text that tells you what to do and a text that tells you what happened.

## M5. The rule that contains improvisation: a map, not a result

The score gives a rule, not a result, and Les Moutons says "Never stop or falter... Stay together as long as you can, but if you get lost, stay lost. Do not try to find your way back to the fold", and Coming Together notates only the bass line and derives the other parts from it in ways specified by the composer, and form, dynamics and articulation are defined, and the rest is improvised, and the LA Phil calls it the medieval canon, a rule for deriving polyphony from a single line, and Rzewski's own theory, in the Algebra essay on Wolff, is that scores "do not de/prescribe the final resulting sound-structure, but provide a map along which the players may travel". **Takeaway:** write the rule and not the result, and the players' travel is the piece. Against the chain: Cardew also gave rules instead of results, M2, but his Treatise gives no rules at all, and Wolff's rules are sentences that tell you what to do, and Rzewski's rule is a bass line that tells you where to go, and the difference is between a map with no legend, a sentence with an instruction, and a road with a direction.

## M6. The pianist's body as the instrument: whistle, shout, slam the lid

The People United requires the pianist to whistle, shout, slam the piano lid, and catch the after-vibrations of a loud attack as harmonics, and De Profundis of 1991-92 adds humming, whistling, beating the instrument, body and piano slapping, and a Harpo Marx horn, and the virtuosity is physical and theatrical, not only digital, and the pianist is a body doing two jobs at once, playing and speaking, and the instrument is the piano and the player together. **Takeaway:** make the player use the whole body, and the virtuosity becomes something you can see. Against the chain: Cardew also made the body the instrument, M8, but his body-print is a trace left on paper, and Wolff's players use their feet for percussion in Accompaniments, and Rzewski's body is the show itself, the slam and the shout in the hall, and the difference is between a body that leaves a mark and a body that makes a noise.

## M7. The prose score: words replace notes

Spacecraft, the MEV piece of 1967, has a score that is "an elaborate set of abstract verbal instructions", an example of what Rzewski called prose music, and words replace notes as the instruction medium, and the piece is a situation to enter, not a text to reproduce, and it was performed over 80 times in Europe, and its goal was "to create unity and harmony among human beings through the creation of a sound-space environment". **Takeaway:** write the piece in words, and the players enter a situation instead of reproducing a text. Against the chain: Cardew also wrote prose scores, M9, but his sentences are events to be performed, and Wolff's prose scores are instructions to be followed, and Rzewski's prose is a room to walk into, and the difference is between a sentence that is an event, a sentence that is an order, and a paragraph that is a place.

## M8. Free improvisation as collective process: the room plays too

MEV made music "as a spontaneous collective process", with homemade electronics, amplified glass plates, motor-oil cans, Moog synthesiser, voice, and whoever was in the room, and performances invited the audience to play along and sometimes ended in riots, police and firemen clearing halls, lights cut at Parma, a street demonstration led by Jean-Jacques Lebel, and the group played in factories and prisons, and the mechanism is the removal of the boundary between performers and audience. The founding members were Allan Bryant, Alvin Curran, Jon Phetteplace, Frederic Rzewski, Richard Teitelbaum and Carol Plantamura, with Ivan Vandor and Steve Lacy among the early members, and the trio shorthand that names only Curran and Teitelbaum omits founders, see C13. **Takeaway:** remove the line between the stage and the room, and the audience becomes the instrument. Against the chain: Cardew also removed the line, M7, but his Scratch Orchestra removed it by letting anyone join, and Wolff's ensembles remove it by cueing, and MEV removed it by inviting the room to play, and the difference is between an orchestra that admits everyone, an ensemble that listens to everyone, and a concert that lets the audience in.

## M9. Style as structural element: quotation is the load-bearing wall

The People United "goes through everything", in Oppens's words, 19th-century romanticism mixed with pandiatonic tonality, modal writing and serial techniques, French impressionism, Shostakovich, pop, Crumb, Liszt, Chopin, Rachmaninoff, gospel, swing, funk, march, blues, and Nonesuch says that in the 1970s he "experimented further with forms in which style and language are treated as structural elements", and quotation is not decoration, it is the load-bearing structure, and Eisler's Solidarity Song of 1932, with Brecht, is quoted inside The People United. **Takeaway:** treat a style as a beam, not as paint, and the piece holds itself up on borrowed music. Against the chain: Cardew also borrowed styles, M10, but his late borrowing is a retreat to tonality as a political act, and Wolff's borrowing is a fragment to be broken, and Rzewski's borrowing is a whole history of piano music laid out as structure, and the difference is between a style adopted as a faith, a style taken as material, and a style used as architecture.

## M10. The tune kept audible inside counterpoint: Bach's rule, Ives's delay

The North American Ballads take Bach's chorale preludes as model, and Rzewski says "in his contrapuntal writing [Bach] consistently derives motivic configurations from the basic tune. In each piece I built up contrapuntal textures in a similar way, using classical techniques like augmentation, diminution, transposition, and compression, always keeping the profile of the tune on some level", and Schell adds the Ives move, in Winnsboro Cotton Mill Blues the full song is withheld until the very end. **Takeaway:** hide the tune in the counterpoint but never lose it, and the last entrance lands like a return. Against the chain: Cardew also kept tunes audible, M10, but his tunes are stated plainly so anyone can follow them, and Wolff's tunes are fragments that dissolve, and Rzewski's tune is a profile kept on some level of the texture, and the difference is between a tune you can sing along to, a tune that breaks apart, and a tune you can always find if you listen for its shape.

## M11. The prison letter as libretto: the prisoner is the author

Three major works take a prisoner's writing as their text, Coming Together, Melville's letter of Sunday 16 May 1971, Attica, Richard X. Clark's words "Attica is in front of me", De Profundis, Wilde's letter from Reading Gaol of 1897, and the letter is read aloud, in fragments, over music that never illustrates it but holds it up, and the prisoner is the author, and the composer is the reader who reads the letter over and over until a musical treatment appears. Rzewski's own programme note says of Melville's letter, "I read it over and over again." **Takeaway:** let a prisoner write the words, and the composer only has to hold them up. Against the chain: Cardew also took texts from outside music, M9, but his texts are Confucius and political slogans, and Wolff's texts are instructions and fragments, and Rzewski's texts are letters from cells, and the difference is between a text that teaches, a text that instructs, and a text that testifies.

## M12. The speaking pianist: one body, two jobs

A whole late lineage: De Profundis of 1991-92, Michael Bakunin, Rentier of 2000, Rubinstein in Berlin of 2008, Flowers 1 of 2009, Dear Diary of 2014, To His Coy Mistress of 1988, plus Lost and Found and To the Earth of 1985 for speaking percussionist, and Fortune of 2005 for four speaking violists, and the player speaks, sings, shouts or whispers while playing, so the instrument and the voice are one body doing two jobs. **Takeaway:** give the player words to say while they play, and the voice and the hands argue with each other. Against the chain: Cardew also made players speak, M9, but his speaking is the score itself, and Wolff's Accompaniments asks the pianist to sing or chant and play percussion with the feet, and Rzewski's speaking pianist is a whole career of pieces, and the difference is between a score that speaks, a pianist who also sings, and a player whose voice is a second instrument.

## M13. The return to graphic notation: the experiment comes back

After the political turn, the experimental notation comes back, and The Price of Oil of 1980 and Le Silence des Espaces Infinis of 1980 use graphical notation, and Nonesuch dates "a return to experimental and graphic notation" to 1979-81, and the political turn did not kill the experimental turn, it shelved it, and it returned. **Takeaway:** put the experiment away when the politics needs a tune, and bring it back when the tune has done its work. Against the chain: Cardew also returned to experiment and then repudiated it, M13 and M16, but his return was a break he then denounced, and Wolff never left the open score, and Rzewski's return is a shelf and a retrieval, and the difference is between a break that is final, a freedom that never leaves, and a tool put down and picked up again.

## M14. The long form as accumulation over years: the novel, not the month

The Road of 1995-2003 is eight parts, 64 pieces, about nine hours, assembled over eight years, "like an epic Russian novel in the tradition of Tolstoy or Dostoyevsky", and the Nanosonatas of 2006-10 are eight books of miniatures, and the mechanism is the opposite of the white-heat month, a form that is built slowly, piece by piece, and only later understood as one work, and the sources disagree on The Road's length, seven hours, nine hours, ten hours plus, and the study gives the parts and piece count and says roughly nine hours, see C14. **Takeaway:** build the work over years, one small piece at a time, and the whole appears only at the end. Against the chain: Cardew also built long forms, M11, but his seven hours happen in one room in one sitting, and Wolff's long forms are durations the players fill, and Rzewski's long form is eight years of small pieces, and the difference is between a day, a duration, and a decade.

## M15. Music as something you do: the participation is the politics

From the Algebra essay: Wolff's music "is primarily meant to be played, rather than merely heard... Anybody with the will and a certain understanding can do it. This idea of music as something you do, rather than something that is done to you, is both ancient and forward-looking", and "Improvisation is the art of the possible... it could therefore, theoretically at least, provide a partial model for some kind of post-revolutionary language", and the political claim is made by the form of participation, not by the text. **Takeaway:** let the audience do the music instead of receiving it, and the politics is in the doing. Against the chain: Cardew also made participation the politics, M7, but his participation is an orchestra anyone can join, and Wolff's participation is a cue anyone can answer, and Rzewski's participation is the idea itself, stated in an essay, and the difference is between an institution, a cue, and a sentence.

## M16. Self-criticism in public: the composer argues with his own reputation

Rzewski argues with his own reputation in interviews, and the popular pieces have "no music in it", and the 1968 revolution was a myth of bourgeois children, and "I did nothing important", and "If I do not write music I will have to die", and he also refuses the career, free scores on IMSLP, "If you write music for a living, you're doing the wrong thing", and the mechanism is the refusal to let the work settle into a monument, including his own. **Takeaway:** disown your own best-known work in public, and the work stays alive because it has to defend itself. Against the chain: Cardew also criticised himself in public, M16, but his self-criticism is a denunciation that ends the work, and Wolff's self-criticism is a change of material that keeps the work going, and Rzewski's self-criticism is a joke told in an interview that leaves the work standing, and the difference is between a repudiation, a revision, and a shrug.

## Evolution

PHASE 1, STUDENT AND SERIAL, 1953 to 1965. Harvard with Piston and Thompson, Princeton with Sessions and Babbitt, Florence with Dallapiccola on a Fulbright 1960-61, Berlin with Carter on a Ford grant 1963-65, and early piano pieces, two-piano sonatas, tape and mixed-media works, a Requiem of 1963-67 with Jew's harp and bull-roarer, and the mechanism is still academic modernism, but the performing career is already improvisatory, first performances of Stockhausen's Klavierstuck X in 1962 and Plus Minus in 1964.

PHASE 2, ROME AND MEV, 1966 to 1971. Co-founds Musica Elettronica Viva in Rome in spring 1966, homemade electronics, contact microphones, amplified glass, oil cans, voice, Moog, and Spacecraft as prose score, and concerts that end in riots, audience participation, playing in factories and prisons, and "Free improvisation was going to change the world... it was going to create an entirely new language, so that people could come together from different parts of the planet and instantly communicate", and his own late verdict, "Well, of course, we were wrong."

PHASE 3, NEW YORK AND THE POLITICAL TURN, 1971 to 1979. Returns to New York in 1971, and Les Moutons de Panurge of 1968 carried over, Coming Together in November-December 1971, Attica in 1972, then the long consolidation into The People United in September-October 1975 and the North American Ballads of 1978-79, and the mechanism changes, from open improvisation to strict forms built on borrowed tunes and spoken texts, and Cardew is the acknowledged influence here, "Cardew wrote pieces that were convincing demonstrations of what could be done by using a tonal language with which large numbers of people were familiar to communicate advanced political ideas", and the Musicians Action Collective runs benefit concerts for United Farm Workers, an Attica defence fund, Chilean solidarity.

PHASE 4, EUROPE AGAIN, LARGE FORMS AND THE RETURN OF EXPERIMENT, 1979 to 1995. Returns to Europe 1976-77, Liege professorship 1977, and The Price of Oil and Le Silence des Espaces Infinis of 1980 return to graphic notation, and the 1980s explore twelve-tone technique anew, Antigone-Legend, The Persians, and The Triumph of Death of 1987-88 is a two-hour oratorio on texts adapted from Peter Weiss's Die Ermittlung, premiered 1965, see C15, and De Profundis of 1991-92 makes the speaking pianist the central figure, and the piano becomes the whole career, "Opera houses don't come asking me to write operas... But there's this piano player I see every day who keeps asking me for music. So that's what I do", and the line is rhetoric, because he had written two operas, The Persians of 1985 and The Triumph of Death, see C23.

PHASE 5, THE LONG PIANO, 1995 to 2021. The Road of 1995-2003, Nanosonatas of 2006-10, and a stream of speaking-pianist miniatures, Michael Bakunin, Rentier, Rubinstein in Berlin, Dear Diary, Flowers 1, and the Piano Concerto of 2013 at the Proms, Prom 50, Monday 19 August 2013, Rzewski himself playing the solo part, and final works to the year of death, America: A Poem of 2020, The Naked Truth of 2021, premiered in Lyon the week before his death, and the late style is freer and more spontaneous, but the mechanisms do not change, borrowed tunes, variation, spoken text, the pianist's body, strict frames with free insides.

## Aesthetic logic

The tune is the politics, and the rule is the freedom, and the body is the instrument, and that is the logic that runs through all sixteen mechanisms, and the piece is not an object but a machine for generating difference out of sameness, and the notation is the machine's instructions, and the improvisation is the machine's fuel, and the room is the machine's floor, and the tune is the machine's input, and the virtuosity is the machine's output. The early serialism is the same logic in its negative form, a system so exact that only a professional could play it, and MEV is the logic turned inside out, a room so open that anyone must play, and the political turn is the logic made into a form, the tune as a flag and the variation as an accumulation, and the speaking pianist is the logic made into a body, one person doing two jobs, and the long forms are the logic made into time, a work built over years instead of a month, and self-criticism is the logic made into a person, the composer putting his own reputation on trial in public, and the late piano pieces are the same logic at its simplest, one person at a piano playing a tune anyone could learn, and the music is not about him, it is about what happens between the tune and the player, and between the rule and the freedom, and between the body and the instrument, and when the situation changed, the music changed with it, all the way down to the notes, but the piano never left the room.

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. THE #403 DATES ARE WRONG BY A YEAR. The #403 research notes and the study log give Rzewski as "(1938-2020)" in the network and future candidates. He died Saturday 26 June 2021, aged 83, NYT, Washington Post, Princeton Alumni Weekly, Wikipedia, Presto all agree. Any study text copied from #403 must say 1938-2021. This is the single most important correction in this leg: the doorway premise itself arrived with the wrong death year.

C2. DEATH-DATE CONFLICT: 24 vs 26 JUNE 2021. The majority of sources say 26 June 2021. But the Sub Rosa / Les presses du reel "Speaking Rzewski" pages and the ANOST page all say 24 June 2021. Use 26 June; note the 24 June variant here. The sin80 mirror adds "at 3 a.m. on June 26, 2021", snippet-only, not carried.

C3. "THE ALGEBRA OF EVERYDAY LIFE" IS NOT A LINER-NOTE ESSAY IN ORIGIN. It is the preface to Christian Wolff, Cues: Writings and Conversations (MusikTexte, Cologne, 1998), reprinted by permission as the liner notes for New World Records 80658, Christian Wolff, 10 Exercises (2006). The PDF read prints the preface credit on its own pages. The doorway's "liner-note essay" is true only of the 2006 reprint. The study calls it "the preface to Wolff's Cues (1998)".

C4. COMING TOGETHER DATE CONFLICT: 1971 vs 1972. The work list and the LA Phil note's chronology give 1971, and Rzewski's own programme note says "written in November and December of 1971", snippet-only. Hyperion and Schell both say 1972, and musicianguide's selected-compositions list also says 1972. Best evidence is Rzewski's own note: composed November-December 1971. The 1972 dates probably reflect the first performance or publication year. The study says "composed November-December 1971" and notes that some sources date it 1972.

C5. ATTICA DATE: 1972, COMPANION. The work list gives Attica (1972); scaruffi agrees. It is the companion to Coming Together, also called "Coming Together, Part Two", snippet-only. Some sources imply 1971; none read end to end does. Carried as 1972.

C6. PEOPLE UNITED DURATION CONFLICT. musicianguide says "45-minute"; Nonesuch says "a 50-minute set of piano variations"; Wikipedia says "a typical performance spans roughly 60 minutes"; the NYT calls it "an hour long". The study says "about an hour" and gives no precise minute count.

C7. THE FREE LIBRARY ARTICLE DATES THE PEOPLE UNITED "(1973)". Wrong. Composed September-October 1975, premiered Saturday 7 February 1976. The 1973 date is the year of the Chilean coup, not of the piece. Not repeated.

C8. MUSICIAN_GUIDE DATES THE ORTEGA SONG TO 1969. Wrong. The song was composed in May or June 1973: Hyperion says Ortega heard the chant "three months before Augusto Pinochet's military coup" and the tune "emerged in a flash"; the #378 Ortega study says Ortega composed it "on a night of May or June 1973". 1969 is the year the Unidad Popular coalition formed, not the year of the song. Presto's "from 1970" is also wrong. Use 1973.

C9. PRESTO SAYS OPPENS "WON A GRAMMY" FOR THE RECORDING. Wrong. Wikipedia: her 1979 recording "was named 'Record of the Year' in that year by Record World and received a Grammy Award nomination." Nomination, not a win. The study does not say she won a Grammy.

C10. THE LIEGE PROFESSORSHIP DID NOT RUN "UNTIL HIS DEATH". The NYT obituary says he "was a professor at the Conservatoire Royal de Musique until his death", and Nonesuch says "From 1977 until his death in 2021, Rzewski was Professor of Composition at the Conservatoire Royal de Musique in Liege". Both are wrong. He taught at Liege 1977-2003 and was Professor of Composition 1983-2003 (Living Composers Project, snippet-only; musicianguide career line says "professor of composition, Conservatoire Royal in Liege, Belgium, 1977-2003"). He retired in 2003 and kept performing and teaching as a visitor. Use 1977-2003.

C11. CHILDREN COUNT CONFLICT. The NYT lists six surviving children: Alexis, Daniel, Jan, Noemi, Esther and Noam. Wikipedia says five children with Nicole Abbeloos and two with Francoise Walot, which is seven. The Princeton memorial lists "his wife, Nicole Abbeloos; his companion Francoise Walot; six children; and five grandchildren", search excerpt. The study states no number; the sources disagree between six and seven.

C12. PIANO START AGE CONFLICT. Wikipedia says he "began playing piano at age 5". musicianguide, Les presses du reel and ANOST all say age 3 ("had his first piano lessons at the age of three"). Unresolved in the sources read. The study states no age.

C13. THE MEV TRIO SHORTHAND OMITS FOUNDERS. "Co-founded with Curran and Teitelbaum" is the standard shorthand, but the MEV article lists the founding members as Allan Bryant, Alvin Curran, Jon Phetteplace, Frederic Rzewski, Richard Teitelbaum, and Carol Plantamura, with Ivan Vandor and Steve Lacy as other early members. The study names Bryant, Phetteplace and Plantamura alongside the trio.

C14. THE ROAD DURATION CONFLICT. Nonesuch calls it "a seven-hour work"; Schell says "64 pieces lasting nine hours in all"; the NYT calls it "the polystylistic, 10-hour-plus cycle". The parts are eight. The study gives the parts and piece count and says "roughly nine hours" with the range noted here.

C15. THE TRIUMPH OF DEATH AND DIE ERMITTLUNG. Nonesuch says the oratorio is "based on texts adapted from Peter Weiss's 1995 play Die Ermittlung (The Investigation)". Die Ermittlung premiered in 1965, not 1995. The 1995 is an error in the Nonesuch copy, probably a reprint or edition date mistaken for the play. Use 1965.

C16. ATTICA DEATH TOLL: 43, NOT 39. LA Phil, NYT and musicianguide all give 43 dead, including 33 prisoners (LA Phil: "at least 43 people died, including 33 prisoners"). The CSULB programme says Melville was killed with "38 other people", snippet-only, i.e. 39; that counts only the retaking, not the four who died during the uprising itself. Use 43.

C17. MELVILLE LETTER DATE CONFLICT: 1970 vs 1971. The Wikipedia Sam Melville article says in its Legacy section that the letter was written "on May 16, 1970", but the same article's search excerpt and the LA Phil note and Rzewski's own programme note all say 16 May 1971, and the letter itself says "it's six months now". Use Sunday 16 May 1971. The 1970 in the Wikipedia article is an internal contradiction in that article.

C18. CARDEW ATTACKED RZEWSKI. The doorway calls Rzewski "the ally", and he was, personally. But in Stockhausen Serves Imperialism (1974) Cardew subjects Coming Together and Attica to ideological criticism (Commentary review, snippet-only), and the Free Library article records that Cardew was "scathing of the minor failures of composers he actually felt closer to musically and politically, including Christian Wolff and Frederic Rzewski". The ally was, in Cardew's published judgment, not political enough. The study carries this: the doorway is a friendship and a shared turn, not an endorsement.

C19. "PIANIST OF WOLFF'S MUSIC" NEEDS PRECISION. It holds: Wolff's Accompaniments (1972) was written for Rzewski, and Rzewski played piano on the 2005 Exercises recording. But Rzewski was one of several pianists of Wolff's music (John Tilbury, David Tudor, Sally Pinkas, Hildegard Kleeb are also named in the sources), and the NWR bio's phrasing is that he "followed in David Tudor's steps as the premiere pianist for new music", which is a claim about his general career, not a post as Wolff's pianist. Say "a pianist of Wolff's music", not "Wolff's pianist".

C20. "PLAYED THALMANN VARIATIONS": EVIDENCE GRADE. The full-page sources read in this leg list Rzewski's Cardew recording as "We Sing For The Future! 2001". The Thalmann Variations recording by Rzewski (recorded 2002, DOXY LP with We Sing for the Future) rests on the Forced Exposure listing, snippet-only, and on the #403 leg's sources. It is almost certainly true, and the #403 study already asserts it, but this leg did not verify it from a full-page read. The study says "recorded Cardew's We Sing for the Future and Thalmann Variations (2002)" and flags the Thalmann half as snippet-supported here.

C21. THE CARDEW DEFENCE: HOLDS, WITH A 2009 COUNTERWEIGHT. The quote is verbatim and sourced: Varela, "A Question of Language", Journal of Experimental Music Studies, March 2004, cited as footnote 35 in the Free Library article and quoted at length there. But the same article records that when Rzewski was asked in 2009 to reflect on Cardew's Marxism, "he expressed his sadness at 'the mind of my dear friend being poisoned'". He defended the late music as experimental while grieving the politics behind it. Both halves belong in the study.

C22. "STAYED POLITICAL" NEEDS THE GROUCHO CORRECTION. Rzewski never joined a party; he called himself a "Groucho-Marxist"; "I did not do politics, I am a composer" (Platypus Review, 2018). He was close to the Italian Communist Party in Rome and played in occupied factories, mental hospitals and prisons with MEV, and he organised benefit concerts through the Musicians Action Collective. The study says he stayed political in the music and in public solidarity, and never in party membership. That is exactly the difference from Cardew, and it is the point of the doorway.

C23. "OPERA HOUSES DON'T COME ASKING ME TO WRITE OPERAS" IS RHETORIC. The NYT 2008 quote is Rzewski explaining why he writes piano music. But he had written two operas: The Persians (1985) and The Triumph of Death (1987-88). The line is a joke about commissions, not a fact about his catalogue.

C24. SNIPPET-ONLY CLAIMS THAT MUST NOT ENTER THE STUDY AS FACT. (a) Second Structure (1972) as the source of the People United six-group scheme. (b) Wolff's "fingers of the hand, closed fist" reading of the six groups; Rzewski's own published description is the six stages (simple events, rhythms, melodies, counterpoints, harmonies, combinations), and the fist reading is Wolff's programme-note suggestion, not Rzewski's. (c) The Scratch Symphony (1997) title echoing the Scratch Orchestra: no source read connects it to Cardew deliberately; do not claim the homage. (d) Rzewski contributing pieces to the Scratch Orchestra collections Nature Study Notes and the Scratch Anthology: from the Varela interview search excerpt only; the PDF failed to open. Do not assert without a full-page source.
