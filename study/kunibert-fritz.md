# Kunibert Fritz: Study Notes

**Site:** Kunibert Fritz -- German painter of constructivism and concrete art; born 1937, Schwerin; died October 2023, Friedberg. No source read gives the day or month of either date: his own site says only "(died Oct. 2023)", the PRO Drawing Collection sheet says "1937, Schwerin -- 2023 October, Friedberg", and no obituary or death notice was found in any search this run. He studied 1958-63 at the HfBK Kassel in Fritz Winter's main class; he lived and worked at Gorbelheimer Muhle 1, Friedberg, the address shared with Edition + Galerie Hoffmann. His practice is one rule system pursued for over 65 years: squaring the square, Fibonacci counts as coloured rectangles, computer-generated randomness since the early 1990s. [C35731]

**Relevance:** Fritz helps investigate what it means to pervade a square field -- how many squares, in what sizes, in what order, and when does counted randomness stop looking random? His evidence is a specific work in Hans's own saves, "Pervasion of square fields" (1969, absgeo-023), plus a fifty-year rule system stated in his own words (divisions, then square grids, then picture series; the grid as matrix; three fill modes -- order, rhythm, random chaos) and a 2020 show titled for the one rule-break, "das unfolgsame quadrat" (the disobedient square). [C35741]

**Doorway:** The 441st doorway. Hop from the #820 Robert Mangold study, which ends on the open question of serial exhaustion -- a series worked until the motif is used up, and the stopping rule is the picture -- and on the discipline of exactly one clean failure per picture. Fritz takes the question from the single failure to the whole field: Mangold asks how little one figure has to miss by for the miss to be the work; Fritz asks how many squares it takes to pervade a field, in what order they arrive, and when counted randomness stops looking random -- and his answer is that the failure is not in the figure at all, but in the one cell of the grid that disobeys. [C35742]

**Depth:** Twenty works catalogued individually (1962-2023), eleven images viewed directly (the saved reference plus ten more: the 1984 Prototype emblem, the 1985 diptychs, the 1989 Prototype 3 and installation views, the Plexicube, and four groups of late Vasarely-show photographs); eight entries listing-only, three text-only, one known through an image-search description only (its lot page returned 403 and its image 502). Read end to end: the Vasarely Museum "Unity in Duality" essay and press room, kunibertfritz.de homepage / biography / film pages, the Galerie Hoffmann 2020 flyer PDF (352 lines), the Invaluable lot 702 page. Not read: any monograph, catalogue raisonne, national-press review, or obituary; the Instagram post behind absgeo-023 was not opened (caption only, via the local manifest); the artist site's gallery subpages were not reachable through the links fetched; no Fibonacci painting was ever seen as an image; no interview was located; no video was watched; no work was handled in person. All weekdays stated in this study were verified with `date -d` in America/New_York. [C35743]

## The question: what does it mean to pervade a square field?

Fritz's system is not one rule but four rule families that share a single matrix, and they must not be conflated. First, division: a square is divided, and the division itself is the picture -- his own account is that he started "from simple division possibilities of the square figure". Second, counting: a number sequence is made visible as countable coloured rectangles, so the picture can be verified by counting. Third, pervasion: identical micro-squares spread from a seed across the field, rotating as they spread, until the pattern implies continuation beyond the canvas edge. Fourth, mode: the same fixed grid is filled by mathematical order, by rhythm, or by computer-generated random chaos -- three fill rules, one matrix. The disobedience that gives the 2020 show its title is a fifth, limiting case: the square's own rule broken exactly once. [C35744]

Pervasion is the word that matters, and the Vasarely text defines it precisely: of the painting "central pervasion", to look at its individual squares enlarged in order to get to the essence of the picture "would contradict the effect of the picture. The whole thing is more than the sum of its parts. It only works as a unit." Its progressions "go beyond the edge of the picture. The claim to uniqueness within the boundaries of the image is extinguished. The exciting thing about the pervasion lies in the invisible, in the space in between, this is where the vibration occurs, which creates a kind of field effect." At the same time "the eye can wander over parts of the image, focusing on something and blending the rest of the image into an undifferentiated background." Contrast with Mangold (#820): Mangold's drama is one figure against one container, a circle that fails to fit; Fritz's drama has no figure at all, only a field with a gradient -- and the gradient, not the figure, is the picture. [C35745]

**Takeaway:** Do not build "a Fritz" as a square picture. Build one of the four rule families on a grid, with the grid held constant and the filling rule as the variable -- and remember that pervasion is a gradient of rotation across a field, not a pattern pasted on a background. [C35746]

## A life in the square: Schwerin to Friedberg

Kunibert Fritz was born in 1937 in Schwerin and died in October 2023 in Friedberg; the day and month of both dates are not established in any source read, and no obituary was found. He studied at the HfBK (Hochschule fur Bildende Kunste) in Kassel from 1958 to 1963, in the main class of Fritz Winter. His preliminary training covered colour, form and material studies; the workshops covered wood construction, bookbinding and textile studies; in parallel he attended the technical workshops for photography, lithography, etching and screen printing. He recalled Winter telling him at corrections: "... es gibt millionen Moglichkeiten." [... there are millions of possibilities.] [C35747]

The engagement with concrete, constructive art at Kassel took place in a group of 10-12 students who presented and intensely discussed their own work with the aim of an exact aesthetic, called the "Kassler Schule" (Kassel school). The Vasarely Museum's press text places him in the first generation of concrete artists from Kassel, the "Kasseler Konkreten", alongside Hartmut Bohm, Werner Krieglstein, Klaus Muller-Domnick and Friedhelm Tschentscher, who defined themselves against the trends of realism, surrealism and action painting; many of their professors were involved in founding documenta, and the students worked as assistants to them. His teacher Fritz Winter (1905-1976) was himself a Bauhaus student of Klee, Kandinsky and Albers, a professor at Kassel from 1955, and an exhibitor at the first three documentas (1955, 1959, 1964). A childhood detail Fritz himself connected to his later work: as a schoolboy he drew his city drawings of Frankfurt am Main on squared paper, which the Vasarely text (after Eugen Gomringer's catalogue text) reads as a premonition of what he had been "orchestrating" since the 1960s. That is his own retrospective framing, not independently verified biography; no source read gives details of his childhood in Schwerin or the move to Frankfurt. [C35748]

**Takeaway:** Fritz is not a late convert to the grid. He was trained inside a Bauhaus-structured school, by a Bauhaus student, in a student group whose stated aim was an exact aesthetic -- and he then spent 65 years on one element of geometry. A piece built on Fritz should feel like a rule obeyed for a lifetime, not a style tried on. [C35749]

## The grid is the work, not the preparation

Fritz's picture "unfinished" (1966) shows the grid, the basis of his works, as an independent image: "It is the coordinate system that connects all his works. His matrix, so to speak." Everything else in his oeuvre is a filling of that matrix. His own website states that "Die Quadratur des Quadrates hat den Kunstler uber 65 Jahre in ihren Bann gezogen" (squaring the square held the artist in its spell for over 65 years), and that his investigations sometimes put the square at the centre of the work and sometimes use it as the micro-element from which a large sign or a regularity develops; the canon of his works always played out in the square grid. The same text places his position as a "neuer Konstruktivismus" (new constructivism) restricted exclusively to the regularities of the relationship of form and colour in a mathematical rule system with a cybernetic aesthetic. The Vasarely text states plainly: "He has been squaring the square for 65 years." The show opened in September 2022, so the 65 years reach back to about 1957 -- the years just before his Kassel studies began in 1958; the arithmetic is the leg's, the "65 years" is the show text's. [C35750]

**Takeaway:** A generative piece in his spirit starts from the grid and varies the filling rule, never from a motif pasted onto a background. The grid is the work; the fill is the variable. [C35751]

## The perfect squaring: one square, all tiles different (M1)

"Squaring the square" is the problem of tiling a square with smaller squares; in its most studied form, the "perfect" squared square, every contained square is a different size. The smallest simple perfect squared square (no subset of the squares forms a rectangle) was found by A. J. W. Duijvestijn by computer search; it uses 21 squares and has been proved minimal, with side 112. Fritz, by contrast, decided early on "to ignore the circle and focus on a single shape" -- the square is sometimes at the centre of the work, sometimes the micro-element from which a large sign or a law develops. [C35752]

The mechanism is a live catalogue search. Parameters: order n (number of tiles); the tiling itself, selected from the catalogue of simple perfect squared squares (orders 21-26 have 1, 8, 12, 26, 160, 441 tilings up to symmetry -- counts from the squaring-the-square literature, not from Fritz). Rule: draw the tiling from its Bouwkamp code (rows of flush-topped squares placed in the highest leftmost slot); colour each tile by its size rank (smallest = one end of a scale, largest = the other), so size order is legible as colour order. Constraints: all tile sizes distinct -- the perfect condition, never relaxed; tiles meet edge to edge with no gaps, and the outer boundary is exactly square; the smallest tile must remain legible (at least about 2% of canvas width) or the tiling reads as texture, not as a countable set. State transitions: step n upward (21 to 22 to ...) and the tiling re-solves -- tiles split and re-pack; the count of tiles is the visible state variable. Step through catalogue entries at fixed n and the same count re-packs differently. The Bouwkamp-code rendering and colour-by-rank are the leg's formalisation, not Fritz's documented procedure. [C35753]

**Takeaway:** A piece that is a proof. The viewer can count the tiles, check that no two match, and watch the machine find another exact fit. Fritz's "millions of possibilities" becomes literal: a catalogue with hundreds of entries at each order. [C35754]

## Fibonacci counting: the sequence you can check (M2)

In Fritz's "Fibonacci sequences" the sequence "can be concretely represented and counted as a series of differently coloured rectangles" (Vasarely Museum text). He writes the sequence out himself in the Hoffmann newsletter -- "0+1=1, 1+1=2, 1+2=3, 2+3=5, 3+5=8 usw." -- and adds that dividing the larger number by the smaller gives the golden number, more exactly the higher the sequence goes: "8:5=1,6, die goldene Zahl ist 1,618033988..." One listed work in the series, "Fibonacci Sequenzen A03" (2009, oil on canvas, 100 x 40 cm), was never seen as an image in any source read -- no count or colour list for it is stated anywhere. [C35755]

The mechanism is counting, not the spiral. Render the sequence as bars: F(k) unit rectangles of one colour per value (the literal "counted" reading), plus the spiral tiling of squares 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8... attached in clockwise rotation around the growing rectangle. Parameters: sequence index k; rotation direction; colour-per-value mapping. Constraints: the number of rectangles of colour c must equal exactly the Fibonacci number c stands for -- a miscount is a failed picture; ratios between successive sizes must approach phi, and the piece can display the running ratio F(k+1)/F(k) as it grows (1, 2, 1.5, 1.666..., 1.6...) so the convergence is visible. State transitions: k advances by one -- one new batch of rectangles appears, the ratio updates, the field grows by one turn of the spiral; reverse k and the picture un-grows. The attachment rule and bar rendering are the leg's formalisation; the counts and colours are Fritz's. [C35756]

**Takeaway:** Most Fibonacci art shows the spiral; this piece shows the counting. The rectangles are there to be counted, and the count is always right -- that verifiability is the mechanism, and the ratio is only its readout. [C35757]

## Pervasion: the field that turns as it spreads (M3)

The saved reference, absgeo-023, viewed directly this run: a square black field carrying a pyramid of small white squares -- a single square at the top centre, rows widening downwards to about fifteen across at the base, the squares in the middle rows rotated 45 degrees so they read as diamonds, the bottom rows upright squares again, the figure stopping short of the bottom edge and the top corners left as empty black field. The manifest caption (instacollectionofart, Instagram post of 25 June 2022) identifies it as "Pervasion of square fields", 60 x 60 cm, 1969; no medium is stated in the caption or in any source read. The Instagram post itself was not opened. [C35758]

The mechanism is a gradient of rotation, not of colour. Identical squares on a lattice of spacing s over a plane larger than the canvas; each square rotated by theta(d) = 45 degrees x clamp(d/D, 0, 1), where d is distance from seed S and D is the distance of full diamond (formalisation); in the middle band theta is quantised so upright and rotated rows alternate, as in the 1969 image; render only inside the canvas so the field is cropped. Parameters: seed S, turn distance D, spacing s. Constraints: squares are identical in size -- size variation is a different mechanism (M1/M2); theta is a function of d only, so the field has perfect radial order, and any square that breaks the function is a disobedience (M7), not noise; the field must be cropped by at least one canvas edge, so the pervasion reads as unfinished and continuing. State transitions: S moves (drag) and the whole field re-pervades from the new seed -- every square's rotation re-solves by its new distance; D grows and the diamond region expands outward like a wavefront. [C35759]

**Takeaway:** Near the seed the squares sit square to the grid; far from it they have turned into diamonds; the in-between is where the "vibration" lives -- the eye cannot settle on one orientation, so the field shimmers between two readings. Dragging the seed lets the viewer re-pervade the picture from anywhere: the centre is a variable, not a given. [C35760]

## Three fill modes on one matrix: order, rhythm, chaos (M4)

"Like a universe of his ideas, he fills these [grids] with geometries. Sometimes with mathematical order, sometimes with rhythms and sometimes with random chaos, which he has been creating randomly with a computer since the early 1990s." All series "have one thing in common. They are always laid out in a square grid." The three modes are named by the sources, not their procedures: no description of his post-1990 computer program (software, algorithm, or random-number method) was found, so every random procedure below is the leg's formalisation, not his documented procedure. [C35761]

The mechanism holds the grid constant and varies only the fill. One fixed n x n grid; per-cell state = rotation (0 degrees / 45 degrees) and colour (black/white, or a small set of contrasts). ORDER: cell state is a deterministic function of position, e.g. rotation = 45 degrees iff (i + j) is even (checkerboard), or rotation steps by a fixed increment along each row (arithmetic progression). RHYTHM: cell state is periodic with period p and phase phi, e.g. rotation follows a repeating pattern of length p down each column, with phi shifting one step per column (a diagonal wave). CHAOS: cell state is drawn from a seeded RNG, one independent coin flip per cell for rotation and one for colour. Constraints: the grid never changes -- only the fill rule does, and a mode change must not move a single grid line; each mode must be recognisable at a glance (ORDER has no exceptions, RHYTHM has an exact period, CHAOS has no detectable period); transitions between modes interpolate per cell (each cell flips on its own schedule), so the mode change is a visible wave, not a cut. State transitions: m cycles ORDER to RHYTHM to CHAOS to ORDER; within CHAOS, re-seeding re-rolls every cell; within RHYTHM, phi advances and the wave travels. [C35762]

**Takeaway:** Fritz's whole working method in one piece: one matrix, three universes. Randomness is used honestly -- as one named mode among three, with the computer's role exactly where Fritz puts it (since the early 1990s), not as a generic "generative" garnish. [C35763]

## The searching gaze: no place to rest (M5)

Fritz's viewing rule governs the whole oeuvre: "Was mich interessiert ist, dass der Blick des Betrachters auf einem Bild sich suchend bewegen kann und nicht an einer Stelle festgehalten wird. Das bedeutet fur mich, dass die Rasterteile gleichwertig und gleich wichtig sind, keine Figur-Grund-Teilung auftritt, sondern Bewegung" (what interests me is that the viewer's gaze can move searchingly over a picture and is not held at one place; that means for me that the grid parts are of equal value and equally important, no figure-ground division occurs, but movement). In "central pervasion" the eye "can wander over parts of the image, focusing on something and blending the rest of the image into an undifferentiated background." [C35764]

The mechanism makes the absence of a focal point measurable. Per-cell contrast weight w drawn from one shared narrow distribution (formalisation: uniform within about 10% of the mean); no unique maximum -- the top weight is shared by at least about 10% of cells. A simulated gaze walk (a random walk over cells weighted by local contrast, with dwell time per cell) records dwell share per cell; the piece passes when no cell holds more than about 5% of total dwell (thresholds are formalisations for the builder's harness). Constraints: no unique maximum anywhere -- one bright cell breaks the rule and the picture becomes about that cell; no figure-ground division, so the fraction of "figure" cells vs "ground" cells must stay near 50/50, never 90/10; the walk must keep moving, with mean dwell per cell below threshold, or the field has a hidden attractor. The walk itself is the motion -- a faint trace of its recent path can be shown (toggleable), so the wandering is visible without adding a figure to the picture. [C35765]

**Takeaway:** The inverse of every hero-figure piece: a picture about the absence of a focal point, with a measurable acceptance criterion (dwell share) instead of a feeling. Interaction is earned by breaking the rule: drag to inject one bright "fixation" cell and watch the walk get captured by it -- the user performs the experiment that proves why the rule matters. [C35766]

## The series machine: one change at a time (M6)

"Ich ging von einfachen Teilungsmoglichkeiten der Quadratfigur aus, dann arbeitete ich mit Quadratrastern und Bildserien" (I started from simple division possibilities of the square figure, then I worked with square grids and picture series). "Konstellation 1/3" (2001, oil on linen, 80 x 80 cm) is a series in at least three parts; parts 2/3 were not located. The mechanism is a systematic walk through one rule's parameter space: a parameter vector v = (n [grid size], theta-step [rotation increment per row], mode [M4's ORDER/RHYTHM/CHAOS], colour rule), walked in Gray-code order (exactly one parameter changes by one notch per step), so consecutive members are minimally different. Display the current member large, with its neighbours as small thumbnails in series order. Constraints: exactly one parameter changes per step -- two changes at once hide causality; the base grid never changes shape (always the square matrix); the series must be finite and enumerable, and the piece shows its position (member k of N) honestly. The Gray-code walking is the leg's formalisation of "one change at a time"; the sources attest the series practice, not the stepping rule. [C35767]

**Takeaway:** Fritz's "Bildserien" made executable, with the causality visible in the stepping. DEDUPE FLAG for the build leg: same form as MAN7 "The Series Machine" (#820, Mangold's W/V/X switches walked in Gray-code order). The alphabet differs (Fritz's grid parameters vs Mangold's switches) and the stepping logic is independently grounded in Fritz's own series practice, but the build leg should treat KFR6 and MAN7 as one family and build the stronger of the two first, not both blindly. [C35768]

## The disobedient square: the rule breaks itself exactly once (M7)

The 2020 show is titled "kunibert fritz + das unfolgsame quadrat" (the disobedient square), Edition + Galerie Hoffmann, Ausstellungshalle Friedberg-Ossenheim, Florstadterstrasse 10b, Friedberg, 5/6 December 2020 to 30 May 2021 (opening weekend Saturday 5 - Sunday 6 December 2020, weekdays verified). The one work in its list that breaks the square format is "ohne Titel a", 2020, at 64 x 60 cm: a rectangle, 4 cm wider than it is tall, in a career of 60 x 60 cm squares. The title is the show's, the dimensions are the newsletter's; reading the 64 x 60 cm canvas as the title made literal is the leg's interpretation, and it is flagged as such. [C35769]

The mechanism is a strict square-grid picture containing exactly one disobedience. A fully obedient n x n grid (all squares axis-aligned, equal, on-grid) plus exactly one disobedience of type d in {ROTATE one cell 45 degrees, STRETCH one row/column by a small factor (formalisation: about x1.07, echoing 64/60 = about 1.067), REPLACE one square with a rectangle, DRAW the grid itself as the subject (the "unfinished" move)}. Parameters: type d, position p, magnitude mu. Constraints: exactly ONE disobedience per picture -- two read as sloppiness, not disobedience; mu must be large enough to be unmistakable at a glance and small enough that the grid still reads as a grid (formalisation: rotation exactly 45 degrees, stretch 5-10%, never 50%); the disobedience must be findable but not shouting -- the piece passes when a first-time viewer locates it within a few seconds but does not see it in the first instant. State transitions: p moves (tap a cell to move the disobedience there -- earned, position is the parameter); mu grows from 0 to full and the grid visibly "gives" at one point; d cycles through the four types. [C35770]

**Takeaway:** The title piece of the 2020 show as a machine for finding the one wrong square. Distinct from MAN1 (Mangold's near-miss is a figure mis-registered against its container; this is a cell misbehaving inside its own grid) -- the one-failure discipline is shared, the failure's object differs. And the sharpest statement of Fritz's lifelong discipline: you can only disobey a rule you have obeyed for 65 years. [C35771]

## The remix: the flat picture becomes a space (M8)

Late in life Fritz turned a 1966 work into something else: it was "updated and technically developed, similar to the musical form of a 'remix'". He "uses both the classic form of the screen and a transformation of the series of images into a virtual space that can be experienced with 3D glasses and is available as an NFT. Here you have the opportunity to blend the four resulting images into one another and fly through them at the same time." In virtual reality "the viewer is immersed in a fractal 3D sculpture"; "the fractal structure of the sculpture in virtual reality makes infinity more physically perceptible." Which 1966 work was remixed is not stated in any source read. The realisation credits on his site's film page: Jan Daniel Fritz; 3D movie design Tilly Hensellek / Raumevent; VR programming Carlos Marruffo / NextLATAM; sound Inner Lotus Music -- and a first NFT in video-loop format, "Progression U 3 / 2022", whose proceeds were donated in full to Ukrainian children. [C35772]

The mechanism is depth as a blend. Four grid-states S1..S4 (four settings of M4's modes / M3's pervasion seeds -- the sources name "four resulting images" but not their settings, so the states are the leg's formalisation) stacked in depth; the visible image at depth z in [0, 1] is the weighted blend of the two nearest layers, weights linear in z; flight advances z slowly (at least 8 s per state, formalisation). Parameters: the four states, flight speed, whether the flight loops (z wraps) or ping-pongs. Constraints: all four states share the identical grid -- the matrix is the constant, the fill is the variable; the blend must be a true crossfade of states, not a slideshow cut; the flight must be slow enough that each state is readable. The fractal note (self-similar structure at depth) is a formalisation to test: each layer could itself contain a smaller copy of the grid; the builder should test whether that reads or clutters, and drop it if it clutters. [C35773]

**Takeaway:** Fritz's own late move -- the flat concrete picture becoming a space -- executed on a flat screen with no headset. It is the most speculative prompt in this set (the sources describe a VR/NFT work, not its parameters); if the build leg finds the blend unreadable, retire this prompt rather than forcing it. [C35774]

## Aesthetic logic

Fritz's aesthetic logic is a discipline of opposites brought to balance. His own words: "die Struktur meiner bildnerischen Arbeiten besteht aus dem Aufzeigen von Gegensatzen, die zum Ausgleich kommen sollen. Was die Form betrifft, beschranke ich mich auf den Gegensatz von senkrecht und waagrecht, z.B. der Begrenzung des Quadrates." (The structure of my pictorial works consists in showing opposites that are to come into balance. As far as form is concerned, I limit myself to the opposition of vertical and horizontal, e.g. the boundary of the square.) For colour he used colour contrasts and progressions. Hans-Peter Riese is quoted describing the square as a democratic form because it has equal sides and equal angles, and Klaus Staudt is quoted on the number four: "The number four corresponds to the square." Eugen Gomringer points to the musical element of the works -- Fritz has been "orchestrating" for us since the 1960s. [C35775]

The evolution across sixty years keeps one constant and changes everything around it. Early work (1962-1974) is square-format and black-and-white or print-based: a central division, a bare grid, a penetration, a pervasion of small white squares on black, then a systematic 8x8 print and small colour serigraphs. The middle period (filed 1984-1989, one object 1987) moves to tall white panels and the figure-ground game: one black square, doubled and split across two panels, then coloured with the three primaries, then carried onto rods ("a work consisting of four rods (1987), on which small squares are painted in the middle -- in changing colors -- not only on the upper side but also on the sides. thus, the third dimension is also included") and a transparent cube (Plexicube 1, undated, its faces covered with tiny printed squares that interfere into a moire-like flicker where they overlap). Late work (2001-2023) returns to the square format but at a larger scale and with denser systems: a constellation, Fibonacci rectangle counts, untitled oils, and finally the woven red/black grids, grey spiral mazes and black dot-fields of the Budapest show. The constant across all of it is the square -- as format, as module, and as the only figure. [C35776]

## Rejected candidates

Six candidates were considered and rejected as prompts, with reasons, so the study does not re-propose them. "Black-and-white duality as a piece": juxtapositions of black and white and "unity in duality" are a palette and a theme, not a rule; black/white survives only as the colour rule inside M4/M5. "The golden ratio as a vibe": phi = 1.618... as a proportion is not a mechanism; any rectangle can be 1.618:1; M2 survives because counting (colour = count, verifiable by counting) is the mechanism, not the ratio. "The watch collector / technology fan": biography, not a rule; the VR/NFT turn is usable only as M8's blend-and-fly structure, not as a piece about watches or technology.
