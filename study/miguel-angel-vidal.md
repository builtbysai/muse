# Miguel Angel Vidal: Study Notes

**Site:** Miguel Angel Vidal, 27 June 1928 - 15 January 2009, Buenos Aires -- Argentine painter, formally trained, co-founder of Generative Art (Arte Generativo) in 1959 with Eduardo MacEntyre, the professor of the founding pair and the movement's other voice: where MacEntyre drew curves from a generating point, Vidal drew only straight lines, repeated by rule until they became light (C9001, C9101, C9073). He died aged 80; the year 1928 is agreed by the Spanish Wikipedia, his official site, the V&A, Tate, the Museo Moderno and Heritage, and this study prints it without a conflict, carrying the single dissenting source -- an arte-online note that opens "Miguel Angel Vidal (Buenos Aires, 1929-2009)" -- as a flagged typo, not a rival date (C9001). The exact days, 27 June and 15 January, rest only on the Spanish Wikipedia plus a low-authority astrology site, and are printed as probable, not doubly verified (C9001). He graduated in 1952 from the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes Prilidiano Pueyrredon, taught drawing there from 1970, was its Rector 1989-1991, and was Professor of Painting and Thesis Director at the Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes Ernesto de la Carcova 1990-2007 (C9005). His base was Buenos Aires for his whole life, his workshop in the Retiro neighbourhood, with shows in London, Rio, New York, Paris, Stockholm, Quito and Tokyo (C9005, C9016, C9017). The work that matters is the straight line made generative: one polygon displaced step by step, hundreds of straight lines joining its successive positions, until the flat field shimmers, lifts, and seems to glow -- and then, in the 1970s, the same rule tested in real space and real light, and in the 1980s the line abandoned altogether for light itself (C9074, C9076, C9077).

## The life: the professor who founded a movement

Miguel Angel Vidal was born in Buenos Aires on 27 June 1928 (C9001). He showed an artistic vocation early; at twelve he began studying drawing with the engraver Eleuterio Navarro, who taught extension classes at a school in the La Boca neighbourhood, and with Navarro he prepared for entry to the Escuela de Bellas Artes Manuel Belgrano, then continued at the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes Prilidiano Pueyrredon (C9002, C9003). His teachers there included Lino Enea Spilimbergo and Eugenio Daneri (C9004). He graduated in 1952 -- the degree title wording varies by source, Professor of Drawing and graphic designer in the Spanish Wikipedia, Professor of Visual Arts on the official site, and this study does not quote one wording as definitive -- and he studied the Old Masters, Leonardo, Michelangelo, Durer, researched naturalism, and studied the line as an expression before his expressive needs led him through Post-Cubism toward abstraction and geometry (C9005, C9006, C9007).

He was a member of Grupo Joven in 1949-1951, by the official biography and the Spanish Wikipedia (C9008); the date tension with MacEntyre, whose joining #634 follows as 1952 on two sources against Artsy's 1949-1951, is carried unresolved, and this study states no single shared-year range for the two men (C9112). From 1954 he took part in numerous exhibitions in Argentina and abroad, starting with "Cinco Artistas Argentinos" at Galeria Comte in 1954, and in 1958 he exhibited with MacEntyre and Berta Guido at Galeria Moretti, Montevideo, and took part in the First Inter-American Biennial of Painting and Engraving in Mexico (C9009, C9010).

In 1959 Vidal founded, together with Eduardo MacEntyre, the Buenos Aires Movement of Generative Art, promoted by the collector Ignacio Pirovano and the art critic Rafael Squirru in 1960 (C9011). The name was proposed by Pirovano, inspired by the words of the Belgian artist Georges Vantongerloo, who in 1915 had written of engendering new forms and reflecting "the generative process of the same" (C9012). The two artists wrote and signed a joint manifesto, dated 1959-1960 and presented at the first Generative Art exhibition in 1960 at Galeria Peuser, Buenos Aires, under the auspices of the Museo de Arte Moderno, with a presentation prologue by its director Rafael Squirru and a concert by the contemporary-music group EUPHONIA in support (C9013, C9014). The manifesto defines the movement's two signature effects as FORCE, forms seem to detach from and penetrate the base plane, and ENERGY, displacements and vibrations produce energy (C9015).

The 1960s carried him out of Buenos Aires without ever moving him out of it. In 1961 he took part in "Modern Argentine Painting and Sculpture", shown at the Institute of Contemporary Art in London and touring Edinburgh, Aberdeen and Cambridge University; in 1962 "Arte Generativo" was shown at the Museo de Arte Moderno in Rio de Janeiro, and his work toured Norrkoping, Lund, Gothenburg, Halmstad and Stockholm; in 1964 he showed in "Ocho Pintores Constructivos" at the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes and in "Del Arte Concreto a la Nueva Tendencia" at the Museo Municipal de Arte Moderno; in 1966 the Academia Nacional de Bellas Artes commissioned him to design the layout of its books; in 1967 he took part in "Mas Alla de la Geometria" at the Centro de Artes Visuales of the Fundacion Di Tella; in 1968 he showed in "Four Argentinian Painters" at Galeria Bonino, New York, and in "Made of Plastic" at the Flint Institute of Arts, Michigan (C9016, C9017, C9018, C9019, C9020).

The cybernetic turn came in 1969. Vidal carried out one of the first editions of digital art in Argentina, with images created on one of the first computers in the country at the Calculus Center of the ORT Schools, and some pieces from that edition were shown in "Arte y Cibernetica", organised by the Centro de Arte y Comunicacion (CAYC) at Galeria Bonino in August 1969 (C9011). Those computer-generated prints are now held by the Victoria and Albert Museum: an untitled 1969 screenprint from a plotter drawing (museum no. E.193-2008, "Generative Structure"), made with an IBM 1130-16K computer and donated by the Computer Arts Society, a copy shown at Tendencies 5 in Zagreb in 1973, and a second screenprint from 1970 (E.218-2008) (C9011). The V&A's own label states Vidal "co-founded the Generative Art Group in Buenos Aires in 1959 and later became a leading member of the Art and Cybernetics Group" and "was one of the first artists to create computer-generated artworks in Argentina" (C9011). Tate also holds Vidal works from the Grupo de Arte y Cibernetica Buenos Aires, dated 1969 and 1970, viewable by appointment (C9011).

In 1970 he was named Professor of the Drawing Chair at the Prilidiano Pueyrredon school (C9019). In 1971 he made the MURAL TOPOLOGICO: 20 interlocking modules of acrylic and aluminium, 250 x 400 x 8 cm in total, exhibited at the Quito Biennial, Ecuador (C9019). In the Topological Period he worked with acrylic and metal boxes to enter real three-dimensional space, where light is reflected on concave and convex surfaces, and transferred that to canvas (C9007). In 1977 he presented the series "Laberintos Topologicos" at Galeria Palatina, works made alongside the texts "Metaforas del Laberinto" by the poet and essayist Jose Edmundo Clemente (C9019). On 21 August 1979 the book "Miguel Angel Vidal, una version clasica del Arte Geometrico" by Tomas Alva Negri was presented; it won a Special Mention from the Asociacion Argentina de Criticos de Arte in 1979 and second prizes from the Camara Argentina de Publicaciones in 1980 (C9019).

From the late 1970s and early 1980s, light gained autonomy and prevailed over every other element in his painting; colour bands worked with glazes, limits became imprecise, and curves with melodic rhythms became almost tactile (C9007). In 1981 the Argentine postal service issued a stamp reproducing his work "Expansion de la Luz sobre la linea", printed at the Casa de la Moneda in offset multicolour, in sheets of 50, in a run of one million; in 1982 the wine firm Navarro Correas chose Vidal to illustrate the label of its 1976 vintage red wine, in a limited edition of numbered bottles; in 1984 "Lux Umbra Dei" was shown at the Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires and at the Fondazione Pagani in Milan; in 1986 the musical work "Trip through Trinity" by composer Francis Miroglio premiered at the Centre Pompidou in Paris with visual accompaniment using works by Vidal alongside Kandinsky, Chagall, Klee, Mondrian, Miro and Picasso, among others (C9019).

Vidal was Rector of the Prilidiano Pueyrredon school from 1989 to 1991, and from 1990 to 2007 he was Professor of the Painting Chair at the Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes Ernesto de la Carcova, where he was also Thesis Director (C9005). He carried out his whole body of work in his workshop in Buenos Aires (C9005). In 1994 he took part in the Second Konex Art Biennial, "Cien Obras Maestras de Cien Pintores Argentinos", at the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes; in 1998 he had an individual exhibition (1-25 October) at the Centro Cultural Recoleta; in 2008 he showed in "Ritmo Orden y Silencio - Un Mundo de Abstraccion Geometrica" at Galeria Vermeer and in "Geometria Sensible" at the Museo de Arte Construido (2008-2009) (C9019).

He died in Buenos Aires on 15 January 2009, aged 80 (C9001). Posthumous shows include "Geometrie Hors Limites" at the Maison de l'Amerique Latine, Paris (2010); "MIGUEL ANGEL VIDAL" at the Angel Guido Art Project, Buenos Aires (2010); "El Imaginario de Ignacio Pirovano" at the Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires (2010); and "Iman Nueva York" at Fundacion Proa (2010). A retrospective was held at the Museo Nacional de Arte Decorativo, Buenos Aires, in the Sala Ignacio Pirovano, opening 10 June 2015 and running 11 June to 30 August 2015, with about eighty works from the 1950s to the 1990s, catalogue by Alberto Bellucci and curation by Hugo Pontoriero; the Buenos Aires Legislature declared it of cultural interest. A later joint exhibition, "Arte generativo: el futuro a crear", curated by Maria Jose Herrera at the Casa Victoria Ocampo of the Fondo Nacional de las Artes, brought together 40 works by MacEntyre and Vidal, noting they first showed together at Galeria Peuser in 1960.

His awards include the First Prize Premio Leonor Vassena 1966, the Premio Paolini 1968, the Premio de la Critica de Arte 1968 or 1969 (the two sources date it differently; this study does not pick one), the First Prize Salon Italo 1971, the First Prize Bienal Sheraton 1979, the Premio Konex Diploma al Merito: Pintura Geometrica 1982, the Gran Premio de Honor de Pintura del Salon Nacional 2000, and Maestro Inolvidable UCES 2009 (C9019). His work is held by the Guggenheim, Albright-Knox, V&A, Tate, Musee d'Art Moderne de Paris, MAMBA, MALBA, MNBA, Museo Moderno (Equinoccio II, 1980, inv. P 472), Moderna Museet Stockholm, and the corporate collections Westinghouse, IBM and Motor Columbus (C9011, C9082, C9083).

**Takeaway:** the biography is the argument: a trained professor, not a self-taught draughtsman, co-founded the movement, and his training is visible in the work as exactness -- the line is an instrument reading, not a gesture.

## M1 -- The generator: one polygon, displaced step by step

A single straight-edged polygon -- triangle, square, octagon, diamond -- is displaced step by step, and lines join the successive positions. The manifesto definition, quoted in Revista Magenta: generative painting "engenders a series of optical sequences through a development generated by a form, for example a circle, a square, a scale, etc., adopting a series of displacements in contrary or consecutive senses that follow a perfect generative development" (C9074). In the viewed works the generator is always a polygon; no circles appear in the paintings seen (C9074). In Focos de luz (W05, 1969) the generator is an octagon: hundreds of straight lines join points around its edge, crossing to form a dense web with a clear X at the centre. In Mandala I (W09, 1971) the generator is a diamond, a square on its point: lines radiate from two points at the left and right middle, fanning up and down to the top and bottom points, nested triangles stacking toward the centre. In the 1969 V&A computer print (W04) the generator is a triangle rotated around four points, and the result reads as a pattern sample rather than a luminous object -- the same rule, executed by plotter instead of by hand.

**Takeaway:** the whole picture is one form, moved. If a piece draws many unrelated shapes, it is not Vidal; it is decoration wearing his palette.

## M2 -- Straight line only, repeated systematically

MCMC states it directly: "Miguel Angel Vidal's work is limited to the exclusive use of the straight line repeated in a systematic way" (C9073). MacEntyre, by contrast, builds from "linear circumferences drawn from generating points, giving shape to wide curves that overlap" (C9073). This is the cleanest visible difference between the two founders, and it holds across every viewed Vidal work: not one curved line in W04, W05, W07, W08 or W09 (C9073). Execution is draughtsman-exact: compositions transferred from huge tracing-paper sheets, paint "administered with chirurgical precision with the use of thin tools and guides... requiring a watchmaker's precision" (C9074). In the images this shows as perfectly straight lines, exact mirror symmetry, even spacing. MacEntyre's curves, per study #634, are freer and more calligraphic. Vidal's line is an instrument reading; MacEntyre's is a gesture. The computer prints (W04, W07) push Vidal's logic to its endpoint: when the hand is removed entirely, the work barely changes, because the hand was already behaving like a plotter (C9075).

**Takeaway:** the straightness is the signature. A Vidal piece that lets a line wobble, curve or drift has broken the one rule the whole system rests on.

## M3 -- Foci as light sources

The lines converge on a small number of generating points, which read as sources of light: four white starbursts in Focos de luz (W05, 1969), the central orange triangle in Mandala I (W09, 1971), bright diamonds at left and right in the 1970 Untitled (W07) (C9057, C9059, C9061). The title Focos de luz names the device. The study author viewed W05 directly: a deep saturated blue ground, a large octagon of fine white and pale-orange lines, four hard white starbursts at the left, right, top-left and bottom-left vertices, the lines seeming to radiate from them, the web turning warm orange-pink where the lines bunch (C9151). In the late work the foci become literal glowing bands and the lines disappear (C9057). The foci are not decoration added to a pattern; they are the generating points themselves, made visible as light.

**Takeaway:** every Vidal picture has a small number of points where the light comes from. Count them; if there are none, the picture has no source, and the lines are only texture.

## M4 -- Symmetry as engine, not decoration

Every viewed generative painting is mirror-symmetrical, across the horizontal axis, sometimes also vertical or diagonal (leg 2, mechanism 5). The symmetry is what makes the vibration readable: the eye compares the two halves and the tiny differences between dense and sparse line areas produce the shimmer. The mandala is the purest case: radial order built only from straight fans (C9061). The study author viewed W09 directly: exact mirror symmetry across the horizontal middle line, near symmetry top-bottom, the densest orange gathering in a small central triangle, the outer fans fading to pale white on a flat lavender-lilac ground (C9152). In I Dinamica Espacial (W08) a horizontal mirror line runs through the middle and two black diamond voids sit left and right of centre -- symmetry holding even when the figure is tilted and spatial (C9060). The symmetry is never broken for variety; it is the instrument that lets the eye measure the vibration.

**Takeaway:** symmetry is the measuring device. Break it and the shimmer has nothing to compare against; keep it and even a tilt reads as rotation.

## M5 -- Optical movement, two kinds

The manifesto claim, via Retroavangarda: the paintings "trigger a series of optical sequences... forms create a feeling of disattachment from the base plane" and "energy is reflected in changing position and vibrations" (C9074). In the images the movement is of two kinds: a pulsing or breathing from line-density gradients (W05, W09), and a spatial turning, as if a transparent solid rotates (W08). In I Dinamica Espacial a tilted, cube-like lattice of fine white and gold lines occupies the centre, drawn as if a transparent box is turning in space: three visible faces, edges defined by dense parallel lines, corners sharp, a narrow vertical band of orange, red, green and pale-blue diamond shapes at the centre like a slit of colour inside the wireframe (C9060). Nothing actually moves; the movement is entirely retinal, from density gradients. The manifesto's two effects map onto the two kinds: FORCE is the detachment of the solid from the ground, ENERGY is the vibration of the lines (C9015).

**Takeaway:** the movement is in the spacing, not in any playback. A piece that animates the lines sliding around has mistaken the effect for the mechanism; the lines must stay put and the eye must do the moving.

## M6 -- Colour: flat field to light

Early colour is flat ground plus white line plus one warm accent: orange on lilac (W09), orange on blue (W05), white on black (W07, W08), red ground with white lines (the untitled red X extra). The palette is described in MCMC as "a refined chromatic gamut" (C9073). Late colour is veiled and atmospheric: olive green and yellow in Equinoccio II (W16, 1980), red and magenta glows in the 1983 installation photographs, pale peach and blue-grey in the late installation views (C9068). The shift is from colour as flat field to colour as light. Equinoccio II proves it visually: square canvas, olive-green ground, no lines at all, three soft horizontal bands of glowing yellow-white light running across a slightly tilted darker rectangle, edges blurred and veiled, the brightest band at top centre fading downward (C9068). The late titles say it directly: light, veils of light, entities of light, sound of light (C9072).

**Takeaway:** early Vidal is one ground, one line colour, one accent -- never a mixed palette. Late Vidal is no line at all. A piece that mixes a busy palette with dense lines has taken the decoration of both periods and the discipline of neither.

## M7 -- Topology and real space

The early-1970s boxes and the 1971 mural take the generative idea off the wall: concave and convex aluminium surfaces inside acrylic cells reflect and decompose real light (C9076). The Mural Topologico (W10, 1971) is 20 interlocking modules of acrylic and aluminium, 250 x 400 x 8 cm in total, exhibited at the Quito Biennial (C9062, C9080). Caja topologica (W14, 1975) is a square green box divided into a grid of cells, each cell holding a curved aluminium sheet, concave or convex, catching the light so the whole box glows emerald with bright horizontal highlights; beside it in the same photograph, Movil generativo (W02, 1966), an orange translucent acrylic plane tilted in space with a clear faceted diamond form mounted on it, casting a soft coloured shadow on the white wall (C9066). This is Vidal's distinct contribution inside the partnership: MacEntyre stayed with the painted line; Vidal tested whether the same rule works in real space and real light. The late paintings then bring that lesson back to canvas as veiled light. The topologic boxes were also made in warm red-orange, not only the green of W14 (extra-escultura-topologica.png).

**Takeaway:** the boxes are the proof that the rule was never about paint. A piece that treats the topologic turn as a different style has missed it; it is the same generator, executed in reflected light instead of drawn line.

## M8 -- The computer as a phase, not a break

The 1969 Bonino/CAYC exhibition and the V&A prints show Vidal using the IBM 1130 at ORT to generate plotter drawings (C9075). The prints (W04, W07) use the same triangle rotation and the same symmetry as the paintings; the computer only executes the rule faster and more regularly. Generative Structure (W04, 1969) is a pale light-blue field on white paper: a strict grid of large diamonds and triangles built from fine horizontal hatching, two columns of diamond shapes, four focal points where diagonal white bands cross and pinch to a point, everything horizontal or diagonal, no curves at all, the hatching spacing never wavering, the four focal points exactly placed (C9056). The late light paintings still carry the computer's trace: arte-online notes the vibratory trama of the computer drawings "can be recognised in the works of his last stage" (C9072). The V&A lists the 1969 print under Computer Art; the paintings themselves were acrylic on canvas, and this study does not call the paintings computer-generated (C9111).

**Takeaway:** the computer changed nothing about the rule and everything about the regularity. A piece that uses code to make random lines has Vidal's tool and the opposite of his discipline.

## Evolution: line, surface, light

Early (1956-1971): the work is a system of straight lines. W01 (1956) is a small ink drawing on cardboard, 26 x 17 cm, pre-generative, its appearance unknown beyond the listing (C9053). By 1966-1971 the rule is fixed: a generating figure is repeated by displacement from generating points, and hundreds of straight lines drawn between the positions accumulate into a luminous solid that seems to hover, rotate, or sink into the canvas (C9074). The ground is flat and saturated and the line does all the work. Colour is restrained: one ground colour plus white and one warm accent. Symmetry is the organising principle. Scale grows: 100 cm squares in 1969, 130 cm in 1971, 150 cm in W08. The 1969-1970 computer prints are the same rule executed by plotter instead of by hand: flatter, more diagrammatic, monochrome.

Middle (1971-1975): the line leaves the flat canvas. The Mural Topologico is 20 interlocking acrylic/aluminium modules, 250 x 400 cm. The topologic boxes and the mobile put the geometry into real three-dimensional space, and real reflected light replaces painted light. The paintings of this period keep the generative titles but, from their captions alone, no visual claim can be made here (C9063, C9065, C9067).

Late (1979-1996): light takes over from line. The turn is dated by the biography to the late seventies and early eighties: "light gains autonomy and outstands any other element... the colour bands appear veiled, the limits are not precise and the curves with melodic rhythms are almost tactilely visualized" (C9077). The trigger named in arte-online is the 1979 Homenaje a Turner show at Canning House, London, and the Turner paintings seen at the Tate (C9078). W16 (1980) proves it visually: no lines, only veiled glowing bands. The 1983-1996 installation photos show the same language at large scale: soft vertical bands, glowing centres, faint line grids surviving only as texture. The 1984 Lux est Umbra Dei exhibition at Museo Moderno Buenos Aires (14 Aug - 2 Sep 1984) gives the late period its doctrine: light is the shadow of God (C9079).

What does NOT change: the centred, symmetrical composition; the square format (100-150 cm throughout); the idea that a small set of elements, repeated by rule, generates the whole image. What changes is the element: from drawn line, to reflective surface, to light itself.

## The network: the pair, the group, the school

The core pair is MacEntyre: co-founders 1959, first joint show September 1960 at the Peuser Salon, the name suggested by Ignacio Pirovano, who saw coincidences with Georges Vantongerloo (C9103). MCMC gives the first joint show as September 1960; the manifesto was presented at the first Generative Art exhibition in 1960 at Galeria Peuser (C9014). The two voices differ in method, and MCMC states the contrast exactly: "While Miguel Angel Vidal's work is limited to the exclusive use of the straight line repeated in a systematic way; Mac Entyre arises from linear circumferences drawn from generating points" (C9103). That straight-line-versus-curve contrast is the factual basis for calling Vidal Generative Art's "other voice" (C9103).

Grupo Joven: Vidal a member 1949-1951 per the official biography; MacEntyre's joining dated 1952 in #634; the membership overlap itself is not in doubt -- both men were in the group -- but the exact shared years are unresolved (C9101, C9112).

Ary Brizzi (born Buenos Aires 1930) is the closest adjacent name: "a major figure among the second generation of Argentine abstract-geometric artists" whose work "has been developed from the theories of Ignacio Pirovano and the Grupo Arte Generativo" (C9109). Shared rooms with Vidal, all evidenced: 1963, Eight Constructive Artists, Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, invited by Romero Brest, Brizzi and Maria Martorell "together with Manuel Espinosa, Raul Lozza, Eduardo Sabelli, Miguel Angel Vidal and Carlos Silva" (C9108); 1963, Del Arte Concreto a la Nueva Tendencia, Museo de Arte Moderno, Buenos Aires, included both Brizzi and Martorell (C9108); 1968, Bonino Gallery, New York, MacEntyre "showed with Brizzi, Polesello and Vidal" (C9112); 1972, "Projection and Dynamics", Museo de Arte Moderno, Buenos Aires, then Musee d'art moderne de la Ville de Paris, "the two members of Generative Art, together with Ary Brizzi and Carlos Silva, exhibited" in Buenos Aires, with Manuel Espinosa's and Miguel Ocampo's works added in Paris (C9103). Institutional overlap: Brizzi taught at the Prilidiano Pueyrredon school 1972-1995; Vidal was its principal 1989-1991; the two men therefore overlapped at the same school in 1989-1991, Vidal as principal, Brizzi as teacher (C9124, derived from C9101 + C9109).

## Aesthetic logic

Vidal's aesthetic is the logic of a trained hand executing a rule without deviation. The straight line is not a style choice among others; it is the whole system, and the system is: pick one polygon, displace it by rule, join the positions with straight lines, and let the accumulation do the work. The beauty is not in any single line -- no line in a Vidal is interesting on its own -- but in the density gradient the lines produce together, the way a field of identical strokes becomes a solid, a glow, a volume. That is why the computer prints barely differ from the paintings: the hand was already a plotter, and the plotter only proved it. It is also why the late work, which abandons the line entirely, is still Vidal: the rule was never the line, it was the generation, and when the element changed from line to reflective surface to light, the generation stayed. The square format never changes, the centred symmetry never changes, the palette never exceeds one ground, one line colour and one accent in the early work. Restraint is not a limitation here; it is the proof. A Vidal that is busy, curved, asymmetrical or multicoloured has not added to the system; it has left it.

The contrast with MacEntyre is the study's spine. Two men founded one movement in the same year, signed one manifesto, showed in one room -- and one drew curves from a point with a compass and the other drew straight lines with a ruler. The movement held both, because the manifesto defined generation, not geometry: "engenders a series of optical sequences through a development generated by a form". The form was MacEntyre's circle and Vidal's polygon; the development was the same. That is why the two studies belong together, and why Vidal is the other voice rather than the second voice: equal, distinct, and defined by the one line the other never drew.
