## The life: the architect who drew the prison instead of building it

Horacio Zabala was born in Buenos Aires on 6 June 1943, eldest son of Rodolfo Zabala, a doctor, and Mabel Mira, an agronomical engineer; his childhood was spent in Villa Pueyrredon and Villa Devoto (C10300). The exact day rests on the CVAA biography alone; all other sources give the year only, and one paragraph in an Arte-Online biography says 1945, evidently a typo on a page that elsewhere says 1943 (C10300). He is a living artist: the latest sources read, including the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes 2024 exhibition, present him as living and working in Buenos Aires, and no death date is reported in any source read (C10349).

The career runs in four phases, and the hinge is the draft: the architectural drawing that is the work, not the preparation for one.

(1) Self-education and the meeting with Vigo, 1960-1971. Zabala entered the architecture faculty of the Universidad de Buenos Aires in 1961; the degree year conflicts, 1971 (CVAA) vs 1973 (Herlitzka/Aguilar, repeated elsewhere) (C10302). He wanted Fine Arts; his father answered that architecture was the closest acceptable course (C10301). He educated himself in modern art from 1960 to 1970, from Cezanne onward, via museums, the Instituto Di Tella, the Florida street galleries, cinema and Cahiers du Cinema; he came to geometric abstraction through Malevich, Mondrian and Rodchenko, and followed the Argentine concrete artists Tomas Maldonado and Raul Lozza (C10303, C10304). The first solo show date conflicts: 1966 at El Galpon (CVAA, with a strip of abstract-geometric drawings and scissors for spectators to cut and modify the work) vs 1967 at Galeria Lirolay (Herlitzka/Aguilar, Lomholt) (C10305). No 1960s work could be catalogued: the 1967 Lirolay show is attested but no source read names its contents (C10352). The turn came after he showed minimal conceptual works to Jorge Romero Brest at the Instituto Di Tella and was rejected; leaving the institute he met Edgardo Antonio Vigo, who was preparing Novisima poesia (opened 1969) and encouraged him to persist and break his isolation (C10307). Vigo, fifteen years older, initiated him in rubber stamps as a cheap printing method; Zabala gave Vigo letrografos, lettering stencils (C10308). From 1968 he included texts in his works and used architectural drawing instruments -- tecnigrafo, tracing paper, heliographs, letrografos (C10309).

(2) The Thirteen, the Anteproyectos and the mail, 1971-1976. Invited by Jorge Glusberg in 1971 after visiting the Arte de Sistemas show at the Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires, Zabala joined the Grupo de los Trece; the joining year conflicts, 1971 (CVAA, Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes collection page) vs member 1972-1976 (Lomholt, Museo de Arte Moderno map, ISLAA, MC Galeria) (C10311). Leg 3 resolves it via ICAA doc. 1476333: Zabala was NOT one of the original members and became an official member in the latter part of 1972; do not write that he founded the group (C10427). Vigo was NOT a member of the Grupo de los Trece, though he took part in CAYC exhibitions; the Vigo-Zabala collaboration ran alongside the group rather than inside it (C10347). In 1972 he showed at Hacia un perfil del arte latinoamericano at the Museo Caraffa, Cordoba, under IRAM norms, works as heliographic copies on ruled tracing paper (C10312). The same year, at Arte e ideologia at the Plaza Roberto Arlt, he laid 300 metros de cinta negra para enlutar una plaza publica, a black plastic ribbon along the perimeter wall commemorating the Trelew massacre; the show closed a few days after opening (C10313). The tape went up at 4 p.m. on 23 September 1972 and was dismantled 24 hours later; nothing material remained, only catalogue, press and photographs (C10354). Also in 1972 he wrote "Este papel es una carcel" (This paper is a jail) in block lettering, photographed his hand writing it, and soon after made another work with the statement Art is a Jail (C10315). And in 1972 he made Hacha (Axe), an iron axe with its red blade embedded in a printed map of South America over Argentina (C10316).

In May 1973 came the solo show Anteproyectos at the CAYC, all three floors, drawings, photographs, objects and texts: plans of prisons and reformatories for artists, alterations of maps of Latin America, chess sets, designs for soup kitchens, a large structure of industrial metal tubes ringed by barbed wire; inaugurated 11 May 1973, a few days before Hector J. Campora took office, with Glusberg writing the presentation (C10317). Zabala considered it the definition of a work program the rest of his production would follow, with written language always present alongside image or object (C10318). The ICAA reading: Zabala suggests art, the art system itself, is like a prison -- not a rejection of the institution but a commitment to keep viewing it critically; prison shown from two sides, as a space where the artist can be alone to create, like an ascetic or hermit, and as a place where society confines the artist for rebellious rejection of the system (C10319). Also in 1973 he won the Premio Marcelo De Ridder with 25 botellas vacias y tres funciones posibles (25 bottles, three photographs showing three possible uses), later recreated with three bottles as Forma y funcion, and joined Systemes artistiques en Amerique Latine, opened in London and travelled to Antwerp, Brussels, Paris and Ferrara (C10320). The mail art with Vigo: in December 1975 they organised the Ultima Exposicion Internacional de Arte Correo at the Galeria Arte Nuevo, Buenos Aires, the first international mail art show in Argentina and the second in Latin America (C10321). The exact day, 5 December 1975, is carried from study #649 and the brief and was NOT freshly verified by leg 1 (sources read there confirm only December 1975); legs 2 and 3 state 5 December 1975 (C10321, C10367, C10401). The participant count conflicts three ways: 210 artists from 25 countries (Lomholt) vs 199 artists from 25 countries (MoMA post on mail art from Mexico) vs 199 participants from 24 countries (Vigo's own 1984 survey, per #649) (C10321). CORRECTION to the brief: the joint definition text Arte-Correo: Una nueva forma de expresion is dated March 1976, published in Buzon de Arte (MoMA post) -- the year 1975 belongs to the exhibition, not the text (C10322). Leg 3 adds that it was first published in Poetas Argentinos, September-October 1975, and reprinted in Buzon de Arte in January 1976, so the two accounts agree the text predates the March 1976 dating only in first publication, and disagree on the Buzon reprint month (January vs March); do not assert a single date without a third source (C10401). The text's mechanism: in mail art the fact that the work must travel a set distance is part of its structure, is the work itself; the post does not merely displace the work but integrates and conditions it, and the recipient stops being the classic collector and becomes an incidental custodian committed to maximum circulation (C10322).

(3) Exile, 1976-1998. Twenty-two years in Rome, Vienna and Geneva in that order, returning to Buenos Aires in 1998 (C10324). A solo show of prison anteproyectos at the Palazzo Taverna, Rome, February 1977, came via Achille Bonito Oliva; then Bonito Oliva's turn to the Transvanguardia and abandonment of the conceptual artists he had supported (C10324). From 1976 the socio-aesthetic operation Hoy el arte es una carcel? (Today, art is a jail?): two hundred millimeter-grid forms sent to artists, poets, critics, operators, theorists, photographers and designers; 146 replies from America, Europe and Japan, including Vigo, Muntadas, Abad, Brecht, Gerz, Thenot, Vautier, Levine, Ginzburg, Romero and Silveira; exhibitions and debates in Italy, Holland, France and Denmark; culminating in the 1982 seminar Oggi l'arte e un carcere? in Palermo, with papers by Luigi Russo, Perniola, Minguet, Crispolti, Baudrillard, Bahr, Migliorini and Ugo; a book of the same name published in Bologna, year in conflict, 1981 (Lomholt) vs 1982 (CVAA) (C10325). Further operations: Test socio-immaginario (1981, about 1,500 reactions from school pupils, show in Rome, catalogue prefaced by Mario Costa) and Il refugio antiatomico - Estetica della catastrofe (1983, survey of atomic shelters, drawings on photographs showing their uselessness) (C10327). From 1984 in Vienna the Duplicaciones: hand-copied covers of Time, L'Express, Der Spiegel and others in pastel on tracing paper, later front pages unfolded, folded, rolled or fragmented in boxes or jars, and coloured-pencil cancellations directly on newspaper pages (Le Monde, The Times, Corriere della Sera, La Nacion, Clarin, The New York Times) (C10328, C10329). In 1991 he moved to Geneva; L'art ou le monde pour la deuxieme fois, an interactive work for the Internet at the Centre pour l'Image Contemporaine; invited by Julien Blaine to the Centre International de Poesie Marseille (C10330). In 1994 he was hired by UNHCR as an architect site planner, posted to Equatorial Africa, designing refugee camps in Zaire for Rwandan citizens fleeing the genocide, six months that changed his outlook (C10331). In 1996, after eleven years of absence from Argentine exhibition rooms (CVAA phrasing, unverified -- no source read establishes a last Argentine show around 1985), he showed a large part of the Duplicaciones at the Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires (C10332). In 1997: El arte o el mundo por segunda vez, a work for the Internet, published as a book in 1998 by the Universidad Nacional de Rosario (C10332).

(4) Return and the monochrome, 1998-present. Friendship and dialogue with Luis Felipe Noe, book El arte en cuestion. Conversaciones (2000, prologue and notes by Rodrigo Alonso) (C10333). In 2002 a Fondo Nacional de las Artes retrospective synthesising 30 years, a poetics of minimal visual and material resources loaded with maximum meaning, requiring the spectator's cooperation and active reception (C10334). In 2003: 4000 cm3 at the Centro Cultural de la Cooperacion with 23 artists; in 2004 Cada dia somos menos recreated at the Centro Cultural de Espana, small multicoloured self-adhesive stickers covering an architectural corner (C10334). In September 2004: El desierto crece at the Montreal video and electronic art biennial, a worn-out car with lights and engine on, interior invaded by a mound of sand, a motion sensor firing Nietzsche's "El desierto crece" recorded in French in the artist's voice on approach (C10335). Small hand-built objects followed (pine wood and cotton thread chairs, Los viajes de Gulliver, Cuentos interrumpidos); a 2007 solo show at Fundacion Alon surveying the Anteproyectos (1972-1978) (C10336). From 2009 the Serie de hipotesis: monochrome paintings with grammatical or mathematical signs, also considered anteproyectos, shown in Monocromos (2010, Centro Cultural Recoleta, with Boullosa and Costa), Analogies and Differences (with Margarita Paksa, Henrique Faria, New York), Anteproyectos and Hipotesis (Carla Rey, Buenos Aires), and (Hipotesis) at 11 x 7 Gallery, 8 May-14 June 2013 (C10337). Books: Vademecum para artistas (2009, Asunto Impreso) and Marcel Duchamp y los restos del readymade (2008 Rosario, republished 2012) (C10338). In 2016 the retrospective La pureza esta en la mezcla at the Coleccion de Arte Amalia Lacroze de Fortabat, Buenos Aires (March-July, curated by Rodrigo Alonso), travelled to the Phoenix Art Museum as Horacio Zabala: Mapping the Monochrome, 19 October 2016-12 March 2017, nearly 40 artworks, the first expansive overview at a major United States museum (C10339). Awards: PEACE '75 (1975, with the Grupo de los Trece, Slovenj Gradec, 30th anniversary of the United Nations), Primer Premio Adquisicion LVIII Salon Nacional de Rosario 2004, Gran Premio Adquisicion Salon Nacional de Artes Visuales 2005, Achievement Award Ella Fontanals-Cisneros Art Collection 2018 (C10340). Holdings include the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes (Buenos Aires), MAC USP, Tate Modern, Metropolitan Museum of Art, Museo Reina Sofia, Museo de Arte Moderno (Buenos Aires), MALBA, Dallas Museum of Art, Phoenix Art Museum, Museo de Arte Contemporaneo de Rosario, Museo de Arte Tigre, Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes (Santiago), Middlesbrough Institute of Modern Art, Daros Latinamerica Collection, University of Essex Collection, J. P. Morgan Chase Art Collection, Coleccion Fundacion Alon, Ella Fontanals Cisneros Collection (C10341). Scale: since 1967 over 50 solo exhibitions and over 140 group exhibitions in America and Europe (Herlitzka biography) (C10342). In 2012 he reinstalled 300 metros de cinta negra for a few hours in the Plaza Roberto Arlt; CVAA's "30 years" is wrong arithmetic (1972 to 2012 is 40 years) (C10343). In 2014-2015 the public project Espacio entre parentesis, one of five selected in the Buenos Aires Sitio Especifico competition (250,000 pesos) (C10344). Most recent: Horacio Zabala. El efecto Piranesi at the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, 3 October-1 December 2024, room 33, curated by Mariana Marchesi, over 30 works from the early 1970s in dialogue with Piranesi's Carceri d'Invenzione etchings, with documentation of the CAYC show and the three main prison models (floating, underground, on column) (C10345). And Un pronostico sombrio: a newspaper article dated 2 March 1976 obturated under a black monochrome, a few weeks before the coup of 24 March 1976; MC Galeria's "24 days" is loose (2 to 24 March is 22 days) (C10346).

## M1 -- The prison drawn to scale: architecture as accusation

The first mechanism is a real architectural protocol applied to an impossible, punitive brief: plans, sections and elevations for single-person cells (underground, floating, on a column, in the Rio de la Plata or the sierras of Cordoba), the viewer reading them as buildable projects until the brief registers -- the occupant is an artist, the cell fits one person standing, the only opening is an oxygen tube (C10381, C10358, C10363). Zabala's gloss (Daros): "Prison is a totality and a unity that limits freedom, separating, excluding and negating, that is, depriving the being of its wholeness and unity" (C10381). The works appeared just before Foucault's Discipline and Punish (1975); the Reina Sofia ties them to discipline and control mechanisms of governments (C10381). Foucault is a parallel, not a contact: no source read shows any exchange between Zabala and Foucault, and he must not be presented as a correspondent (C10405). The watchable behaviour is the viewer's double reading: the viewer starts by reading a plan and ends by realising the plan is the accusation; the sarcasm lands when the drawing's competence makes the cruelty legible. Grounded in W05, W06, W10, W13.

**Takeaway:** a piece on M1 must show a competent architectural drawing of a cell for one artist, with the brief visible in the drawing itself. A scary building is not M1; a buildable plan for an unbuildable cruelty is.

## M2 -- The sentence that is the work: text plus rubber stamp

A short declarative sentence, stamped until it becomes the image: Este papel es una carcel states the thesis in words on paper; Revisar/Censurar repeats two bureaucratic verbs across a map until the territory disappears under them; Censura de libros de arte stamps the books themselves (C10382, C10353, C10364, C10357). The stamp is, in the MC Galeria essay's phrase, "the weapon/tool of a bureaucracy that is rushed to catalogue, select or stigmatize", imprinting its thirst for censorship on mountains, plains, and rivers (C10382). The watchable behaviour is the repetition doing the work: the viewer reads the sentence first, then watches the repetition do the censoring in front of them -- the words do not describe obturation, they perform it. Grounded in W01, W03, W04, W11.

**Takeaway:** one sentence and one stamp, repeated until the surface is unreadable. A slogan on a poster is not M2; the stamp doing the censoring is.

## M3 -- The map intervened: cartography as material

A familiar school map subjected to one violent or bureaucratic action: stamping, burning, cutting, deforming, hiding a country under ink (C10383). School maps remind us geopolitical identity is constructed in school; Zabala's operations question their merely descriptive character and turn them into analytical and critical instruments (C10383). The Reina Sofia on Deformaciones: through cutting, censoring, diluting colours, or scoring, Zabala denounces the power games behind a particular territorial order (C10383). The watchable behaviour is recognition followed by uselessness: the viewer recognises the map first -- the map from their own classroom -- and then cannot use it, because the action has broken the territory it described. Grounded in W03, W07, W08, W11, W12.

**Takeaway:** start from a recognisable school map and apply exactly one intervention. An invented map is not M3; the familiar map made unusable is.

## M4 -- The public action that is destroyed: mourning in the square

A cheap, temporary public gesture the authorities destroy, so the destruction completes the work: 300 metres of black plastic tape around a square, up at 4 p.m. on 23 September 1972 and dismantled 24 hours later, memorialising sixteen people shot at Trelew a month earlier; nothing material remained, only catalogue, press, and photographs (C10384, C10354). His governing question (Diecisiete interrogantes acerca del arte): "Can [art] offer a maximum of possibilities with a minimum of resources?" (C10384). The watchable behaviour is two acts: the public sees a square put into mourning, then sees the mourning removed by police; the second act is part of the work, and the 2012 re-enactment restages it as a mental reconstruction based on memory (C10355, C10384). Grounded in W02.

**Takeaway:** a public gesture made of cheap material that does not survive the day. A permanent memorial is not M4; the removal is the second half of the work.

## M5 -- The anteproyecto: the project as the work, the building refused

The architectural draft project treated as a finished work, with construction permanently withheld (C10385). Zabala prefers anteproyecto to sketch or draft because, in his words, an anteproyecto is "the premeditation of a problem, the condensation of an essay, the result of a suspicion"; some anteproyectos stayed as such and others were realised (C10385). The labyrinths of 1973 were envisioned with the knowledge they would never be built; the prisons were never meant to be built; the 2013 and 2018 anteproyectos are drafts for monochromes (C10362, C10376). The 1973 catalogue announced a whole programme: a journey, a distortion of Argentine territory, a shanty town, an altered chess set, a prison architecture, a playful and ideological monument, the design of trash, an act of freedom, a soup kitchen, and the destruction of a vegetable, an animal, and a mineral (C10377). The watchable behaviour is a rational design protocol -- plan, section, model -- followed toward an object that must never exist, and the refusal to build is where the meaning sits. Grounded in W05, W06, W09, W17, W20.

**Takeaway:** a complete, competent project for something that must not be built. A sketch for a real building is not M5; the finished draft of an unbuildable thing is.

## M6 -- The mail circuit: distance as structure

The postal journey as part of the work's structure, not its transport: when a sculpture is sent by post the creator merely uses transport, but in mail art the fact that the work must travel a set distance is part of its structure; the post does not exhaust its function in moving the work, it integrates and conditions it (C10386). The 1975 show put the definition into practice with hundreds of participants across two dozen countries (counts conflicted) (C10368). Hoy, el arte es una carcel extends the logic into a survey: Zabala solicited images and documents about art as a closed system from around the world, and the replies were the work (C10369). The watchable behaviour is the work arriving changed by its journey, or arriving as other people's answers to a posted question; the network, not the object, is what the viewer reads. Grounded in W14, W15.

**Takeaway:** something that travels a set distance and comes back changed, or a question sent out whose answers are the work. A mailed picture is not M6; the journey doing the making is.

## M7 -- The newspaper duplicated and crossed out: the simulacrum

A real newspaper, duplicated and crossed out until news becomes colour and layout (C10387). In Vienna in the early 1980s Zabala worked on series of real newspapers, ready-mades duplicated and crossed out; original and duplicate sit side by side so the viewer sees what it is and what it seems (C10387). Perniola, writing the prologue, read the crossing-out as the claim that nothing in the mass media deserves to be saved, and Zabala's gloss is "don't let yourselves be manipulated" by biased, pre-digested information -- while keeping the page layout that grants newspapers their value of truth (C10387). Crossing out and creating are, in his work, one indivisible act, moving from the legible to the visible, from writing to colour (C10387). The watchable behaviour is failed reading: the viewer tries to read the news, fails, and is left looking at the coloured lines of the crossing-out and the dated masthead -- the date (La Nacion, 14 July 1988; Le Monde, Bourse de Paris, 24 November 1993) is often the only legible fact (C10373, C10387). Grounded in W16, W18, W19.

**Takeaway:** a real dated newspaper page crossed out until it cannot be read, with the duplicate beside it. A fake newspaper is not M7; the real page made unreadable is.

## M8 -- The monochrome plus a sign: the hypothesis

A hand-painted monochrome placed next to an industrially made sign, so the two systems of meaning cancel each other out (C10388). The Hipotesis works combine monochrome canvases with laser-stencilled wood objects carrying grammatical or mathematical signs; the operations are, in Aguilar's press-release text, "hypotheses that lack demonstration" -- the conceptual simulacrum of the formula allows no result but conjecture, a stumbling, a tremor (C10388). Davis's gloss (same text): by articulating signs from different domains and codification systems, Zabala "perverts the very logic of the monochrome, its self-referential condition as a degree zero of painting" (C10388). The watchable behaviour is attempted solution: the viewer tries to solve the equation the signs propose, counts the monochromes, and finds the result is always a conjecture -- the painting stays mute and the formula stays unproven. Grounded in W20, with W12's school-map colour conventions as the early form of the same counting (C10365, C10388).

**Takeaway:** monochromes and a sign that together propose a sum that cannot be solved. A monochrome alone is not M8; the unsolvable equation is.

## The aesthetic logic: the draft, not the building

Zabala's aesthetic is the logic of the anteproyecto. Everything he makes starts as a protocol and ends as a refusal: the prison is drawn to scale and never built, the map is intervened on and never used again, the newspaper is duplicated and crossed out until it cannot be read, the monochrome is paired with a sign until it cannot be solved. The drawing is not the preparation for the work; the drawing IS the work, and the building, the territory, the news and the answer are all withheld. The viewer is never a spectator in the old sense: the viewer is the reader of the plan, the counter of the monochromes, the one who tries to read the crossed-out page and finds only the date. The work is not finished when Zabala finishes it; it is finished when a stranger reads the blueprint and understands that the cell is for them.

Against Vigo, his predecessor in this chain, the contrast is exact and it is the study's hinge. Vigo thinned the object until only the instruction, the journey and the filing remained; the work left the maker's hands and travelled to a stranger. Zabala keeps the object in the maker's hands -- the plan, the map, the newspaper, the monochrome -- and makes the object itself the accusation: the drawing is competent, the protocol is correct, and what the protocol produces is a cell, a censored country, an unreadable page. Vigo's gesture was to hand the work to the stranger; Zabala's is to hand the stranger a blueprint of their own confinement and let them read it. Both left the canvas and both were architects of a sort -- Vigo sewed files in a hexagonal office, Zabala trained as an architect and drew prisons instead of buildings -- but Vigo took the network as the canvas and Zabala took the drawing as the canvas. And where Vigo's gesture stayed the same while the support thinned, Zabala's stayed the same while the subject changed: from the prison to the map to the newspaper to the monochrome, the operation is always the same -- take a surface the viewer trusts, apply one competent intervention, and let the surface accuse the system that made it. A study that misses the draft misses Zabala; a piece prompt that shows a prison without the plan, a map without the intervention, a newspaper without the crossing-out, or a monochrome without the unsolvable sign, is not a Zabala prompt. The prompt must contain the protocol, the intervention, and the thing that only works when it is read wrong.
