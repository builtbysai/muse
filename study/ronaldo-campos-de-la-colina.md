## M8. Staging the forms

Each Afro-Peruvian form gets its own stage code, and the code is the mechanism. The lando is slow and melancholic, couples, handkerchief, and the zamacueca is its courtship variant, handkerchiefs and straw hats. The festejo is joyful and fast, skirts rippling, men double-timing the women's pace, "Festejo Ritmo" opening the Lincoln Center show, "Ollita" in minute rhythms, feet and hips and shoulders and arms and heads. The zapateo is a tap ensemble, six men in "Zapateo II", feet as percussion, and the feet are the second drum answering the cajon. "Son de los Diablos" is horned masks and Crayola-colored costumes, scampering devils, hand-jiving mischief, secret little vices made lovable. The alcatraz is sensual, a candle and fire game, "Alcatraz quema tu" in the cancionero. The mechanism is codification: village practice becomes repeatable stage numbers with fixed costumes and fixed counts and fixed fugues, and the fixing is the company, and the company is the code, and the code is the form, and the form is the evening, and the evening is the repertoire, and the repertoire is the system, and the system is the stage. **Takeaway:** give each form its own code, and the code is the costume and the count and the fugue. ## Network

- **Caitro Soto de la Colina (1934-2004):** first cousin raised as a brother, voice of Peru Negro. Evidence: raised together in San Luis, mothers Lucila de la Colina Orellana and Benedicta de la Colina taught both the music; Caitro sang in Peru Negro from the founding lineup, "Toro mata" and "Lando" and "Ollita noma" on the Gran Premio album; the blog, "ambos se criaron practicamente juntos... la sangre llamaba a su primo Ronaldo Campos, a quien lo consideraba como un hermano". He was a voice, NOT a founder; the four founders were Ronaldo, Lalo Izquierdo, Victor Padilla and Rodolfo Arteaga. The doorway link for #367.
- **Lucila Campos (1938-2016):** the other voice of Peru Negro, cousin, not sister, not mother. Evidence: Clara Lucila Campos Marcial, born in Lima on 16 August 1938, father Pedro Campos Ochoa of San Luis; voice of Peru Negro from 1969, seventeen years by the precise claim and around twenty by the rounded one; her unamplified "El Payande" won Buenos Aires in 1969 after the microphone failed; "Pobre Negrita" and "Navidad Negra" and "Cancion para Ekue" are her album showcases; died in Lima on Monday 12 December 2016, aged 78. TRAP: do not merge her with Lucila de la Colina Orellana, Ronaldo's mother, a different generation, different surnames, different towns.
- **Lucila de la Colina Orellana:** Ronaldo's mother, aunt of Caitro Soto and Susana Baca, teacher of the music with Benedicta de la Colina, and the mother-in-law who pushed Ronaldo to found the company. Evidence: the Perez Manrique blog, "tia de Caitro Soto y Susana Baca"; Paredes, the wife pressed him and then enlisted her mother to persuade him.
- **Chabuca Granda (1920-1983):** patron, the bridge to the cultural elite. Evidence: she backed the 1969 Buenos Aires trip with Thorndike and Calvo; she called Caitro "Hijo"; the cultural elite's bridge to the company, phase one of the patronage ladder.
- **Cesar Calvo (1940-2000):** poet, librettist, the company's literary author. Evidence: he created "Y la Tierra se hizo nuestra" with Thorndike and Chabuca, wrote "Navidad Negra" and "Cancion para Ekue", and is co-credited on "Ollita noma".
- **Guillermo Thorndike (1940-2009):** journalist, collaborator on the 1969 show and on the Banchero-era organization. Evidence: the Gran Premio credits and the Gladys Palmera stage history.
- **Victoria Santa Cruz (1922-2014):** teacher, then antagonist. Evidence: Ronaldo in her Teatro y Danzas Negras del Peru 1967 to 1969; she fired the four founders for playing a restaurant and called Peru Negro a "grupito de baile"; Paredes's book gives her "rhythmic awakening" a chapter of its own.
- **Nicomedes Santa Cruz (1925-1992):** teacher, theorist of the forms. Evidence: Ronaldo in his Cumanana from 1958; Nicomedes popularized the lando and gave the forms their stage theory; director and animator of the first Festival de Arte Negro in 1971.
- **Porfirio Vasquez (1902-1971):** mentor. Evidence: decimista and folklorist, vital for Afro-Peruvian music, mentor and direct influence on Nicomedes; Ronaldo names him with the Santa Cruz siblings as a teacher; his festejo "Mi compadre Nicolas" is in the company's repertoire, sung by Caitro.
- **Luis Banchero Rossi (1929-1972):** patron, banker, mecenas from the second half of 1971 until his death on Saturday 1 January 1972. Evidence: the Gladys Palmera stage history, corporate reorganization, new costumes and repertoire, push to self-management.
- **Bertha Ponce (1930-2017):** wife, matriarch, the pusher. Evidence: married Ronaldo, four children; pressed him to found the company and enlisted her mother to push him; Paredes's main oral source, she gave him a room in her house.
- **Rony Campos (b. c. 1964):** son, successor, full name Jaime Ronaldo Campos Ponce. Evidence: member since fourteen, director since August 2001, fulfilling his father's wishes; first United States tour May 2002; "Jolgorio" and "Zamba Malato" and the Grammy nominations under his direction.
- **Susana Baca (b. 1945):** first cousin, singer and researcher. Evidence: niece of Lucila de la Colina Orellana, "prima hermana de Ronaldo Campos y Caitro Soto"; on "The Soul of Black Peru" with Peru Negro.
- **Lalo Izquierdo:** co-founder, second guitar and dancer. Evidence: one of the four who walked out of Victoria's company; Paredes's longest interview, over eight hours.
- **Luis F. Paredes:** historian and archivist, the reason this study is researchable at all. Evidence: sixty-plus interviews from 2006 to 2013, doctoral thesis 2015, 429 pages, book 2021; he wrote scripts for the ballet's most recent works; he became "one more member of the Peru Negro family".
- **Daniela Darcourt:** the next study, #368. Evidence: Eva Ayllon's goddaughter, the chain's third link after Chabuca's two goddaughters, Eva and Susana Baca, carried from #362 and #366.
