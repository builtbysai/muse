# Magali Lara: Study Notes

Study date: 2026-11-05. ig-artist-study #345.

**Site:** Magali Lara, born Margarita Rosa Lara Zavala (Mexico City, 5 November 1956 - ), Mexican painter and drawer; studied visual arts at the Escuela Nacional de Artes Plasticas (ENAP), San Carlos, 1976-1980; master's degree from the Universidad Autonoma del Estado de Morelos (UAEM), 2011, where she teaches painting; member of the Sistema Nacional de Creadores since 1994; member of number of the Academia de Artes de Mexico, 2023 (entered 2020 in the grafica section); Medalla al Merito en Artes 2019 and Medalla Bellas Artes 2024; works in the collections of MoMA, S.M.A.K. Ghent, Archivo Lafuente, MALBA, Museo Reina Sofia, Museo de Arte Carrillo Gil, and Museo de Arte Latinoamericano de Buenos Aires (MOLAA). Her 70th birthday is today, Thursday 5 November 2026.

**Doorway:** From Mayer's written card to Lara, the artist who paired the same confessional drawing with writing, and who stood beside Mayer in the 1978 Salon 77-78: Nuevas tendencias show at the Museo de Arte Moderno that launched El Tendedero. The bridge between the two studies is literal and dated: in March 1978, in the same rooms, Mayer hung about eight hundred pink cards on a clothesline and asked what women most hated about the city, and Lara hung ten framed collages in dark wood frames, her Ventanas, each one a small room that looked inward instead of out. Mayer's mechanism was the question that changes meaning by being answered; Lara's mechanism is the sentence that changes meaning by being written inside the drawing, by hand, in red, where the image cannot get rid of it. Mayer handed the sentence to strangers; Lara keeps it for herself, and that is why her work is a diary and Mayer's is a census. The two artists stayed in the same circle for forty years: the Muestra Colectiva Feminista of September 1978, Mi casa es mi cuerpo in 1983, the Fem cover Lara designed for Mayer's issue in 1984, Mayer's Rosa chillante (2004) describing Lara's 78 small-format drawings, and a CONICET article by Maria Laura Rosa that pairs the two of them on drawing and writing. Where Mayer's line grows sideways because strangers keep pinning cards to it, Lara's line grows in a spiral, returning to the same house, the same bathroom, the same tree, and finding a different sentence there each time.

## M1. The Siamese-twin text/image

Her own formulation, quoted in the English Wikipedia from Abstraction in Action (2016): "My work is found in an intersection. Text and image coexist in different ways. As Siamese twins, that have developed different personalities but that, in the end, share a vital nucleus." The mechanism is literal on the page: the drawing and the handwritten sentence share one sheet and one body, but they do not agree with each other. "El secreto es: como hay una parte que es imagen y otra es texto y que no necesariamente parten del mismo lugar. No hay una concordancia identitaria entre una y otra." The image shows one thing, the text says another, and the gap between them is the work. In Luego lo lavo (1984), viewed first-hand, the painting shows laundry on a line and a toilet in a red side panel, and the sentence "llevo mi destino cosido al cuerpo luego lo lavo" is written large across the bottom band, half in black, half in green, so the sentence is in the painting, not under it. In Intimidad (1984), the labels are arrows pointing at objects: "lo tuyo" and "lo de antes... lo de siempre" at the bottles, "el agua" at the shower head, "lo mio" at the towel, "el aire" at the window. The words do not describe the picture; they divide it by ownership. The drawing is also like handwriting in her account: "Para mi el dibujo y la escritura son semejantes, hermanas gemelas. Es posible que el dibujo contenga un relato de la misma manera que una frase dice en lo no dicho." And: "Elegi dibujar porque era mas cercano a ese cuerpo que no entendia del todo... El dibujo, como la escritura manuscrita, es un lugar entre la imagen y el texto. Y yo necesitaba algo que fuera fragil, que no tuviera la densidad de la pintura porque estaba casi afuera." The fragility is the point: the drawing is chosen because it is close to a body she does not fully understand, and the text is chosen because it can say what the drawing leaves unsaid. The narrator is the body, not the writer: "Queria armar historias, pero no queria ser escritora porque me interesaba que el narrador fuera el cuerpo, el mio, que es un perfecto desconocido." **Takeaway:** the word is a second voice and a second image, and the two do not agree. Watch the gap: when the sentence on the page says something the picture does not, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's pink card (#344 M2) was also one sentence on one surface, but her card is written by a stranger and pinned to a line; Lara's sentence is written by her own hand inside her own drawing, which is why her version is a diary and Mayer's is a census.

## M2. The window as an inward frame

Ventanas (1977-78) is 54 unique collages in dark wood frames, and the frame is the mechanism: a window that looks in, not out. The ISLAA essay calls them "wobbly rectangles" that are "a self-portrait, an X-ray of her body, a photo of a chair, an opaque grey wash". The installation photograph, viewed first-hand, shows 12 small dark-wood-framed collages in a 4x3 grid on a white wall, each one a different small room: blank paper with red stitching; text around a white rectangle; a crosshair with a small figure; a grey wash; photos of a woman and a chair; eyes through blinds; text that reads "se vuelve hacia la cama, alcanza la cobija"; two photos, a woman and a chair; a grid of words ("eran", "entonces", "llegar", "bebieron ron", "el") and a black rectangle; a brown panel with a V cut. The materials are photocopied images, newspaper cuttings, found objects, rubber-stamp text, fingerprints, lipstick stains. Ten of the collages were shown at Salon 77-78 in March 1978, the same show that launched El Tendedero. A window in a house looks out at the street; Lara's window looks in at the room, and the room is the body. The frame does the work of the wall: it says this small rectangle is a place, and the place is private, and the viewer is looking into it without permission. The collage inside is not a view; it is an interior, a self-portrait made of fragments, a chair, a pair of eyes, a sentence. The dark wood frame is also a domestic object, the kind of frame that hangs in a hallway, so the museum wall becomes a hallway of small rooms, and each room is a different mood of the same house. **Takeaway:** the frame is a window that looks in, and the collage is the room. Watch the frame: when a dark wood rectangle holds a chair, a pair of eyes, and a sentence instead of a view, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Herrera's suitcase museum (#343 M3) was also a portable exhibition, but his case carried objects he had collected to show a city; Lara's frame carries a room she has built to show a body, which is why her version is a house and his version is a museum.

## M3. The notebook page as a confessional field

Infancia y eso (1980) is a series of large sheets, Chinese ink, watercolor, and photography on cotton paper, 56.5 x 75.5 cm each, that work like school notebooks opened flat. The sheet viewed first-hand, Soledad no es y luego cambia, is lined notebook paper with six small circular cut-out childhood photos of the same girl arranged around the page, red stars, pale pink lipstick kisses, a brown wash blob with the handwritten title, and phrases in red and black around the edges: "muy bien regular sucio", "no hay tiempo", "19 de marzo", "soy antipoda", "soy...", "asi", "es inteligente", "son algo", with a row of black teardrop marks. The other sheets in the series work the same way: Infancia puts a big red star on the forehead of a childhood portrait, sensual mouths, a profile photo of the young self without a mouth, torn face fragments, and an arrow to her gaze labeled "el narrador", next to teacher-blackboard mandates including "no saber lo que quieren que uno sepa"; Dibujo no. 2 repeats her niece's face in photo and photocopy, sharp and blurred, with pre-verbal graphisms; En medio de este estupido silencio draws a board-game path with her niece's face multiplying between arrows and losing sharpness, empty-frame circles for mama, papa, yo, sealed tongues, open taps spilling ink; Lo que soy draws a family genealogy as a diagram of organic shapes, stains, sensual lips, and words explaining characters, with Frida at one end. The notebook is the mechanism because the notebook is where a child is judged: lined paper, red stars, grades, the teacher's mandates, the diary that must be kept. Lara treats the page as a field where the evidence of a life is laid out, and the error is part of the evidence: the misspelling, the crossed-out word, the blurred photocopy, the torn fragment are all kept, because they are the proof that something happened. "Son sintomas de que estaban pasando otras cosas... El cuaderno tiene que ser respetado. La falla es corregida", she said in 2019. The diary is also the format of the artist's books that follow: the 1980 calendar-day planner, a book of clippings, letters, notes, drawings, and photographs ("Soy feliz como una lombriz"), and the whole later practice of notebooks, loose sheets, and leporellos. **Takeaway:** the lined page is a courtroom and the error is the testimony. Watch the page: when a school notebook holds childhood photos, red stars, lipstick kisses, and a sentence that does not add up, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's Diario de las violencias cotidianas (#344, carried as 1983 per ISLAA with a 1984 variant) was also a diary sheet, but hers recorded violence done to women by the city; Lara's notebook records violence done to a child by the school, which is why her version is a report card and Mayer's is a police report.

## M4. The misspelling as mechanism

Sielo is a series of collages from 1978 whose title is a misspelling of "cielo", sky, and the misspelling is the work. Lara has dyslexia, which she names in her own essay: "Hasta la fecha padezco de una dislexia que monta un desorden tremendo a la hora de redactar." The misspellings that got her published in a visual-poetry magazine in the 1970s became a method: deliberate spelling faults, "fallas deliberadas hasta de ortografia", including "Sielo y deseo", where the mouth is a vagina, "la herida primordial". Ledesma's Nexos essay states the theory: the evident misspelling "speaks from the lack of orthography", and "la falta enuncia", the lack speaks, after Deleuze and Guattari's minor literature. Lara herself: "Soy una lectora obsesiva de las historias de infancia, en las que me interesa la relacion entre lo que se dice desde el error, la falta de ortografia, los codigos inventados que hablan de una identidad siempre cambiante" (2014). The error continues through her whole practice as a crack in ceramic or a blur in a photograph: the Madre ceramic of 2002, viewed first-hand, is a white plate with thin red crack-like lines and the handwritten word "madre" at center, the crack doing the work of the writing; the photocopies in Infancia y eso blur and tear; the diagrams in De lo amoroso label a kiss with a misspelled or half-finished word. The misspelling is not a mistake to be corrected; it is a mechanism that reveals a darker structure underneath the correct word, the way a crack reveals the inside of a plate. The glossary she wrote for the MUAC catalog is full of these slips turned into definitions: "Sielo y deseo" is an entry, not a correction. **Takeaway:** the wrong letter is the right word, and the crack is the sentence. Watch the spelling: when a word is spelled wrong and the wrongness is the point, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Herrera's typewritten card (#343 M14) was also a sentence that did the work, but his card was typed correctly and its power was the axiom; Lara's word is spelled wrong and its power is the fault, which is why her version is a crack and his version is a rule.

## M5. The animated domestic object

The objects in Lara's house speak, and they speak in the first person. The shower head is the clearest case: in Lealtad (1980-81), the shower head on page 7 spits water onto disembodied lips that mutate into bloody undergarments, next to Boullosa's poem "Moraleja: ahorquese usted con sus calzones en la regadera / pero nunca piense en el acto: / eso le permitiria sobrevivir". The shower heads have had animated personalities since 1979, with speech balloons that say "No llores por mi" and "Tengo ganas de un hijo". The mechanism is the object given a voice and a desire: the shower head wants a child, the toilet knows a secret, the baby bottles in Un lapso (1981), viewed first-hand, stand like small penises with nipples, and the handwritten words "un lapso" and "no te olvido" hang over them, with a real string threaded through two red circles at the bottom and hanging below the frame, so the drawing physically leaves the frame. The bathtub in Intimidad (1984) is labeled by ownership, "lo tuyo", "lo de antes... lo de siempre", "lo mio", as if the bottles and the towel were witnesses in a dispute. Medina's formulation is the theory: "Creo que los objetos cotidianos estan impregnados del cuerpo de sus duenos... circunstancias detenidas que regulan nuestros movimientos afectivos." The objects reproduce a stopped circumstance, and the stopped circumstance is emotional: the bed, the bathroom, the kitchen, the clothesline are all stages where a small drama has already happened and is still happening. The bed is the favorite stage: "Si los objetos reproducen una circunstancia emocional, los banos y la cama son mis favoritos... La cama es la madre o donde suceden las otras vidas como la del sueno, la enfermedad y la muerte." The bathroom is the confessional: "El bano es muy revelador sobre la relacion que tenemos con nuestro cuerpo... Es un espacio existencial", with "confesion, perdon y purificacion". The object does not illustrate a feeling; it is the feeling, stuck in place, waiting for someone to read it. **Takeaway:** the house is full of witnesses, and the witnesses talk. Watch the object: when a shower head, a toilet, or a baby bottle has a sentence of its own, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's domestic object as stage (#344 M16) was also a familiar thing turned political, but her apron becomes a placard worn in public; Lara's shower head stays in the bathroom and speaks to the bather alone, which is why her version is a confession and Mayer's is a protest.

## M6. The lipstick kiss as stamp

The lipstick kiss is a print, and the print is a signature. In De lo amoroso (1982), viewed first-hand, three framed sheets treat the kiss as a scientific process: the left sheet diagrams "proceso creador de un beso" (the creative process of a kiss) with a spiky seed-pod outline, red lipstick smears inside, a red lipstick-kiss label at top, and notes on a "zona de calor"; the center sheet shows two vertical ladder-like tracks with two red lipstick kisses and the word "LUNA"; the right sheet shows red concentric spiral rings with a grey lipstick-kiss at center and another below. The kiss is stamped, not drawn: the lip is pressed to the paper the way a rubber stamp is pressed, and the print is the index of a contact that really happened. The same stamp recurs across the whole practice: the red lipstick-kiss labels on the 1979 Principio/fin sequence, viewed first-hand, where a column of kisses runs down the center panel next to an ink razor; the pale pink kisses on the Soledad notebook page of 1980; the lip-shaped hearts and heart-shaped lips spilling across the asymmetrical grid of Lealtad; the three small lip drawings in a vertical strip on page 10 of Lealtad alterado (2021). The kiss is also a unit of mutation: in Lealtad the lips become hearts, the hearts become undergarments, the undergarments become blood. The mechanism is the body leaving a mark that is also a word: a kiss is a signature, a seal, a stamp, and a wound, all at once, and because it is stamped it can be repeated, counted, arranged in a grid, turned into a diagram. The diagram is the joke and the seriousness together: the kiss, the most intimate act, is treated like a laboratory process with zones, tracks, and spirals, and the laboratory treatment is what makes the intimacy visible. **Takeaway:** the lip is a stamp and the kiss is a print, and the print is the evidence of a contact. Watch the kiss: when a lipstick mark is stamped in a row like a rubber stamp, and labeled like a diagram, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's pink card (#344 M2) was also a repeated unit, but her unit is a sentence written by a stranger; Lara's unit is a body pressed to paper, which is why her version is a contact and Mayer's is a testimony.

## M7. The clothesline as a destiny sentence

Luego lo lavo (1984), viewed first-hand, is the clothesline as a sentence. An orange-yellow wall, a clothesline with a pink shirt, a white undergarment dripping blue drops into a red mug, blue stockings; handwritten words scattered: "lo mio" (top right), "lo otro" (middle left), "la humedad" (right of the mug); a right panel with a red wall, a shelf with jars, a toilet with a paper roll; and across the bottom band, a large handwritten sentence on ruled lines: "llevo mi destino cosido al cuerpo luego lo lavo", with "llevo mi destino cosido al cuerpo" in black and "luego lo lavo" in green. The sentence is in the painting, not under it, and the painting is the sentence: destiny is sewn to the body, and then it is washed. The washing is the liberation: the undergarment drips into the mug, the water carries the destiny away, the line holds the clothes up to dry. The clothesline is also a line of text: the garments hang on it like words on a line, and the line is the sentence that holds them. The mechanism is the domestic chore turned into a spell: washing is what you do every day, and doing it every day is what lets you go on living, and the painting says so in a sentence that is itself a line of washing. The doorway echo with Mayer is exact and opposite: Mayer's Tendedero of 1978 hangs pink cards on a line in a museum and asks strangers to write their confessions; Lara's line hangs laundry on a wall in a house and writes the confession herself, in her own hand, across the bottom of the painting. Mayer's line is public, collective, and growing; Lara's line is private, singular, and finished, and the finishing is the washing. **Takeaway:** the line holds the laundry and the laundry holds the destiny, and the washing lets it go. Watch the line: when garments hang on a line and a sentence runs underneath them like a second line, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's clothesline (#344 M1) was also a line with things pinned to it, but her line asks strangers a question and collects their answers; Lara's line states her own sentence and washes it, which is why her version is a destiny and Mayer's is a confession.

## M8. The leporello book that unfolds

La enemiga (1986) and La gigante (1987) are leporellos, accordion books from El Archivero, and the fold is the mechanism. La enemiga, ink and watercolor, 15.5 x 16 cm, is a folded strip whose watercolour flora bleeds beyond the black outline across the pages; La gigante, serigraph and ink, 12.5 x 12.5 cm, tells of a giantess who must bend to enter doors and shrink to fit under roofs, and the perspective shifts from third person to first person as the book unfolds. The book is a body that must bend and shrink, and the reader's hands do the bending: to read it you must unfold it, and unfolding it is the story. The mechanism is the page turn as a bodily act: the book does not sit open like a codex, it stretches out like a body, and the stretching is the narrative. The same logic runs through the whole book practice: Lealtad (1980-81) is a hand-printed book whose pages are a grid that the lips spill across; the 1980 calendar-day planner is a book of clippings and notes that must be turned day by day; Esto es... (1996) and El principio (2012) are loose sheets and folded books; Lealtad alterado (2021) revisits the 1981 book by clipping, pasting, and rewriting it, so the old book is unfolded and refolded forty years later. The artist's book is also a conversation: "Es con un nosotros como escribo", she writes, and the books are made with Boullosa, with Grobet, with Morales and Rosell, with Torres Maldonado, so the book is a body shared between two authors. The leporello is the smallest version of that sharing: a strip of paper that only becomes a book when two hands pull it open. **Takeaway:** the book is a body that must be unfolded, and the unfolding is the story. Watch the fold: when a strip of paper stretches out and the figure on it bends to fit through the door, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Herrera's suitcase museum (#343 M3) was also a portable object that opens to become an exhibition, but his case opens to show a collection; Lara's leporello opens to show a body, which is why her version is a giantess and his version is a museum.

## M9. Water as channel

Water runs through the whole work as a channel, not as a subject. Borowitz's dissertation names the figure: "vasos comunicantes", communicating vessels, the system of connected containers where the level in one is the level in all. The water appears as showers, taps, sinks, drains, vases, and spilled glasses, and it always moves: the shower head in Lealtad spits water onto lips; the white undergarment in Luego lo lavo drips blue drops into a red mug; the open taps in En medio de este estupido silencio spill ink across the board-game path; El origen (1981) paints a round sink on a tiled floor with a curvilinear pipe and a small arrow labeled "el origen", the source; Se escoge el tiempo (1983), made with Lourdes Grobet, intervenes Grobet's bathroom photographs with text and ornaments, and the exchange is written on the work itself: "El agua no me basta", wrote Lara, "para beberme la vida", added Grobet. Agua (1979) takes Kahlo's bathtub painting Lo que el agua me dio (1938) and extends the bathwater with wavy pastel lines into a diagram of life stages, infancy to old age crossed out. The mechanism is the receptacle and the channel together: the work is a vessel that holds water, and a pipe that lets it flow, and the flow is the feeling. "El agua no me basta para beberme la vida" is the title of the 2021 Laguna show, and the sentence is the theory: water is not enough to drink a life, but it is the figure for how a life moves from one vessel to another, from mother to daughter, from body to page, from shower to drain. The bathroom is the place where this happens because the bathroom is where the body meets water every day, and the meeting is private, repetitive, and confessional. **Takeaway:** the work is a vessel and a pipe, and the water is the feeling moving through it. Watch the drip: when water leaves a tap, a shower, or a garment and falls into a mug, a sink, or a drain, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Aquino's posters (#342 M4) were also distributed through channels, but his channel was the street and the message was printed; Lara's channel is the pipe and the message is the water, which is why her version flows and his version sticks.

## M10. The pruned tree as self

The tree is Lara's self-portrait, and it is always pruned. Melancolia (1989), viewed first-hand, is a vertical gouache of a pruned tree trunk, pinkish-brown, with thin brown branches and small bright green leaves, against a huge dark navy/indigo wash, with the handwritten word "melancolia" lower right and the signature "89". La Tormenta (1989), viewed first-hand, is the same tree bending left under a blue storm wash, branches arcing up-right with green leaves, a red shape below with a smaller shoot, and the green handwritten words "solo el viento", only the wind. Y entonces escuche el fuego (1990), viewed first-hand, is three red pruned trees against a swirling blue/indigo sky, with a huge orange-red-yellow flame exhaling horizontally from the central trunk, paint drips running down, green hills at bottom, a small sprig floating mid-left: the fire is a mouth-breath from the tree. Her glossary states the identification plainly: "Mi identificacion con un arbol podado." The mechanism is the body translated into a plant: the trunk is the body, the cut branches are the losses, the small green leaves are the sprouts that come back anyway. The vegetal turn has a dated cause in her own account: "Pensar en terminos de la naturaleza me resultaba del todo indiferente hasta que senti la muerte... La maternidad, o la fragilidad en la maternidad, me llevo al consuelo del mundo vegetal." The death is Elso's, in November 1988, and the fragility is motherhood, and the tree is what grows after both. The series that follow are all vegetal: Herbolario in the early 1990s, Ramificaciones (1995-96), the tree of intuition (1993), the medieval tree (1992), the column of vegetation (1995). The tree also carries the landscape claim that runs through the late work: "Ser paisaje, y ya", to be landscape, and that is all, and the identification with a pruned tree is the identification with a landscape that has been cut back and keeps growing. **Takeaway:** the tree is the body after the cut, and the sprout is the proof that it keeps living. Watch the trunk: when a pruned tree stands against a dark wash and breathes fire from its trunk, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Bustamante's masks (#341 M2) were also a body translated into an object, but her mask hides the face and is worn by someone else; Lara's tree shows the cut and grows it back herself, which is why her version is a self-portrait and Bustamante's is a disguise.

## M11. Still life as portrait

Naturaleza muerta (1986), viewed first-hand in two independent crops, is a portrait with the person removed. A bright yellow ground; a red vase with roses, drooping, dark red, on a red rumpled bed or ground; a snake coils around the vase base; two high-heeled shoes, one upright, one lying; top right, a woman's bare legs and feet dangle from a red skirt, cut off at mid-thigh; two striped curtain tassels hang beside; the signature "86" at bottom right. The body is evacuated and replaced by its objects: the vase, the snake, the shoes, the legs, the bed. Borowitz's dissertation names the tactic: the blurring of still life and portraiture, and the evacuation of specific bodies, so that the objects have to carry the person. The mechanism is the substitution: the still life is a family genealogy, because the objects in a house belonged to someone, and the someone is gone, and the objects are what is left. Medina's formulation is the theory: "Creo que los objetos cotidianos estan impregnados del cuerpo de sus duenos", and the still life is "a family genealogy", because her mother and grandmother painted still lifes. The legs dangling from the top of the frame are the joke and the proof together: the body is present only as a fragment, cut off at mid-thigh, and the fragment is enough to make the whole painting a portrait. The same substitution runs through the whole practice: the baby bottles in Un lapso stand in for the child; the toilet in Escusado stands in for the body that used it; the empty chair in Guarde mi infancia stands in for the grandmother who peeled a mandarin at its foot; the bathtub in Intimidad stands in for the bather. The portrait is never painted directly, because the direct portrait would be a face, and the face is public space; the body is the place that happens from inside, and the objects are what it leaves behind. **Takeaway:** the objects are the person, and the person is gone. Watch the vase: when a still life holds a snake, a pair of shoes, and a pair of legs cut off at the thigh, and no face at all, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Herrera's comb (#343 M4) was also an object that stood in for a person, but his comb's second edge was a weapon; Lara's vase stands in for a woman who is absent, which is why her version is a portrait and his version is a knife.

## M12. Tapestry as transferred drawing

The drawing leaves the paper and becomes wool. The Madre tapestry of 2014, viewed first-hand, is a single continuous thick black line looping into an organic knot on a natural wool ground: bulbous forms, loops, a head-like oval at top. The line is a drawing, but it is woven, so the drawing has a body you can touch, and the body is warm. Cae (1999) goes further: an embroidery on a flatwoven wool tapestry dyed with mineral anilines and handwoven, laid over a mattress with vinyl texts, 300 x 400 cm, the tapestry preserving the imprint of Elso's body on the futon, ringed with lines from Silvio Rodriguez's "Causas y azares" (1986). The mechanism is the transfer: a line drawn on paper is translated into thread, and the translation changes what the line is, because thread has weight, warmth, and a back side. The transfer is done at the Taller Mexicano de Gobelinos, and the workshop matters: the drawing is handed to weavers, and the weavers' hands remake it, so the line is no longer only hers. The same transfer runs through the Leer series tapestry Leo y no entiendo (2008), handwoven wool, 90 x 128 cm, and the Madre tapestry of 2014, 100 percent wool, 226 x 172 cm, and the Ojos tapestry of 1999, 250 x 330 cm. The tapestry is also a body you can lie on: the futon in Cae is a bed, and the bed is the place where the other lives happen, sleep, illness, death, and the tapestry laid over it is a drawing that has become a bed. The line that was fragile on paper, "algo que fuera fragil, que no tuviera la densidad de la pintura", becomes durable in wool, and the durability is the mourning: the body is gone, the imprint stays, and the wool holds it. **Takeaway:** the line becomes wool, and the wool becomes a body you can lie on. Watch the thread: when a single black line loops into a knot on a wool ground, and the knot has a head, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's archive (#344 M5) was also a fragile thing made durable, but her durability was filing, boxes, and DVDs; Lara's durability is weaving, which is why her version is a blanket and Mayer's is a box.

## M13. The black ball of the future

Futuro (2013-19) is a black ball, off-center, on a large canvas, and the ball is the mechanism. The origin story is told in her own words in the glossary: in 2013, on her sister's terrace in the south of France at night, she saw a black hole, a darkness, and understood that "no sabemos nada del futuro", we know nothing about the future. The paintings follow: Futuro, 2013, oil on canvas, 180 x 250 cm; Futuro 1, 2013, 250 x 180 cm; Futuro 3, 4, and 5, 2013; Hoy, 2014, oil on linen, 180 x 250 cm, viewed first-hand, a large abstract oil of white and cream cloudy masses, drips, a dark gold and green half-dome, deep blue sweeping strokes, a black bird or branch-like form, two tall black vertical strokes, ochre and gold patches, no text visible; La Luz, 2014, a 20-page artist's book. The black ball is linked in the interviews to the planet in Lars von Trier's Melancolia, the film, a dark sphere approaching the earth, and the link is the theory: the future is a mass that approaches and cannot be known, and the painting holds it still long enough to look at. The mechanism is scale: the ball is small against the canvas, and the canvas is large, and the smallness is the point, because the future is not monumental, it is tiny, and the tiny is what structures everything. "No es para nada un trabajo abstracto, me interesa lo nimio de la existencia, el poder de lo microscopico que nos da estructura." The micro and the macro oscillate in the series: corpuscles, chains, organelles, dendrites, Brownian motion, the same forms at the scale of a cell and the scale of a planet. The ball never moves in the painting, but it drifts off-center, and the off-center is the argument: the future does not sit in the middle of the picture, it sits to one side, approaching, and the picture is the waiting. **Takeaway:** the future is a small black ball, off-center, and the painting is the waiting. Watch the ball: when a dark sphere sits off-center in a large field of cloud and drip, and nothing explains it, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Herrera's prize (#343 M1) was also a small thing that did the work of a large thing, but his prize was a sentence that invented an award; Lara's ball is a shape that refuses to be explained, which is why her version is a waiting and his version is a fiction.

## M14. Grey as a female perspective

Robar lo que me pertenece (Steal What Belongs to Me), Galeria RGR, 2025, her first solo show at the gallery, is a show about grey. The installation, viewed first-hand, is a grey concrete gallery with three large abstract paintings on the left wall, blue-grey, yellow-red, and blue-white washes, and five small framed works on the white right wall. Rangel's text states the mechanism: Lara "ha confrontado el gris en una busqueda localizada dentro de los confines de la poesia visual", and the show gathers four decades of series "que exploran las tonalidades politicas y poeticas de la grisalla desde la experiencia femenina". The grey is not the absence of color; it is a color with a politics, the color of the Miedo series grey grounds, of the opaque grey wash in Ventanas, of the charcoal on the wall, of the concrete of the gallery itself. "Su gran habilidad para pintar en gris va de la mano de la mutabilidad retiniana, atmosferica y metaforica del color que mejor enuncia la subjetividad del presente." The grey changes with the eye, with the atmosphere, with the metaphor, and the changing is the mechanism: grey is the color that refuses to stay still, so it is the color of a subjectivity that refuses to be fixed. The Miedo nucleus in the show is four paintings, vases, pots, and plants on grey grounds, made before her widowhood, and the grey there is the ground of fear: not black, not white, but the color in between, where the object is visible and the feeling is not. The title is the theory too: to steal what belongs to me is to take back the grey, the domestic, the small, the overlooked, and to hang it on the wall as a painting. **Takeaway:** grey is not the absence of color, it is the color that changes with the eye, and the changing is the subject. Watch the grey: when a wash of grey holds a vase, a pot, or a plant, and the grey seems to move as you look, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's pink (#344 M2) was also a color with a politics, but her pink is loud, hot, and public, the color of the women-only metro car; Lara's grey is quiet, shifting, and private, which is why her version is a whisper and Mayer's is a shout.

## M15. The wall as the next page

From early 2020, the drawing left the paper and went onto the wall. Tropico fantasmatico: borramientos (2023), at La Tallera in Cuernavaca, curated by Willy Kautz, was the largest mural of her career, an ephemeral charcoal on paper answering Siqueiros's La America tropical oprimida y destrozada por los imperialismos (1932, Los Angeles, restored 2012). The mural is charcoal on paper on the wall, and it changes: stains become trees, volcanoes, earth, eyes; chthonic deities watch; branches extend onto the floor; Pizarnik's Arbol de Diana speaks from speech balloons; the mural changes with humidity, and the sun bleaches parts between windows, revealing an involuntary drawing from previous wall repairs. The wall murals made for the MUAC show, La piel son nubarrones negros (Skin of Black Storm Clouds, title from Jon Fosse) and Estiro los dedos (I Stretch My Fingers), both 2025, charcoal on wall, variable dimensions, work the same way: the drawing is made on the wall, and the wall is the page, and the page is the room. The mechanism is the scale jump: the notebook page becomes the wall, and the wall becomes the landscape, and the landscape watches you back, "la naturaleza te mira", nature watches you. The drawing that was fragile on paper, close to a body she did not fully understand, becomes a room you can stand inside, and standing inside it is the reading. The summer 2021 show at the Seminario de Cultura Mexicana, "Toda historia de amor es una historia de fantasmas", made charcoal murals with Luis Hidalgo and Minerva Ayon, and the same logic ran there: the wall is the next page, and the page is the size of a room, and the room is the body. The mural is ephemeral by design: it will be painted over, bleached by the sun, changed by humidity, and the ephemerality is the point, because the wall, like the body, changes, and the drawing changes with it. "La civilizacion, como la entendemos, es destruccion sin memoria." **Takeaway:** the wall is the next page, and the page is the size of a room, and the room watches you back. Watch the wall: when charcoal on a wall becomes trees, volcanoes, and eyes, and the sun bleaches a part of it into a drawing nobody made, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Mayer's hallway laundry (#344 M12) was also a wall turned into a work, but her wall held a clothesline that visitors kept filling with cards; Lara's wall holds a charcoal drawing that the sun keeps erasing, which is why her version is a weather and Mayer's is a census.

## M16. Cut-and-paste writing

Her writing method is stated in her own essay: "Copio, corto y pego, subrayo y anoto en mis cuadernos." Copy, cut, paste, underline, annotate in the notebooks. The mechanism is appropriation as authorship: she copies from other writers, cuts the copy, pastes it into her own page, underlines it, and the pasted fragment becomes hers by being placed. The sources she names are the method: Brazilian concrete poetry (Noigandres, 1952), Oulipo and "los plagiarios por anticipacion", Queneau's Cent mille milliards de poemes, Mario Bellatin's scissors-marked paragraphs, Kathy Acker, Deleuze and Guattari's Kafka: por una literatura menor and the flight from the Oedipal room. The glossary she wrote for the MUAC catalog is the method turned into a form: she defines her own words, Abstracto, Accidente, Alzheimer, El animal, El centro, Coraza, La escatologia de los sentimientos, Las flores y el sexo, Futuro, Historias de casa, Hurtar en ti lo que me pertenece, Infancia y eso, Intemperie, Los ojos no, El paisaje, Pausa separacion, La piel despues de los cuarenta, Rojo, negro y blanco, El rostro y el cuerpo, Sielo y deseo, Toda historia de amor es una historia de fantasmas, each entry a definition that is also a memory, a wound, a joke. Lealtad alterado (2021), viewed first-hand, is the method as a book: three pages side by side, the 1981 book revisited by clipping, pasting, and rewriting, a heart mutating into a black blob across four boxes, a hanging heart with the handwritten text "tenia piernas... no me sorprendia...", a photo clipping of a body fragment with the text "Era mi cuerpo contra... mi cuerpo". The artist's book is a conversation with other authors, and the conversation is the work: "Es con un nosotros como escribo", it is with a we that I write. The book is also a body that must be cut to be read: Bellatin's scissors-marked paragraphs, the leporello that must be unfolded, the collage that is made by cutting, the tapestry that is made by cutting thread. **Takeaway:** the copy is the writing, and the cut is the signature. Watch the page: when a pasted clipping, a copied line, and a handwritten note sit together on one sheet, and the sheet is the work, the mechanism is on. Against the chain: Aquino's posters (#342 M4) were also made by cutting and pasting, but his paste was a poster for the street; Lara's paste is a book for the hands, which is why her version is a conversation and his version is a broadcast.

## Aesthetic logic

Lara's aesthetic is the fragile line that holds a heavy sentence, in the cheapest possible materials, aimed at the most private possible target, read by one person at a time. The materials are Chinese ink, watercolor, photocopies, lipstick, rubber stamps, lined notebook paper, charcoal, wool, ceramic: the materials of the school, the bathroom, the kitchen, and the sewing basket, and the cheapness is the argument, because a work made of a notebook page cannot be owned by a museum and cannot be stopped by a wall. The palette is black ink, red lipstick, and white paper first, then the yellow of the house paintings, the indigo of the storm washes, the green of the sprouts, the grey of the grisaille, the blue-grey of the glaciers, and the black of the future ball: red, black, and white are the first colors, Snow White's colors, and a series in those colors appears almost every year. The composition is the spiral: the work returns to the same house, the same bathroom, the same tree, the same sentence, and each return is different, because the spiral is an unfinished line in flight, a line that returns to its origin without repeating it. The white of the canvas is part of the composition, the pauses between brushstrokes, the silences between words and images, the tiny voids that hold the work together: "todo parece que esta por caerse... pero a la vez se sostiene", everything seems about to fall, but at the same time it holds. The scale is conceptual and affective and political: "De que tamano es lo monumental? Donde empieza lo nimio?" The monumental is a charcoal mural on a wall, and the tiny is a lipstick kiss on a notebook page, and the work moves between them, micro and macro, corpuscle and planet, crack and glacier. The motion is the unfolding, the washing, the dripping, the sprouting, the drifting of the black ball: the work moves slowly, the way a plant grows, the way water drips, the way a sentence is washed. The logic is the diary: the work is made day by day, page by page, and the pages accumulate into a life, and the life is the work. The viewer is a detective completing the enigma: the image shows one thing, the text says another, and the reader must put them together, and the putting together is the reading.

## Study-method note

This study was built from the research leg in ~/workspace/goals/fly-generative-lane/hidden_files/study345/research-notes.md (21 sources read end to end, partial reads including the Borowitz dissertation chapters 1-4, 123 works cataloged, 22 image files viewed first-hand, 21 distinct Lara works, 32 load-bearing corrections). Images were downloaded sequentially in one foreground loop and viewed first-hand as images, not described from captions; 7 initial ArtNexus files were HTML error pages from wrong timestamp guesses, deleted, and re-downloaded with exact URLs copied from the re-opened ArtNexus page. No misdated files were found in this batch, unlike #344's Wikimedia case: signature years visible in the images confirm catalog dates (Irse "85", Melancolia "89", La Tormenta "89", Naturaleza muerta "86", Intimidad "84", Un lapso "81", Escusado "84"), and Naturaleza muerta appears in two independent sources with the same date. EXIF dates were not extracted; dates rest on source captions plus internal signature evidence. Counts that are different denominators are carried separately throughout and never merged: the Ventanas count (54 unique collages in the series versus 10 framed collages shown at the salon), the Historias de casa span (1982-83 per the MUAC checklist, 1984 per ISLAA, 1984-86 per Artishock, 1977-83 per Medina's essay header), the Sistema Nacional de Creadores years, and the Tendedero-adjacent "first feminist exhibition" claims (Tyner's Collage intimo 1977 versus Rosa's Muestra Colectiva Feminista 1978). The brief's suggested MUAC show "El agua nunca fue tan clara" (2023-2024) was investigated and is not real: the phrase is a lyric from Christina Rosenvinge's "Cancion del eco", and the real MUAC retrospective is "Cinco decadas en espiral", 5 April to 19 October 2025. The nearest real water-titled show is "El agua no me basta para beberme la vida" (Laguna, November 2021). Single-sourced claims are flagged where they appear: the No-Grupo participation (MUNAE only), the mother's suicide in Mayer's biography (Tyner, carried from #344), and the "200 million viewers" figure for Madre por un dia (not repeated, carried from #344).
