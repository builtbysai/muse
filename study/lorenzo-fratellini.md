# Lorenzo Fratellini: Study Notes

**Site:** Lorenzo Maria Fratellini (Saturday 14 January 1690 - Wednesday 12 October 1729), Florentine painter in oil and especially pastel -- son and pupil of the pastellist Giovanna Fratellini (#509), also trained under Anton Domenico Gabbiani; worked at the Medici court in Florence; died aged 39, two years before his mother, and was buried in the church of Ognissanti, near whose tomb his mother was buried in 1731. The record is thin: Neil Jeffares's Dictionary of pastellists entry (J.3262, updated 5 March 2024) is one page long and names three works -- the pastels of Tommaso Micheli (Uffizi inv. 1890 no. 2586) and Giuseppe Vanni, c.1720 (Uffizi inv. 1890 no. 2589), both pastel on paper 56 x 42, and the portrait of Giovanni Lorenzo Stecchi, known through an engraving by Vincenzo Franceschini. Everything else about him comes through three early witnesses: Francesco Maria Niccolo Gabburri's Vite di pittori (manuscript, c.1730-1742), who praised his "suo buon gusto, suo spirito e suo talento" and singled out his little pastel stories crowded with tiny figures; Francesco Moucke's Serie di ritratti, vol. IV (1762), who records that his mother first entrusted him to Gabbiani and then taught him pastel and miniature herself, and that once placed in the Medici court he made portraits of courtiers and guests; and the English Wikipedia stub, whose sole reference is Hobbes's Picture Collector's Manual (1849) and which alone asserts that he specialized as a still-life and vedute painter. No monograph, no exhibition, no catalogue raisonne, no surviving self-portrait, no documented pupil, no recorded Accademia membership, no prices for his work. He survives visually inside his mother's work: her Self-Portrait of 1720 (pastel, 72 x 57 cm, Uffizi inv. 1890 no. 2064) shows her at work painting a miniature of him, idealized, while she paints herself plain. ## M1. The Crowded Little Pastel Story -- Storiette Copiose di Figurine

His one distinctive subject is the small pastel crowded with tiny figures, and it survives only as a sentence. Gabburri, quoted by Jeffares: "Despite his early death, there were in many Florentine houses not only his portraits but also 'di storiette a pastelli copiose di figurine preziosissime, nel che fare ebbe un talento particolare'" -- little pastel stories crowded with most precious tiny figures, in the making of which he had a particular talent. No individual storietta is named, dated, located or reproduced in any source read; the mechanism is attested by a period witness, not by a surviving example, and the study says so. What the sentence gives is a scale and a density: a small pastel sheet, many figures, each figure small and precious, the crowd itself the subject. It is the opposite of his mother's court-lady format -- one face, one oval, repeated -- and the opposite of a history picture -- few figures, large. The watchable behaviour is the crowd arriving one figure at a time on a small field: the sheet fills, the tiny figures cluster and jostle, the story reads at two scales at once -- the whole little scene, and each tiny figure inside it. **Takeaway:** the storiette -- a whole narrative crowded into a small pastel at miniature scale, his particular talent, known only from Gabburri's sentence because no example survives with a title.

## M2. Oil First, Pastel on Top -- The Medium Switch

He worked in oil and especially pastel, and the order matters. Jeffares: "He worked in oil and especially pastel, where he demonstrated 'suo buon gusto, suo spirito e suo talento.'" The training split explains the order: Gabbiani first, for oil and drawing; his mother second, for pastel and miniature (Moucke). So the hand was trained to build in oil -- broad masses, structure, the large ambition -- and then taught to finish in pastel and miniature -- dry grain, chalky highlight, small tolerance. His mother stages exactly this paradox in her Self-Portrait of 1720: she shows herself holding oil paints and working on a miniature, while the picture itself is pastel on paper. The son's practice is the same switch in the other direction: the oil painter's structure executed in the pastellist's medium. No oil painting of his is named in any source read, and no pastel technique detail (binder, ground, fixative, working order) is documented; the study claims neither. The watchable behaviour is the switch itself: a scene first lays down in broad flat oil-like masses, then resolves into dry pastel grain and chalky highlights -- the same image existing in two mediums at once, and the viewer watching it change hands. **Takeaway:** oil training, pastel mastery -- the large ambition built in oil, finished in pastel at miniature scale, and the storiette is the payoff of the combination.

## M3. The Miniature Inside the Portrait -- Scale Collapse

A small portrait held inside a life-size portrait, and the small one carries the identity. Giovanna's Self-Portrait of 1720 (pastel, 72 x 57 cm, Uffizi inv. 1890 no. 2064) shows her at work painting a miniature portrait of her son Lorenzo -- idealized, in Davis's words "very idealized and typical of the Rococo taste for courtly elegance" -- while she paints herself plain, "unflinchingly naturalistic". The miniature is a portrait of a living adult son: Lorenzo was 30 in 1720, and died nine years later. The mechanism is scale collapse: the big face works and moves, the small face holds still and idealized, and attention collapses from the large face to the small face and back. It is his mother's mechanism, not his -- the miniature in the picture is her work, not his -- but it is the only portrait image of Lorenzo that exists, and it fixes how he is seen: not as himself, but as his mother's idealized miniature. The watchable behaviour is the tiny portrait sitting inside the main image, holding still and idealized while the large figure moves and works around it. **Takeaway:** the miniature inside the portrait -- the son present in his mother's signature work only as a tiny idealized face, the large figure working around the small one.

## M4. The Repeated Small Court Portrait -- One Format, Changing Sitter

Both fully documented Lorenzo pastels share one size, and the sameness is the system. The Micheli portrait (Jeffares J.3262.101) and the Vanni portrait (J.3262.105) are both pastel on paper, 56 x 42, both in the Uffizi portrait collection (inv. 1890 nos. 2586 and 2589). Two data points only -- the study does not overclaim a standard from them -- but the observation holds: a fixed small cabinet format, the sitter changing inside the fixed slot. And the sitters are the point: a court jester (Tommaso Micheli, dwarf and buffoon at the court of Violante Beatrice) and a goldsmith (Giuseppe Vanni, c.1647-1716), with a philosophy reader and poet (Giovanni Lorenzo Stecchi) as the third. Not princes. Working notables of the Medici court -- the entertainer, the craftsman, the scholar -- each one settling into the same dimensions, the same paper, the same court light. The watchable behaviour is the fixed small frame staying put while sitters cycle through it: jester, goldsmith, scholar, each one arriving in the same slot and leaving the format unchanged. **Takeaway:** pastel on paper, 56 x 42, repeated -- the court portrait as a fixed small slot, and the sitters who fill it are the court's working people, not its princes.

## M5. The Jester and the Goldsmith -- Sitters Below the Rank

His mother's sitters were marchesas and countesses; his were the people who worked for them. Tommaso Micheli was the dwarf and buffoon -- "nano, buffone" -- at the court of Violante Beatrice, the same patroness who commissioned his mother's twenty court-lady portraits. Giuseppe Vanni was a goldsmith, an orefice, a maker in metal. Giovanni Lorenzo Stecchi was a reader in philosophy at the University of Pisa and a poet, whose portrait survives only because Vincenzo Franceschini engraved it. Moucke says that once placed in the Medici court, Lorenzo "executed portraits of courtiers and guests" -- the court as a workplace, not a throne room. No prices for his work were found; no commission from Violante to Lorenzo personally is documented, and his mother's commissions must not be transferred to him. The watchable behaviour is the rank inversion: the portrait format his mother used for noblewomen, used here for the jester who entertained them and the goldsmith who made their jewels -- the same small pastel, the same care, a different rung of the court. **Takeaway:** the jester, the goldsmith, the philosophy reader -- the court portrait turned downward, to the people who worked at court rather than the people who ruled it.

## M6. The Reported Second Lane -- Still Life and Vedute, Unverified

The one claim about him that would make him different from his mother is the one the record cannot support. The English Wikipedia stub: "he specialized as a still life and vedute painter." The French Wikipedia repeats it; Prabook repeats it. No source found in any leg names, dates, locates or describes a single still life or veduta by him. Jeffares, the pastel specialist, records only portraits and pastel storiette and names no still life or veduta. The stub's sole cited source is Hobbes's Picture Collector's Manual (1849, p. 92), which was not read. This may be real -- a second lane distinct from his mother's portrait practice, and the reason the chain's next study turns to the Florentine still-life and vedute masters -- or it may be a repeated error, the same thin thread copied three times. The study presents it as a REPORTED specialty, not a documented practice, and no still life or veduta goes in the works table as an individual entry. The watchable behaviour, if the lane is real, is the opposite pole to the court portrait: observation of things and of the city, painted without sitters -- fruit and flowers catalogued like specimens, streets and villas recorded like views, the painter looking at the world instead of at a face. **Takeaway:** still life and vedute are reported, not proven -- the specialty that would set him apart from his mother rests on one stub and its echoes, with no example attached, and the study says so rather than repeating it as fact.

## M7. Gabbiani First, Mother Second -- The Training Sequence

The order of his teachers is the mechanism, and the sources disagree about it, which is itself the evidence. Moucke (via the Italian Wikipedia): "the mother first entrusted him to the teaching of A. D. Gabbiani and then taught him personally the technique of pastels and of miniature." Gabbiani first, mother second, and the mother taught the pastel and miniature technique specifically. The French Wikipedia reverses it: his mother taught him painting, and he "completed" his apprenticeship with Gabbiani. The English Wikipedia suggests parallel training: "like his mother, he trained under Antonio Domenico Gabbiani." The study uses the Moucke sequence, since it is the most detailed and the most plausible mechanically: oil and drawing from the shared master, pastel and miniature from his mother. What it means is that his pastel came to him second-hand -- not from Tempesti, his mother's own pastel teacher, and not from Galantini, her miniature teacher, but from his mother herself, who had learned from both. The watchable behaviour is the handoff: the boy passed from the master's studio to his mother's room, the medium changing hands with him, the oil training staying underneath the pastel finish. **Takeaway:** Gabbiani first, mother second -- the pastel he worked in came to him through his mother, not from her teachers, and the sequence, not just the names, is the training.

## M8. Teaching Beside His Mother -- The Joint Pupil

He taught, but only ever beside her, and only one pupil is documented. The English Wikipedia Giovanna article: "Along with her son, Lorenzo, Fratellini taught Violante Beatrice Siries." Siries (1709-1783), named after the patroness Violante, was taught by mother and son jointly, and took Giovanna's place as court painter after her death. No other pupil of Lorenzo is named; no independent school or class of his own is documented. The joint teaching is the whole of the claim, and it is also the image of the family workshop: mother, son and pupil working in the same room, the self-portrait showing her painting his miniature while she taught them both. The watchable behaviour is the shared room: two teachers at one table, the pupil between them, the mother's hand and the son's hand on the same lesson -- the workshop as a household, not an academy. **Takeaway:** he taught beside his mother, not alone -- one joint pupil, Siries, taught by mother and son together, and the school passed to her, not to him.

## M9. The Attribution Problem -- Mother or Son

Same surname, same court, same medium, same small format, son trained by mother, and the mother's most famous work depicts the son: the attribution problem is structural, not a single disputed picture. The far larger Fratellini pastel corpus is catalogued under Giovanna (Jeffares J.326, roughly 100 works); Lorenzo holds three entries (J.3262.101, .103, .105). Unsigned Florentine pastels of this circle are liable to be assigned to the wrong Fratellini, and no source in any leg documents a specific misattribution, so the study names the risk structurally, not as a proven case. The J-number gaps sharpen it: Jeffares lists J.3262.101, .103 and .105 under Lorenzo, and the intervening numbers .102 and .104 do not appear in his article -- they may belong to Giovanna's entries in the shared J.3262 sequence, or they may be omitted works, and the study does not invent entries for them. The "Fratellini or Piattoli" confusion that blurs the Villa La Petraia fancy heads does NOT apply to Lorenzo: no source raises a Lorenzo-versus-Piattoli question, and Piattoli belongs to the succession after Giovanna, not to a confusion with her son. The watchable behaviour is the label sliding: a small pastel on paper, 56 x 42, a court face in soft high-key colour -- mother or son -- and the attribution changing while the picture stays the same. **Takeaway:** mother or son is the standing question of this subject -- the same court, medium and format, a hundred works under her name and three under his, and the unsigned pastels in between could be either.

## M10. The Short Life -- Thirty-Nine Years, Then the Footnote

He died at 39, his mother outlived him by two years, and the career simply stops. Lorenzo died in Florence on Wednesday 12 October 1729, aged 39; his mother died on Wednesday 18 April 1731 and was buried in the church of Ognissanti, near the tomb of her son. No work is dated to 1727-1729, and no late style can be traced. Gabburri, writing within a few years of the death, already treats the output as a closed body, widely dispersed: "there were in many Florentine houses not only his portraits but also" the storiette. The two Uffizi pastels survive in the portrait collection; the Stecchi survives at least as a Franceschini engraving; the storiette and most court portraits are untraced, probably dispersed from private Florentine houses. The mother overshadows the son in the modern record: Giovanna was rediscovered by Monteverdi (1981), Meloni Trkulja (1989), Jane Fortune (2009) and the Emmy-winning documentary Invisible Women, while Lorenzo has no comparable modern study -- the Dizionario biografico degli Italiani entry cited by Jeffares (2008) could not be opened, and no Treccani web page for Lorenzo surfaced in search. He survives as a footnote to his mother, which is exactly why the study matters: the footnote has a specialty -- still life, vedute, storiette -- that his mother does not share. The watchable behaviour is the dispersal: pictures leaving the house one by one into Florentine drawing rooms, the name thinning to a footnote, the two Uffizi pastels the only fixed points left. **Takeaway:** thirty-nine years, then dispersal -- the career stops in 1729 with nothing dated after, the works scatter into private houses, and the son survives as a footnote to his mother, with a specialty she never claimed.

## Evolution

Phase 1 -- Training: Gabbiani, then his mother, c.1700-c.1710. Born Saturday 14 January 1690 in Florence, the only child named in any source read. His mother first entrusted him to Anton Domenico Gabbiani (1652-1726), the leading Florentine history painter of the generation, and then taught him pastel and miniature herself (Moucke). The French Wikipedia reverses the order; the study uses Moucke. No dated work, no dated travel, no dated commission from this phase; the training is known from biography, not objects. His mother's own teachers -- Galantini for miniature, Tempesti for pastel, Gabbiani for drawing and oil -- are hers, not his, and must not be transferred to him.

Phase 2 -- Early work and the Micheli problem, c.1700-c.1710. The only work attached to the early years is the Micheli portrait, and its dating is incoherent as printed. Jeffares gives "Villa Lappeggi c.1700" for J.3262.101; if c.1700 is the work's date, Lorenzo was about 10, which is not credible for a court portrait in the Uffizi series. More likely c.1700 belongs to the Villa Lappeggi provenance than to the work, or the date is simply loose. Berti 1979 p. 760 is cited as literature and was not accessible, so the ambiguity is unresolved. Treat the Micheli portrait as Lorenzo's earliest attested pastel, but do not date it to c.1700 without this caveat.

Phase 3 -- Mature pastel portraiture, c.1710-1729. The two solid works sit here: the Stecchi portrait (undated, but Stecchi's poem Delle Meteore was published in 1726, so the portrait plausibly belongs to the 1720s) and the Vanni portrait (c.1720). Both are small-format pastel portraits of notable Florentines outside the royal family: a philosopher-poet and a goldsmith. Together with Moucke's statement that Lorenzo, once placed in the Medici court, "executed portraits of courtiers and guests," the picture is of a working court portraitist in his mother's medium and format: pastel on paper, 56 x 42 cm in both Uffizi examples, a repeatable cabinet size. The Vanni portrait carries its own date problem: the sitter Giuseppe Vanni died in 1716 (c.1647-1716), so a c.1720 portrait is either posthumous, or the date is loose, or the death date is wrong -- unresolved, and flagged.

Phase 4 -- The reported second lane: still life and vedute, claimed, undated. The English Wikipedia, French Wikipedia and Prabook all state that Lorenzo "specialized as a still life and vedute painter." No source found names, dates, locates or describes a single still life or veduta by him, and all three pages appear to derive from the same thin thread (the English Wikipedia cites only Hobbes 1849). This may be real -- a second lane distinct from his mother's portrait practice -- or it may be a repeated error. The study presents it as an UNVERIFIED claim, not a documented body of work, and no still life or veduta goes in the works table as an individual entry.

Phase 5 -- Death and afterlife, 1729 onward. No work is dated to 1727-1729, and no late style can be traced. He died Wednesday 12 October 1729, aged 39. Gabburri, writing within a few years, already treats the output as a closed body dispersed in Florentine houses. Pilkington's Dictionary of Painters (1770) lists him; Hobbes's Picture Collector's Manual (1849, p. 92) still carries him; Moucke's 1762 life of his mother is the fullest early source on him. His mother lived two years longer and was buried beside him in Ognissanti. The modern record never caught up: no monograph, no exhibition, no catalogue raisonne as far as any leg could establish.

What changed: scale (miniature training to 56 x 42 cm pastel); subject (portrait the documented base, storiette the distinctive talent, still life and vedute the reported but unproven second lane); the teaching role (pupil, then joint teacher of Siries). What stayed the same: pastel on paper as the core medium; the small bust portrait as the unit; the Medici court as the whole world of the work; the family workshop as the place it happened.

## Aesthetic Logic

The logic is the small picture that holds more than its size, made by a hand trained twice. He learned oil from Gabbiani and pastel from his mother, and the combination is the whole practice: the oil painter's structure -- a scene, a story, a crowd -- executed at the miniaturist's scale, in dry pastel on a small sheet. The storiette is the proof of it: a narrative with many tiny figures, crowded into a pastel no bigger than a court portrait, because the hand that learned miniature could put a crowd where his mother put one face. The court gave him the sitters and the format at once -- one small pastel, 56 x 42, repeated -- but his sitters were the court's working people, the jester and the goldsmith and the philosophy reader, not its princes, and the format that flattered marchesas in his mother's hands recorded working notables in his. The miniature inside his mother's self-portrait is the other half of the logic: the son exists in the record as his mother's idealized miniature, the small face inside her large picture, while his own pictures -- the ones that would show what he saw -- are mostly lost, dispersed into Florentine houses, surviving as two Uffizi pastels and a sentence of Gabburri's. The reported still life and vedute, if real, would complete the picture: the same small precise hand turned from faces to things and to the city, the court portraitist looking outward. If they are not real, the career is smaller still: three portraits, a lost crowd of little stories, and a footnote. Either way the logic holds: a short life, a small format, a double training, and a reputation that survives as a sentence about tiny figures -- the crowd in miniature, the story in pastel, the son in the mother's picture.

## Network

- N1. Giovanna Fratellini (27 October 1666 - 18 April 1731; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Mother and teacher. Presented him to Gabbiani, then taught him pastel and miniature herself; he worked beside her at the Medici court; she was buried in Ognissanti near his tomb. The birth day, 27 October, rests on the Italian Wikipedia and Davis; Jeffares and the English Wikipedia give the year only (see #509 C4). STUDIED #509. Evidence: leg 3.
- N2. Giuliano Fratellini (no dates in any source read) -- Father of Lorenzo; husband of Giovanna (married 1684 per Jeffares and the Italian Wikipedia, 1685 per the English Wikipedia -- see #509 C3). No artistic activity is recorded for him. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Judgement exclusion: family, no artistic activity, not a maker.
- N3. Anton Domenico Gabbiani (13 February 1652 - 22 November 1726; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Teacher. Giovanna first entrusted Lorenzo to him; Lorenzo then learned pastel from his mother. The leading Florentine history painter of the generation and the hub of Lorenzo's training circle: Luti, Hugford, Redi, del Pace, Marchesini and Campiglia were all his pupils. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N4. Giovanni Gaetano Gabbiani (b. late 17th c., d. before 26 March 1734; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Nephew of Anton Domenico, studied under his uncle; a meritorious portrait painter in pastel as well as in oil, with at least five Medici portraits. A parallel career to Lorenzo's: the younger Gabbiani in the same medium, the same court, the same years. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N5. Benedetto Luti (17 November 1666 - 17 June 1724; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Fellow pupil under Gabbiani. Luti's Rome drawing academy (attended by Panini until about 1717-18) is the bridge from Lorenzo's Florentine circle to the vedute line. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N6. Ignazio Enrico Hugford (1703-1778; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Fellow pupil under Gabbiani, and Gabbiani's biographer; described Gabbiani's Palazzina Meridiana iconography. The witness who preserves the training circle's memory. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N7. Tommaso Redi (22 December 1665 - 10 October 1726; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Fellow pupil under Gabbiani; teacher of Campiglia (N10). Same generation as Lorenzo, same master, same city. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N8. Ranieri del Pace (7 May 1681, Pisa - 27 February 1738, Florence; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Fellow pupil under Gabbiani; later a Santa Verdiana collaborator with Moriani, Lapi, Puglieschi and Veracini. The link from Lorenzo's training circle to the Santa Verdiana group. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 reserve. Evidence: leg 3.
- N9. Pietro Marchesini (7 April 1692 - 24 October 1757; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Fellow pupil under Gabbiani, of Pistoia. Lorenzo's exact contemporary in the same workshop. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 reserve. Evidence: leg 3.
- N10. Giovanni Domenico Campiglia (1692-1768; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Fellow pupil in the Gabbiani circle (Redi's pupil). Same generation, same city. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N11. Domenico Tempesti (c.1655-1737; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Giovanna's pastel teacher, hence Lorenzo's grandmother-teacher in the chain: the man who taught his mother the medium in which Lorenzo worked. Tempesti is the technical root of the Fratellini pastel school. Not studied; CARRIED in #508 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N12. Violante Beatrice Siries, later Cerroti (26 January 1709 - 20 April 1783; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Co-pupil under Giovanna with Lorenzo; succeeded Giovanna as court painter after her death. The school's second generation, taught in the same room as Lorenzo. Not studied; CARRIED in #508 main 20. Evidence: leg 3.
- N13. Maria Maddalena Baldacci, nee Gozzi (1718-1782; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Giovanna's pupil; the school's second generation after Lorenzo. Not studied; CARRIED in #508 main 20. Evidence: leg 3. (Correction to leg 3: its note attributes Edward Wright's 1730 judgement -- "comes nearest to Rosa Alba of Venice for Miniature" -- to Baldacci. The judgement is about Giovanna Fratellini; see #509 Q2. It is not repeated here. See C7.)
- N14. Rosalba Carriera (12 January 1673 - 15 April 1757; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; some sources give 1675 as the birth year) -- The Venetian pastellist Giovanna met in Venice, who extended her "gentilissime accoglienze" (Jeffares). Carriera is the model for the whole Fratellini pastel practice and the comparison against which Giovanna was measured ("la Rosalba Carriera fiorentina"); Lorenzo's pastels belong to the fashion she set in motion. No evidence Lorenzo met or knew Carriera. STUDIED #508. Evidence: leg 3.
- N15. Giovanni Camillo Sagrestani (15 December 1660 - 7 May 1731; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Leading Florentine late-Baroque painter of Lorenzo's generation; pupil of Cignani's workshop line, teacher of Bonechi, Moriani and del Pace. The dominant Florentine alternative to Gabbiani's circle, working in the same churches and palaces. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N16. Matteo Bonechi (8 November 1669 - 27 February 1756; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Sagrestani's pupil; works in San Frediano in Cestello, Oltrarno. The next generation of the Sagrestani line, painting in Lorenzo's Florence. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N17. Giuseppe Moriani (1681-1731; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; the English Wikipedia gives no dates, only "late 17th and early 18th century") -- Sagrestani's pupil, strongly influenced by him; painted the Story of Santa Verdiana in Castelfiorentino in collaboration with Sagrestani, del Pace, Lapi, Puglieschi and Veracini. The Santa Verdiana group is the Florentine collective of Lorenzo's years. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N18. Bartolomeo Bimbi (15 May 1648 - 14 January 1729; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Medici court painter and the leading Florentine still-life painter of Lorenzo's specialty: encyclopedic scientific still lifes of fruits and flowers cataloguing the Medici botanical collections, some 30 works in the Museo Botanico / Pitti. Pupil of Lorenzo Lippi and Onorio Marinari; in contact with the naturalists Francesco Redi and Pier Antonio Micheli. He died Friday 14 January 1729 -- Lorenzo's 39th birthday. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3; parent. Top pick for #511.
- N19. Andrea Scacciati (1642-1710; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- With Bimbi the leading Florentine still-life painter of the late Baroque; six flower still lifes commissioned 1683 by Vittoria della Rovere for Poggio Imperiale (four identified, now Palazzo di Montecitorio, Rome). "The best painter in Florence in this sector" (via search results); loved by the Medici, especially Vittoria della Rovere and Cosimo III. The other half of Lorenzo's still-life specialty. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N20. Vincenzo Meucci (baptised 6 April 1694 - 7 November 1766; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Florentine painter; pupil first of Sebastiano Galeotti, then Giovanni Gioseffo dal Sole in Bologna; masterpiece the cupola of San Lorenzo, Glory of Florentine Saints (1742), commissioned by Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici. Pupil Tommaso Gherardini; taught Violante Ferroni with Ferretti. The Florentine who carried Lorenzo's generation into the mid-century. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N21. Giovanni Domenico Ferretti (15 June 1692 - 18 August 1768; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Florentine painter; taught Violante Ferroni with Meucci. A member of Lorenzo's generation working in the same Florentine churches. Not studied; CARRIED in #509 reserve. Evidence: leg 3.
- N22. Alessandro Gherardini (16 November 1655 - 6 April 1727; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; Jeffares gives the death at Livorno 6 April 1727, the English Wikipedia gives 1726) -- Pupil of Alessandro Rosi; competitor for commissions with Gabbiani; teacher of Sebastiano Galeotti; fresco of Ferdinando de' Medici's marriage at the Pitti. The rival master in Lorenzo's Florence, and the root of the Galeotti line. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N23. Sebastiano Galeotti (1675/76-1746; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; birth disputed -- English Wikipedia infobox 22 December 1675, article text 1656, auction sources Florence 1676; death Mondovi 1746, some sources 1741) -- Pupil of Gherardini, Felice Torelli and Giovanni Gioseffo dal Sole; peripatetic (Florence, Genoa, Parma, Piacenza, Turin); director of the Turin academy precursor to the Accademia Albertina; died at Mondovi working for the House of Savoy. First teacher of Meucci (N20); father of Giuseppe Galeotti (reserve candidate). Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N24. Giovanni Paolo Panini (17 June 1691 - 21 October 1765; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Leading vedutista; trained in Piacenza under Giuseppe Natali, Andrea Galluzzi and Francesco Galli-Bibiena, moved to Rome 1711, attended Benedetto Luti's drawing academy until about 1717-18; principe of the Accademia di San Luca 1754; pupils Hubert Robert and his son Francesco Panini. The vedute master of Lorenzo's other specialty, trained in the academy of Lorenzo's fellow pupil Luti. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N25. Giuseppe Zocchi (c.1711-1767; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; the Victoria and Albert Museum gives 1711 or 1717 as the birth year) -- Florence painter and printmaker, best known for vedute; Marchese Andrea Gerini commissioned him to record Florentine landmarks; Scelta XXIV vedute (25 views, 1744) and Vedute delle ville e di altri luoghi della Toscana (50 views); drawings in the Pierpont Morgan Library. The Florentine vedutista after Lorenzo. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N26. Niccolo Cassana (1659-1713 or 1714; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; death year disputed -- Royal Collection Trust 1713, 1911 Britannica 1714) -- Venetian-born portraitist; sent a self-portrait to Florence 1681 and failed in a bid for court painter; later Medici court painter under Ferdinando; portraits of Ferdinando and Gian Gastone; invited to England, Queen Anne sat to him; died in London. The court portraitist whose Florentine career frames Lorenzo's own. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N27. Lorenzo Lippi (3 May 1606 - 15 April 1665; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Painter and poet; pupil of Matteo Rosselli; friend of Salvator Rosa; court painter in Innsbruck; most prominent pupil Bartolomeo Bimbi. The teacher of Lorenzo's top-pick predecessor in still life; also, per Jeffares, a sheet of his is an early Florentine example of a sheet covered fully by pastel. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N28. Onorio Marinari (1627 - 5 January 1715; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; Sotheby's once gives 1627-1716) -- Pupil and cousin of Carlo Dolci, influenced by Simone Pignoni and Francesco Furini; short apprenticeship with Volterrano; Bimbi was one of his pupils; Palazzo Capponi fresco dated 1707; published Fabbrica ed uso dell'Annulo Astronomico 1674. Bimbi's second teacher, and the Dolci-Volterrano bridge in Florentine painting. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N29. Antonio Puglieschi (1660-1732; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Trained first with Pietro Dandini, then in Rome with Ciro Ferri; St Ignatius of Loyola before the Virgin, San Giovannino degli Scolopi, Florence; canvas in San Frediano in Cestello; pupil Giuseppe Bottani; Santa Verdiana collaborator with Moriani, Lapi and Veracini. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N30. Niccolo Lapi (c.1667-1732; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Follower of Luca Giordano; patronized by Prince Ferdinando de' Medici; colleague of Gabbiani and Francesco Conti; churches San Jacopo Soprarno, Santa Maria dei Candeli, San Michele Visdomini; Santa Verdiana collaborator. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N31. Agostino Veracini (14 December 1689 - 1762; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; death disputed -- article text 18 November 1762, infobox 20 February 1762) -- Second son of Benedetto Veracini (also a painter); cousin of the violinist and composer Francesco Maria Veracini; religious painter, frescoes and restoration; trained Giulio Traballesi; Santa Verdiana collaborator; Saint John Gualbert Crushing Simony and Nicolaitism, 1744. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N32. Carlo Cignani (15 May 1628 - 8 September 1719; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source; some sources give 6 September 1719 as the death day) -- Bolognese master; pupil of Battista Cairo then Francesco Albani; influenced by Correggio; director and Principe of the Accademia Clementina; his workshop included his son Felice Cignani, Marcantonio Franceschini, Giuseppe Maria Crespi, Federico Bencovich, Francesco Mancini, Giovan Camillo Sagrestani, Ferdinand Galli-Bibiena and Luigi Quaini. The head of the Bolognese line that feeds the candidate list (Sagrestani, Franceschini, Crespi, dal Sole, Torelli). Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Also a #511 candidate.
- N33. Giovanni Battista Piazzetta (1682/83-1754; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Venetian painter of chalk heads and altarpieces; the pastellist-adjacent Venetian whose small-scale heads parallel Lorenzo's own pastel heads. Held as an alternate in two blocks. Not studied; CARRIED in #508 ALTERNATE and #509 ALTERNATE. Evidence: leg 3.
- N34. Marco Ricci (1676-1730; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Venetian landscape painter; London connection; uncle of Sebastiano Ricci. The Venetian landscape line beside Lorenzo's vedute. Held as an alternate in two blocks. Not studied; CARRIED in #508 ALTERNATE and #509 ALTERNATE. Evidence: leg 3.
- N35. Cosimo III de' Medici (1642-1723; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Grand Duke of Tuscany; main patron of Bimbi (botany interest) and of Scacciati; the ruler of the court Lorenzo worked in. Not a maker. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Judgement exclusion: patron, not a maker.
- N36. Violante of Bavaria (1673-1731; as commonly cited, not verified from a primary source) -- Violante Beatrice of Bavaria, Grand Princess of Tuscany, wife of Ferdinando; Giovanna's patron and employer (official portraitist 1717-22); the court whose buffoon Micheli (N38) Lorenzo portrayed. Not a maker. Not studied; NOT carried. Evidence: leg 3. Judgement exclusion: patron, not a maker.

## POSITIONS

- P1. [FROM RECORD] Son and pupil of Giovanna. Jeffares: "Son, and pupil, of Giovanna (q.v.)." The Italian Wikipedia agrees: his mother first entrusted him to A. D. Gabbiani, then taught him pastel and miniature herself. Evidence: leg 3.
- P2. [FROM RECORD] Also trained under Anton Domenico Gabbiani. Jeffares and both Wikipedias: like his mother, he trained under Gabbiani. The training order (Gabbiani first, mother second) is the Italian Wikipedia's. Evidence: leg 3.
- P3. [FROM RECORD] He worked in oil and especially pastel. Jeffares: "He worked in oil and especially pastel, where he demonstrated 'suo buon gusto, suo spirito e suo talento.'" Evidence: leg 3.
- P4. [REPORTED] He specialized as a still-life and vedute painter. English Wikipedia: "he specialized as a still life and vedute painter"; French Wikipedia: "Il s'est specialise dans les natures mortes et les vedutes". The English Wikipedia's sole cited source for Lorenzo is Hobbes 1849 p. 92, which was not read; treat the specialty as reported, not as established from a primary source. Evidence: leg 3.
- P5. [REPORTED] Court portraitist for Medici courtiers and guests. Italian Wikipedia (summarizing Moucke, Serie di ritratti vol. IV, 1762, pp. 209-21): "Inserito nella corte medicea, Lorenzo Maria esegui ritratti di cortigiani e ospiti." Moucke's volume itself was not read. Evidence: leg 3.
- P6. [FROM RECORD] His pastels hung in many Florentine houses. Gabburri, via Jeffares: "Despite his early death, there were in many Florentine houses not only his portraits but also 'di storiette a pastelli copiose di figurine preziosissime, nel che fare ebbe un talento particolare.'" Evidence: leg 3.
- P7. [FROM RECORD] Little pastel stories crowded with tiny precious figures were his particular talent. Gabburri's phrase (Q3), via Jeffares. This is the one concrete description of what made his work distinct: not grand portraits but small crowded narrative pastels. Evidence: leg 3.
- P8. [FROM RECORD] Portrait of the goldsmith Giuseppe Vanni, c.1720, Uffizi inv. 1890 no. 2589. Jeffares J.3262.105: Giuseppe Vanni (c.1647-1716), orefice, pastel on paper, 56x42, c.1720 (Uffizi, inv. 1890, no. 2589; Villa Lappeggi c.1700). Lit.: Berti 1979, Ic5054 repr. The best-dated surviving Lorenzo work. Evidence: leg 3.
- P9. [FROM RECORD, WITH A FLAG] Portrait of Tommaso Micheli, court buffoon of Violante Beatrice. Jeffares J.3262.101: Tommaso Micheli, nano, buffone alle corte di Violante Beatrice, pastel on paper, 56x42 (Uffizi, inv. 1890, no. 2586; Villa Lappeggi c.1700). FLAG: the "c.1700" date sits against Lorenzo's birth on 14 January 1690 -- a portrait dated c.1700 would make the painter ten years old. The "Villa Lappeggi c.1700" note most likely dates the Lappeggi series, not the individual work, but the record as printed is ambiguous; do not resolve it by guessing. The work itself is not in doubt, the date is. Evidence: leg 3.
- P10. [FROM RECORD] Portrait of Giovanni Lorenzo Stecchi, lettore di filosofia at Pisa, poet, engraved by Vincenzo Franceschini. Jeffares J.3262.103. No dates were found for Stecchi or for Franceschini in the sources searched; the portrait survives through the engraving, not as a located pastel. Evidence: leg 3.
- P11. [FROM RECORD] He taught beside his mother. The English Wikipedia Giovanna article: "Along with her son, Lorenzo, Fratellini taught Violante Beatrice Siries." Siries was his mother's pupil and successor as court painter (Jeffares; both Wikipedias), which places Lorenzo inside a working teaching school, not a solitary practice. (Leg 3 tagged this REPORTED; the parent corrects the tag to FROM RECORD on leg 1's finding of the specific form. See C8.) Evidence: leg 1; leg 3.
- P12. [FROM RECORD] His miniature appears in his mother's Self-Portrait of c.1720. Davis (Cole): Giovanna's Self-Portrait c.1720 (pastel on paper, 72 x 57 cm, Uffizi) depicts her "working on a male miniature of her son Lorenzo that is very idealized and typical of the Rococo taste for courtly elegance". The son is present in the mother's signature work as an idealized miniature, not as himself. Evidence: leg 3.
- P13. [REPORTED] He died 12 October 1729, aged 39; his mother was buried in Ognissanti near his tomb. Italian Wikipedia: Giovanna died 18 April 1731 and was buried in the church of Ognissanti, near the tomb of her son Lorenzo Maria, dead 1729 aged 39. Jeffares gives the same death date, 12 October 1729. Evidence: leg 3.
- P14. [INFERRED -- labelled as such] No Accademia del Disegno membership is recorded for him. His mother was accepted in 1706 and made a full member in 1710; no source read records any academy membership for Lorenzo. This is an absence in the sources read, not a proven negative. Evidence: leg 3.
- P15. [INFERRED -- labelled as such] No surviving self-portrait is known. No source read names or locates a self-portrait by Lorenzo; the only portrait image of him is the idealized miniature inside his mother's Self-Portrait (P12). Absence in the sources read, not a proven negative. Evidence: leg 3.
- P16. [INFERRED -- labelled as such] No documented pupils. No source read names a pupil of Lorenzo. His teaching, if any, was inside his mother's school (P11). Absence in the sources read, not a proven negative. Evidence: leg 3.
- P17. [INFERRED -- labelled as such] His works are routinely confused with, or subsumed under, his mother's. The record gives him two secure Uffizi pastels and a body of "storiette" known only from Gabburri's sentence; the far larger Fratellini pastel corpus is catalogued under Giovanna (Jeffares J.326, roughly 100 works). Attribution between mother and son is the standing problem of this subject. Evidence: leg 3.
- P18. [INFERRED -- labelled as such] The "storiette" show a miniaturist's eye carried into pastel. Gabburri's phrase (Q3) describes pastels "copiose di figurine preziosissime" -- crowded with tiny precious figures. That is miniature-painting scale and miniature-painting subject matter, executed in his mother's second medium. The inference is that his training in miniature (P1) shaped his pastel manner. Evidence: leg 3.
- P19. [INFERRED -- labelled as such] His vedute have no securely located surviving example in the sources read. The vedute specialty (P4) rests on the Wikipedia stub and Hobbes; no veduta by Lorenzo is named, located, or reproduced in any source read. Absence in the sources read, not a proven negative. Evidence: leg 3.
- P20. [INFERRED -- labelled as such] He is the hinge between the Fratellini pastel school and the Florentine still-life and vedute line. On one side: his mother, Tempesti, Gabbiani, Siries, Baldacci -- the pastel school. On the other: Bimbi and Scacciati in still life, Panini and Zocchi in vedute, the court that employed them all. Lorenzo is the only figure who stands in both lines at once, and the only one whose reported specialties name both. Evidence: leg 3.

Reception position (for the record, not counted above): near-total obscurity after death. Lorenzo is known only through three layers: Gabburri's Vite di pittori (manuscript, 1719-41), Moucke's Serie di ritratti vol. IV (1762, pp. 209-21), and the modern record (Jeffares; Pilkington 1770; Saur; Dizionario biografico degli Italiani 2008, s.n. Marmocchini). There is no monograph, no exhibition, and no full-size reproduction of a work was opened in this leg. The English Wikipedia entry is a stub of four sentences whose sole reference is Hobbes 1849 p. 92. He survives as a footnote to his mother -- which is exactly why the study matters: the footnote has a specialty (still life, vedute, storiette) that his mother does not share.

## Load-bearing corrections

- C1. STILL LIFE AND VEDUTE ARE REPORTED, NOT DOCUMENTED -- AND #509'S TOP-PICK LINE STATES THEM AS FACT. The English Wikipedia stub asserts he "specialized as a still life and vedute painter"; the French Wikipedia and Prabook repeat it. No source found in any leg names, dates, locates or describes a single still life or veduta by him, and Jeffares, the specialist source, records only portraits and pastel storiette. VERDICT: say "reported to have specialized in still life and vedute", never state the specialty as established fact, and put no still life or veduta in the works table.
- C2. TRAINING ORDER -- GABBIANI FIRST, MOTHER SECOND. Moucke (via the Italian Wikipedia): his mother first entrusted him to Gabbiani's teaching and then taught him pastel and miniature herself. The French Wikipedia reverses the order; the English Wikipedia suggests parallel training. VERDICT: use the Moucke sequence; Gabbiani was the first teacher, his mother the second, and she taught the pastel and miniature technique specifically.
- C3. THE MICHELI PORTRAIT "C.1700" IS INCOHERENT AS A WORK DATE. Jeffares attaches "Villa Lappeggi c.1700" to J.3262.101; Lorenzo was born 14 January 1690, so a c.1700 portrait would make him about 10. VERDICT: do not date the Micheli portrait c.1700; the note most likely belongs to the Villa Lappeggi provenance, and the ambiguity is unresolved (Berti 1979 not accessible).
- C4. THE VANNI PORTRAIT "C.1720" SITS AGAINST A SITTER DEAD IN 1716. Giuseppe Vanni (c.1647-1716) died four years before the c.1720 date Jeffares gives the portrait. VERDICT: flag it; the portrait is either posthumous, or the date is loose, or the death date is wrong. Do not resolve it by guessing.
- C5. THE JEFFARES NUMBER IS J.3262, NOT J.327. The task brief guessed J.327 or adjacent to his mother's J.326. The retrieved entry numbers Lorenzo's works J.3262.101, J.3262.103, J.3262.105 -- Lorenzo is J.3262, the decimal extension of his mother's J.326. VERDICT: cite J.3262 throughout.
