## Core engine

THE TECHNIQUE IS THE PIECE AND THE SOURCE MATERIAL IS REAL: each project starts as a self-taught educational journey into a specific graphics technique, and its input is almost always something human, analog, or natural. In his own words (Le Random, 2026): "A lot of my work is prototypical in that I want to look closer at a particular technique or technology and that's the starting point for many of my prior projects." And (Art Blocks, Jan 2022): "Almost everything I make involves the input of something very human, analog, or natural, like photos or videos of things that I experience, music, or 3D scans of biological objects. The output of my work is usually something on the other end of the spectrum, something highly digitized, synthetic, and simulated. This transformation of source material to something new is the most critical metaphor behind the artwork I create." He describes his position as a generation "between real and synthetic": "We can still determine, most of the time, what is real and what is fake, what has grown and what has been generated. But this will not last."

Watchable behavior across the whole catalog: something familiar (a dancer, a bouquet, a photo of Joshua Tree, a gallery room) is run through a system until it becomes synthetic, but the system stays visible. Painted flowers are built from visible 3D brushstrokes. Dancers leave layered shells of their own bodies. A gallery room shows a smaller version of itself. The viewer's eye is always allowed to find the mechanism.

## Evolution timeline

- 2020-04/05 (f19 down to f15): the optical-flow laboratory. All videos, all in Unity, all tagged #creativecoding #realtime. "Fluid in vector field with audio" (f19, Apr 7): an abstract body-form dissolving into thousands of pink/teal brush-stroke particles, set to Johann Johannsson. Then the movement-history series: dancers (@raesrivastava, @vinnybalbo, @zoe_cavedon, @oonaland__, @papilouise; original footage credits @nivnovak, @eyeofqyul) whose bodies accumulate as layered ribbon shells in amber/red/teal/pink, color read from motion vectors (f18, f17, f16). "Optical flow with hand movement" (f15, May 28): first-person hands over a rug, luminous particle trails rising from the fingertips, a live kinect-style interaction. "Recursive cloth test" (f13, July 10): cloth simulation in a rainbow-striped room, the cloth itself textured with the recursion.
- 2020-06/10: physical displays and video sculpture. "Feed" (f12, June 30, with @obake_ai): a 3D gallery room whose walls are grids of GAN-generated eye portraits with heavy makeup, built with Unity real-time raytracing; a gallery as a container for synthetic faces. "Movement-reactive cloth" (f14, June 23): a dancer visible behind a translucent cloth sheet on a stone wall, the cloth deforming with her. "Recursive video in gallery" (f11, Aug 4): a 3D Droste room, a framed picture on the wall showing the same rug and gallery recursing; recursion as a picture-within-a-picture. "Interactive fluid mirror on physical display" (f10, Aug 24): an abstract point-cloud figure on a portrait monitor on a pedestal in a dining room; second slide is an anaglyph red/blue version for old-school 3D glasses ("I have been using anaglyph glasses to see my stuff in 3D and it is really nice not putting on a headset"). "Video Sculpture with Waves" (f08, Oct 8, footage by @jperl): a hexagonal column with wave textures on a white pedestal in a tall frame; caption calls it "another real-time trompe l'oeil experiment, no post fx or compositing involved. It is so hard to match ambient lighting conditions accurately!" The trompe-l'oeil discipline: the fake sculpture must sit in the room's real light.
- 2020-12 to 2021-05: the Manifold still-life era. "Manifold Still Life with Hydrangeas" (f07/f06, Dec 2020 / Jan 2021): painterly bouquet, cream petals, green foliage, cobalt-blue vase, built from thick visible 3D brushstrokes, sold as a limited edition of 5 with his custom glasses-free 3D display. "Manifold Still Life with Carnations" (f05, Feb 2021): the same mechanism, yellow and red blooms. "Perpetuation: Figure Study" (f04, Apr 2021, Foundation): porous eroded humanoid figures in rust/coral/teal speckle inside a transparent cube on pastel plinths; winner received his custom holographic display. "Perennial: Spring" (f03, May 2021, lgnd.art): full realtime 3D impressionist forest, light shafts, ferns. "EVOLVE" (f02, June 2021, with @thankyoux on Foundation): a folded, wavy wall relief with colorful abstract patches; ThankYouX was one of the first people who talked to him about NFTs, and the collab landed the month after he quit his job. "Joshua Tree" (f01, Oct 2021, lgnd.art at ComplexCon): "I use photographs I have taken in the park over the years to create the color and movement, while custom machine learning software transforms the images to 3D"; the framed result reads as flow-field impressionism, blue sky over brown rock.
- 2022: Chimera (Art Blocks, Jan 2022 interview): the still-life mechanism pushed to 12 stroke styles (oil, charcoal, watercolor, textile) rendered from simple geometry with no textures; 8 flower species (dahlias, roses, sunflowers as the Van Gogh/Monet reference) defined exclusively by mathematical functions, no 3D models, no signed distance fields. He calls still lifes "time capsules" that are "simultaneously timeless and historically specific", and Chimera "seminal" for bringing "an age-old tradition into the new medium of on-chain artwork". The charitable portion went to multiple myeloma research; the project is dedicated to his mother, diagnosed Dec 2021. TECTONICS (Sept 2022, his words in the IG caption): "a 90-piece generative collection... my first fully independent release, not reliant on any platform or institution. The same bit of code produced all these outputs and more." The seed was an iPhone note reading "Recursive render textures, projection on grids of scaling geometry"; he calls it "a visual metaphor of recursion, a pervasive and critically important process that occurs widely throughout nature, narrative, and computer science." The Verse text adds: "an exploration that produced unlikely associations with traditional cubism."
- 2023: Metropolis (referenced in the 2026 Le Random interview): continued paint and analog art-making simulation, including photorealistic paint texture emulation for Avante Art.
- 2024: the SDF pivot. Bright Moments Finale Collection contribution (untitled, detail shown at the top of the Le Random interview): "essentially a verbose exploration of signed distance fields (SDF)... a different way to render a shape than the traditional mesh and material pipeline." Built in about a month, mostly on transatlantic flights, two weeks past deadline, and only about seven kilobytes total (two kilobytes of JavaScript, five of GLSL). "It is just code" when asked if any part is AI. He loves relief sculpture ("Somebody on Twitter compared it to Rodin's The Gates of Hell, which resonated"); the images "splash and swirl on the screen like an alien substance", driven by light and shadow. This marks the turn: "I had a lot of fun doing [photorealistic paint] but I am bored of it now and I want to move on to something else... I want to go back to those things [from my software career] and approach them from an artistic point of view instead of a corporate efficiency point of view."
- 2025-12: Tessellations (f00): "a ten piece series of hyper-detailed metal plated 3D resin prints. The wall reliefs are coated with copper and the sculptures with bronze. All ten pieces sold this weekend at @artbasel Miami. It was my first time showing at any art fair." Presented by @heft_gallery, show put together by @eli_schein. The macro frame shows dense rose-gold tessellated geometry, triangular and hexagonal prism fields. The digital relief has become a physical relief.

## Prompt material (mechanisms, each tied to an observed source)

1. Movement history: a dancer's poses accumulate as layered ribbon/shell geometry over time, colored by optical-flow vectors; the body becomes a temporal sculpture (f16-f18). The mechanism IS the piece: motion trails with decay, stacked by rotation.
2. Real-to-synthetic transformation: familiar input (dancer, bouquet, Joshua Tree photo) run through a system until it reads as synthetic, with the system left visible (Art Blocks interview; f01, f05-f07). Watchable behavior: the source dissolving into its simulation and back.
3. Trompe-l'oeil video sculpture: a realtime scene presented as a physical sculpture in a framed display, ambient lighting matched so the fake sits in the real room (f08, f04, f09). No post fx, no compositing; the honesty of the match is the effect.
4. Recursion as visual metaphor: recursive render textures projected onto grids of scaling geometry; the Droste room; "the same bit of code produced all these outputs" (f11-f12; Tectonics). Watchable behavior: self-similarity you can fall into.
5. Stroke-as-geometry still lifes: flowers defined by mathematical functions, 12 analog stroke styles from simple geometry, no textures (f05-f07; Chimera). Watchable behavior: the bouquet assembling one brushstroke at a time.
6. The technique excuse: each project as a self-taught journey into one graphics technique (Le Random: SDF in 7KB, "only about seven kilobytes, maybe two kilobytes of JavaScript and five kilobytes of GLSL", "It is just code"). The piece is the notebook; simplicity is the discipline.
7. Relief sculpture: the through-line from the Finale SDF work to Tessellations, copper and bronze, "a wall but also three-dimensional", Rodin's Gates of Hell as a reference point (Le Random; f00). Watchable behavior: light raking across a relief as it slowly morphs.
8. The evolving codebase: Please Respond, 10 artists, 1 evolving algorithm, with mpkoz's original code as the seed (Hobbs caption via #221; #220/#221 network). The mechanism: one shared algorithm passed hand to hand, each hand adding one rule.
