## M1. The graphic score as map: 193 pages of lines and shapes

Treatise of 1963-67 is 193 pages of lines, symbols and geometric shapes that largely eschew conventional musical notation, and the pages hold complex combinations of circles, lines, squares, triangles and their derivatives, and staves criss-cross, weave, swoop and jounce, and noteheads, accidentals, clefs and the looping p's and f's of pianos and fortes appear in abstracted form, and the title nods to Wittgenstein's Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, a direct translation of the Latin tractatus, and the work was published in 1967 by The Gallery Upstairs Press in Buffalo, New York, and realisations present the score as visual art and as map-reading. What you see is a page that is itself the object, read spatially rather than left to right, and the performer navigates it the way a walker reads a map. **Takeaway:** draw the music instead of writing it, and the page becomes a place. Against the chain: Wolff also drew music, M4, and his fragments are provocations the player plays around, and Cage also drew music, M10, and his drawings are laid over a staff and read by chance operations, and Cardew's 193 pages are neither provocation nor chance-reading, they are a continuous map with a lifeline through every page, and the difference is between a drawing you answer, a drawing chance reads, and a drawing you travel through.

## M2. The score with no instructions: the negotiation before the sound

Treatise is not accompanied by any explicit instructions to the performers in how to perform the work, or what sound-producing means are to be used, and no instrumentation is specified, and the relationship of the images to pitch or rhythm is not explained in any way, and the performer must devise rules in advance, and the Treatise Handbook of 1971 is a separate exegesis, not instructions, and it lacks precise instructions but clarifies Cardew's philosophical and political thinking. What you see is the negotiation before the sound, players agreeing on rules for a page that gives them none, and the piece begins before the first note, in the argument about what a circle means. **Takeaway:** give the players a map and no legend, and the rules they invent are the piece. Against the chain: Wolff also freed the player, M5, but his prose scores are plain-language instructions, and a sentence tells you what to do, and Cardew's pages tell you nothing at all, and the difference is between a score that instructs in words and a score that refuses to instruct, and the freedom in the second case includes the freedom to decide what the score is.

## M3. The lifeline: one line through every page

A bold horizontal line runs through the centre of every Treatise page, and Cardew called it "the lifeline of the reader, his center around which all manner of activity takes place", and two empty five-line staves run along the bottom of each page to indicate that the interpretation is to be musical, apparently not to suggest piano, and realisations use the lifeline as a divider, material above the line against material below it, curves and circles given to percussion. Against the chain: Wolff also fixed one thing and freed the rest, M2, but his fixed thing is a proportion, the square-root structure, a clock the player cannot see, and Cardew's fixed thing is a line the player can see, and the difference is between a structure you hear and a structure you look at.

## Aesthetic logic

## Load-bearing corrections

C1. BIRTH AND DEATH WEEKDAYS, VERIFIED WITH date -d. Born Thursday 7 May 1936; died Sunday 13 December 1981; aged 45; 16,656 days between the two dates. All sources agree on the dates; the weekdays are this leg's computation.

C2. STOCKHAUSEN ASSISTANTSHIP DURATION, CONFLICT CARRIED. Wikipedia and Zoamorphosis: 1958 to 1960. Bookforum: "For two years, beginning in 1958". Stockhausen himself, quoted in Wikipedia: "he worked with me for over three years". Two years versus over three years cannot both be exact; the study gives 1958-60 and does not assert a single duration without noting the conflict. The 1958 start is agreed everywhere.

C3. SCRATCH ORCHESTRA FOUNDING YEAR, CONFLICT CARRIED. Weekly Worker says "in 1968". Wikipedia, Bookforum, the Free Library essay and Bandcamp all say spring 1969, with the Draft Constitution in The Musical Times June 1969 and the first meeting Tuesday 1 July 1969, weekday verified. 1969 is the evidenced date; 1968 in the Weekly Worker is treated as an error.

C4. THE GREAT LEARNING DATES, CONFLICT CARRIED. Pytheas and Bookforum: 1968-70. Weekly Worker, Squidco and the task brief: 1968-71. Pytheas adds Paragraphs 1 and 2 revised 1972. The seven paragraphs were composed 1968-70; performances and revisions ran into 1971-72. The study gives 1968-70 with the note.

C5. QUAX TREATISE CONCERT DATE, CONFLICT CARRIED. Mode Records and Classical Net: Sunday 15 October 1967, weekday verified Sunday. AllMusic snippet: 17 October 1967, Tuesday, verified. Majority and the label's own page say the 15th; the 17th is treated as an error, but carried, not smoothed.

C6. TREATISE PAGE COUNT, CONFLICT CARRIED. Consensus across Wikipedia, the Pytheas catalogue, Bandcamp and Mode Records: 193 pages. AllMusic snippet: "183 pages". KFJC snippet: "200-page picture book", rounding. 193 is the number used; 183 is likely a slip, and it is also the number of the famous Sonic Youth page, which may explain it.

C7. SCRATCH ORCHESTRA DEMISE, CONFLICT CARRIED. Cardew Wikipedia: "until its demise in 1972". Scratch Orchestra Wikipedia: "effectively inoperative by 1974". Bandcamp: "from 1969 to 1974". The last listed concert is 5 March 1974, "Cornelius Cardew Revolutionary songs and arrangements", and the last Scratch programme in Bandcamp is also 1974. The orchestra faded 1972-74; the study uses "effectively inoperative by 1974" with the 1972 date noted.

C8. DEATH LOCATION, CONFLICT CARRIED. Wikipedia: "near his London home in Leyton". Zoamorphosis: "near his London home in Leytonstone". Leyton and Leytonstone are adjacent East London districts; sources differ on which. The hit-and-run itself is agreed everywhere; the murder suspicion is Tilbury's and Maharg's claim, explicitly contested by Skempton's icy-pavement account and by the coroner's accident verdict. The study presents all three.

C9. AMM MEMBERSHIP DATES. The AMM members list gives Cardew piano and cello 1966-73. Wikipedia: "In 1966, Cardew joined the free improvisation group AMM". The Free Library essay: "From 1966 to 1972, Cardew played with the free improvisation group AMM". Bandcamp: "From 1966 to 1972, he was a member". The 1972 versus 1973 end is fuzzy; the political turn pulled him away around 1972. The study uses "1966 to about 1972".

C10. FIRST SCRATCH CONCERT AND FIRST MEETING, VERIFIED. First meeting: Tuesday 1 July 1969, St Katharine Docks, weekday verified. First concert: Saturday 1 November 1969, Hampstead Town Hall, weekday verified, and the Scratch Orchestra article's own list also says "Sat 1/11/69". Designed by Christopher Hobbs, then 18.

C11. THREE MORE VERIFIED WEEKDAYS. Voice from Thel's Grave premiered Friday 21 June 1957, Aldeburgh, verified. Bun No. 2 at the BBC Proms: Friday 20 August 2010, verified. The Crypt sessions: Wednesday 12 June 1968, verified. The Great Learning at the Proms: Thursday 24 August 1972, verified.

C12. TREATISE HANDBOOK DATE. Published 1971, Edition Peters, consistent across Bookforum and Mode Records. Cardew's essay "Towards an Ethic of Improvisation" comes from the Handbook. Not read end to end; the UbuWeb link is dead, see the Depth line.

C13. "CAGE; GHOST OR MONSTER?" AND THE BOOK. The Cage essay and the Stockhausen talk are both 1972; the book "Stockhausen Serves Imperialism" is 1974, Latimer New Dimensions, 126 pages, collecting essays from 1972 to early 1974 with linking commentary and twenty pages of notes, per the Freedom review snippet. A 2020 reissue exists from Primary Information. The study does not date the book to 1972.

C14. AGE AND OUTPUT SPAN. Years active 1960-1981 per the Wikipedia infobox, but works in the Pytheas catalogue run from 1955, Three Rhythmic Pieces, to 1981, We Sing for the Future and Boolavogue. The infobox span counts the mature career; the catalogue starts in 1955. The study uses the catalogue span when listing works.
