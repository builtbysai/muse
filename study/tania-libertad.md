## M1. The restart from below

She arrived in Mexico in 1980 already famous, and then she started again from zero. The Wikipedia biography states it plainly: after winning festivals and recording ten successful albums in Peru, she travelled to Mexico, where she had lived since 1980, "y decide empezar, desde abajo, una nueva experiencia artistica que la llevaria a alejarse de los medios masivos de comunicacion y a cantar durante seis anos en escuelas preparatorias, universidades, carceles, hospitales, casas de cultura y plazas publicas de toda la republica mexicana". She decided to start from below, a new artistic experience that would take her away from the mass media and into singing for six years in high schools, universities, prisons, hospitals, houses of culture and public plazas across the whole Mexican republic. The mechanism is the deliberate erasure of the first fame: the child star who had her own television programs in Peru, who had sung in nightclubs in Lima since she was a teenager, who had a contract with RCA Victor, walks into a new country and refuses the television studios, and sings instead where nobody famous sings. The payoff is stated in the same sentence: the work "la convierte en una de las artistas mas queridas y reconocidas tambien en este pais", it makes her one of the most loved and recognized artists in this country too, and "desde el cual construyo la plataforma de su internacionalizacion", from which she built the platform of her internationalization. The six years in the prisons and the hospitals are the platform, not the detour. Watch the restart: when the famous singer is singing in a prison yard and the room does not know her name yet, and she sings anyway, and six years later the same room fills the Palacio de Bellas Artes, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** start again from below. The prison yard is the platform. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M9) also let her voice cook slowly over fifty years, but her cooking was the career, album by album, in public; Libertad's cooking was six years out of the media entirely, which is why her version is a disappearance and Leon's is a discography.

## M2. The single-microphone record

"Mi primer disco criollo lo hice en un solo dia, con un solo microfono y tres guitarristas al costado." My first criollo disc I made in a single day, with a single microphone and three guitarists beside me. Libertad said it to El Comercio in October 2026, and the sentence continues into the mechanism: "Pase por los 45 RPM, las cintas de cuatro canales, el vinilo, el cassette, el CD y lo digital. Recuerdo que cuando grababamos con orquesta sinfonica en el Peru, debiamos mezclar por secciones en el momento para liberar canales. En una ocasion, tuve que grabar la voz directamente sobre la placa con la que se fabricarian los discos: no habia margen de error, debia salir afinada a la primera. Esa version aun se puede escuchar en Spotify." She went through the 45 RPM, the four-track tapes, the vinyl, the cassette, the CD and the digital, and once she had to record the voice directly onto the plate from which the discs would be pressed: there was no margin of error, it had to come out in tune the first time, and that version can still be heard on Spotify. The mechanism is the record as a single take with no safety net: one microphone, three guitarists, one day, and later a voice cut straight onto the pressing plate, so the performance and the product are the same event, and a mistake is not a retake but a ruined plate. The career that follows is the same mechanism at every scale: the voice has to be right the first time, because the room, the plate and the microphone are all listening at once. Watch the take: when the singer steps up to one microphone with three guitarists and no second chance, and the song that comes out is the record, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** one microphone, one day, no second chance. The take is the record. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M12) also recorded into a phone at 69, a voice memo becoming a mariachi, but her phone was a sketchbook, a draft to be arranged later; Libertad's single microphone was the finished product, which is why her version is a plate and Leon's is a memo.

## M3. The high voice that takes the opera

She is a soprano, and the Wikipedia infobox says so in one word, "Soprano", and the lead says it in a sentence: "Es reconocida por su forma de voz, un estilo muy agudo que le ha permitido incursiones en generos tan dificiles como la opera y otros." She is recognized for her way of singing, a very high style that has allowed her incursions into genres as difficult as opera and others. The incursion has a date and a title: in 2001 she accepted the challenge of recording "Arias de opera... Y, por que no?", which became one of her greatest successes, platinum for more than 300,000 copies sold in Mexico, more than a million copies internationally, and the gold disc was handed to her by the engineer Carlos Slim, who was also the producer of the album, and the former president of the Spanish government Felipe Gonzalez. The mechanism is the high voice as a passport: the same voice that sang the vals criollo at five and the bolero at thirty-three can walk into the opera house, because the height of the voice is not a genre, it is a range, and the range goes where it likes. The album's title is the mechanism stated as a question, "and why not?", and the sales are the answer: the audience that bought the boleros bought the arias too, because they were buying the voice, not the genre. The discography proves the passport was used more than once: the opera album sits between Mujeres apasionadas (2000) and Costa negra (2001), and the career keeps crossing the border in both directions, from the opera house back to the cajon. ## M4. The forbidden song

"Cardo o ceniza" is a song by Chabuca Granda, and Chabuca forbade Tania Libertad to record it when she was a girl. The JC Magazine report of the Gran Teatro Nacional solo debut of Sunday 10 November 2019 gives the mechanism in two sentences: after "Himno al amor" and "Cerrado por derribo" came "Cardo o ceniza" de Chabuca Granda, "aquel tema que la maestra le prohibiera grabar cuando Tania era una nina". "Chabuca, si viviera el proximo ano cumpliria 100 anos. Hay que preparar mucha fiesta como le gustaba a ella", said Tania. For the centenary of the birth of the great composer, she was already preparing a musical homage with mariachis. The mechanism is the prohibition as a debt: the teacher says no to the child, the child grows up, the teacher dies, and the grown singer pays the debt by singing the forbidden song on the biggest stage in her country, in front of her colleagues, and then by preparing a whole mariachi homage for the centenary. The tribute album came first, Lo inolvidable de Chabuca Granda in 1983, but the forbidden song waited thirty-six years, and the waiting is the mechanism: the song is not refused, it is reserved, and the reservation is what makes it worth singing. The same report gives the other half of the debt: she sang "Todo me habla de ti" by Alicia Maguina and "Himno al amor" by Edith Piaf in her own Spanish translation, so the night was a ledger of the women she owed songs to, and Chabuca was the largest entry. Watch the debt: when the singer finally sings the song she was forbidden as a girl, and the room hears the thirty-six years in it, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** sing the song you were forbidden. The prohibition is the reservation. Against the chain: Boliver (#355 M4) also played the woman who was a letter to her mother, but her letter was the exile, her mother's flight from the Spanish Civil War; Libertad's letter is a forbidden song, which is why her version is a debt and Boliver's is an inheritance.

## M5. The duet as conversation

In February 2014 Libertad published her third and last album with Armando Manzanero, Desarmando a Tania, on Sony Music, and the Wikipedia description of it is the mechanism: "Incluye interpretaciones a dueto de varios temas, otros en solitario o mediante la combinacion de momentos donde solo aparece la voz de alguno de los dos en una estrofa, Manzanero y Libertad juegan con sus voces, con la personalidad que tiene cada uno y con las frases que le van mejor a cada quien." It includes duet interpretations of several themes, others solo or by combining moments where only one of the two voices appears in a verse, Manzanero and Libertad play with their voices, with the personality each one has and with the phrases that suit each one best. The mechanism is the duet as a conversation, not as a harmony: the two voices do not blend, they trade, and each one takes the phrase that fits its personality, so the song is a dialogue between two people who know each other. The project closes the trilogy of albums the two recorded, Armando la Libertad and La Libertad de Manzanero, and there was a fourth, Manzanero a tres pistas in 2013, a tribute that took seven years of planning. The conversation outlived the composer: Manzanero died on 28 December 2020, and Libertad closed the Festival Internacional Cervantino 49 on Sunday 31 October 2021 before twenty thousand people at the Alhondiga de Granaditas in his memory, and returned to the Zocalo on Monday 21 March 2022 with "Contigo aprendi. Homenaje a Armando Manzanero", singing his songs with his own voice present through multimedia, beside Joaquina Mertz and his son Juan Pablo Manzanero. The Cindy Byram bio gives the mechanism's sharpest example: on Negro Color, "Por debajo de la mesa" by Manzanero is sung to lando rhythm, "una bellisima cancion que se enriquece mucho con el estilo lando", a most beautiful song that is much enriched by the lando style. The bolero becomes a lando, the Mexican song becomes a Peruvian rhythm, and the duet becomes a translation. Watch the trade: when the two voices stop singing together and start taking turns, and each phrase sounds like the person singing it, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** trade verses, do not blend. The duet is the conversation. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M5) also closed her interviews with a duet, the host proving she had heard the guest by singing with them; Libertad's duet is not a proof, it is a friendship, three albums and two posthumous homages, which is why her version is a conversation and Leon's is a verdict.

## M6. The Afro-Peruvian map

"Yo no soy una folclorista." I am not a folklorist. Libertad said it to the Cindy Byram bio in November 2004, without remorse, and the sentence is the mechanism: "Negro Color es mi mas reciente experimento. Costa Negra significo mi regreso a lo acustico. Pero a mi no me gusta grabar un disco que suene igual o parecido a algo que ya he hecho. Todos mis discos son diferentes." Negro Color is my most recent experiment. Costa Negra meant my return to the acoustic. But I do not like to record a disc that sounds the same or similar to something I have already done. All my discs are different. The bio gives the geography: she was born in Zana, on the coast the Peruvians call "La Costa Negra", the Black Coast, and "Me crie en la costa, por lo que mi contacto con la musica afroperuana comenzo practicamente desde que naci", I grew up on the coast, so my contact with Afro-Peruvian music began practically from when I was born, singing waltzes and boleros to the cajon, the wooden box that plays the rhythmic function in Afro-Peruvian music, as the congas do in Cuban rumba and mambo. The map is the discography: Lo inolvidable de Chabuca Granda (1983), the tribute to the greatest exponent of the style; Africa en America (1990/1994), "un exuberante mapa musical de la influencia africana a lo largo del continente", an exuberant musical map of the African influence along the continent; Costa Negra (2001), begun in Paris and finished in Senegal, with Cesaria Evora, the diva of Cape Verde, her return to her Afro-Peruvian roots for her United States debut; Negro Color (2004), "mi mas reciente experimento", with no pre-recorded segments and no synthesizers, the guitar of Felix Casaverde, who played beside Chabuca Granda in Peru and went to Mexico with Libertad in 1980, and the piano and musical direction of Sonia Cornuchet, born in Cuba, "Estamos buscando ese punto en el que se conectan la musica cubana y la peruana", we are looking for the point where Cuban music and Peruvian music connect. The mechanism is the map, not the museum: she does not preserve the Afro-Peruvian tradition, she draws it, from the coast to the continent to the Atlantic, and each album is a different route. Watch the map: when the same singer who sang the bolero is now singing a lando, and the cajon is keeping the time, and the song is not a folk song but an experiment, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** draw the map, do not guard the museum. The coast is the route. ## M7. The chord of three

In June 2015, in the legendary Estudios Churubusco in Mexico City, Sony Music recorded for CD and DVD a project reuniting for the first time in a recording the three best exponents of Latin American folklore of their generation, "Las Tres Grandes" in the Primera Fila format, where the artists interpreted several of their greatest hits, plus new songs in their voice, in solo, duo and trio, with special guests Paquita la del Barrio, Lila Downs, Ely Guerra and Maria Leon. On Wednesday 25 November 2015 the three arrived at the Auditorio Nacional with their show, a full house of more than ten thousand people, and El Universal's report of the night gives the staging: the three in passion red adorned with traditional Mexican details, each singing her most representative songs, then joining voices until the three became one voice, "las voces de cada una de ellas se volvian una misma", with guests Maria Leon, Ely Guerra and Lila Downs, and a mariachi finale with the hats and the instruments, before almost ten thousand people. The mechanism is the trio as a chord, and it is Leon's M7 seen from Libertad's side: three solo careers, each complete, singing separately and then together, so the audience hears the harmony as the argument, that these three voices belong in the same sentence. The project has lasted: the three closed the Quimera festival in Metepec in 2019, played the Expo 2020 Dubai Mexico pavilion on Sunday 30 January 2022, and opened Cumbre Tajin on Wednesday 19 March 2025, still billed as Las Tres Grandes, ten years on. The El Universal report adds the detail that makes the mechanism visible: between songs the three sat down to drink tequila to the sound of Joaquin Sabina and talked about the past, and they gave poems as well as songs, Saramago, Sor Juana Ines de la Cruz and Pablo Neruda, so the night was a conversation between friends that happened to be sung. Watch the chord: when the three stop taking turns and start singing together, and the room hears one voice made of three, and the Peruvian voice is inside the Mexican chord without losing its accent, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** sing the chord, not the solo. Three complete voices are the argument. Against the chain: this is Leon's M7 from the other chair, and the difference is the passport: Leon's chord is three Mexican voices, Libertad's chord is two Mexican voices and one Peruvian, which is why her version is a border crossing and Leon's is a reunion.

## M8. The small band that sounds like eighty

"Me acomoda mucho porque, aunque son pocos musicos, suenan como una orquesta de 80." It suits me very much because, although they are few musicians, they sound like an orchestra of 80. Libertad said it to El Comercio in October 2026 about the Banda Chica she took to the Gran Teatro Nacional on Saturday 24 October 2026: "Tengo dos tecladistas que interpretan multiples instrumentos y hacen coros, ademas de un percusionista versatil. Rechazo categoricamente el uso de secuencias o pistas grabadas. El uso de pistas te limita; si en el escenario te sientes inspirada y quieres alargar una frase, la secuencia no te espera. Me gusta interpretar 100% en vivo para que los musicos me vayan arropando de manera organica." I have two keyboardists who play multiple instruments and do choruses, plus a versatile percussionist. I categorically reject the use of sequences or recorded tracks. The use of tracks limits you; if on stage you feel inspired and want to stretch a phrase, the sequence does not wait for you. I like to perform 100 percent live so the musicians can wrap me organically. ## M10. The AI album at 73

"No dejare que la IA me domine." I will not let AI dominate me. Libertad said it to El Comercio in October 2026, and the sentence is the mechanism: "Es uno de los momentos mas dificiles para la industria discografica... Sin embargo, lo de la Inteligencia Artificial (IA) genera cierto temor. No sabemos hacia donde va la musica, pero queremos ser parte de esa evolucion: ya andamos preparando un disco completo con IA. La premisa es clara: no vamos a dejar que la inteligencia artificial nos domine; nosotros la vamos a usar a ella." It is one of the most difficult moments for the record industry, and AI generates a certain fear. We do not know where music is going, but we want to be part of that evolution: we are already preparing a complete album with AI. The premise is clear: we are not going to let artificial intelligence dominate us; we are going to use it. The method is the mechanism's second half: "al no ser compositora, encargo canciones que deben cumplir no solo con mis exigencias artisticas, sino tambien con las normas de los organismos que regulan los derechos de autor", not being a composer, I commission songs that must meet not only my artistic demands but also the rules of the bodies that regulate authors' rights, and "Pido que me hagan canciones en lando, candombe, pasillo o chacarera, pero con un toque moderno. Se trata de usar la tecnologia actual para que nuestros generos sigan vivos." I ask them to make me songs in lando, candombe, pasillo or chacarera, but with a modern touch. It is about using current technology so our genres stay alive. The mechanism is the late turn as a commission: she does not write the songs, she orders them, in the old rhythms, with a modern touch, from a machine she refuses to obey. The album that came just before it proves the turn is not a novelty: "Ay, amor!", album 46, is a covers album of songs that were little known, "canciones que se conocen poco, pero tienen un enorme valor", produced and arranged by herself in her home studio, "soy productora, soy arreglista", because after forty-six productions she had explored almost every genre and the ballad was the one she had barely touched. Watch the commission: when the singer who recorded onto a pressing plate with no margin of error is now ordering songs from a machine in lando and candombe, and the machine obeys her instead of her obeying it, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** commission the machine in lando. The singer gives the orders. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M12) also made her late turn at 69, composing into her phone, but her turn was to become the author; Libertad's turn is to remain the commissioner, ordering songs in the old rhythms from the newest tool, which is why her version is a commission and Leon's is an authorship.

## M11. The return to Zana

On Saturday 14 November 2026, Tania Libertad will sing Schubert's Ave Maria for Pope Leo XIV in the Santuario de Santo Toribio de Mogrovejo in Zana, her hometown, as part of a prayer meeting closing the Jubilee Year. The Radio Mitre piece of 24 October 2026 gives the mechanism: the invitation came unexpectedly, after she sang at a benefit event for the Curia de Chiclayo and met several priests, and when she learned that Robert Prevost, now Leo XIV, had done much of his pastoral work in Peru, including in Zana, she said she would like to meet him on a possible trip to the Vatican, and the Church's representatives called her and proposed that she offer him a song. "Para mi es una maravilla poder regresar a mi tierra, a Zana, para poder cantar este Ave Maria de Schubert, que se lo quiero ofrecer con mucha admiracion y con mucho amor a mi tierra y al Papa, por supuesto." For me it is a marvel to be able to return to my land, to Zana, to be able to sing this Ave Maria by Schubert, which I want to offer with much admiration and with much love to my land and to the Pope, of course. The mechanism is the return as a circle: the girl who left Zana for Chiclayo, then Lima, then Mexico, then the world, comes back at 74 to sing in the sanctuary of her own town, for a pope who worked in her town, and after her Ave Maria the program includes a danza negra, a Black dance, so the sacred and the Afro-Peruvian close the same ceremony. The performance will be brief and conditioned by the Pope's agenda, and no full Mass will be celebrated in Zana, only a homily with set times, which is the mechanism's discipline: the song is one small, exact thing inside a timed ceremony, not a concert. Watch the circle: when the singer who left at seventeen comes back at seventy-four to sing one song in her hometown sanctuary, and the song is Schubert and the dance after it is Black, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** come home to sing one song. The circle is the career. Against the chain: Leon (#354 M2) also sang the day the city needed, but her day was the earthquake, a disaster that made her the right winner; Libertad's day is a homecoming, a pope in her hometown, which is why her version is a circle and Leon's is an event.

## M12. The name as the legacy

"Es sumamente halagador y me emociona profundamente. Me llevan a los conciertos a ninas que se llaman Tania, e incluso muchas que llevan mi nombre completo, Tania Libertad." It is extremely flattering and it moves me deeply. They bring me to the concerts girls who are called Tania, and even many who carry my full name, Tania Libertad. Libertad said it to El Comercio in October 2026, and the sentence is the mechanism: "Lograr que tu musica trascienda de esa manera, que se quede en el imaginario de la gente y que abuelos o padres se la hereden a sus hijos y nietos, es una de las satisfacciones mas grandes de mi carrera." To manage that your music transcends in that way, that it stays in people's imagination and that grandparents or parents bequeath it to their children and grandchildren, is one of the greatest satisfactions of my career. The mechanism is the name as the legacy: not the gold discs, not the twelve million copies, not the halls, but the girls named Tania, and the girls named Tania Libertad, brought to the concerts by the grandmothers who first heard her. The name was given to her by her parents in Zana, and the audience gave it back to their daughters, so the singer's name is now a Peruvian and Mexican name, carried by children who were not alive when she recorded her first disc. Watch the name: when a grandmother brings a little girl called Tania Libertad to the concert, and the singer on stage has the same name as the child in the audience, the mechanism is on. **Takeaway:** let the audience name their daughters after you. The name is the legacy. Against the chain: Boliver (#355 M13) also carried her family's history as her method, but her method was the rebozo and the muralist's studio, a place; Libertad's method is a name, which is why her version is a christening and Boliver's is a childhood.

## Evolution

1959-1980, the Peruvian child star: born in Zana, singing from the age of five while her older siblings looked after her and her mother worked long days from seven in the morning to nine at night; career begins in 1959 at seven, singing in radio contests; first disc at nine; moves to Lima in 1967 as a teenager after winning many prizes on Radio Chiclayo, her father becoming her manager; nightclubs, a contract with RCA Victor, the first national hit "La Contamanina"; still a girl, she sings on television and then hosts "Danzas y Canciones del Peru" for five years, the most important program on Peruvian television, learning her country's folklore from the inside, then her own program "Tania Presenta"; ten successful recordings; her father wants her to end her career after secondary school at the Colegio de La Inmaculada, and she convinces him to let her study Ingenieria Pesquera at the Universidad Nacional Tecnica del Callao, the same career as her brother, which she never practised and never regretted. 1980-1986, the restart: she moves to Mexico and lives there since 1980, starts from below, six years in schools, universities, prisons, hospitals, houses of culture and public plazas, off the mass media, becoming one of the most loved artists in the country. 1985-1998, the bolero years: the first Polygram boleros album in 1985, platinum for more than 400,000 copies in Mexico alone; "Nuevamente Boleros" in 1986; "Me Voy Pa' La Pachanga" with the orchestra of Irving Lara; "Trovadiccion" in 1987; "Mucho Corazon" in 1988; "Alfonsina y el Mar" in 1989, one of her best-selling discs; "Tania canta a Jose Alfredo Jimenez" in 1990, a duet with Vicente Fernandez; "Boleros hoy" in 1991, duets with Manzanero, Miguel Bose, Ivan Lins and Azucar Moreno; "Africa en America" in 1990/1994; the Manzanero albums, "La Libertad de Manzanero" in 1995/1998 and "Armando la Libertad" in 1997/1998. 2000-2004, the world-music years: the opera album in 2000/2001, platinum, more than a million copies internationally; "Costa Negra" in 2001, begun in Paris and finished in Senegal, with Cesaria Evora; "Negro Color" in 2004, no pre-recorded segments, no synthesizers, the most ambitious attempt to redefine Afro-Peruvian music. 2012-2016, the anniversaries: fifty years of artistic life celebrated in 2012 with two sold-out gala concerts at the Palacio de Bellas Artes with the Filarmonica de Acapulco and Manzanero as special guest, the album "Tania, 50 anos de Libertad" with 34 songs on two CDs and a DVD, the Auditorio Nacional on Friday 5 October 2012, the international tour ending at UNESCO headquarters in Paris; "Desarmando a Tania" in February 2014, the third and last Manzanero album; "Por Ti y Por Mi" in May 2015, recorded in an Argentine restaurant in Mexico City with a cellar, bars and billiards, three guitars and choruses, gold in weeks; Las Tres Grandes in Primera Fila, recorded June 2015, the Auditorio Nacional on Wednesday 25 November 2015, gold in 2016, the Latin Grammy nomination on Thursday 17 November 2016. 2019-2026, the late years: the solo debut at the Gran Teatro Nacional on Sunday 10 November 2019, the whole life in one set list; the Cervantino closing on Sunday 31 October 2021 before twenty thousand people; the Zocalo on Monday 21 March 2022; the Expo Dubai concerts on Tuesday 25 January and Sunday 30 January 2022; the Lunario on Saturday 24 February 2024; the 2026 album "Ay, amor!", produced and arranged by herself at home; the Gran Teatro Nacional gala on Saturday 24 October 2026, her seventy-fourth birthday, with the Banda Chica; the Bogota concert on Sunday 8 November 2026; the Zana Ave Maria on Saturday 14 November 2026; and the AI album in preparation, songs commissioned in lando, candombe, pasillo and chacarera with a modern touch. The arc is a circle with a passport: she left Peru famous, arrived in Mexico unknown, became famous again, and at seventy-four she is going home to sing one song.

## Aesthetic logic

Libertad's aesthetic is the voice as a passport, sung in one night across every border it has crossed: the vals criollo, the bolero, the trova, the ranchera, the mariachi, the opera aria, the lando, the candombe, the pasillo, the chacarera, and the scarcity is the argument, because a singer with no costume, no mask and no character has only the voice to hold the room, and the voice has to be high enough, and exact enough, to be believed in every genre. The materials are a soprano voice, a cajon, three guitars, a small band that sounds like eighty, a forbidden song, a traded verse, a map of the Black Coast, three voices in a chord, a set list that is a biography, a machine commissioned in lando, and a name given to little girls. The palette is the one the photographs give: the white sheer blouse and black jacket of tl-01 and tl-05 against the royal blue and gold of Los Tigres del Norte; the loose light blue of tl-02 under blue spotlights; the red-orange patterned blouse of tl-03 against blue and orange lights; the dark blue-green shimmering shawl of tl-04; the long blue floral dress with wide sheer sleeves of tl-06; the red blouse of tl-07 against dark blue; and the passion red of the Tres Grandes dresses that no photograph in this leg shows. Where Leon's logic was the whole country sung by one voice in many genres, and Orozco's was the whole country sung by one voice in many song-forms, and Pie's was the whole country interviewed by one dead woman, Libertad's is two countries sung by one voice in one night, and the difference is the passport: Leon changes genre per album, Orozco changes song-form per request, Pie changes title per show, and Libertad changes country per song, which is why her version is a set list and theirs are a discography, a jukebox and a poster.
