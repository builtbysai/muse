## Aesthetic logic

Mack Smith's aesthetic is the Englishman abroad with a document in his hand, and the document is always smaller than the story it undoes. Nothing in his work arrives as a system: the grand pronouncement is replaced by the smaller thing actually said, the heroic partnership is replaced by a bitter conflict, the parenthesis is replaced by a crack in the foundation, the 6,000-page biography is punctured by one dry sentence, and the piece that follows him must therefore be built from shrinkages, not from spectacles. The palette follows the same law: the archive in paper cream and printer's ink, the street-stall book in faded red and dust, the portrait gallery in gilt and shadow, the Mediterranean map in sea blue and sand, the TV studio in harsh white light, the one warm portrait in lamplight among the cooled ones. Colour must arrive as a verdict, the crowd staying lit while the figures above darken, the lone figure keeping its colour while the room changes, never as decoration laid on top. His time is the particular that refuses the general, so the loop must never generalise: the year must keep expanding, the document must keep being held up, the sentence must keep hanging, and the end must come from the one warm portrait, because after a career of demolition there is exactly one hero, and he is the exception that proves the rule.
