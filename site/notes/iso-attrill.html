Study date: 2026-11-01. ig-artist-study #261.

Site: @isoaattrill (2,923 followers, public, no verified badge; bio is a single emoji; site isoattrill.co.uk). Iso Attrill is a London filmmaker who makes playful short-form work for clients and brands (Netflix, Dr Martens, Givenchy Beauty, Atmos Magazine among her listed clients), writes and directs short fiction and documentary on environmental issues and community building, co-runs SparkBoom Productions with Colton Kempf, trained at UAL Camberwell then an NFTS Certificate in Filmmaking on a BFI Scholarship, teaches Film as an Associate Lecturer at UAL Lime Grove, and is a member of Trans+ On-Screen. Her public anchor in this study series is co-directing Nukuluk's "I Just Wanna Luv U" (2023) with Luke Kulukundis, where Mateo Villanueva Brandt was editor and 1st AD.

Her practice has one through-line and it is the lit stage inside the dark. Every register she works in puts a small, deliberate, handmade world against a surrounding void: the tuxedoed man on a rumpled bed floating in pure black in the I Just Wanna Luv U still, the face peeking out of total darkness in a commercial horror still, the cartoon girl standing on a crescent moon against an illustrated navy starfield, the palm of foraged things held up over the grass like a specimen tray. The darkness is never empty in her frames; it is the stage the small world plays on. Where her closest collaborators isolate one discipline, she keeps the client reel, the music video, the animated short, and the 35mm travel diary in the same hand, and the register shifts while the instinct stays put: arrange it small, light it honestly, let the person be themselves inside it.

M1. Void-stage theatricality

The signature mechanism, and the I Just Wanna Luv U still is the cleanest statement of it. f01 (2023): black and white, a tuxedoed man with a cravat and a wilting orchid boutonniere seated on a rumpled white bed, a nightstand with a glass and an alarm clock, and behind and around all of it, pure black. The bed is a set, the black is the theatre. Nothing in the frame pretends the room continues. f04 repeats the grammar in a music/fashion register: a split diptych of two portrait panels (one man gesturing in a white tee, one in a black tee with a chain, head bowed) laid on a black ground. The mechanism is exact: isolate the staged figure against a surround that refuses to be a location, and the refusal becomes the style. The watchable behavior is stagecraft without a stagehouse: the props (rumpled sheets, alarm clock, orchid) do all the world-building, and the void does all the focusing.

Takeaway: put the staged figure on a small lit set, drop the set into total black, let the props build the whole world.

M2. Miniature, hand-built worlds

The production mechanism behind the signature video, evidenced in text rather than in frames. The Still Listening interview with Kulukundis: the pair built a miniature child's bedroom for "I Just Wanna Luv U" that the full band could barely fit inside, stocked with 90s toys, working from Lynch/Kaufman/Gondry reference points, with production design by Matty Mancey Jones. The surviving visual trace is f01's bedroom set: the rumpled bed, the tiny nightstand, the alarm clock scaled like a dollhouse object. The mechanism matters because it is the shared instrument with the #260 study: Kulukundis built wrong-scale rooms to let adult bodies fail at fitting them; here the same build serves a black-and-white theatrical register, the miniature bedroom as a stage rather than a gag. Two directors, one built room, two different watchable behaviors.

Takeaway: build the room small and by hand, then choose whether the misfit is the joke or the stage.

M3. Cabinet-of-curiosities close-up

The documentary mechanism, and the Paradise stills are its thesis. f02/f03 (2025): a palm held up over grass, presenting foraged finds, a staghorn fungus, a twig skeleton, a citrus-peel ring, a seed, a tiny insect, with shells glued to the fingernails and a blue cuff at the wrist. Bright daylight, tactile, arranged like a specimen tray. The ecological argument of the film is made by arrangement, not narration: identity shown through the collection of found things. The mechanism is portraiture by inventory. The person is never separated from what they gather, and the gathering is composed, the palm is a display case. This is the daytime answer to the void-stage: same instinct to isolate and arrange, but the surround is the garden and the light is the sun.

Takeaway: show the person through the things they gather, arranged on the open palm like a specimen tray.

M4. Chiaroscuro peek

The commercial mechanism, and the most economical frame in the corpus. f06 (2023): half a face emerging from near-total black, one wide eye catching the light, the rest swallowed. Horror/thriller register, extreme chiaroscuro, the frame edge of darkness used like a curtain the face is pushing through. Where M1 stages the whole figure in the void, M4 admits only a fragment: the darkness is no longer the stage but the thing being looked out from. The watchable behavior is the held half-reveal, the eye that stays a second longer than comfort allows. It is the grammar of the fashion and horror client work, and it sits at the opposite end of her practice from the bright commercial register, which is proof the same lighting instinct serves both.

Takeaway: use the darkness as a curtain, admit half a face, hold the eye a second longer than comfort allows.

M5. Hand-drawn night-sky collage

The animation register, and the outlier that proves the range. f05/f07 (2023): "The Moonflower", a 2D cartoon-collage short. A figure in a pink dress stands on a face-crescent moon against an illustrated navy starfield; the title card sets rounded serif type on the same starfield. Flat, whimsical, child-story register, no camera at all, and yet the same compositional instinct as the void-stage: a small lit figure against an infinite dark ground, the moon doing the work the bed did in f01. The mechanism is collage as staging: cut-out figure, illustrated sky, the dark doing the focusing. It is also the only work in the corpus where the handmade quality is literal, drawn rather than built.

Takeaway: collage the small figure onto the infinite dark, let the illustrated sky do the focusing.

M6. Film-grain travel diary

The parallel photographic practice, and the largest single block of the corpus. f10-f19: ten film photographs from the Cargo manifest, scan-style filenames (R1-05746-0018, numbered roll frames), all in the same Kodak-color, long-shadow, Americana-roadside language: bathers in glittering water shot top-down (f10), a ferris wheel behind a puffer jacket (f11), lacrosse from a high angle (f12), a B&W rock texture near-abstract (f13), a bronze statue with pigeons in flight (f14), two women talking down a stairwell (f15), a Badlands road at dusk (f16), beach snapshots (f17), the "Golden West CAFE" roadside sign with a red car and white barn (f18), Joshua trees at dusk with one silhouetted figure (f19). The mechanism is the observational diary as location scouting and personal work in one: shoot the ordinary on film, keep the grain, keep going. It is the same discipline as the point-and-shoot diary in the #260 study, which is suggestive given the shared crew graph, but the evidence here is only the images, no captions or dates of travel, so the study claims the images, not the habit.

Takeaway: shoot the ordinary roadside on film, keep the grain and the long shadows, date nothing, keep going.

M7. Playful commercial brightness

The client register, and the deliberate opposite of the void-stage. f08 (2023): a fashion-ad still, salmon-pink shirt, sunglasses, a man leaning on motorcycle handlebars against a painted alpine backdrop, stylized and saturated. f09 (2024): a young girl in a purple floral raincoat looking through binoculars in a bright park, which also opens the "Iso Attrill Showreel (2024)". Optimistic, colorful, outdoors, people mid-gesture. The mechanism is brightness as professionalism: the same director who drops a tuxedoed man into total black can light a park scene like a cereal ad, and the About page's client list (Netflix, Dr Martens, Givenchy Beauty, Atmos) says this register pays. The watchable behavior is uncomplicated joy, which is harder than it looks and is the reason the commercial reel leads with the binoculars girl.

Takeaway: when the brief is joy, light it like a cereal ad and put the gesture mid-motion.

M8. Queer-ecology portraiture

The documentary thesis, and the mechanism the whole study points toward. "Paradise" (2025, five minutes): a poetic portrait of Mol Benge, a trans/non-binary artist-gardener and Attrill's friend, framed by the festival copy as "meditations on creativity, queerness and ecology" and by the Short Focus catalogue as identity "standing against erasure". The garden is the instrument: Benge navigates the world through it, and the film reads queerness and ecology through each other, never separated. The f02/f03 foraged-palm still is the visual thesis: the hand in the grass, the gathered things, the shells on the nails. The mechanism is portraiture where the subject's identity and their patch of land are the same shot. It played the 9th Short Focus festival, the 16th Peckham and Nunhead Free Film Festival (covered by BBC News), and LandxSea in Montrose as that festival's 100th film since 2023, with a live Q&A. The mechanism earns its place last because it is the synthesis: the void-stage instinct turned inside out, the small world now a real garden, the arranged things now actually foraged, the person actually themselves.

Takeaway: never separate the person from their patch of land; let the garden do the portraiture.

Evolution

  • Training: the art-school route. UAL Camberwell College of Arts, then an NFTS Certificate in Filmmaking on a BFI Scholarship. The earliest portfolio asset is dated 2021; the NFTS-era work itself was not located.
  • 2023: the breakout year. Co-directs Nukuluk's "I Just Wanna Luv U" (March, black-and-white, miniature bedroom, Lynch/Kaufman/Gondry references); makes the animated short "The Moonflower"; shoots the f06 horror-register still and the f08 fashion-ad still. Four registers in one year, all still in the portfolio.
  • 2024: the commercial register. Cuts the "Iso Attrill Showreel (2024)"; the binoculars-girl park scene (f09) becomes the reel's opener and the emblem of the bright client work.
  • 2025: the documentary turn. "Paradise" (five minutes), the Mol Benge garden portrait; the foraged-palm stills become the film's visual signature.
  • 2026: the festival run. Paradise plays Short Focus (Programme Two), the Peckham and Nunhead Free Film Festival (16th edition, Peckhamplex, BBC News coverage), and LandxSea Montrose (Scottish premiere, the festival's 100th film, live Q&A with Attrill).
  • Present: the dual practice. Associate Lecturer in Film at UAL Lime Grove; co-runs SparkBoom Productions with Colton Kempf; Trans+ On-Screen member; client roster spanning Netflix, Atmos, Givenchy Beauty, Dr Martens.

The arc: art-school training, a breakout year that established four registers at once (theatrical B&W, animation, horror-commercial, fashion-commercial), a showreel that consolidated the commercial lane, then a documentary turn that fused the staging instinct with a real garden and a real person. The through-line never moves: the small lit world against the surrounding dark, arranged by hand, with the person being themselves inside it.

Aesthetic logic

Everything Iso Attrill makes is a small, lit, handmade world set against a dark that refuses to be a location. The black void of the music video, the navy starfield of the animation, the curtain of darkness the half-face pushes through, the garden at the edge of the foraged palm: the dark is the stage, the instrument, and finally the subject. She works the full budget range without changing the instinct. A tuxedoed man on a rumpled bed in total black and a girl with binoculars in a bright park are the same director solving the same problem: arrange the world small, light it honestly, put the person inside it, and let the surround do the focusing. The playfulness is the tell. Where the void-stage could go solemn, she keeps it theatrical, costumed, a little absurd: a wilting orchid boutonniere, a painted alpine backdrop, shells glued to fingernails. The craft is serious and the mood is a game. Paradise is where the game turns sincere without changing the rules: the arranged things are actually foraged, the small world is actually a garden, the person is actually themself, and the dark around the lit palm is simply the rest of the world, out of focus.
