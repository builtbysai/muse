Core engine

SET UP A SYSTEM, THEN LET GO: build a bounded machine (a modular rack, a walkable organ, a three-inventor stage, a four-hour set), patch it, and then react to what it does instead of conducting it. His own words: "you prepare a lot and you set up a system or a setting where you can let yourself go and just react to what is happening." The modular synth "totally controls me by its boundaries and possibilities, and everyday it's completely different." One hand runs club techno as Efdemin, the other runs sound art as Phillip Sollmann; since about 2014 the two worlds have been merging into one practice.

Evolution

b. 1974 Kassel. Mid-90s: alternative rock band Derrick (Hamburg); trained cellist. Late 90s: duo Tobin with Alexander Polzin (one 12", picked for Kompakt Total 1); co-founds Dial Records (Hamburg) with David Lieske, the "Autoren-Techno" label (home to Pantha Du Prince, Lawrence). 2002: studies electroacoustic music, sound art, contemporary composition at the University of Music and Performing Arts Vienna (Institute for Computer Music): sound installations, microtonality, musique concrete. 2004: debut Efdemin EP Kleine Wirrniss (Dial). 2005: moves to Berlin; Berghain residency begins and never ends. Duo Pigon with Oliver Kargl; drone album under his own name 2006. 2007: Efdemin debut LP (Dial), analog plus electronic plus bells plus natural sounds. 2008: Carry On mix CD (Curle). 2010: Chicago (Dial). 2013: Autotune project with Konrad Sprenger begins. 2014: Decay (Dial), assembled in Kyoto from Berlin sketches; "very linear, long drawn-out shapes that don't really have a beginning or an end." 2017: Monophonie premiered at the Volksbuehne (Ensemble Musikfabrik); Modular Organ System with Konrad Sprenger begins (Kestner Gesellschaft Hannover). 2018: Panama/Suez trio with Oren Ambarchi and Sprenger (A-Ton); Naif 29-track mix album (Curle). 2019: New Atlantis (Ostgut Ton), first overtly concept-driven Efdemin LP. 2020: Monophonie album; MOS at KW Institute Berlin. 2022: CTM silent green Betonhalle, 10-day MOS run with daily interventions. 2023: MOS LP (choose440, ed. 300, liner notes Arnold Dreyblatt, cover Dan Solbach). 2024: Kalkül der Form with Max Eilbacher (CTM at Berghain; St. Gertrud Cologne; NAK Aachen). 2025: Poly (Ostgut Ton, OSTGUTLP38, 5th studio album, 11 tracks); Philara Duesseldorf MODULAR ORGAN exhibition (18.10.2025-25.01.2026). 2026: Mirror Phase EP (Dekmantel, analogue studio jams).

Mechanisms (each: what it IS, how it shows as watchable behavior)

M1: The system plays the player. The Berlin studio ("the meadow") is photographed as the portrait: walls of modular synths, multicolored patch cables, a wooden binaural dummy head listening on top of the rack (f00). His caption: "Under the influence of my modular system. Lost in sound..." (f01). Watchable behavior: cables re-patched slowly by unseen hands, drones shifting as routings change, the human reduced to a listener inside his own machine.

M2: Three dead inventors, one stage. Monophonie unites Harry Bertoia (sounding sculpture), Harry Partch (microtonal instruments), Hermann von Helmholtz (double siren) in a single composition for Ensemble Musikfabrik. All sound generators are exclusively mechanical, yet the music "repeatedly achieves the impression of electronics." A color spectrum traverses the stage during the piece "to paraphrase the microscopically small musical developments." Watchable behavior: three material stations sounding without players while a slow band of color maps changes too small to hear at first.

M3: The loop is ancient. His Robert Johnson lecture "100 Years of Techno": repetitive music predates techno by a century (Helmholtz acoustics, Raymond Scott, the 1931 Rhythmicon, Moog, Buchla, Stockhausen, musique concrete, Xenakis, Laurie Spiegel, Morton Subotnick, Ethiopian ritual patterns "so much like Detroit techno"). His hand-drawn white-on-dark canon map runs Kraftwerk to Chicago to Detroit to Techno to Rave, annotated with 808, 303, KLF, Acid, Shoom (f03). Watchable behavior: one pattern traveling a drawn timeline, changing materials at each node but never changing its shape.

M4: Bacon's sound-house. New Atlantis takes Francis Bacon's unfinished 1627 utopia and its "sound-houses, where we practise and demonstrate all sounds, and their generation... We have harmonies which you have not, of quarter-sounds, and lesser slides of sounds." The album answers with just intonation, wooden percussion, electric hurdy-gurdy, a motor-controlled guitar (Sprenger's), and a Charles Wesley hymn ("Oh, lovely appearance of death") sung by visual artist William T. Wiley. Watchable behavior: a building of small rooms, each tuned a quarter-tone off the last, a 270-year-old melody drifting through and bending room by room.

M5: Phasing without end. The Decay opener, his description: "an abstract, droney loop where it's just about shifting frequencies and some phasing, and that's basically it." His sequencers are fetish objects: a vintage unit labeled PRIME and UNCAL in red LED (f02), a circular step-sequencer UI on a tablet with concentric colored rings (f10). Watchable behavior: two near-identical rings turning at near-identical speeds, coincidences flashing rarely, the slip itself as the content.

M6: Ten sines in formation. Kalkül der Form (2024, with Max Eilbacher of Horse Lords): multichannel digital synthesis from "ten sine wave" generators in geometric configuration, housed in tall white sculptural towers in churches and galleries (f12). Watchable behavior: ten white towers in a room, each holding one pure tone, interference beating drifting audibly between them as phases shift.

M7: The four-hour tide. Frieze on his craft: keeping a dance floor energized over a four-hour DJ set. His sets sound like his records: long rippling shapes with a dull gleam, tracks given room to breathe, energy sculpted, never peaked. Watchable behavior: a dark field of small lights breathing in one slow tide; no drops, no breakdowns, intensity as weather.

M8: The untranslated panel. The Subharchord, a rare East German subharmonic synth ("little brother of the Mixturtrautonium"), turquoise panel, toggle switches labeled OBOE, FRANZ-KRUMMHORN, FAGOTT, TROMP, POSAUNE, KLARON, SAX, KORNETT, filter sliders below, his reflection in the glass above (f06, Akademie der Künste 2021). Watchable behavior: switches flipped by unseen fingers, each German label summoning a different subharmonic stack, the labels never translated.
