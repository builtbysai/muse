Core engine

Tune the whole world to the overtones of one string: stretch piano wire across rebuilt instruments, never fret or shorten it, keep the fundamental fixed while a steady bowing pulse shakes a 20-tone just-intonation system into shimmering, rock-loud harmonic storms; and keep every concert, archive, and text installation running the same rule: the system is declared in advance, then performed.

Mechanisms (what each IS, how it shows up as watchable behavior)

M1. The Excited String. #12 and #11 unwound music wire stretched on an adapted double bass body (40.5" speaking length); brushing and striking open strings with the bow coaxes a complex chord of the overtone series. His own 1989 text: adapted with piano wire, first concert 1979. Watchable: one long string always visible at full length; hands brush, strike, bow; sound blooms above a root that never moves.

M2. The Nodal Regions. His 1979 Nodal Excitation notes: a shorter speaking length is NEVER created by stopping or fretting; instead harmonic partial vibrations are calculated, coaxed, and occasionally isolated at the nodes of the string; the integrity of the fundamental vibration is maintained at all times; all movement of pitch occurs in the overtone structure. Watchable: a touch point slides along the string like a surveyor's mark and each node station releases a different partial chord; the root does not budge.

M3. The Bowing Pulse as Clock. Early work used a steady pulse provided by the bowing motion itself (Wikipedia); the downtown scene tagged him "the most rock-oriented of American minimalists" (ISSUE Project Room). His own Mudd Club story (1989): Rhys Chatham warned "you're dead without drums"; he played 25 minutes anyway. Watchable: the bow arm IS the metronome; the whole ensemble locks to the arm's stroke; drone becomes backbeat.

M4. The 20-Tone Tuning System. His own liner notes for The Adding Machine (Cantaloupe): all instruments perform in the 20-tone per octave just intoned microtonal scale derived from the overtone series. Applies to restrung cimbaloms, piano wire basses, hurdy gurdys, refretted guitars with magnetic driver-sustainers. Watchable: a 20-slot octave grid with unequal just-intonation spacing; every note lands on a slot, never between; changing key keeps the same fundamental (Bandcamp Daily on Resolve).

M5. The Orchestra as Rebuildable Machine. Ensembles under the title "The Orchestra of Excited Strings" since 1979: NYC downtown lineup (two adapted basses, retuned miniature piano, hurdy gurdy, drums) -> Berlin from 1984 -> 2023 all-star rebuild (Oren Ambarchi, Konrad Sprenger, Joachim Schuetz; Resolve, Drag City) -> Tokyo 2025 (Jim O'Rourke, Eiko Ishibashi). Watchable: same chassis, interchangeable players; group portraits in front of the same dark curtain decades apart (f10).

M6. Memory as Archive (the visual strand). Wikipedia: his installations create "complex textual and spatial metaphors for memory" as "a media discourse on recollection and the archive." Works: Archive Carousel (77 Akademie archive objects displayed in 28 variations, modeled on Cage's Rolywholyover A Circus, 2021, f05/f06), Repertoire (membership database as media installation, f00), Gedachtnispalast / Memory Palace: A Living Archive (Schloss Leuk 2018, f01), Lesezeichen (layered identity text on backlit lightboxes, Jewish Museum Franken 2023, f11), Who's Who (biographical data), Memory Arena, Innocent Questions (Oslo 2006, sculpture laid out like an IBM punch card). Watchable: the archive performs itself; objects rotate through catalogued variations; text layers until it becomes texture.

M7. Turntable History / Spin Ensemble (2011). His own recordings of an MRI scanner (Siemens Magnetom Symphony, Berlin radiology practice) treated as a giant Tesla coil: the alignment and resonances of a powerful magnetic field gradually altered by rotating radio frequencies; recordings deconstructed and grouped by pitch, rhythm, density (ISSUE Project Room). Watchable: an alien machine's signals sorting into three visible streams; the machine composes.

M8. Lapse (1975, stroboscopic video). Early video work, still screened (f14: projected at Epsilon Spires 2024): stroboscopic light breaks the analog image into standing wave interference patterns (purple/gold ripples in the frame). Watchable: a strobe freezes motion into wavelets; the image is a beat frequency between light and movement.

Evolution

1953 born NYC (mother Lucille Wallenrod, painter) -> 1970s Wesleyan/Buffalo: media art with Steina and Woody Vasulka; music with Pauline Oliveros, La Monte Young, Alvin Lucier; downtown NYC scene (Arthur Russell, Rhys Chatham, Julius Eastman, Phill Niblock circles) -> 1979: first excited-strings concert, Mudd Club gig, Orchestra of Excited Strings formed; Nodal Excitation composed -> 1982: Nodal Excitation LP (India Navigation); Wesleyan MA thesis "Nodal Excitation" -> 1984: moves to Berlin -> 1986: Propellers in Love (HatART) -> 1990s: Jim O'Rourke-led revival; Animal Magnetism (Tzadik 1995); Drag City reissues -> 1998: The Sound of One String (Table of the Elements) -> 2000s: Adding Machine (Cantaloupe 2002), Lapse (2004), Innocent Questions Oslo sculpture (2006), Akademie der Künste elected 2007, Resonant Relations (2008), Turntable History (Important 2011) -> 2013: Appalachian Excitation with Megafaun (Northern Spy) -> 2021: Archive Carousel -> 2022: Black Mountain College as Multiverse 560-page book (co-edited with Petra Maria Meyer, f08) -> 2023: Resolve (Drag City, first new Orchestra music in 20+ years) -> 2025: Yale Oral History of American Music interview (Sept 20); frkwys "extended field" with Horse Lords (RVNG, Nov 21); Tokyo Orchestra concert with O'Rourke and Ishibashi (Dec 18) -> 2026: Descendants for four organs (Unsounds, Orgelpark Amsterdam); reverse_time 3-channel video at Jewish Museum Berlin (Sept 2026 - Jan 2027, f18).
