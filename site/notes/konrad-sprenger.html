Core engine

The instrument is a programmable machine: strings and pipes are treated as frequency generators set in motion by computer-controlled mechanical action, so the composition lives in the tuning, the rhythm algorithm, and the moving air, never in a performer's hands; a rigorous acoustics science coexists with a pop sensibility and a sly sense of humor.

Mechanisms (what each IS, how it shows up as watchable behavior)

M1. Strings as Frequency Generators. The computer-controlled multi-channel electric guitar: strings tuned and set into motion by electronically controlled mechanical actions; instrument conceived by Sprenger, built by Sukandar Kartadinata and Daniel van den Eijkel (konradsprenger.com/solo). He contributes "overtone-rich motorized guitar" to Oren Ambarchi's Hubris (2016). Watchable: mechanical fingers or strikers hit strings while a pattern readout advances; the instrument plays itself and nobody touches it.

M2. Euclidean Rhythms. Rhythmic patterns based on the Euclidean algorithm, meant "to evoke a kind of cognitive insecurity through metrical dissonance" (konradsprenger.com/solo; berlinerfestspiele). The music relates to "the insistent rhythms of Minimalism, Krautrock and Techno, and their shared focus on transcendence through propulsive, full-spectrum sound." Watchable: strikes distributed unevenly around a cycle; the eye expects a grid and gets an irregular one that still grooves; the downbeat keeps slipping sideways.

M3. The Organ as a Walkable Room. The Modular Organ System with Phillip Sollmann (begun 2017): tubes, air pumps, vibrating reeds, ceramic and papier-mache pipe objects (made with Nico Ihlein), computer-controlled air flow and non-tempered fine tuning, displayed in the space "as if the instrument were a sculpture," reconfigured from venue to venue (sollmann-sprenger.com; after.berlin; ctm-festival.de). LP on Choose (choose440, 2023). Watchable: the room IS the instrument; visitors walk among the pipes (f02: pale fiberglass double horn on a stand under a fluorescent grid; f07: graduated copper pipe rack with a blower unit and a brass horn on a plinth in a concrete hall); the layout changes every venue.

M4. The Acoustic/Digital Blur. Set's Black Truffle copy: much of his production uses "midi-controlled sampled instrumentation," so "it's anyone's guess where the acoustic ends and the digital begins." Watchable: particles drawn half as plucked strings, half as glitch pixels; sources you can never quite pin down.

M5. Skittering Polyrhythmic Pointillism. Set described as "skittering polyrhythmic framework calling up the detail-rich percussive constructions of contemporary techno filtered through the pointillism of the post-serialist European avant-garde"; "at once propulsive and fragmented, essentially static in form yet ever-changing in detail." Watchable: an almost static overall frame whose micro-events never repeat; macro form holds, detail never does.

M6. Mechanical-Music Lineage. His Threads Radio Recorded Research mix: an hour of music made by machines, "not electronic but mechanical," player pianos, Conlon Nancarrow studies, Ligeti's Poeme Symphonique for 100 metronomes, a French Bontems automaton (ca. 1800); his thesis: Pianola and Orchestrion were "early computer systems for creating unique combinations of pitches" (hhv.de). Watchable: paper rolls and pinned cylinders; mechanical fingers playing pitch combinations no human could.

M7. The Miniature as Form. Miniaturen (2006, Choose): 32 tracks in 35 minutes, sketches collected over ten years, didactic game-like etudes (Stylus review: turning "Scarborough Fair" into a piano exercise). Watchable: short forms in quick succession, each a different mechanism, none overstaying.

M8. Science Plus Pop Plus Sly Humor. Black Truffle: "a scientific approach to acoustic phenomena co-exists with a pop sensibility and a sly sense of humour." Watchable: a rigorous system that suddenly does something funny; the corpus itself gives the example (f13: a reel captioned "having fun with #supercollider" thumbnailed on four meerkats standing upright in grassland).

Evolution

1977 born Lahr, Germany (Joerg Hiller) -> since 1997 builds mechanical string and wind instruments, incl. a bass banjo -> 1999 collaboration with the Honey-Suckle Company begins (Neu Westend at PS1 NY 1999; Eswerde Stuttgart 2003: three mechanically revolving harps and a room-sized hurdy-gurdy inspired by Ellen Fullman's Long String Instrument) -> 2000s: bands Ethnostress, Ei, Rom; regular work with Arnold Dreyblatt begins; co-produces Ellen Fullman's Ort (2004); founds Choose and produces recordings by Fullman, Dreyblatt, Robert Ashley, Terry Fox -> 2006 Miniaturen (Choose) -> 2009 Versprochen (Schoolmap, 300 copies) -> since 2009 core member of Dreyblatt's Orchestra of Excited Strings -> 2016 motorized guitar on Ambarchi's Hubris -> 2017 Stack Music (PAN): palindromic suite for the computer-controlled guitar, "Opening" recorded at Phill Niblock's Intermedia Foundation Loft; Modular Organ System with Phillip Sollmann begins -> 2018 Panama/Suez EP (A-Ton) with Ambarchi and Sollmann -> 2022 Monheim Triennale signature project (Sollmann + Sprenger + Ambarchi: hypnotic groove, Krautrock, guitar swarms); MOS 10-day installation at CTM, Silent Green Betonhalle -> 2023 Modular Organ System LP (Choose) -> 2025 Set (Black Truffle BT127, Jan 17): with the "mysterious Ensemble Risonanze Moderne," guest Oren Ambarchi on side 2 (18:01 / 15:21) -> Nov-Dec 2025 Japan cluster (IG corpus): Yodobashi Church Sonic Experience with Bill Kouligas, Florian Hecker, Midori Takada; Orchestra of Excited Strings at Goethe-Institut Tokyo (Dec 18); Dreyblatt/Sprenger duo in Fukuoka -> 2025-2026 MODULAR ORGAN walk-through installation at the Philara Collection, Duesseldorf (three months, opening Oct 18 2025).
