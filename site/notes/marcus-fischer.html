Study #230: Marcus Fischer (2026-10-31, cron expansion)

studied 2026-10-31/8 min read/1,593 words

Account: @marcusfischer, display name "marcus fischer", bio "sound/art/photography / @12k_label artist / 2024 Hallie Ford Fellow in the Visual Arts". Public, not verified, 10,846 followers. b. 1977, Torrance CA; Portland, Oregon-based. Former LA punk drummer turned 12k ambient musician and interdisciplinary artist; day job in the photography industry as a commercial stylist. Sites: mapmap.ch, dustbreeding.com. Research: 922 own IG posts pulled via instagram-cli (2015-2026; 2021-23 silent, real gap), 20 frames stratified and visually inspected, web leg: A Closer Listen 2017 interview, 12k Twine press notes, MacDowell 2025, Ford Family Foundation, Oregon Contemporary, Converge 45.

Core engine

Two or more loops of identical analog material are played back at different lengths and speeds, so the same sounds drift out of phase with themselves and keep re-arriving changed; studio records, live sets, 40-foot ceiling loops, and speaker installations are all this one machine at different scales.

Evolution

  • 2015 (early, dense posting): the home-studio daily practice. Tape loop + guitar + modular tableaux ("sounds on sounds", "loop weaving" metallophone + Nagra loops), Dust Breeding audio posts. The Deupree collaboration intensifies: "night music (tape loop session w/ @taylor12k)" 2015-07-22, "input: piano / output: 1/4 inch tape" sessions 2015-07-24, Twine recorded and released Oct 30 2015. The account is a process diary: gear photos as windows into the process.
  • 2016: the loop becomes sculpture. "ribbons" (fresh tape loop for the tape echo, tape draped in scalloped swags through pulleys and guides), "loom" (two 35-40 ft loops strung from ground floor to 2nd floor at SIX, held by bars and helium balloons, 10 tracks mixed to 6-channel surround). Cassette-looping workshops at S1. The loop has a shape now, not just a sound.
  • 2017: the Loss year. Rauschenberg Residency (Captiva, Florida, Jan 2017, during the Trump inauguration): sound-on-sound generation-loss loops, cast-brass tuning forks (casting inaccuracies as micro-variability), "untitled (words of concern)" (a 3-minute loop of fellow residents' voices played all inauguration day). Loss released Sept 1 on 12k ("loss through sound degradation, reverberant spaces"). Lowlands (IIKKI, with Deupree and Ester Vonplon's photos), the first Deupree collaboration done entirely by correspondence.
  • 2018: Shape Memory with Simon Scott (Slowdive) on 12k, cover photos by Taylor Deupree. Field-recording/nature register deepens; natural found object + piece of gear becomes his album-art method (the commercial stylist's eye).
  • 2019: On Falling (self-released letterpress covers, then 12k reissue); Whitney Biennial (two sound works + two performances, sole Pacific Northwest artist).
  • 2020: leap-day cello+vibraphone performance with Ted Laderas; first of two Ryuichi Sakamoto collaborations.
  • 2021-2023: account silent. Mass developed at the Bemis Center residency; solo show "What Was Lost and What Remains" at Oregon Contemporary (Nov 2023 to Feb 2024).
  • 2024: institutional turn. Hallie Ford Fellowship in the Visual Arts (announced July 30; recognized as a VISUAL artist). First Buchla Easel live set (Synth Library Portland).
  • 2025: residencies (MacDowell, Headlands); Mass documented on IG (Feb 2025); group show "Trajectories" at Oregon Contemporary.
  • 2026: Converge 45 (curated by Lumi Tan): "Reliquary at the Veil" (two vintage tape recorders sharing one loop of a slowed Swiss folk song around a reliquary of his late father's objects) and "We Are the Confluence of Two Rivers" (tape looped between two columns). Oct 2026: "the temple bell stops but i still hear the sound coming out of the flowers" (analog tape machines, audio tape, speakers, wire, hemp cord, wood).

Arc summary: 2015-2020 = the studio-table tape-loop practice documented as daily process photography; 2021 onward = the same machine moved into public space as sculpture and installation, with institutional recognition marking the transition. The mechanism never changed; the scale and venue did.

Key mechanisms (what each IS + watchable behavior)

  1. Asynchronous dual loops (the Twine engine). Two mono tape loops of DIFFERENT lengths recorded onto separate reel-to-reels, played back together so identical material constantly drifts in and out of phase. Watchable: two spools turning at imperceptibly different rates, the same motif echoing itself with a slowly changing delay, the pair "twining" like two knots (the album title's stated image). Frames: f01, f02; 12k Twine press notes.
  2. Sound-on-sound generation loss. Re-recording onto the same tape pass after pass; each pass adds a new sound and partially erases the last, unpredictably. Watchable: layers that fade a little with every cycle while new marks land on top, ghosts bleeding through, overlapping micro-rhythms appearing from the residue. His quote: "with each pass over the recording head a new sound is introduced and the previous generation starts to slowly disappear." Frames: f07 (Loss).
  3. Long-loop architecture. Tape loops 35-40 ft long strung across rooms and floors, held by bars and helium balloons, feeding multi-channel systems (SIX: 10 tracks to 6 speakers). Watchable: thin lines stretched through a whole building like guy-wires, loop length made spatial, the room becoming the instrument's body. Frames: f04 ("loom").
  4. Speed-shift pitch transformation. Record at the fastest tape speed, play back at the slowest, dropping pitch by several octaves: "something I do a lot of... all over Twine". Watchable: a single element slowly sinking and stretching downward while remaining itself, big slow motion from small fast input. Source: A Closer Listen interview (glockenspiel breakdown).
  5. Speakers as exposed sculptural instruments. Bare drivers hung from ceilings (f18, f19), speakers on concrete cylinders topped with bowls of bullet casings (Mass, f17), built-in reel-to-reel speakers captured by room mics. Watchable: objects visibly vibrating; no cabinets, no hidden sound source: "the audience always gets to see where those sounds are coming from." Source: A Closer Listen; f17, f18, f19.
  6. Data sonification (Mass). Gun Violence Archive 2022 data plotted to charts, charts used as graphic scores; 12 channels where each speaker's movement matches the frequency and intensity of shootings in its month. Watchable: months-as-movements with bursts proportional to real events; the viewer "feels it in their body." Source: Oregon Contemporary; f17.
  7. System-to-improvise-within (live method). Before every performance he fixes a toolset (instruments + effects + one sound-layering device), starts with nothing, builds layers up and takes them down, everything improvised. Watchable: an empty stage gradually acquiring voices, each from a visible object; the performer reacting more than driving. His quotes: "I always start with nothing and slowly build up and take down layers of sound"; "Prior to the performance I typically decide on a system to improvise within." Frame: f16 (first-time Buchla live set).
  8. Brutal subtractive editing (studio method). Additive improvisation in long takes, then "an almost brutal subtractive editing process": cutting until nothing more can be removed, keeping the imperfections ("the most interesting parts"). Watchable: a composition that accumulates density then thins itself to essentials; only non-redundant elements survive. Source: A Closer Listen; f13 (10-year revision with "slightly expanded sound").
  9. Words as loops (vocal looping). At the Rauschenberg residency he recorded fellow artists listing their concerns and ran it as a 3-minute tape loop all inauguration day ("untitled (words of concern)"). Watchable: human voices cycling, layering into a slow crowd; the political spoken into the machine that usually sings. Source: A Closer Listen; IG reel 2024-11-06.
  10. Room-as-reverb instrument. Recording spaces chosen for natural acoustics (Rauschenberg main studio: a handclap rings ~7 echoes before dissolving; he took impulse responses but "it just doesn't sound the same"); Twine captured by miking the reel-to-reels' built-in speakers with room mics. Watchable: a big empty white room where every gesture visibly and audibly decays; reverb as an envelope around action. Source: A Closer Listen (Loss room description); f02.
