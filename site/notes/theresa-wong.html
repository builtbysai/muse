Core engine

A MICROSCOPE SET TO AN OBJECT. Every work asks one question, her words: "What CAN the cello sound like?" She rediscovers the cello as wood and string and hair, even as a tree, through amplification, string preparation, percussive use of the wooden body, and alternative tunings (just intonation, often centered on A=432Hz). The voice gets the same treatment: breath, clicks, slurps, and sung tones treated as raw material. And crucially she sings WHILE playing, so one body emits two signal paths that merge into a hybrid third voice: "timbral merging of the voice and cello, giving birth to new acoustically synthesized sounds." The instability of improvisation is layered on top of very precise harmonic realms. Her notation is graphic and sparse (point fields, arcs, fingering timelines), a visual artifact of the same inquiry.

Evolution (early vs recent)

  • Origins, design fusion: B.S. product design (Stanford), graphic design in Vienna, one-year fellowship at Benetton's Fabrica research centre in Treviso, Italy. The Fabrica years are the pivot: classical training fuses with design thinking, and performance becomes an inquiry into materials. MFA at Mills College follows (teachers: Fred Frith, Joan Jeanrenaud, Alvin Curran, Joelle Leandre, Annie Gosfield).
  • 2009-2011, song cycles and theater: "O Sleep", an improvised opera for an 8-piece ensemble on the conundrum of sleep and dream life (Southern Exposure, 2010). "The Unlearning" (2009-2011, Tzadik 2011): 21 songs for violin, cello and two voices inspired by Goya's "Disasters of War" etchings (with Carla Kihlstedt), premiered 2013 at Roulette, featured 2016 New Frequencies Festival. "Meet Me At The Future Garden" (2009): two voices plus eight intonarumori with the Magik*Magik Orchestra for Performa 09's "Music for 16 Futurist Noise Intoners". "Xenoglossia": live and pre-recorded voice as soundtrack to Jonathon Keats' plant documentary "Strange Skies" (Berkeley Art Museum). The mode here is narrative and literary: scores for stories, bodies on stages.
  • 2015-2020, the duo and the instrument-body: Playing with Ellen Fullman since the 2006 Roulette workshop; "Harbors" premieres 2015 and the duo album releases on Room40 in 2020 (Wire top 50). Quintillions Green (2015, Otoroku): improvisations with Fred Frith and John Butcher. Layered Events (2016, Important): with Soren Kjaergaard. Founding fo'c'sle in 2018 (inaugural releases: Fullman with David Gamper and Stuart Dempster, Chris Brown, powerdove, Lijiang Quintet). Crossing A River By Rope (2018, fo'c'sle): with Lao Dan, Deng Boyu, Li Xing, John McCowen. Practicing Sands (fo'c'sle, 2022): her first album of pure cello and voice, no narrative armature. The arc bends from story toward material.
  • 2020-2022, installation and site: "As We Breathe" (2020): an INSTALLED song commissioned by Long Beach Opera for the 2020 Songbook, with David Castilyo; a song dispersed into a space rather than staged. "O Horizon": an improvised performance where audience members bring objects of any kind as instruments; combined with cello and voice it becomes a ritual "bringing awareness into the sensual microcosm of everyday materials" (the O horizon is the forest-floor layer where decomposition transforms fallen matter). 2022: inaugural Sound/scapes artist at the SF Asian Art Museum, three site-specific performances inspired by collection artworks; "In The Cave of Guanyin" for cello and voice amplified through four channels in Samsung Hall (the ancestor of the 2025 album). "Fluency of Trees" (Other Minds 2022): solo cello and voice "exploring connection to the primal sentience of the natural world". "Tuyshtak" (2022): for six Bb clarinets in just intonation (Jeff Anderle, Helia Collective); the score is a fingering-timeline with cents deviations, a microtonal choreography.
  • 2023-2026, maximal resonance and the opera: "Soundless" premieres 2023 (Art Gallery of New South Wales forecourt), MOCA Los Angeles 2024, Roulette 2025. The key concept is "bloom": both instruments become a unified whole; background and foreground blur into "a dynamic sound field constantly in flux" (Sun Bu-er poem: "In the soundless is the ultimate pleasure"). Wong plays cello and electric guitar through Ableton Live used as a giant harmonizer (banks of overtones: add octave, fifth, 3rd harmonic, 7th harmonic per note) plus pre-recorded looping textures; the cello is amplified and panned cross-stereo across four corner speakers. Fullman's rhythmic shoveler tool punctuates the drones. "Journey to the Cave of Guanyin" (Room40, 2025): multitracked acoustic cello in just intonation, a solitary figure returning from sea to offer prayer to Guanyin. "Sound Forest / Between Worlds" (Kinosaito, 2025): installation with hollowed-log drums and a totem resonator, activated by a dancer. "Weather Eye" (2026): first recorded duo with Italian guitarist Francesca Naibo. "Vox Lumina" (in development): intermedia opera and installation on hidden and marginalized identities; Kinosaito Residency spring 2025 sketches with Taina Larot and Kia LaBeija; premiere Jan 23-24, 2027 at Brava Theater, commissioned by Other Minds. "Bodywork" (2026): homage to caring hands for two pianists, commissioned by Sarah Cahill and Regina Myers.
  • Through-line: The vehicle shrinks as the ambition grows: from eight intonarumori and Goya song cycles to one cello, one voice, one body, and finally to the listener's position in a room. The constant is the microscope: every stage of the career zooms INTO the material rather than adding more of it.

What she builds (the mechanisms, technically)

  • The fused body-instrument. She sings into a close microphone while bowing the cello. The mic rig is compositional: session photos (frame f00, 2018) show ribbon and condenser mics positioned as deliberately as the instruments. The sung tone and the bowed tone share the body's resonance and merge into a hybrid third voice. Amplification is the microscope lens: subtle vocal sounds grow into loud clicks, slurps, and squeaks (2010 Outsound Summit review).
  • Just-intonation retuning. Cello and voice tuned in just intonation, often centered on A=432Hz, sometimes in Partch's 43-tone octave (Chromelodia Project with Chris Brown). Retuning is not color; it is the material the pieces are made of (Harbors is "structured around the extended harmonics of the open strings of the cello").
  • Spatialized amplification. In the Fullman duo the cello is amplified and panned in cross-stereo across four speakers at the room's corners; the listener sits inside a dimensional field. "Soundless" runs both players through four-channel amplification so the room becomes the sounding body.
  • The Ableton harmonizer. In "Soundless" she runs cello and electric guitar through Ableton Live as a giant harmonizer: banks of overtones (octave, fifth, 3rd harmonic, 7th harmonic) added per note, plus pre-recorded textures looped on 1-4 tracks. The electronics do not decorate the instrument; they clone and detune it.
  • Graphic scores as visual artifacts. Frame f05 (2021): sparse colored arcs on white for the Keats "Xenoglossia" film-installation music. Frame f08 (2022): "Tuyshtak" scored as clarinet fingering diagrams laid over a timeline with cents deviations (-31, +4, -14), just intonation written as choreography. Frame f10 (2022): scores for the Asian Art Museum performance as point-fields (white dots, red blobs on black; dots drifting over a watercolor wash), made after Carlos Villa's work.
  • The installed song. "As We Breathe" (2020) takes the song form apart spatially: a song installed in a gallery with David Castilyo's objects, viewable online, rather than performed on a stage.
  • The object ritual. "O Horizon": audience members bring objects of any kind as instruments; Wong combines them with cello and voice into a ritual attending to "the sensual microcosm of everyday materials." The mechanism is surrender of the instrument list to strangers.
  • The ancestral altar. Frame f06 (2022): stills from "The Secret of Growth", commissioned by Naked Eye Ensemble, described as "a sort of ancestral altar made of sound and film"; the frame shows an archival elder portrait bisected by a teal field. Sound plus film as altarpiece.
  • The activated sculpture. Frame f14 (2025): "Sound Forest / Between Worlds" at Kinosaito: hollowed-log drums, a totem resonator with an oval sound hole, and a dancer in white mid-motion igniting the space. The sculpture is silent until the body moves through it.
  • Suminagashi. Frame f01 (2018): an album cover made by floating-ink marbling, fluid dynamics frozen at the moment of the print.
  • Ma as program. Frame f16 (2026): a promo built around the question "What is Ma to you?" Negative space as the organizing principle of a performance program.
