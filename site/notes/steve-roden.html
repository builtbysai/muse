Visual and sound artist, Los Angeles (b. April 27, 1964; d. September 6, 2023). Singer for the LA punk band Seditionaries 1979-1982, then BFA Otis 1986, MFA ArtCenter 1989, exhibiting since 1986. Core engine

EVERYTHING IS A SCORE FOR EVERYTHING ELSE: take an arbitrary external text (a book title, a place name, a score, a word list, a map label), fracture it through a self-invented equivalence (a letter becomes a line length, a color, a height, a duration, a material), and let that translation dictate every formal variable of the work across painting, sculpture, installation, and sound, with intuition allowed to play only in the gaps the system leaves open.

The named mechanisms:

  1. The letter-to-measure score. The Silent World paintings (2002-2005,

f-gallery 2026-01-31): "a = 1 inch line, b = 2 inch line, etc."; each painting is a visual translation of the title of Cousteau's book. The letters are a measuring device, not decoration (Pomona 2003, GalleryPlatform press). "Listen (4'33")": Cage's 93-word score text sets line lengths AND color (one hue per word), a polychrome lattice of 434 wayward lines. "Roden confounds the distinction between score and performance" (inbetweennoise.com correspondences text).

  1. Graphic scores as performances. "pavilion score 1-5" (2005, f00):

hand-drawn colored grid, each cell a note with a dot, handwritten "7 notes" / "6 notes" arrows, "Top" marker; drawn for the site-specific performance at the Serpentine Gallery summer pavilion (Siza / Souto de Moura / Balmond), performed with Paul Panhuysen. The score is the work and the performance is its realization. "sounding architecture (and pavilion scores)", 2005 (f19): a literal color legend ("Big C - red ... small C - pink") drawn into the work, the key shipped with the cipher.

  1. Text as physical instruction. "the surface of the moon" (2001,

f15): 490 objects matching 490 land formations on a turn-of-the-century moon map; "each object's height and materials was determined by the vowels in a name. the number of vowels determined the height of an object" (estate caption). Hammer Snapshot 2001, MOCA LA collection

  1. Text determines the work's physical presence; the first work

defined by predetermined formal variables.

  1. Listening scores. "50 Sounds of the sea" (1990, f01): 50 drawings

made while listening to the ocean, kept in a hand-lettered box; the ocean is the score and the hand is the transducer. "another another green world" (2003): sculptures made with eyes closed while listening to Brian Eno's LP; glaze colors grouped by rounding song lengths to the nearest half minute. "speak no more about the leaves": the vowel structure of a Stefan George poem becomes a score (five tones for five vowels), generated acoustically then processed.

  1. The coded visual on the release. Forms of Paper (2024 remaster

sleeve, f14): ruled notebook paper with erratic hand-drawn line clusters in red and green pencil, the visual score leaking onto the packaging. LINE "six sound installations" cover (f16): typewritten columns of numbers and labels, handwritten blue ink, translucent blue and orange circles overlaid on photographs; "his unique coded visual scoring" (LINE caption). The album cover is a score document, not a brand.

  1. Lowercase amplification. The sound works take quiet, usually

unheard materials (paper crumpled, breath through an organ pipe, ambient room sound) and amplify them into "possible landscapes" (his term, per ArtReview). Forms of Paper (2001): first digitally composed work, one hourlong track of highly amplified paper sounds, commissioned by the Los Angeles Public Library. Airforms (2004): five plaster-over- balloon objects built from his own breath; sound from breath blown through an old wooden organ pipe. Sound and object share one source.

  1. Collage as tactile combination. "this is a song from under the

floor boards" (2022, f04): limited edition of 50 with dynamic accordion foldouts and an original collage. "Collage has long been part of my practice, it allows for an intuitive and tactile approach in combining disparate elements on a single visual plane" (Roden, in the caption). The foldout object unfolds in time like a score being read.

Evolution

  • 1979-1982: punk singer. Seditionaries, LA. The band years are in the

obituary, and the feed's 2021-2023 captions carry the ethos forward.

  • 1986-1999: painter with a sound practice. Otis 1986, ArtCenter 1989,

exhibiting since 1986. The anatomy of touch (1989/99, f03) and sanctuary (rorschach) (1997, f06): lush, spiritual-abstractist paintings with bead-like clusters and maze glyphs. The Hot Wheel stencil drawings (1998-2000, f02): found systems (toy stencils) as drawing engines.

  • 1993 onward: In Be Tween Noise. The recording moniker for 40+ solo

releases; lowercase music named and pioneered in this decade.

  • 2001-2005: the system breakthrough. The surface of the moon (490

objects, vowels as variables) is "one of the first works to be defined by predetermined formal variables" (Vielmetter press). The silent world series (letter-to-line); Lines and Spaces later (2008): four years of work generated from fragments of one twelve-page music score, notes becoming lines, shapes, colors, hand movements.

  • 2008-2015: international survey years. Kaprow re-visioning at LACE

(2008), DAAD at the Walter Benjamin Archives, Chinati Marfa, Civitella Ranieri, Pomona "when words become forms" and the Armory "In Between: A 20 Year Survey" (2010), Dust-to-Digital book (2011), LACE video survey (2012).

  • 2017-2023: diagnosis and late work. Early-onset Alzheimer's diagnosed

2017; dies September 6, 2023. Late posts: the "this is a song from under the floor boards" edition (2022), "stars of ice" (Room40, 2022), ongoing studio documentation.

  • 2024-2026: the estate. Forms of Paper remaster (Aurora Central +

LINE, Taylor Deupree, 2024), "ear(th)" in PST Energy Fields (2024), "floating over the silent world" tribute at Vielmetter (2024), LINE "six sound installations" (2025), museum acquisitions across 16 institutions, "Steve Roden: wandering" at UCI Langson OCMA (2026).

What to take for prompts

Roden's grammar is the translation vocabulary of the whole lowercase strand: a text becomes a score, a letter becomes a measurement or a color, a word list becomes a count of objects, a poem's vowels become tones. For generative pieces the translatable moves are: show the cipher (the legend, the grid, the annotated arrows), let a fixed rule set determine every variable (line length, color, count, duration) while intuition handles only the residue, stage the score AS the image (his covers and drawings are scores first), unfold in time like the accordion foldout, and keep the visual hand modest: pencil, ink, ruled paper, felt-tip, Xerox. The work should look like something figured out on a table, not rendered. No prompt needs his sound: everything he did was already visual.
