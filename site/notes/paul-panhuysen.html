Shelf III, Sound & Music Systems

Paul Panhuysen, study #237 (2026-10-31, cron expansion)

studied 2026-10-31/10 min read/1,903 words

IG: @paul_panhuysen_legacy, the estate/archive account (links to paulpanhuysen.nl, ~441 followers, public). Paul Panhuysen (1934-2015), Dutch artist, composer, and venue builder, co-founded Het Apollohuis in Eindhoven in 1980 with his wife Helene Panhuysen and ran it as a home for sound art, installations, and experimental performance. Over 250 Long String Installations between 1982 and 2012 anchor his practice: wire stretched across chapels, pavilions, and museum halls, played by rosin-coated fingertips drawn ALONG the wire, tuned by length alone. One weak bookseller source credits Remko Scha as Apollohuis co-founder; the dominant account, repeated by ZKM, the Van Abbemuseum press release, The Wire obituary, and the estate's own captions, is Paul plus Helene. The Scha claim is marked disputed and is not used.

Core engine

MAKE NUMBER AND MATTER AUDIBLE: stretch long strings across a space, tune them by length alone, and let the room's geometry, the wind, or a rosin-coated fingertip decide what sounds.

The named mechanisms:

  1. Longitudinal rubbing, not plucking. The Long String

Installations are played by coating fingertips with rosin and drawing them lengthwise ALONG the wire, which excites longitudinal waves, not the transverse vibration of a plucked string. The strings are spatial and sculptural first, instruments second. Source: Soundohm ("long wires cross a space in different patterns and are set ringing by fingers coated in rosin, drawn along them like a bow"); ZKM long-string release page.

  1. Tuning by length only; the room is the score. The longitudinal

mode of a wire is tuned purely by its length, so pitch comes from the distance between anchor points. Watchable behavior: wires span whole chapels (St. Bernard Chapel, Plasy Monastery 1992), pavilions, and signal-hill masts. The geometry of the site composes the piece. Source: Fullman on Rewire ("The longitudinal mode is solely based on tuning by length; therefore a longer room offers...").

  1. Pythagorean ratios as literal construction. "Tunes blown by the

Wind" (2004, Cabot Tower, Signal Hill, Newfoundland): four steel strings anchored to the tower mast, tuned by length to the integer ratios 6:8:9:12, with four wooden cubes as resonators; wind chimes, resonating tins, and tins with marbles that sound when the wind blows. Corpus 2025-04-21, full caption; frame f15 shows the tins suspended on cables against the sky. The ancient integer ratio becomes construction, and the weather becomes the performer.

  1. Eyes and ears equally important. Panhuysen's preface to "Long

Strings 1982-2011": "In the long string installations, eyes and ears are equally important. This improves the understanding of reality and daily life." (forcedexposure 3-LP catalog). More than 250 installations, 1982-2012, each site-specific, positioned between constructivism and situationism. Not an instrument on a stage: a sounding event you walk inside.

  1. Numbers made audible. Number systems are turned into instruments.

"NUMBER MADE AUDIBLE 1" (1992, Bethanien, corpus 2024-06-11); the 1993 CD of eleven pieces played on invented instruments (Duochords, Spring Strings, Musical Bows, Guitars with Tails, Tubular Aluminum Monochords); "Number Made Visible" (1997, a book as artwork: 64 variations of Benjamin Franklin's magic square of 8); "THIS IS NOT A TRIANGLE OF PASCAL" (2002, Serralves, corpus 2021-12-01; frame f04 shows strings with colored discs hanging from the ceiling and tripod-mounted devices). Late and civic: "OOG+OOR EYE+EAR" (2014-15, his last public artwork): 365 sudoku-pattern compositions for the municipal carillon, played on the hour at the Rietveld bus stop in Eindhoven; frame f01 shows the illuminated geometric facade panels at night.

  1. Self-willed materials. "Ars Artis Simulacrum" (1974-1986, corpus

2023-11-02): machine-made paintings using misprints, trial and end pieces of discarded fabrics, collected and selected by the artist; the work "deals with the collaboration between a self-willed person and self-willed machines". Frame f12: a white field of dense falling dashes in blue/yellow/red against a blue plaid check fabric. The machine's errors and the fabric's weave are the composer.

  1. Living performers. Projects with animals and plants from 1990

(ZKM CV). The Kanary Grand Band aviary (2005, Klankenbos, Dommelhof, Neerpelt): a permanent aviary with electronics and purpose-built instruments where about 20 canaries perform electro-acoustic music (corpus 2021-11-23; frame f03 shows the wooden mesh aviary); the 1997 Apollohuis release documents the same interspecies collaboration ("their most enthusiastic improvisers have wings"). Instruments designed for non-human players; the score is whatever the birds do.

  1. Serial counting systems made visible. The "LEGACY IN LINES"

corpus post (2025-01-28): larger artworks "based on mathematical counting systems and chance procedures". Early anchor: the 1978 Van Abbemuseum solo (paintings, serial ordering systems, environmental designs; corpus 2022-06-23 shows "Variabele compositie" being installed). And "Equal rights for Red, Yellow and Blue" (1990 screen print, corpus 2023-04-10; frame f11: a strict grid of tilted red/blue/yellow egg-blobs on white).

Evolution

  • 1960s: material beginnings. Sewn-paper collages (1962-64, frame f00);

the 1965 "situasies" happenings; the Band of the Blue Hand; the 1967 Museumfeest; public commissions including the bright geometric glass applique of 1964 (frame f06). Also 1965's "Egg tray", gestural drips on board (frame f10).

  • 1970s: the systems turn. Serial ordering paintings, environmental

designs, the 1978 Van Abbemuseum solo; play objects for preschoolers 1972-75 (frame f17: children playing in the polka-dot structure, now donated to the Van Abbemuseum); Ars Artis Simulacrum 1974-86, the self-willed-machine paintings.

  • 1980s: venue and wire. Apollohuis directorship begins 1980; Long

String Installations begin 1982 (with Johan Goedhart until 1991); the ECHO festivals 1984/87; outdoor number-sculpture ('Mijne Heren Vieren, Vijven en Zessen' 1984: 20 columns, 2 squares, 8 pentagons, 10 hexagons).

  • 1990s: animals, machines, recordings. Animal and plant projects;

NUMBER MADE AUDIBLE; "Galvanos" and "Lost for Words": recordings fed into galvanometers with springs that vibrate at their preferred frequencies, then reamplified; 1993 Exploratorium residency.

  • 2000s: the wind takes over. "Tunes blown by the Wind" 2004; the

Kanary aviary 2005; "Coffee Time II" 2010 (mechanical long strings).

  • 2014-15: the sudoku carillon, OOG+OOR, his last public artwork.

Posthumous: the archive goes to ZKM; Apollohuis closes with the 2025 farewell exhibitions; "Museum as Sundial" at the Van Abbemuseum (17 Oct 2026 - 21 Feb 2027, eleven artists) activates his 1974 play object daily.

Network (evidenced only)

  1. Helene Panhuysen. Wife, Apollohuis co-founder, "ally, anchor,

advocate" (estate caption 2021-10-26; Van Abbemuseum press release: "under the direction of artist Paul Panhuysen and his wife Helene"). Corpus-evidenced and web-evidenced (ZKM biography, Van Abbemuseum, The Wire obituary).

  1. Johan Goedhart. Long-string collaborator 1982-1991. Web-evidenced:

ZKM CV and the 3-LP liner notes ("until 1991 mostly together with Johan Goedhart").

  1. Ellen Fullman. Panhuysen, as Apollohuis director, invited her in

1985 to document her Long String Instrument in the Netherlands; she recorded her debut album there with Arnold Dreyblatt. Web-evidenced (Rewire feature). Note the lineage direction: Fullman's instrument descends from Alvin Lucier's "Music on a Long Thin Wire"; Panhuysen hosted the work, he was not her influence.

  1. Arnold Dreyblatt. Duo Geloso; a Black Truffle LP of an unearthed

concert recording; shared fascination with numerical relationships and the infinite complexity of string harmonics. Corpus-evidenced (2022-08-23) and web-evidenced.

  1. Steve Roden. The adjacent-network pick rationale: "Park Sounds",

Serpentine Gallery, London, 2005. Panhuysen strung the Serpentine Pavilion (Siza/Souto de Moura/Balmond) to make the building resonate; Roden worked with found objects; concert 23 Sept 2005. Web-evidenced (Tokafi; Roden estate CV). This is the Serpentine 2005 mention from the #234 Roden corpus.

  1. Phill Niblock. "Loyal collaborator and above all a good friend"

(estate tribute 2024-01-14). Corpus-evidenced only; no independent web verification was run.

What to take for prompts

  • The LONG WIRE is the whole aesthetic: one thin line crossing a large

space, played LENGTHWISE by a slow fingertip, not plucked. Prompt material lives in that single gesture: visible longitudinal shimmer traveling down the wire, the room as resonator.

  • Tuning by length means the architecture is audible. Prompts can make

the anchor distances the composition: strings pinned to a chapel's far walls sound different from strings pinned to a pavilion.

  • The integer-ratio wind works (6:8:9:12) give a concrete harmonic

system to build from, with the weather as the performer. Watch-only; the piece is the weather.

  • The number works (sudoku carillon, magic squares, Pascal triangle,

equal-rights grids) give strict, legible visual systems: colored discs on hanging strings, red/yellow/blue grids, carillon panels. These suit watch-only or gently explorable pieces.

  • The self-willed materials (Ars Artis Simulacrum) invert the control:

the machine misprints, the fabric weave decides. Prompts can hand composition to a flawed process.

  • The Kanary aviary inverts the performer: instruments built for birds.

A watch-only aviary piece where non-human agents play.

  • Avoid: plucking or strumming the wire (wrong physics), treating the

installations as stage performances (they are walked inside, not watched), inventing siren/horn works or Cage encounters (unverified).
