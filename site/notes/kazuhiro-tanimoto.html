Core engine

THE PIECE IS A RULE SYSTEM RUNNING LIVE, AND THE VIEWER WATCHES EMERGENCE: Tanimoto designs computational rules from scratch (cellular automata with species, vitality, and custom neighborhoods; rectangle field systems; wave and caustic systems), builds a testable system the way a chemist builds an experiment, runs it on GPU in real time, and the artwork is the sustained instability that emerges, blooms, faces, brushstrokes, seas. Sound is never a soundtrack; it is derived from the same grid (oscillators assigned to representative pixels, color change mapped to frequency and envelope). Interaction is never decoration; the viewer always enters the system's own logic (click to release the wave sound the white noise was built for, edit the calligraphy the code generated, curate which outputs get minted, inject a shape into the running frame so the automaton absorbs it instantly).

Signature devices observed

  1. The rectangle everything (Memories of Digital Data). His Art Blocks Curated work (May 12, 2023), archived in Rhizome ArtBase. From his site: everything is composed of rectangles to evoke the digital and the pixel; depth is linked to rectangle size (distant objects are small rectangles, as if in focus); dispersion in position and color indicates the degree of deterioration of the world; sky, blocks, and water surface are identified separately, each with its own characteristics. Occasional red clouds or blocks mean missing information: they are rendered as red, the first color, because the data is gone. f10 (release announcement): a dusk seascape of rectangles, fiery data-clouds over a dark blocky "submerged city" with a water band below. f19 (2026): bright blue rectangle sea, dark vertical block-towers (the abandoned chain nodes) rising from pixelated water, white data-clouds above. The visual is the thesis: what I believed to be an eternity turns out to be a fleeting dream.
  2. Sound first, from white noise. Memories of Digital Data started with sound, not image. His site: the work is "a digitally native animated piece and is not suitable for static images"; click to play the wave sound. Since huge sound data cannot live on chain, the sound was generated from white noise; to reproduce rippling waves he studied academic papers on sound structure, completed the sound, and "the visual imagination expanded from there." The system order is sound, then image. f11 (Dec 2023, "turn on the sound"): foam-like sea of white noise texture with magenta, teal, and black churn.
  3. The 14 KB living face (GHOST IN THE CODE). Art Blocks, July 8, 2022, sold out. From his site: a project "to dwell undeletable souls on the blockchain"; a system that generates human faces with different personalities and expressions from a minimum amount of code. Personality is determined from the hash; the face keeps changing expressions after minting. "It is a living portrait, a fragment of the minter's soul, forever adrift in the vast net." His reasoning: DNA is 3 billion base pairs, and individuals differ by 0.1 percent (750 KB); he succeeded in expressing personality, emotion, and complex feeling with 14 KB. The soul is "a function described in DNA, the human program code." f04: a face barely emerging from pink noise, eyes as dark smudges; f05: a pale pixelated face with green glints in the eyes.
  4. The editable calligraphy (Wabi Sabi). Art Blocks, Nov 7, 2022. From the Art Blocks page: code dynamically generates brushstrokes on motifs of Shodo (Japanese calligraphy); it begins with a deliberate movement, an ink-soaked brush plopped onto the paper, ink splattering. The key feature: each artwork can be edited, infinitely, by the viewer. "The viewer is the artist and the artist is the viewer." Blank space is a conscious choice: wabi-sabi accepts the lacking. f06: the words "Wabi Sabi" written by the system itself in black ink with magenta bristle splits and splatter on pale paper; f07: a violent ink plop with radiating streaks and green edge fringing; f08: his parody of Snowfro's Chromie Squiggle rendered as a rainbow brushstroke by the Wabi Sabi engine.
  5. Participant curation as the mint (GHOST IN THE NET). ICONIC release (New Orleans platform): 150 generative NFTs, each with access to a matching 18x18 inch museum print, his first physical prints. From ICONIC: the drop lets participants "explore the algorithm and test an infinite number of outputs before minting," then mint up to three; "the participant [can] curate their own piece using Kazuhiro's backend code as the palette." His quote: "People interact and intertwine with each other in networks as individuals and groups, in cooperation or conflict, in empathy or feigned indifference. I wanted to express this through generative art using code." f12: soft violet network clouds with dark voids (missing nodes) and glowing purple dots.
  6. The multi-species cellular automaton with vitality (Rain Blooms). Two years of rule design. From the MASSAGE interview: non-standard neighborhoods (cells reference positions beyond any fixed Moore or von Neumann grid, set per execution); multiple species coexist with attack, assimilation, or ignore relationships; each cell carries a vitality parameter that rises and falls through interaction (attack winners absorb the opponent's vitality; assimilation shares it slightly). The system tends toward sustained instability: continuous transformation without collapsing into chaos or stasis. f14 (2025, "A Lush World of Life Through the Power of Computing"): the automaton at full bloom, a dense mottled field of magenta, cyan, green, orange colonies competing; f16 (2025): a 3D cellular automaton, a glowing diamond lattice surrounded by colored voxel clusters with an orange beam.
  7. Shapes absorbed without rupture. The GPU computes the next CA frame from the previous one; before the computation, simple shapes are drawn into the frame. His words: "There is no moment when the inserted shapes are not behaving as part of the automaton." His analogy is paint dropped on canvas, but the canvas has opinions. This is the honest version of interactivity in his work: the system never breaks character to accept input.
  8. One grid, two senses (the shared-source audio). Rain Blooms: 20x20 representative pixels, one oscillator each; pixel screen position sets stereo position; when a pixel's color change passes a threshold, frequency and ADSR are set from its hue, brightness, and saturation; harmony follows chord progressions (openly human music theory, which he calls "a very human expression," a collision of ideal and reality). His test: not whether it works as music, but whether "the density of change and the murmur within the screen can also be felt auditorily." He names Christoph Stahli's Game Of Sound as the closest kin.
  9. Small systems that run on their own (Rain Blooms Lattice). Aluminum-framed LED panels running the algorithm on embedded ESP32 microcontrollers. They are not monitors; they execute the work. His point: a PC program leans on an enormous shared infrastructure (OS, drivers); porting the core algorithm to a microcontroller proves the system is genuinely small. He is drawn to "small systems," and values that a diverse world can arise from a mechanism small enough for the blockchain.
  10. Caustics as computation made visible. 2025-2026 reels: "Generative caustic jellyfish," "generative caustics," "caustics simulation," "Living Caustics," "Caustics from the Game of Life," "Multilayer Rain Blooms," "Raindrop Ripples." f13: pale blue water, dark translucent jellyfish bells, caustic light webs with rainbow refraction along their edges; f18 (2026): blocky pixel-collage slabs in cyan and magenta around a bright yellow mass, halftone dots, datamosh-like rectangles. Light on water is his laboratory for emergence: interference you can watch.
  11. The rule-switching notebook (2026 research notes). He publishes "research notes on rule-switching cellular automata": instead of applying one rule repeatedly, rules switch mid-run. Reels show cockroach-like automata, drifting jellyfish rules, "A New Species Is Born," Conway gliders as reference points, Game of Life caustics. The IG account doubles as a lab notebook; the audience watches the experiments.
  12. The Japanese pattern engine. f09 (Mar 2023, "Japanese traditional patterns"): an asanoha (hemp-leaf) lattice, dark starburst nodes on cream with a green-to-magenta drift across the field. Traditional craft patterns generated in code, a thread that connects to his stated interest in the overlap of computer art and craft-like refinement in Japan.

Evolution: early vs recent

  • 2021: p5.js sketch era. Short looping videos, cell clusters, particle sketches. "BIO munch" (Dec 2021, Processing): dense organic colonies rendered as bright wiggly contour outlines on black (f01). "10,000 lines" (Jan 2022): a swooping bundle of deep blue and teal brush arcs on off-white (f02). genuary2022: violet hairline fields, line density as form (f03). f00: sparse glowing cells with motion-blur trails.
  • 2022: the ghost year. GHOST IN THE CODE on Art Blocks (July 8, sold out): the 14 KB face system, personality from hash, living portraits. CAT IN THE CODE. "The Four Seasons." Then Wabi Sabi on Art Blocks (Nov 7): the Shodo engine, ink plop and splatter, viewer-editable, the Chromie Squiggle parody (f08). A year of faces and brushstrokes: code pretending to be ink and soul.
  • 2023: the sound year. Memories of Digital Data on Art Blocks Curated (May 12): sound built first from white noise, the rectangle world, the abandoned-chain seascape, Rhizome ArtBase archiving. GHOST IN THE NET via ICONIC: the participant-curated mint, first physical prints. Yuragi on fxhash 2.0 (Dec, partner Volume DAO): "the interaction and flow of objects at various scales... even phenomena that appear stable are always changing when observed at different scales of size and time." f11: the white-noise sea, audiovisual.
  • 2024: the laboratory year. "Sea of Code" at NEORT++ (2024). Patterns of Flow group exhibition at NEORT++ (Sept 2024, curated by AXEL of Right Click Save and Shono Yusuke): the encounter with Hiroshi Kawano's "The Birth of Network Aesthetics" (bottom-up agents, no central command) that pushed him toward original automata. Serious ALife work begins; early Game of Life experiments prove too isotropic, too monochrome. Caustics studies and "Generative caustic jellyfish" begin.
  • 2025: the CA maturity era. Two years of rule design converge: multi-species automaton with vitality, non-standard neighborhoods, sustained instability. "A Lush World of Life Through the Power of Computing." "Patterns of Entanglement" at NEORT++ (Dec 5-21, observed through a Fresnel lens; f15: a gallery photo of the screen work with the lens rod in frame). 3D cellular automata (f16). Rhizome ArtBase inclusion announced for Memories (f17).
  • 2026: the bloom year. Rain Blooms at NEORT++ (May 15-31, Bakurocho): the immersive multi-wall installation, the ESP32 "Rain Blooms Lattice" hardware, the 128-edition Art Blocks Studio release (May 22). Generative Dialogues V at Blanc! festival (Oct, La Nau Barcelona): "Memories," a new composition every minute on sixteen synchronised screens. Rule-switching CA research notes, Game of Life caustics, cockroach automata, the lab notebook continues. "Rain Blooms" IG posts: "Raindrop Ripples," "Multilayer Rain Blooms," "Parallel," "A moving QR code that never loses its meaning."
