Core engine

Six interlocking mechanisms, each named from observed behavior:

  1. The organ as playable terrain, not sacred relic. Downes treats every pipe organ as a machine to be taken apart by hand. His captions read like a disassembly log: "Messing with the wind pressure on Stadtcasino organ (Basel)"; a tour of the Grüneberg organ's faults, "broken mixture, (2) very ripe clarinet and (3) intermittent 16' pedal reed" (f08, hand flat on the lower manual, backpack on the floor, the console shot like a workbench); "Imagine making an organ stop that just made air noise" (Jazztopad 2021); the Bristol Organ Crawl through three historic builders (f09); "Pipe forest" internals, ranks of square wooden pipes photographed from inside the case (f06). f04 shows him at a console pulling rows of long draw stops, hand mid-reach. The watchable behavior: each organ is named, dated and fault-listed like a field specimen, then played at its defects rather than around them. Web echo: Obsidian (ECM 2018) is formally a study of extended techniques "from instruments sometimes in states of disrepair", and Vyamanikal (2015, with Tom Challenger) sets one rule per local church organ: document what this instrument can do that no other can. The instrument is the composition's material, not its vessel.

Takeaway: a generative piece can start from the defect list of a fictional organ: each stop is a broken behavior (a mixture that misfires, a reed that cuts in and out), and the piece is the console that lets them be played as intended.

  1. Solo organ as orchestra: slow processes, duration, vibration. Downes describes Obsidian as "a musical response to volcanicity, slow processes that cause extreme reactions". The All About Jazz interview (Nov 2025) states it plainly: "What I love about the instrument is certainly its range, which lets me perform like an orchestra... if I am playing solo, I focus on the instrument." f16, the overhead console shot at Cologne Dom, is the mechanism made visible: four manuals cascading, curved ranks of stop tabs fanning outward like contour lines, two hands on the manuals, a notebook and phone on the bench. The console is a control surface the size of a desk, and he plays all of it. The watchable behavior: one player, leviathan spaces (Cologne Dom, "the most magical environment"; the 4-manual Marcussen at Laurenskerk, "largest of its type in Europe", f07), sound that accumulates rather than narrates. Nothing hurries.

Takeaway: watchable slow processes suit motion pieces with geological pacing: layers that take minutes to arrive, where the change is in density and beating between sustained voices, and the payoff is a state arriving, not an event occurring.

  1. Constraint-made systems. 52 Studies for Right Hand (2017, web-only): 52 piano pieces for the right hand only, one per day of a left-hand-injury recovery. The injury is the score. Vyamanikal: one rule (document one extended technique per local church organ). WHERE THEY GATHER (2026, f17, with writer Sally O'Reilly): a lockdown album with an archival-object book aesthetic, the constraint being the found materials. The watchable behavior: each project arrives as a named rule-set with a finished, bounded body of work. He does not jam toward a record; he declares the box, then fills it.

Takeaway: prompts can state their constraint as the piece's identity ("one rule, 52 instances"), and the generative system should be able to enumerate its full output set, not just loop.

  1. Long-running duo partnerships as the core unit. Norma Winstone (Outpost of Dreams, ECM 2024, recorded Udine 2023; f12, the sunlit selfie: two people, equal billing, named place). Lucy Railton (Subaerial 2021, Dreamlife of Debris 2019, Breaking the Shell 2024). Tom Challenger (Wedding Music 2013 through Dreamlife 2019, the Vyamanikal organ tours). Delphine Joussein (Happy Hurricanes 2026). Susanna Sundfor (Revelations of Divine Love 2026). The duos span decades, not sessions, and the recordings are usually named for the place they happened in. The watchable behavior: two voices, one room, a title that is often a location.

Takeaway: a duet system (two agent voices with different rule-sets, sharing one field) is the natural generative translation: not one complex voice, but two simple ones in negotiation.

  1. Parallel band ecologies, distinct instrument rules. ENEMY (piano trio with Petter Eldh and James Maddren: Enemy 2018, Vermillion 2022, The Betrayal 2023, Fiend 2025) is piano bass drums with a brutalist graphic identity; f13, the grunge black-and-white tour flyer for "THE BETRAYAL" release tour 2023, is typography as blunt as the music. Deadeye (Hammond trio with Reinier Baas and Jonas Burgwinkel: Deadeye 2022, In Orbit 2025 recorded live in one day) is a different organ, different ethic. Exhaust (Camila Nebbia, Andrew Lisle, Intakt 2026) is a third. Each band is a different instrument ecosystem, a different recording ethic, a different visual world. He does not develop one project; he keeps several ecologies alive in parallel and moves between them.

Takeaway: one artist can supply three prompts from three projects without repeating a driver, as long as each project's instrument rule is honored. Parallelism is the mechanism, not indecision.

  1. Visual collaboration as an integral layer. Juliane Schütz (@iwontsignuphere) recurs across the corpus like a band member: 'Rekærblæd' music with microscopic visuals (f03, the icy-liquid blue field with thread-lines and dark speck clusters, first-hand: it looks like melting ice seen through a microscope), live visuals for Dr SNAP at Bimhuis (f14), the double-exposure Deadeye promo (f05), the ENEMY Fiend video, tour flyers (f13). Nearly every release arrives inside a Schütz visual world, and each project gets a distinct visual grammar: grunge typography for ENEMY, double exposure for Deadeye, microscopy for Rekærblæd. The music posts never arrive bare.

Takeaway: a piece's visual identity can be treated as a collaborator with its own grammar per project, not as a skin applied at the end.

  • Mid (2013-2021): the ECM turn. Plays on Chris Hyson's Little Moon Man / Alive with Closed Eyes piano EPs (2013, RAM-era friendship). 52 Studies for Right Hand (2017) turns injury into method. ECM move: Obsidian (2018, solo organ, volcanic slow processes), Dreamlife of Debris (2019, quintet), Subaerial (2021, with Lucy Railton), Vermillion (2022, ENEMY).   • Late (corpus, 2021-2026): Berlin-based organ-tourism diary: a named instrument per city, faults logged, consoles photographed like workbenches. Deadeye, Dr SNAP, the Norma Winstone duo (Outpost of Dreams, ECM 2024), Breaking the Shell with Bill Frisell and Andrew Cyrille (2024), curator of the Luisen105 improv night (from April 2026), Deutsche Jazzpreis 2025 (Piano/Keyboard), Southern Bodies solo organ album upcoming. One-liner: a Berlin-based organ fieldworker whose practice is equal parts instrument, place and partnership.

Aesthetic logic

Downes's logic is materialist: the instrument is the score, the room is the arrangement, the constraint is the form, the partner is the counterweight. Nothing is decorative. A broken mixture is not a flaw to edit out; it is the reason the piece exists. A duet is not accompaniment; it is two rule-sets negotiating a shared field. The visual world is not packaging; it is a second voice (Schütz). For generative work, this means: start from a physical system with named faults, give it a slow process and a strict constraint, put two agents in it, and let the visuals be a collaborator with their own grammar. The organ console, four manuals and curved stop tabs, is the perfect image of this logic: a machine whose every control is visible, and whose music is what happens when a person takes it apart while playing it.
