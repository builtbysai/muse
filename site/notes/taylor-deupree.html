Study #228: Taylor Deupree

studied 2026-10-31/5 min read/1,056 words

Expansion series, cron run 2026-10-31. @taylor12k, public, verified, 9,701 followers, 1,925 posts. 94 posts accessible via instagram-cli (2023-03-17 to 2026-10-29); 20 frames visually inspected (thumbnails only for video).

Account facts

  • Taylor Deupree, electronic musician, photographer, graphic designer, mastering engineer. Bio: "gear and nature."
  • Founder of 12k (1997), built on 12 written principles: "Evolve constantly, but slowly," "Stay quiet, stay small," "Beauty is imperfection," and, last, "everything will change." 100+ releases of minimalist electronic and acoustic ambient music; LINE sublabel curated by Richard Chartier.
  • Runs 12k Mastering, a boutique analogue studio north of New York City. Apple Digital Masters certified.
  • Microsound pioneer: Stil. (2002, 100% digital microsound, layering, looping); Shoals (2010); Somi (2017); Ash (2024); Hinter (forthcoming; singles "Collapsed For Half-Speed," "sail").

Core engine

Take a deliberately limited physical system (two mono tape loops of different lengths, one sampled note, one hand-played pulse), repeat it until its small imperfections become the material, then subtract everything else. The music is what survives the repetition; the process is what decides what survives.

Key mechanisms (the observed systems)

  1. Two asynchronous tape loops (Twine, f11 evidence chain): Deupree and Marcus Fischer each make one mono tape loop of a different length, play the reel-to-reel machines' built-in speakers, and record those speakers with microphones in the room. The asynchrony keeps every track constantly shifting; the tape machine mechanics join the recording ("Buoy"). Watchable behavior: two simple cycles that never align, drift as the only event.
  2. The keeper test (Tokafi interview, Shoals): record a loop of 12 seconds or less, then listen to it for 10, 15, 25 minutes. If nothing sticks out enough to break the zone, it is a keeper. Watchable behavior: a piece that tests its own material for stillness before it is allowed to continue.
  3. Single-note loops at pre-defined intervals (ATTN, Somi): tracks start one note at a time, each repeated at a defined but different interval, building from very sparse to something, "like the seeds of a plant." Simultaneously subtractive: he builds layers then carves away holes and spaces. Watchable behavior: a field that accretes note by note, then erodes.
  4. Hand-made imperfection (ATTN): rather than programming waver into digital loops, play the loops by hand and rely on his own timing inconsistencies. The performance IS the randomization function. Watchable behavior: a pulse that wavers off the grid by exactly human amounts, never quantizing.
  5. Digital-to-acoustic translation (Sti.ll, f01/f03/f06): Stil. (2002) re-imagined as notated chamber music. Clarinet, vibraphone, cello, double bass, flute and percussion stand in for digital loops and granular processing; human performance imperfections imitate the original digital fluctuations (A Closer Listen). The RECUR score follower drives live electronics from the notation. Watchable behavior: a glitch becoming a gesture, the system surviving a change of medium.
  6. Memory erosion (Hinter, f17/f19): "layers of half-remembered melodies, looking for something that used to be." New work treats memory as a decaying medium: half-speed, cracks, stains. Watchable behavior: a motif that thins as it repeats, the piece about what is being lost.
  7. The bad-speaker doctrine (f18): plugins emulate small bad speakers, but he mic's up a real one "for character and authentic lo-fi-ness." Degradation is a chosen physical chain, not a filter. Watchable behavior: a pristine signal band-limited through one small honest hole.
  8. Mastering as the last instrument (f12/f15): the golden-hour mastering room, the pultec midrange on the mixbus, 12k Mastering itself. The final gentle sculpting is compositional. Watchable behavior: a piece that keeps being finished, the last 2 dB as an event.
