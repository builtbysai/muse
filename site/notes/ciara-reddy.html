Shelf V, Light, Color & the Eye

Ciara Reddy

studied 2026-11-01/11 min read/2,257 words

Study date: 2026-11-01. ig-artist-study #258.

Site: @ciara.is.reddy (976 followers, 1,415 following, 295 posts, public, no verified badge; bio and name fields empty). Ciara Reddy is a UK photographer and cinematographer, the principal visual collaborator of feeo (Theodora Laird): press photographer, co-director and cinematographer across the 'Goodness' (AD 93) campaign of 2024 to 2025. On her own feed she is a diarist first and a hired eye second: 13 years of posts, from a 2013 sky-up selfie to a 2026 overhead elemental portrait, with the professional register arriving in the last two years.

Her work has one through-line and it is positional, not stylistic. The camera is always inside the event. In the early years that means candids taken from within the group, night photos taken inside the night, art-space documentation taken beside the work. In the mature register it means the camera hangs directly over the subject's body while that body is placed inside water, seaweed, foam. The early backlit silhouettes and the late chiaroscuro dance cinematography are the same engine with better tools: hard light, one bright plane, everything else surrendered to shadow or burn. She does not compose scenes from outside. She arranges conditions (a body in water, a rope pinned sharp against a dissolved field, a smoke column at sunset, a bread loaf wired to headphones) and then stands inside them.

Doorway: Hop from the Theodora Laird study (#257): Reddy is the principal visual collaborator named across that study's whole visual system, the credited author of the 'Goodness' press portraits and the 'The Mountain' cinematography. Where the Laird study is about a musician who makes the body the image, this study is about the image-maker who built that body's visual register. M1. Overhead elemental portraiture, the late grammar

The mature register, visible twice and already consistent. The 2026 carousel cover (f18, own feed, newest post): a blonde figure in a black top and red trousers stands on a vast green textured ground, likely algae or seaweed, camera directly overhead, subject looking up into the lens. The 2025 feeo 'Goodness' press shots (research-leg frames, credited to her in The Everyday's files): feeo prone in dark water ringed by milky foam swirls, looking straight up, and floating on her back with arms out, sun glitter on ripples, pale rocks. Same grammar: body small in an element, camera at the zenith, direct eye contact or total surrender. The humble ancestor is the 2017 trash-bin overhead (f5): tulips, a coffee cup, a Metro newspaper, shot straight down into a bin. The vertical axis is the oldest habit in the feed; what changed is what lies underneath it. Her own shadow crosses the water-press frame, which is the M1 rule in one detail: the photographer never leaves the scene, she only changes altitude.

Takeaway: put the camera at the zenith and the body inside the element; the shadow in frame is the signature.

M2. Backlit hard light as compositional material

The longest-running engine in the feed, from 2016 to the professional work. f3 (2016): two silhouettes, sun flare blowing out the frame, one arm raising a drink that catches the light like a lamp. f9 (2018): a hand reaching into the sun over green water, the frame mostly flare, the hand a dark red cut-out. The mature form is the 'The Mountain' cinematography (2025, co-directed with feeo, shot by Reddy): feeo mid-dance in near-darkness, a white tank top the only bright surface, face in shadow, darkness itself doing the composing. The mechanism is consistent: one hard source, one bright plane (skin, white cloth, a glass), everything else exposed for the burn. The early frames blow out by accident of the phone; the late frames blow out on purpose. The through-line is that the light is never illumination, it is the subject's partner in the frame.

Takeaway: one hard source, one bright plane; expose for the burn, let the rest go.

M3. Bodies placed inside elements

The content version of M2. f16 (2020 carousel cover): a body submerged in green water, sunlight caustics drawing lines across the skin, the frame mostly water. The 2019 waterfall video still: moss and rushing water, no figure at all, the element photographed close enough to become abstract. The 'tnteen' set piece (2024, art department credit): a huge smoke or fire column at sunset with a small hooded figure crouching before it. Water, fire, green earth, the 2018 moonlit coastal path with its pink cloud streaks: her subjects are always elements, and figures are placed inside them the way props are placed on a table. The feed's early version is candid (friends swimming, sunsets), the late version is directed (fee o in dark water), but the mechanism never changes: pick the element first, then put the body where the element performs.

Takeaway: choose the element before the figure; the figure is the probe, the element is the instrument.

M4. The staged deadpan tableau

The comic register, and the one where her art-department thinking is most visible. f7 (2017): a bearded man in headphones crouches on a concrete floor, listening intently to a loaf of bread with a cable coming out of it, deadpan. f12 (2019): night, two people sitting in an open car boot with a black cloth draped over the roof so only their legs dangle, faces hidden, a backpack on the tarmac, a hoarding behind them. Both are built from mundane materials, arranged with complete seriousness, and photographed straight. The humor is never winked at; the camera treats a wired bread loaf like a product shot. This is the same hand that does 'tnteen' art department: arrange conditions, then document the result without commentary. The deadpan tableau is the stills version of her M3 set pieces.

Takeaway: arrange the absurd with total seriousness; photograph it straight and let the materials be the joke.

M5. Candid flash night, inside the social event

The diary register that never left. f6 (2017): a group crouching under a bus shelter at night beside a giant backlit Coke ad, wet pavement, flash on, one woman drinking from a bottle, everyone mid-laugh; the frame is tilted and crowded and alive. f8 (2018): two women in a long restroom mirror, motion blur, one holding the phone; the shot admits its own making. f11 (2018 video cover): dancing bodies close up, blurred into color bands. The technique is phone-native: on-camera flash, motion blur, tilted framing, the photographer clearly a participant in the night she is shooting. This register persists even as the professional work gets formal; it is the engine that lets her be inside the scene rather than outside it, and it feeds directly into M1's photographer-visible-in-frame rule.

Takeaway: shoot from inside the night, flash on, blur admitted; participation is the lens.

M6. The elevated snapshot and found humor

The editorial eye applied to nothing. f5 (2017): the trash-bin overhead, tulips and a coffee cup arranged by chance, seen by someone who photographs it instead of throwing it away. f14 (2019): a sheep behind a wooden gate under a sign reading BEWARE OF THE DOG, ENTER AT YOUR OWN RISK, the wrong animal under the warning photographed straight. f13 (2019): a football match photographed off the television screen, the players blurred, the Gazprom boards sharp, the mediation admitted. These are the feed's comic relief and its training ground: the overhead axis (M1), the straight deadpan (M4), the candid inclusion (M5) all get exercised on throwaway subjects before they become campaign tools.

Takeaway: photograph the found joke straight; the throwaway frame is where the grammar is trained.

Aesthetic logic: altitude and inclusion

Her whole development is a change in altitude with the inclusion rule held constant. 2013 to 2016 is the ground-level diary: selfies, friends sprawled on gallery chairs, art spaces documented beside the work, all candid and phone-lit. 2017 to 2020 is the grammar forming: the overhead axis appears (trash bin), the staged tableau appears (bread, car boot), the elements appear (water body, moonlit coast), and the exposure pushes harder. 2024 to 2026 is the professional register: the same engines with cinema tools, co-directing with feeo, shooting press portraits for Clash and The Everyday, the zenith camera returning as a personal signature in 2026. The one thing that never changes across 13 years is inclusion: the photographer is never outside the picture's world. Her shadow is in the water frame, her hand-shadow is in the projection frame, her reflection is in the mirror frame, and the feeo caption that names her says it plainly: "By my woman." The images are always taken by someone who is in them.

Evolution

2013-2016 (the diary): sky-up selfies, backlit friend silhouettes, gallery candids, art-space documentation. Phone, available light, the group as subject.

2017-2020 (the grammar forms): overhead compositions, staged deadpan tableaus, flash night candids, bodies in water and landscapes, exposure pushed until detail burns. The feet get personal-project ambition: the 2020 submerged body carousel is the first image in the feed that could be a portfolio piece.

2024-2026 (the professional register): feeo campaign work only, as far as the evidence shows: 'tnteen' art department (2024), 'run over' press photography (2024), 'The Mountain' co-direction and cinematography (2025), 'The Hammer Strikes The Bell' cinematography (2025), the 'Goodness' water press portraits (2025). Her own feed goes quiet (no posts 2021 to 2023 in the sample, two posts in 2024-2026), which reads as the diary becoming a working photographer's account. The 2026 overhead elemental portrait is the first personal post that fully speaks the professional grammar.
