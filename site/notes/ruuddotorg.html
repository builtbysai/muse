The distinctive thing

Exhaustive combinatorial enumeration as art. He does not sample or randomize: he picks a discrete mathematical object, generates EVERY configuration of it, and plots them all as a tidy grid of line-art panels. The count IS the title of the piece. The caption cites an OEIS sequence number as a certificate of completeness ("enumerated by OEIS sequence A000085"). The work has the feel of a beautifully drawn mathematical inventory, a census of possibilities.

Visual signatures (what makes it his, not generic generative art)

  1. The full census. Never 20 random samples. If the object has 76

members, all 76 are shown, one per panel, on a strict grid.

  1. Dots: filled vs open. The saved post's atom is six dots, three filled

black and three white with black outlines, connected by thin straight or slightly curved pairing lines. Minimal ink, no shading.

  1. Monoline pen-plotter linework on light paper. Everything reads as

single-pass pen work: uniform stroke weight, no fills except the dots, no decoration. Occasional single accent color (a Valentine hearts row, four colored variations of one theme); default is black ink.

  1. The count as caption. Titles are statements of number + object +

enumeration ("The 19 ways to split a 2x2x2 cube into two equal tetracubes"). The OEIS citation is the signature flourish.

  1. The plot is the artifact. He posts timelapse videos of the AxiDraw

drawing the piece; the physical line quality is part of the work.

How the style translates to code

  • Enumerate, don't sample. Use backtracking/recursion to generate the

complete configuration list (involutions, matchings, spanning trees, polyominoes, cube nets), then lay them out on a grid, one panel each. Completeness is the concept; the algorithm must provably cover all cases.

  • Fixed dot placement. Choose a canonical node layout (points on a

circle, or a 3x2 / 4x4 lattice). Dots are small circles: filled #1a1a1a or white with #1a1a1a outline. Pair/edge lines are single thin strokes; keep them straight or barely curved so they read as plotted lines.

  • Grid layout math. panels = configurations; cols = ceil(sqrt(n)) with

aspect tuned to the page; generous margins like a print on paper.

  • Palette. Ground near-paper #f4f1ea or #efe9dc, ink #1a1a1a, accent

at most one flat color (rust #b5542d or indigo #2b3a67). No gradients, no glow, no photorealism.

  • Plotter honesty in digital pieces. Thin strokes, no thick outlines,

avoid fills larger than dots. The digital piece should look like it COULD be plotted in one pass.

Fit for the doodle pipeline

Hans's taste (from the 2026-10-13 direction): motion loops, motion, 3D are the direction; static gets discarded. Ruud's static grids become pieces by turning enumeration into time: one hero panel cycling through all configurations (a net folding, edges regrowing, chords retargeting), or the full grid as a scan that sweeps row by row. Touch/drag scrubs the enumeration; idle auto-plays the loop. No click-to-reload.
