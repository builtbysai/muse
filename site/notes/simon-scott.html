Study #231: Simon Scott (2026-10-31, cron expansion)

studied 2026-10-31/8 min read/1,645 words

Account: @_simonscott, display name "Simon Scott", bio "Mastering engineer at @spsmastering + Dr of the East Anglian Fens and the @slowdiveofficial drummer". Public, not verified, 11,076 followers. Cambridge-born, UK-based; doctorate (PhD) awarded May 2026 at the Royal Festival Hall, UAL CRiSAP, thesis "The Multiphonic Fens" (supervisors Angus Carlyle, Mark Peter Wright), available at the British Library. Slowdive drummer (Souvlaki with Brian Eno, Creation); founded Kesh Recordings 2006; mastering engineer SPS Mastering. Research: 1,221 IG posts pulled via instagram-cli (1,217 own, 2011-08-13 to 2026-10-31), 20 frames stratified across eras and visually inspected, web leg: Fluid Radio 2012 interview, Secret Thirteen "Sounds from the Marshes", Caught by the River "The Sounds Below Sea Level" 2015, Peek-A-Boo 2019 interview, 12k.com Apart notes, Headphone Commute, Soundohm, Forced Exposure (Index/Kainos), Clot Magazine, UAL thesis record.

Core engine

One flat landscape (the East Anglian Fens) treated as a complete instrument: he records it with alien ears (hydrophones, contact mics, piezo on resonating wire), processes the recordings electronically (MaxMSP, modular, two banks of reverb), and plays the mix back into the place itself, so the landscape is simultaneously the score, the instrument, and the venue.

Evolution

  • 2011-2012 (the _sealevel research years): hydrophone recordings in the Basilica Cistern (Istanbul) and Gdansk, Fenland field trips with piezo on resonating wire, two years of four-season Fen recording. The _sealevel exhibition May 25-28 2012 (ink sketches, photographs, sound); the live ~sealevel rig (laptop, mixer, pedals, f04); Below Sea Level released on 12k (12k1071) with a 74/80-page journal containing the essay "An Exploration of the Subterranean Fenland Environment". Account is a fieldwork diary: what he hears, what he draws, what he carries.
  • 2013-2014: the Fen photos deepen (Red Fen, Holme Fen lake); Kompakt Pop Ambient 2014 package; Slowdive reformation (2014). _sealevel (return).
  • 2015-2016: 'FloodLines', his debut installation, set up May 2015 (Touch, TONE 053CD); Insomni (Ash International 2015); Grace seeds; jamming with James Blackshaw (#nostudio).
  • 2017: live collaboration with Marcus Fischer at S1 Portland on Oct 27 (cassette loops, slow improvisation, later released as Shape Memory); teaching listening and field recording with Chris Watson (The Colour of Sound); eurorack/modular strand intensifies (Morphagene, Mutable Instruments, Make Noise).
  • 2018: Shape Memory with Marcus Fischer on 12k (12k1088, June); Grace EP on Touch (12-string acoustic through modular, Unitarian Church pipe organ recorded with windows open in the rain); LongLake festival lineup: Slowdive, Fennesz, Mogwai.
  • 2019: Soundings (Touch), "sonic flux": fragments recorded in airports, hotel rooms, on the tour bus, modular treatments, live strings (Zachary Paul, Charlie Campagna); Clot Magazine calls it an audio diary / sonic passport.
  • 2020: two hard turns. Apart (12k): his father died of Covid-19 on April 3, 48 hours later he recorded skylarks at New Decoy and composed piano around the Fen recordings (mastered by Taylor Deupree). Index debuts on iDEAL Recordings (Kainos): Brexit-era Houses of Parliament recordings modular-mangled into caustic noise sculpture with Rachel Goswell's voice. Migrations (Touch, 300 copies): Holme Fen contact-mic recordings, May 22-25.
  • 2021: 'Emergency Exit' in the HYPER-POSSIBLE festival exhibition at Coventry Cathedral (climate emergency); "ice mirror - sonority of the fens" (video); Californian wilderness modular performance.
  • 2022-2023: the mastering years (SPS): Seefeel for Warp, Kate Carr, Deary, Mildred Maude, Longform Editions; Burberry autumn/winter show soundscape commission (environmental recording, London).
  • 2024-2026: snow-melting rhythms recorded in Koln while on tour with Slowdive; Three Quarter Skies project; PhD awarded at the Royal Festival Hall (May 2026), thesis "The Multiphonic Fens" at the British Library.

Arc summary: 2011-2014 = the Fen as fieldwork diary (record, sketch, exhibit); 2015-2020 = the practice formalized into albums, installations, and a grief record; 2021 onward = the practice institutionalized (PhD, cathedral, mastering studio) while staying in the same few square miles of drained wetland. The landscape never changed; the listening apparatus around it deepened.

Key mechanisms (what each IS + watchable behavior)

  1. Hydrophone listening (alien-ear capture). Submerging hydrophones (Istanbul's Basilica Cistern, Gdansk harbor) to hear a place from inside its water instead of above it. Watchable: a rope or cable disappearing into dark water, the visible world going quiet while an invisible one is monitored through headphones. Frames: f00, f02.
  2. Piezo on resonating wire ("The Liminal"). Homemade contact mics on fences and wires; wind oscillating the One Hundred Foot Drain's wire fence like a taut piano string. Watchable: bare infrastructure (fence posts, guy wires) humming and vibrating in wind, the mundane object revealed as an instrument. Frames: f05, f06; Caught by the River.
  3. Four-season recording as composition. Below Sea Level built from two years of Fen recordings across four seasons; the stated aim was to capture what is temporary and changing. Watchable: one flat place revisited under different skies, the same view accumulating different sounds; the mirror lake of f08 as a held image that the piece never leaves.
  4. Organic/processed balance (MaxMSP post-production). The hard part, in his own words, was keeping the Fenland character present while twisting recordings into compositional forms in MaxMSP; live he used "two banks of reverbs" with controllable liveliness and length, scrubbing to find loopable areas. Watchable: a laptop+mixer rig (f04) turning lake and gate-post recordings into loops in real time, the same source flickering between document and music. Source: Secret Thirteen; Caught by the River; f04.
  5. Playback into the place (landscape as monitor). Finished mixes played out of portable speakers and re-recorded in the open Fens, so the environment becomes the final reverb. Watchable: speakers set down in a field, the piece visibly re-entering the place that made it. Source: Caught by the River.
  6. The bridge as gong (found percussion). A water bottle accidentally striking the steel bridge over a drainage ditch rang "like a gong"; he then played the bridge with soft mallets ("Long Drove"), and looped steel Fen posts driven in 1851 for ground readings ("Holme Fen Posts"). Watchable: a person gently striking large rusted steel in a flat landscape and listening to it ring; the strike point becoming a rhythm. Source: Cortical Art/Room40; f18 (rusted steel, snow).
  7. Skylark grief (Apart). 48 hours after his father's Covid-19 death he recorded declining skylarks at New Decoy, "with urgency to create an archival audio document before the skylarks disappeared", and wove a piano piece around the Fen recordings. Watchable: a recorder pulled from a backpack in the moment of grief; one bird's song held against loss. Source: 12k.com Apart.
  8. Cassette-loop improvisation (Shape Memory). At S1 Portland he and Marcus Fischer made cassette loops and improvised "a gradually shifting sound tone that evolved and developed sonically very slowly", released because the captured moment "sounded beautiful enough to share". Watchable: cassette loops turning, two players with a shared listening aesthetic, no fixed structure, the tone drifting. Frames: f12 (S1 flyer), f13 (Shape Memory cover art). Source: Peek-A-Boo.
  9. Modular-mangled live streams (Index/Kainos). Houses of Parliament recordings from the Brexit prelude fed through a modular synth into "caustic, dissonant style of noise sculpture", eventually resolving into "a more spacious, textured sense of relief", with Rachel Goswell's voice threading through. Watchable: news and politics entering machines and exiting as convulsive clangor, then calming. Frames: f15 (Kainos cover). Source: Forced Exposure.
  10. Ice as instrument (ice mirror). Contact microphones placed in huge sheets of Wicken Fen ice (Below Sea Level era); the 2021 "ice mirror" video holds on cracked ice and meltwater. Watchable: close-ups of ice surfaces crackling and shifting, the frozen mere as a percussion skin. Frames: f16 (video poster, motion/sound unseen); Caught by the River.
